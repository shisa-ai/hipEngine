# Accuracy recalibration proposal — predeclared 2026-09-16

Status: **proposal for user decision; not a threshold change and not an
admission of any candidate**

This document freezes a campaign *before* any of its measurements exist. It
implements the recommendation in
[`PRODUCTION-ACCURACY-POLICY-REVIEW-2026-08-31.md`](PRODUCTION-ACCURACY-POLICY-REVIEW-2026-08-31.md).
Nothing here changes `EvaluationThresholds` or `Bf16NoninferiorityThresholds`.
The current envelope in
[`EXECUTION-PROFILES.md`](EXECUTION-PROFILES.md) remains normative and remains
the automatic-admission lane.

## The decision this asks for

The current production numerical gate applies the same point limits to scopes of
very different sizes: 450 rows overall, 100–150 rows per category, and **18 rows**
for the prefill transition. An 18-row scope cannot support a `0.001` mean-KL
decision, because the sampling error of an 18-row mean is comparable to the limit
itself. The gate is therefore strict as a fixture-acceptance rule while being weak
evidence about deployed quality.

That mismatch currently blocks measured performance. The largest directly
measured case is the layer-2 Q5_K grouped-WMMA route: **+5.34% on the p508
protocol with all 20 category-balanced pairs faster**, passing the aggregate
envelope, every category, determinism, state, and lifecycle, and failing only the
18-row prefill mean KL at `0.001179` against a `0.001` limit.

The proposal is to add a **borderline-review lane** that requires more evidence
rather than lowering a limit. The choice is:

1. **Approve** the campaign as specified, run it, and decide the borderline lane
   from its results.
2. **Reject** and keep the current envelope as the only lane.
3. **Amend** specific predeclared items before any measurement, which is
   permitted and is the reason this document is written first.

The one thing this document must not be used for is to justify a band chosen to
include `0.001179`. The revised numbers must come from the campaign.

### Frozen metrics

Per scope, all of: mean KL, p95, p99, max row KL, overall top-1, per-scope top-1,
row count, and the prompt-clustered interval for mean KL. Plus, where BF16 is
available, the additional-budget metrics defined below. A scope passes only if
every metric passes. Categories still cannot compensate for one another.

### Frozen decision rule

One rule, applied unchanged to every borderline candidate:

- **Hard reject** on any semantic or control-ownership mismatch, non-finite
  output, nondeterminism, isolation or lifecycle failure, task regression,
  missing strict fallback or provenance, a T3 change presented as same-quant
  drift, or any row above the absolute safety ceiling.
- **Automatic admit** when the current envelope passes. Unchanged.
- **Borderline review** only when all of the following hold:
  1. the global envelope passes;
  2. every exact semantic, state-ownership, determinism, isolation, and
     lifecycle requirement passes;
  3. no row exceeds the manual-review boundary;
  4. the *only* failures are a small-scope mean or p95, or a single-row top-1
     boundary; and
  5. the candidate can supply the expanded task and BF16-relative evidence.

  The lane means **requires more evidence**. It is not promotion on a waived
  failure. A candidate that enters it is still rejected until the review
  completes and a separately frozen policy authorizes a fresh decision.

The rule is applied to **every** candidate on the borderline inventory, not only
the largest speed win. Selecting by measured gain is how a recalibration becomes
a speedup in disguise.

## Predeclared sample sizes

The 18-row prefill scope is the binding constraint. Choosing a larger sample
requires knowing the between-prompt spread of per-prompt mean KL, which the
campaign must estimate from the frozen controls before the review rule can be
fixed. The procedure is fixed here; the number is not.

Procedure:

1. Compute per-prompt mean KL for each frozen control at its current sample size.
2. Estimate the between-prompt standard deviation `sigma` of per-prompt mean KL
   within a scope. Treat prompts as the independent unit.
3. Choose the target half-width `h` for the prompt-clustered 95% interval of
   mean KL, as a fraction of the limit under review.
4. Required prompts `n = ceil((1.96 * sigma / h)^2)`.
5. Take the maximum over the controls, then round up to the next multiple of the
   prompt-packet size.

For orientation only, and not as a substitute for step 2: if `sigma` is on the
order of `8e-4`, the numbers implied are roughly

| Target half-width `h` | Fraction of the `0.001` limit | Prompts needed |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `3.7e-4` | 37% | ~18 |
| `2.0e-4` | 20% | ~62 |
| `1.0e-4` | 10% | ~246 |
| `5.0e-5` | 5% | ~984 |

This table is illustrative arithmetic on an assumed `sigma`. It is included so
the scale of the problem is visible before the campaign runs, not to pre-commit
a sample size that the controls have not yet earned.

**A campaign that does not expand the 18-row scope cannot distinguish the current
policy from a policy that admits `0.001179`.** That is the whole reason the
sample size is being chosen at all.

## Prompt-clustered uncertainty

Correlated rows must not be counted as independent evidence. The current
evaluator computes percentiles and means over rows, and a single prompt
contributes 24 rows to a category. The campaign computes:

- per-prompt mean KL as the unit of observation;
- a prompt-clustered bootstrap interval for the scope mean, resampling prompts
  rather than rows;
- the row-level percentiles unchanged, because p95 and p99 are row statements
  and should stay row statements; and
- a paired comparison between strict and candidate on the same prompts.

The campaign reports both the row-level and prompt-clustered numbers. Where they
disagree, the prompt-clustered interval governs the mean and the row-level value
governs the tail.

## BF16-relative comparison

The current BF16 defaults were **not** re-estimated from the same-quant
calibration controls. The campaign closes that gap by measuring, for each frozen
control and each borderline candidate:

- candidate additional mean KL versus BF16, relative to strict;
- candidate top-1 drop versus BF16, relative to strict; and
- the paired prompt bootstrap interval for both.

The limits stay at their current defaults during the campaign. Re-estimating them
is a separate decision that requires its own frozen controls.

## Task-grounded checks

Proxy metrics do not establish deployed quality, and the 2026-08-31 layer-2
artifact records `review_performed=false`. The campaign runs, per candidate and
per frozen control:

| Check | What it measures | Predeclared pass rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executable code | Generated code is run against unit tests | No test that passes under strict fails under the candidate |
| Structured output | Generated JSON/schema parses | Parse rate not lower than strict by more than the paired interval allows |
| Multilingual | Scored outputs on the `general_ja` and `mixed_ja_en` categories | No category regression beyond the paired interval |
| Retrieval / long context | Answer accuracy with the answer placed at increasing depth | No depth shows a regression beyond the paired interval |
| Longer generation | Quality over generations longer than the current 32–48 token checks | No regression beyond the paired interval |

Each check reports its own sample size and its own interval. A check that cannot
reach a predeclared sample size is reported as **unmeasured**, not as a pass.

## Sampled-generation seeds

Greedy output is not enough, because the measured differences here are small
deterministic perturbations that can change a near-tie argmax without changing
task quality. The campaign predeclares a fixed seed set for sampled generation
and compares strict against candidate at each seed. The seeds are fixed in the
campaign artifact before any generation is run, and the same seeds are used for
every candidate so that candidates are comparable to one another.

## What the campaign must publish before any default changes

- the frozen controls with their raw hashes and labels;
- the estimated `sigma` and the sample size it implies;
- every candidate's complete metric tuple at the expanded sample size;
- the task-check results, including any check reported as unmeasured;
- the sampled-generation results at the predeclared seeds;
- the keep or reject outcome for each candidate under the frozen rule; and
- an explicit statement of which item above, if any, was amended after
  measurement began.

Only after that may a change to `EvaluationThresholds` be proposed, and it must
be proposed as its own decision with its own evidence.

## Cost and sequencing

The campaign is dominated by the expanded prefill scope. At roughly 246 prompts
for a 10% half-width, the prefill evaluation alone is about 14x the current
18-row scope. That cost is the substance of the decision: the current policy is
cheap and strict, and a defensible replacement is neither cheap nor quick.

A staged version is permitted and is the recommended sequencing:

1. Measure `sigma` on the frozen controls. This is small and decides whether the
   rest is affordable.
2. If the implied sample size is acceptable, run the expanded prefill scope and
   the BF16-relative comparison on the frozen controls only. Do not run
   candidates yet.
3. Freeze the review rule from those results.
4. Then, and only then, run the borderline inventory under the frozen rule.

Steps 1 and 2 must not be skipped, because they are what makes step 4 a
calibration rather than a search for a threshold that admits a known candidate.

## Why this is a policy decision and not an engineering step

The measurement work is ordinary. What cannot be decided by engineering is
whether the project wants a second lane at all: a lane that is slower to
evaluate, requires task evidence that does not exist today, and would admit
routes the current policy rejects. The current envelope is defensible as an
automatic-admission policy, and keeping it is a legitimate answer to this
proposal.
