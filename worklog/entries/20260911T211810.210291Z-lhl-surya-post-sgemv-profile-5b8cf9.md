# Surya post-SGEMV profile: decode is weight-bandwidth-bound at 138 GB/s

## Summary

The quoted ~11 ms/token floor for Surya decode was an estimate derived from
weight bytes divided by an assumed bandwidth, not a measurement. This unit
measures it, so the next optimization is chosen from data rather than from the
estimate.

Measured on gfx1151, one decode token at a 106-token context is **16.967 ms** of
kernel time across **497 dispatches**, of which 187 are single-row SGEMV calls.
The text model is 2342 MB of fp32 weights, so a token streams them at **138 GB/s
aggregate, 167 GB/s inside the GEMMs alone**.

Transfers are not a bottleneck: 58 ms over 200 decode steps is 0.29 ms/token,
1.7% of decode.

## Changes

`scripts/surya_gpu_rocprof_driver.py` gains `--phase vision|prefill|decode|request`
and `--repeat`. A single trace of a whole request cannot attribute time to a
phase, because the kernels overlap in name and the CSV alone does not say which
invocation belonged to which phase. Each phase now runs in its own process and
reports how many calls it made, so per-call cost is the kernel total divided by
that count with no assumption about warmup.

`scripts/surya_profile_report.py` aggregates the traces: kernel families, top
kernels, transfers by direction, and per-call cost. Two things it had to get
right, both found by looking at real traces rather than inventing them:

- **rocBLAS dispatches to Tensile assembly kernels named `Cijk_<...>`**, which
  carry no `gemm` substring. Without a rule for them, 77.5% of the large-page
  vision profile and 66% of prefill landed in the catch-all bucket and hid most
  of the answer. A test asserts no kernel in the profile artifact is
  unclassified.
- **`rocprofv3 --memory-copy-trace` records direction and duration but no byte
  count.** The reader reports volume as unavailable rather than guessing, and a
  test pins that, because inventing bytes would have produced a plausible and
  wrong transfer figure.

`_read_kernels`/`_read_copies` search recursively: rocprofv3 writes its CSVs
into a hostname subdirectory, so a flat glob silently returned nothing.

## Validation

Artifact: `benchmarks/results/2026-09-11-gfx1151-surya-post-sgemv-profile.json`.
JIT libraries were prebuilt outside the profiler and the profiled process ran
cache-only (`HIPENGINE_REQUIRE_CACHED_BUILD=1` with a precomputed compiler
version file), so no hipcc time is in any trace.

| phase | ms/call | kernels/call | top family | transfers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vision 256 patches (16x16) | 27.097 | 247 | gemm 93.5% | 2.80 ms |
| vision 4096 patches (64x64) | 1019.203 | 247 | gemm 77.5% | 3.87 ms |
| prefill 106 tokens | 95.599 | 591 | gemm 73.1% | 2.67 ms |
| prefill 1066 tokens | 793.623 | 591 | gemm 66.4% | 2.68 ms |
| decode @106 ctx | 16.967 | 497 | gemm 87.2% | 0.29 ms |
| decode @1066 ctx | 26.737 | 497 | gemm 82.7% | 0.29 ms |

What the numbers say:

- **Decode is weight-bandwidth-bound and the GEMMs are already efficient.**
  2342 MB / 16.967 ms = 138 GB/s aggregate; within the GEMMs alone 2342 MB over
  82.5% of 16.967 ms = 167 GB/s, which is ~83% of a realistic ~200 GB/s on this
  part. The ~11 ms estimate assumed ~200 GB/s end to end, so the measured gap is
  ~6 ms/token: about 3.0 ms of it is the 17.5% of decode that is not GEMM (GDN
  recurrent alone is 10.1%, 1.71 ms/token) and the rest is bandwidth headroom.
- **497 dispatches per token is itself a cost.** 187 of them are
  `rocblas_gemvt_warp_reduce_kernel` at 74.88 us each. That is a launch and
  tail-effect tax on top of the streaming, and it is the largest single
  structural item this profile exposes.
- **Context length is the second axis.** 1066 tokens costs 1.58x the per-token
  time of 106, and the growth is in attention (Tensile `Cijk_` kernels, 22.2%)
  and `evie_softmax` (2.5%), not in the projections.
- **Vision is superlinear in patch count**: 37.6x the time for 16x the patches
  (27.097 ms at 256, 1019.203 ms at 4096), with `evie_softmax` at 12.4% on the
  large page. This is the same quadratic score matrix that the separate
  admission cap in the preceding unit bounds, seen from the cost side.
- **Prefill is close to linear** (8.3x time for 10x tokens).

Consistency check against the corrected harness: decode-small measures 16.967
ms/token here and the suite baseline measured 51.7-55.4 tok/s (18.0-19.3
ms/token including the per-step readback that the kernel trace does not count).
The two agree, which is the cross-check that the profile and the benchmark are
measuring the same thing.

Tests: `tests/test_surya_profile_report.py` (33 tests) covers Tensile
classification, GDN-before-EVIE ordering, no-unclassified-kernels over both the
committed trace and the profile artifact, recursive CSV discovery, per-call
arithmetic including the zero-call case, malformed/reversed row rejection, and
transfer aggregation with and without a byte column.

Not addressed here: the profile measures the current default path only. It does
not evaluate any candidate, and it makes no claim that the ~6 ms/token gap is
recoverable in full.

## Next

Candidate directions, in the order this profile supports:

1. Reduce the 187 per-token dispatches (launch/tail overhead on top of
   streaming), e.g. by batching the projections into fewer, wider GEMMs.
2. The GDN recurrent kernel at 10.1% of decode.
3. Attention and softmax for long contexts, which is where the context-length
   growth sits.
4. Tiled vision attention, which both the superlinear vision scaling and the
   quadratic-scratch admission cap point at.

Rollup update for the corrected lane-comparison numbers is still outstanding.
