# the dense linears are all q8_0 and fall off with K, not with residency

## Summary

`launch_gguf_linear` is 32.0 percent of the 512-token prefill -- 276.2 ms over 206 calls
against attention's 170.7 -- and had no attribution. It has one now: every one of the nine
distinct shapes is **Q8_0**, and the rate is a function of the reduction length.

| in | out | rows | calls | ms | ms/call | GFLOP/s | weight MB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2816 | 2112 | 512 | 60 | 57.65 | 0.961 | 6338 | 379.1 |
| 4096 | 2816 | 512 | 25 | 56.58 | 2.263 | 5219 | 306.4 |
| 2816 | 2048 | 512 | 50 | 46.32 | 0.926 | 6375 | 306.4 |
| 2816 | 4096 | 512 | 25 | 40.86 | 1.634 | 7227 | 306.4 |
| 8192 | 2816 | 512 | 5 | 28.22 | 5.644 | 4185 | 122.6 |
| 2112 | 2816 | 512 | 30 | 22.41 | 0.747 | 8154 | 189.6 |
| 2816 | 8192 | 512 | 5 | 15.86 | 3.172 | 7448 | 122.6 |
| 2816 | 262144 | 1 | 1 | 3.51 | 3.513 | 420 | 784.3 |
| 2816 | 1024 | 512 | 5 | 1.86 | 0.372 | 7933 | 15.3 |

Total 273.3 ms, 206 calls, measured with the same event-timed spy the kernel census uses,
keyed by shape instead of by function name.

## The rate tracks the reduction length

The five shapes sharing `out = 2816` isolate it, because their `MMQ_M` tile count is fixed
at 22 and only `k_iters = K / 256` moves:

| K | k_iters | GFLOP/s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2816 | 11 | 6350 |
| 4096 | 16 | 5192 |
| 8192 | 32 | 4233 |

Monotone, 1.50x across the range. That is the opposite of the usual GEMM picture, where a
longer reduction amortizes the prologue. It says each k-step costs more than the last rather
than the same, and the k-step is a weight-tile load, an activation-tile load, a barrier, and
the dot products.

The `2112 -> 2816` row is the control. Its K is not a multiple of 256, so the MMQ route
refuses it (`hidden % 256 != 0`) and it takes the WMMA route instead -- and it runs at 8154
GFLOP/s, the fastest row in the table, on the same hardware and the same quant. The route
gate is therefore sending shapes to the slower of the two available kernels for at least
that shape.

`HIPENGINE_GGUF_WMMA_PREFILL=1` does **not** move shapes between the routes. Measured: the
per-shape table under the env var is 274.9 ms against 273.3 ms unset, with every row within
noise of its unset value. The env var gates a different decision than the one that picks MMQ
over WMMA, so it is not the lever for this.

The `2816 -> 262144` row is the lm head and is the only row that is genuinely
bandwidth-bound: 784.3 MB of weights in 3.51 ms is 223 GB/s, close to this part's practical
ceiling. It is 1.3 percent of the route and needs nothing.

## Residency is not the constraint, and the tile geometry is closed

The MMQ tile's shared footprint is a static assertion in the kernel:
`(128 + 128 * 36 + 128 * 76) * 4 == 57856` bytes, 56.5 KB against a 64 KB limit. With
`WAVES = MMQ_M_T / 16` that is 256 threads per block, so the natural reading is one block
per CU and a residency-bound kernel.

That reading is wrong, and it is wrong for a reason that has already been measured. The
smaller-tile instantiations exist in the source -- `launch_mmq_tile<64, 64, ...>` and
`<64, 128, ...>` -- and `docs/campaigns/QWEN3.8-FLASH-NEXT-PERFORMANCE-CAMPAIGN.md` records
the sweep that tried them: *"64x64 on actual Q layers3/7 at512/1024 rows yields 0.984/0.985x
... Threads256->128, dynamic LDS48384->28928B, VGPR160/scratch0 unchanged. Smaller residency
footprint does not improve operation-complete time; candidate removed, raw-Q128x64 retained.
Do not repeat this same-host/staging combination unchanged."* A separate GR-targeted 64x128
screen is also recorded as rejected at 0.722x/0.720x.

The register arithmetic says the same thing independently. At 160 VGPRs per thread, the
retained 128x128 tile occupies 256 threads x 160 = 40960 VGPRs and fits one block in a
65536-VGPR CU. The 64x64 tile at 128 threads fits three blocks by registers and two by its
28928-byte footprint -- call it 256 to 384 threads resident, against 256. Residency roughly
doubles at best and the kernel still loses 1.5 percent. **The kernel is not waiting on
occupancy; the time is in the work.**

This is the second time today that a residency story looked like the explanation and was not
one. The gemma4 prefill attention query tile was the first: padding the single-row kernel's
shared-memory request to the tile's exact footprint cost 5 percent while the tile itself cost
20. Two independent kernels, two different parts of the model, same conclusion. Treat
"increase occupancy" as a hypothesis to measure on this part, not as a default explanation.

## What follows from it

At 5 to 8 TFLOP/s of int8 DP4A the kernels are issuing far fewer MACs per cycle than the
hardware allows, and residency has been ruled out as the reason. That leaves the work per
instruction -- the tile-load mapping, the dot-product packing, and the barrier count per
k-step -- as the thing to measure next. `vgpr_count = 160` and `scratch = 0` say the kernel
is register-hungry but not spilling, so the pressure is real and any restructuring has to
buy its parallelism without more VGPRs.

## Changes

None. The measurement instrument is a scratch probe (`/tmp/dense_linear_probe.py`), not a
repo artifact; it is the census script's spy keyed by `(in_features, out_features, rows)`
instead of by name.

## Validation

- Per-shape table measured on the 8060S (gfx1151) at 512 tokens, 2 repeats after 1 warmup,
  event-timed around `launch_gguf_linear` itself.
- The metric device was confirmed rather than assumed: this host has exactly one HIP device
  (`hipGetDeviceCount` = 1, `AMD Radeon 8060S Graphics`), so the loop metric and this profile
  are the same part. `scripts/gemma4_campaign_bench.py` defaults `--expect-gpu` to
  `RX 7900 XTX` and refuses to run on this host without `--expect-gpu ""`; the loop's 0.876
  was produced with that override.
- The WMMA comparison is a measured A/B: the same probe, same warmup, same repeat count, with
  and without `HIPENGINE_GGUF_WMMA_PREFILL=1`.
- The tile-geometry conclusion is quoted from the recorded campaign, not re-measured here.

## Next

- Measure the MMQ inner loop's work per MAC. Residency is excluded; the remaining candidates
  are the per-k-step tile-load instruction count and the barrier count.
- The route gate is worth a second look on its own: `2112 -> 2816` runs at 8154 GFLOP/s on the
  WMMA route while every `hidden % 256 == 0` shape is sent to MMQ at 4233 to 7227. If WMMA is
  genuinely faster at these shapes, the gate is backwards. That needs the same shape measured
  on both routes, which the env var does not provide -- it would need a dispatcher-level
  override.
