# W7900 Qwen3.8: qualify the QKV rows16 C8 wall claim against the measured arm-order band

## Summary

A completed background chain (rows 16/24/32, unequal-dual floor 512 vs 16) arrived
with no retained per-arm output, which prompted a re-read of the promotion that
chain supported. The prefill and byte-identity claims in that artifact are
order-controlled and survive; one number is not strong enough to carry its own
weight. The artifact's own decision text says the shipping C8 AR gain was
"45.62 -> 45.94 tok/s = +0.7% in a same-chain pair where the candidate ran
second". Today's order-swap A/B (`20260830T021032.680079Z-`) measured how much
running second is worth: a dispatch-identical control pair spans **+0.01% to
-0.84%**. A +0.7% effect from the second arm is therefore inside the band and is
not separable from arm order. It stays in the record as an observation, qualified,
and stops being quotable as an independent speed win.

## Validation

- Order-swap evidence is in
  `20260830T021032.680079Z-lhl-w7900-qwen38-prefill-ab-orderswap-bcc9fe.md`: rows 45
  gains **+4.24%** (control first) and **+3.69%** (candidate first); dispatch-identical
  rows 16 control reads **+0.01%** and **-0.84%**. Bias magnitude, not sign, is what
  bounds the C8 claim.
- What is unaffected: the `+1.81% packed prefill crossover (10/10 configurations)`
  is a crossover protocol, and byte-identity is asserted over 20/20 packed
  trajectory hashes in both measurement orders - both are insensitive to which arm
  runs second.
- Process gap worth naming: that chain logged only `LOWBAND_DONE` and wrote no
  retained per-arm file, so its raw numbers are unrecoverable while the GPU work is
  not repeatable from the log alone. Row-gate A/B chains must write one distinct
  `--json` path per arm (as `/tmp/he_oswap.sh` does) or they cannot be audited.
