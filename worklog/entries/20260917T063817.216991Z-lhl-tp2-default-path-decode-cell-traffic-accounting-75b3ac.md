# TP2 default-path decode cell on one revision, and the per-token traffic accounting that bounds the next lever

## Summary

Re-anchored the TP2 decode metric on current `HEAD` and then bounded the next
speed lever with arithmetic instead of assumption. Two results, both of which
change what the next optimization should be.

**The default path reproduces, and it now has same-revision denominators.** The
recorded loop metric (24.147 ms/token) came from the graphed-schedule arm before
the head shard and the device reduction were combined, so the shipped
combination had no fresh end-to-end measurement. Three repeats of
`scripts/tp2_mlp_generate_e2e.py` (4 prompts x 16 greedy decode transitions, all
three arms in one session on one revision) give decode p50 **24.155 / 24.167 /
24.155 ms/token** for TP2 against same-run resident TP1 controls at
**31.753-31.764** (W7900) and **26.320-26.355** (RX 7900 XTX) ms/token, i.e.
**1.315x** and **1.090x**, with every arm keeping mean KL 3.945e-04, max KL
2.365e-03, top-1 100% and bit-identical tokens/logits across the run. The
harness previously stored `None` for the TP1 arms' forced eager/host control
schedule and for the head shard the TP2 default enables, so the artifact could
not be read back as the route it measured; it now records the session's resolved
`schedule`, `reduce_mode`, `head_shard` and `driver`.

**The remaining speed headroom is not weight traffic.** `scripts/
tp2_traffic_accounting.py` derives, from the same GGUF index and the degree-2
shard plan, that the current route reads **9.676 GiB per rank per token**
(MLP 9.650 GiB sharded to 4.825 GiB/rank, attention 3.301 GiB and GDN/SSM
1.062 GiB replicated, head 0.971 GiB sharded to 0.486 GiB/rank, embedding not
read per token) against the resident control's **15.652 GiB**, paying **64**
cross-rank reductions per token. The slow rank's own measured resident
bandwidth is **529.3 GB/s** (W7900: 15.652 GiB in 31.753 ms), so the same
traffic would take **19.629 ms**; the measured wall is **24.155 ms**, i.e.
**4.53 ms/token above traffic-proportional**, and the route achieves
**430.1 GB/s** per rank against the resident route's 529.3 GB/s on the same
rank. Two consequences:

- The **4.53 ms/token gap** (in-graph exchange, rank imbalance, shard-shape
  kernel efficiency, any exposed GDN latency) is bigger than anything a
  structural traffic reduction buys, and it is the first thing to attribute.
- **Finishing Packet 3's attention/GDN sharding is a small speed lever.** It
  cuts per-rank traffic 1.38x (9.676 -> 8.646 GiB for the slow rank; 17.54 ms
  implied at the same bandwidth) but adds 64 more cross-rank reductions per
  token to the 64 already paid, so the net throughput gain is bounded by
  roughly 2.1 ms minus the extra reduction cost. Its justification is capacity
  and correctness, not throughput. This is a decision-relevant correction to
  the earlier reading that attention/GDN sharding was the next big speed win.

## Changes

- `scripts/tp2_mlp_generate_e2e.py`: the arm record now stores the **resolved**
  route (`session.schedule`, `session.reduce_mode`, `session.head_shard`,
  `session.driver`) plus `head_shard_requested`, instead of the raw CLI
  argument. The `sharding_scope` and `no_speedup_claim` prose now describes the
  route that actually runs (graphed segments, device in-graph reduction,
  compiled driver, sharded head) and states the claim limit that matters (a
  16-transition cell, not a sustained product comparison).
- `scripts/tp2_traffic_accounting.py` (new): derives per-role and per-rank
  per-token weight traffic from the GGUF index (dropping the MTP/NextN block via
  `qwen35.block_count - qwen35.nextn_predict_layers`, which is what makes the
  derived `model_bytes` equal the shard plan's) and the degree-2 shard plan
  report, then reports the implied walls at a *measured* resident bandwidth.
  Every rate it prints is either derived from those files or quoted from the
  benchmark artifact named on the command line; the implied walls are labelled
  as arithmetic, not measurements.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-09-17-w7900-tp2-default-path-e2e.json` (retained),
  `benchmarks/results/2026-09-17-w7900-tp2-per-token-traffic-accounting.json`
  (derived), `benchmarks/README.md` (`Last updated` 2026-09-17, the TP2
  section's matched-cell paragraph and the corrected historical-ratio note),
  `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md`, `docs/QWEN38-27B-GFX1100-TP2.md` (new
  "Default-path decode cell and the traffic accounting" section).

    --json benchmarks/results/2026-09-17-w7900-tp2-default-path-e2e.json

  | repeat | TP1 W7900 | TP1 XTX | TP2 (graphed/device/head-shard/compiled) |
  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | 1 | 31.764 | 26.320 | 24.155 |
  | 2 | 31.757 | 26.355 | 24.167 |
  | 3 | 31.753 | 26.329 | 24.155 |

  All three runs: `tp2_vs_tp1_teacher_device0` and `..._device1` PASS at mean KL
  3.945e-04 / p99 2.137e-03 / max KL 2.365e-03 / top-1 100% (16 rows),
  `tp1_device0_vs_device1_control` PASS at KL 0.000e+00, TP2 determinism
  `tokens_identical=True, logits_bit_identical=True`, exchange `64 reductions,
  device mode`. Run-to-run spread is 0.05% (TP2), 0.03% (W7900 TP1), 0.13%
  (XTX TP1). The third repeat is the retained artifact.
- **Accounting cross-check.** The derived AR tensor set is
  `16806250496` bytes, identical to `model_bytes` in
  `benchmarks/results/tp2_shard_plan_report.json`, and the derived reduction
  points (`ffn_down` 64 + `ssm_out` 48 + `attn_output` 16 = 128) equal that
  report's `reduction_points` for degree 2. The script's own unit check: at the
  reported 529.3 GB/s, 9.6755 GiB takes 19.631 ms.
- **No new kernel or production-code path**, so no strict/profile numerical gate
  applies; the harness's own teacher gates above are the correctness evidence
  for the route this cell measured.
