# The shipped short-context decode route is 5e-3-1e-2 from the exact chain

## Summary

Every decode-attention promotion this project has judged was measured against the
chain the candidate *replaces*. Nobody had measured the shipped route set
against the **exact** chain. Doing that at keys 1,025-2,047 -- the band the D2/D4
gates covered, same frozen prompt chain (`chain_sha256 b8c28ab5`), 1,023 rows --
gives this:

| arm (same tree, vs that tree's own `--slices 1` exact arm) | mean KL | p99 | max | top-1 | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HEAD, shipping routes (flash on all 30 layers) | 5.38e-3 | 1.92e-1 | 6.36e-1 | 99.32% (7 flips) | fails mean/p99/max |
| HEAD, sliding layers flash only (512 admission disabled) | 1.23e-2 | 1.73e-1 | 5.89 | 99.71% (3 flips) | fails mean/p99/max |
| `e27781214` (2026-09-30), shipping routes then | **7.75e-6** | 2.96e-5 | 6.70e-3 | 100% (0 flips) | **passes** |

Envelope (`docs/EXECUTION-PROFILES.md`): mean 1e-3, p95 5e-3, p99 2e-2, max
5e-2, top-1 99%. The shipped route meets p95 and top-1 and exceeds mean, p99 and
max. The same route *set* -- 25 sliding layers on flash, 5 global layers on the
two-phase split, identical route summary in both runs -- moved from 7.75e-6 to
1.23e-2 between the two trees. That is a 1,000x change in the route's measured
footprint on the trajectory.

Three consequences for decisions already made:

- **The 2026-10-08 global promotion rejection stands on its own gate.** It was a
  candidate against the staged chain it replaced and it failed that gate. This
  entry does not reopen it.
- **But the envelope bars are not met by the shipped route either.** The
  incumbent short-context route sits at 5.4e-3-1.2e-2, the same order as the
  1.11e-2 candidate that was rejected. So the bars are being applied as a
  *promotion* gate (candidate vs predecessor), not as a *production* contract.
  That reading is the one the artifacts support -- and it means "production KL
  limit mean <= 1e-3" does not describe the shipped decode route. A reader who
  takes it as a production contract would be wrong about the incumbent, not
  about the rejected candidate.
- **The d2/d4 gates were correctly referenced and are not invalidated.** Flash
  replaced the split, and the split is exact-preserving (`decode_slices`'s own
  contract: the value pass partitions output dimensions and preserves
  ascending-key accumulation, with fp32 weights). The identical mean KL of `d2`
  and the `e27781214` re-run confirms it -- the two different baselines
  (split@`7ddffcfa6`, single@`e27781214`) produce bit-identical logits. So
  "flash vs split = 5e-6" and "flash vs exact = 5e-6" were the same statement at
  that time.

**Not established: whether the flash kernel's arithmetic regressed, or the model
became more sensitive to it.** Both produce a larger measured footprint for the
same route choice. For an arithmetic regression: the footprint changed by
1,000x, and this unit's tuning ladder (`ea5597a1c`, `5faef25f4`, `3a55f9ad5`,
`37ba3b3da`, `308c4c38c`) changed `kFlashThreads` 128 -> 256, added
`__launch_bounds__`, added the phase-B dimension loop, and rewrote the
denominator broadcast in that window. Against it: the kernel-level battery
passes on HEAD, bounding flash against the single kernel at ~1e-4 in f32, which
a 30-layer trajectory can amplify to this order -- but that bound was never
measured tightly and is the same bound on both trees. The slice count is **not**
implicated: `flash_request_slices` returns 64 for both packings at these keys,
the same value the old `flash_slices` policy returned.

## Validation

- **Instrument control.** The exact arm against itself, same tree
  (`benchmarks/results/2026-10-08-gemma4-short-selfgate-control.json`): mean KL
  **exactly 0.0**, max 0.0, top-1 100%. The harness is deterministic, so the
  headline numbers are signal.
- **Baseline-route control.** `d2`'s mean KL (7.750959403558968e-06) and the
  `e27781214` re-run's (7.750959403558968e-06) are identical to every printed
  digit while their baselines were different routes on different trees -- the
  split and the single kernel produce bit-identical logits.
- **Route control.** Each verdict's `observed_routes` confirms which routes ran:
  HEAD `selections: [3]` on both head dims; the isolation arm `selections: [2, 3]`
  with `split_launches: 5080` = the 5 global layers; `e27781214` the same; the
  exact arm `selections: [1]` with no split and no flash launches.
- **Kernel-level bound.** `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_flash_decode.py`, 60 cases, both
  packings x f32/bf16 x keep/holes/window x keys 1024/2055/4096: passes on HEAD,
  asserting flash against the single kernel at `rtol/atol` 1e-4 (f32) and 6e-3
  (bf16).
- **Cross-commit control.** HEAD against the 2026-09-30 split arm reads mean KL
  **0.131** (max 27.8, 26 flips), so a week of other work moves this instrument
  far more than the attention route does; cross-commit comparisons cannot
  attribute anything.

## Next

- **Task 54: the discriminating experiment.** Compare flash against the single
  kernel *at the kernel level* with a tight bound -- f32, head_dim 256, ratio 2,
  keys 2,047, 64 slices, window mask -- reporting the observed relative
  deviation instead of asserting a tolerance, on HEAD and on `abf639894`. It is
  immune to the model's sensitivity, and it separates "the kernel's arithmetic
  regressed" from "the model got more sensitive to the same arithmetic".
- **If it is an arithmetic regression**, the tuning commits named above are the
  window; the fix is to restore the kernel-level agreement and re-run the D2
  reference (`d2-baseline-7ddffcfa6.npz`) to confirm the route returns to ~5e-6.
- **A decision the lead owns:** whether the envelope bars are a promotion gate or
  a production contract. If they are a production contract, the shipped
  short-context route needs work before any further attention promotion is
  judged by them; if they are a promotion gate, that should be said where the
  bars are defined, because the current wording reads as the latter and measures
  as the former.
