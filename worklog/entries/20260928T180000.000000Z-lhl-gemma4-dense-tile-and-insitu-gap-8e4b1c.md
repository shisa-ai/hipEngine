# the dense tile is already swept, the orientation effect is real, and every shape is 10 to 31 percent slower in situ

## Summary

Reading the Q8_0 dense prefill kernel closed the tile question, gave the orientation
correlation a structural home, and turned up a larger and uniform in-situ penalty that the
previous unit's prompt-length test could not see. **The tile is already the swept optimum**
(`(32, 64)` is in force and `_default_tiles` does not consider `in_features`); the two
orientations are **arithmetically identical** in FLOPs, bytes and K-step-blocks and differ
only in grid shape; and **every dense shape runs 10 to 31 percent slower in situ than in
the isolated sweep**, which is about 28 ms of the 189.8 ms dense term.

## The tile question is closed before it was asked

The plan for this unit was to change the tile. **The kernel already carries the sweep**:
`gguf_q8_0_prefill.py`'s docstring records a 2026-09-28 gfx1151 sweep over eleven shapes
that found `tile_m=64` best or within noise on every one, then a `tile_n=64` follow-up
that moved the family again -- `(32, 64)` fastest on three of gemma4's four dense prefill
geometries, family total 4.121 -> 3.639 ms. The previous `(16, 32)` / `(32, 32)` ladder was
replaced by a single tile.

`_default_tiles` resolves to `tile_m = 32, tile_n = 64` at rows = 512, and it reads only
`rows` and `out_features` -- **`in_features` is not an input to the choice.** So the
in-force tile is the swept optimum and there is no tile lever to pull.

## The grid, and why the two orientations differ

```cpp
const dim3 block(32);
const dim3 grid((out_features + tile_m - 1) / tile_m,   // gridDim.x
                (rows + tile_n - 1) / tile_n);          // gridDim.y
```

**The input width is not in the grid.** It appears only as the inner WMMA K loop
(`K_TILE = 16`). So the two orientations at rows = 512, tile `(32, 64)` are:

| shape | grid | blocks | K steps per block |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `4096 -> 2816` | 88 x 8 | 704 | 256 |
| `2816 -> 4096` | 128 x 8 | 1024 | 176 |

**And they are arithmetically identical**: same FLOPs (512 x 4096 x 2816 x 2), same weight
bytes (11.5M weights), same activation bytes, and the same total K-step-blocks --
704 x 256 = 1024 x 176 = 180,224. **Everything except the grid shape is equal, and the rate
differs 1.5x.** The correlation found in `20260928T174000` therefore has a structural home:
it is a grid-shape effect, not an arithmetic one.

## The orientation effect reproduces in isolation

The sweep's own numbers, at the in-force tile, for the same 11.5M-weight matrix in its two
orientations:

- `(in=4096, out=2816)` at `32x64`: **1.482 ms**
- `(in=2816, out=4096)` at `32x64`: **0.939 ms**

**1.58x in the isolated harness against 1.46x in situ.** The effect is not a call-site
artifact and not a prompt-length artifact; it is a property of the shape, confirmed by two
independent measurements.

## The new finding: a uniform in-situ penalty

Comparing the sweep's isolated best-of-8 against the in-situ probe's per-call mean, **every
shape is slower in situ and none is faster:**

| shape, rows = 512 | isolated `32x64` | in situ ms/call | gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `4096 -> 2816` | 1.482 | 1.792 | **+21%** |
| `2816 -> 4096` | 0.939 | 1.229 | **+31%** |
| `2816 -> 2112` | 0.534 | 0.587 | **+10%** |

**This is a different and larger lever than the orientation effect.** The orientation gap
affects 64.9 ms of the 189.8 ms dense term; the in-situ penalty affects all of it, and at a
conservative 15 percent it is about 28 ms -- comparable to the whole orientation prize, and
unlike it the penalty is not confined to three shapes.

**Two explanations are open and the measurement does not separate them:**

1. **The statistic.** The sweep reports best-of-8 and the probe reports a mean over the
   step. Best-of-8 is an optimistic estimator by construction, and part of the gap is
   simply that.
2. **The environment.** In situ the dense projections run interleaved with the expert
   kernels, attention, and the MoE elementwise. L2 is shared and the expert routes are
   streaming weights at 49 GB/s, so the dense shapes may be losing cache or clock state
   they have when run alone.

**The first is cheaper to rule out than the second and should be first**: re-run the
isolated microbench reporting the mean rather than the best, and see how much of the gap
survives.

## Next

- **Separate the statistic from the environment.** Re-run the isolated microbench reporting
  the mean instead of the best-of-8. Whatever survives is the environment, and the
  environment is where the money is: at 15 percent it is about 28 ms of the dense term,
  against the orientation effect's 22 ms, and it is not confined to three shapes.
- **Do not change the tile.** `(32, 64)` is the swept optimum on three of four gemma4
  geometries and `_default_tiles` does not even consider `in_features`. The sweep is in the
  docstring, with the alternatives' numbers.
- **The orientation effect has a structural home now** -- identical FLOPs, bytes and
  K-step-blocks, differing only in grid shape -- but no mechanism. The grid-shape
  explanation would be testable by launching `4096 -> 2816` with a transposed grid
  ordering, which is a kernel change and not yet worth one.
