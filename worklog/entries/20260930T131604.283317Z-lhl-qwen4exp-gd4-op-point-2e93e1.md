# GD-4's operating point: my two artifacts disagreed, and the LDS arithmetic says chunk 128 was never buildable

## Summary

Two of this campaign's own artifacts disagreed on GD-4's operating point. The feasibility
artifact chose **chunk 128** as "the balance"; the build plan chose chunk 32. **The LDS
arithmetic settles it against chunk 128: 320.0 KB of workgroup LDS with full fp32 staging
and 192.0 KB with fp16 staging, against the 64 KB limit - 5x and 3x over.** The feasibility
artifact picked its operating point on the numerics axis alone - fp16 NMSE improves
monotonically with chunk size - **without checking whether that chunk is resident**, so a
builder acting on it would have started at chunk 128 and hit a 5x overrun. Chunk 32 is also
right on that artifact's own terms: it has the lowest break-even in its own table.

## Why this is worth recording as its own unit

The failure mode is the one this campaign keeps finding in the ledger, except here it
was in my own artifacts: **a conclusion drawn on one axis and carried into another
where it does not hold.** The feasibility artifact was correct that fp16 NMSE improves
with chunk size; it was not correct that chunk 128 was therefore the operating point,
because a resident working set is a hard constraint and NMSE is a soft one.

The tell was that the two artifacts disagreed. Neither was checked against the other
until the build plan's LDS table made the conflict arithmetic.

**Note also that the reconciliation strengthens the recommendation rather than
weakening it.** Chunk 32 is not a compromise forced by memory pressure: it has the
*lowest break-even in the feasibility artifact's own table*, and its NMSE is 251x
better than the reference the route has to clear. The rows that chunks 64-128 buy are
orders of magnitude nobody asked for.
