# Gemma 4 MoE runs at 12% of peak bandwidth: the fused megakernel is refuted and the t16 repack is the lever

## Summary

With attention down to roughly 8.5 ms and the host gap cut by 6.4 ms, the MoE
decode linears are now the largest single item in the step: 6.2 ms of ~21.8 ms,
**28%**. The campaign had attacked them only through specific rejected variants
and had never established the achieved-bandwidth baseline, so this unit measured
it. The production chain moves 31.35 MB per layer in 0.3002 ms - **~104 GB/s,
12% of the RX 7900 XTX's peak**, against a ~35 us memory roofline for the same
bytes. The MoE is **6-9x off the roofline and latency-bound, not
bandwidth-bound**.

Two candidates changed status as a result. The fused megakernel is **refuted**:
0.5545 ms against 0.3002, so consolidating three launches into one is 1.85x
slower at these shapes (and it applies SiLU where Gemma 4's MoE is `gelu_tanh`,
so it was never admissible). And the campaign's own next candidate, the q4_k
decode re-route, is re-scoped rather than unblocked: its recorded obstacles
split differently than the record said, and the decode-shaped kernels that exist
read a repacked weight layout.

## Validation

- **Method.** `scripts/gguf_q4_k_moe_ffn_fused_microbench.py` times the real
  kernels the runtime selects (`gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_gemv_bf16_bf16_out` for
  gate/up, `gguf_q4_k_selected_gemv_bf16_bf16_out` for down) at hidden 2816,
  ffn 768, top-k 8, 128 experts, 50 iterations after 20 warmups, on physical
  GPU1.
- **Shape caveat, recorded rather than papered over.** Gemma 4's real ffn
  length is 704, which is not a multiple of 256, and the synthetic-weight
  fixture refuses such an `in_features`. 768 is the nearest legal substitute at
  ~9% wider, so the byte count is ~9% high and the derived bandwidth is a slight
  over-estimate. The real kernels handle 704 - the artifact loads and runs - so
  this is a fixture limitation, not a kernel one. This is also why the earlier
  attempt to run the bench at the exact shape failed.
- **Byte accounting.** Per layer, 8 experts x (768 x 2816 x 0.5625 B gate +
  768 x 2816 x 0.5625 B up + 2816 x 768 x 0.6875 B down) = 31.35 MB.
- **Cross-check against production.** The microbench gives 300 us per layer;
  iteration 19's profile gives 6.2 ms per step, or 207 us per layer over 30
  layers. Same order, and the microbench is the slower of the two because it
  pays launch overhead and a cold cache per call.
- **The refutation.** The fused arm writes the same output shape and its KL
  against the unfused arm is 0.000e+00, so the timing comparison is
  like-for-like on the arithmetic it shares - but it shares the activation only
  by accident, since the fused kernel hardcodes SiLU.

## Next

- **The lever is the t16 repack, and it is now measured rather than suspected.**
  The decode-shaped kernels exist (`gguf_t16_selected_gemv`, registered under
  `gguf_q4_k_t16_v1` and siblings, including
  `selected_t16_pairreuse_gemv_decode_compact_bf16_bf16_out` for q4_k), but they
  read a repacked layout, so reaching them needs a loader-side repack plus the
  routing change. That is the campaign's G4/G5 scope.
- **The `expert_ffn % 256` guard splits differently than the record says.** The
  gate_up projection's `in_features` is 2816 = 11 x 256, which is legal; only the
  down projection's 704 is not. So the gate_up half of the re-route does not need
  a new kernel at all, while the down half does (or needs a tail-handling
  variant). The campaign doc has been corrected.
- **Probe the dense projections the same way before starting the work.** They
  are 3.9 ms of the step and share the dispatch, so if they show the same 10-12%
  of peak they are the same unit of work, not a separate one.
- The fixture's `% 256` restriction is the reason the real shape could not be
  measured directly. Lifting it would make future MoE probes exact instead of
  substituted, and it is a test-infrastructure change rather than a kernel one.
