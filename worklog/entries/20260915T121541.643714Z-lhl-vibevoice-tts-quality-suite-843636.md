# Unassisted multi-request VibeVoice-TTS quality suite: 17 of 18 request-runs pass

## Summary

Numerical agreement with the oracle cannot be the acceptance evidence for the
two-speaker requests: that fixture's first diffusion solve is chaotic, so its late
trajectory is unreproducible by construction. The review that flagged this asked
for the requirement to be replaced with intelligibility, speaker and turn
attribution, missing or repeated speech, duration and listening checks on several
held-out requests. `scripts/vibevoice_tts_quality_suite.py` does that, and the
result is that the two-speaker path is **mostly** good: 17 of 18 request-runs
pass over three seeds, with one real failure.

## The suite

Six held-out scripts (`benchmarks/prompts/vibevoice-tts-quality.json`): one to
four turns, 7 to 40 words, over the one- and two-speaker voice sets the fixtures
pin. Nothing is injected — the session draws its own voice-prompt VAE noise, its
own diffusion noise and its own negative-LM branch — and each request's token
budget comes from its declared `max_audio_seconds` rather than from the oracle's
token count, so `finish_reason` reports truncation instead of hiding it.

All 18 runs reach EOS. No output clips (peak 0.34-0.49), RMS stays in
0.045-0.060, the longest internal gap is 0.98 s, and no repeated 4-gram appears.
The three WER values below 0.06 are single words, one of them the
`cancelled`/`canceled` spelling difference rather than a mispronunciation.

## The one failure

`two-2turn` seed 20260915 measures WER 0.353. Its second turn is word-perfect;
the first comes back as "I can see you in the world this morning" for "I think
the meeting went well this morning". The same request measures 0.059 on the other
two seeds, so this is seed-specific degradation of the shortest two-speaker
script, not a systematic break. It is left failing and reported.

## Two instruments were wrong before the checks were

**The ASR's speaker labels are not an attribution gate.** On these requests the
ASR reports one speaker for two-speaker scripts that demonstrably contain both
voices: the 2-turn script on all three seeds, the long 2-turn script on two of
three. The first version of this suite gated on those labels and reported both
requests as failures. It was wrong.

The evidence that the audio is fine came from the acoustic encoder. Assigning
each 1 s window of generated audio to the reference voice with the higher cosine
similarity to its mean `encode_reference` latent gives a clean single transition
on every two-speaker run — for example `000000000000011111111111` on the long
script, and `000000111111` on the 2-turn script — while the ASR had merged those
same utterances into one segment. The instrument is deterministic, because
`encode_reference` does not sample the VAE. As a negative control it does not
manufacture a second voice where the script has one: against their own single
reference, all nine single-speaker runs assign every window to it.

**Pitch-based attribution does not discriminate at these durations.** Three
variants were measured and discarded: a first-third/last-third F0 comparison
passes single-speaker requests too, because F0 declines naturally over an
utterance; a p10/p90 F0 span check passes everything, including single-speaker
runs (79-234 Hz of within-voice variation against an 85 Hz reference
separation); and a first/last 0.5 s window is decided by a single ambiguous
window at the onset. Only the encoder assignment separates one voice from two.

A margin was still needed on the encoder assignment: one window of the 4-turn
script's onset measures 0.305 against 0.345, a near tie, and letting it decide a
turn produced a spurious failure. Windows where neither reference leads by 0.10
are ignored, and at least three must remain. The verdict is identical at 0.05 and
0.15, so it is not a tuned threshold.

## Requests were not independent

The session's generator is shared by the voice-prompt draw and every diffusion
frame, so a request's audio depended on how many requests ran before it: the
2-turn two-speaker script came back with one voice in a full run and two when run
alone. `VibevoiceTtsSession.reseed()` now restarts that stream, and the suite
reseeds each request to `base_seed + its index`. This was not a quality finding
about the model; it was a measurement bug, and it would have been reported as a
model defect.

## Changes

- `scripts/vibevoice_tts_quality_suite.py` (new): `synthesize` and `score`
  phases, the declared checks and thresholds, the encoder-based attribution
  instrument, and per-seed stability reporting.
- `benchmarks/prompts/vibevoice-tts-quality.json` (new): the held-out request set,
  with its limitations recorded in the file.
- `hipengine/runtime/vibevoice_tts_session.py`: `reseed()`.
- `tests/test_live_vibevoice_tts_session_gpu.py`:
  `test_reseed_makes_a_request_independent_of_session_history`, which asserts both
  that the same seed reproduces the audio and that a different seed changes it.
- `docs/MODEL-VIBEVOICE-TTS.md`: a "Generated-audio quality suite" section.
- `benchmarks/README.md`, `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md`, `benchmarks/HARNESSES.md`:
  rolled up, and the harness catalog now lists both TTS benches.
- `.gitignore`: the suite's local PCM directory.

## Next

The suite's held-out axis is the script only. Using another voice file means
reproducing the fork's WAV preprocessing (librosa 24 kHz resample plus its
-25 dBFS normalizer) in-tree; that is the next step if voice coverage matters more
than script coverage. Chinese and punctuation/numbers coverage, blinded listening,
and the interrupted-request case that `docs/MODEL-VIBEVOICE-TTS.md` lists are
still absent.

The benchmark's generation budget still comes from the oracle's expected token
count, and its reported "cold" figure still follows a traced synthesis. Both were
flagged by the review, both are carried as caveats in the artifacts, and neither
is fixed here.
