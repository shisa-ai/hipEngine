# Layer 29's shapes are identical to layer 0's - the 17x is the route

## Summary

Resolves the question the previous two entries left open, and it goes the
opposite way from my assumption.

Parsing the GGUF tensor table directly settles it. `blk.0.ffn_gate_up_exps.weight`
and `blk.29.ffn_gate_up_exps.weight` are both `(2816, 1408, 128)`; they differ
only in type, 12 (Q4_K) against 13 (Q5_K). Their `ffn_down_exps` are both
`(704, 2816, 128)` at type 8 (Q8_0), so the down side is quant-identical and only
gate_up differs. **The 30 MoE layers share expert shapes.** The 17x is therefore
entirely a kernel-route difference, and Q5_K's roughly 22% larger weights cannot
account for any of it.

That corrects the previous entry's framing twice over. It is not that the shapes
might differ - they do not - and the prize is not bounded at 1.9-2.8%.

The reason is in how the 1.50-2.00x was measured. The changelog records it as
"operation-complete (m=1..8)", which are decode-shaped row counts, and notes
"floor m<1 misses". Layer 29's prefill runs at m in the hundreds. The row4 parent
is a 128-thread kernel with strided k ownership and a serial wave sum - a GEMV
shape, which degrades as m grows - while a WMMA prefill route should not. So
1.50-2.00x is a lower bound for this use rather than the expected value, and the
ceiling is set by the q4_k MMQ32 route at 6.53 ms per layer. The honest range is
**1.9% to about 5% of prefill**, and the only way to narrow it is to wire the
route and measure at m=512.

## Next

  when the previous entry proposed it: the shapes are confirmed identical, so any
  speedup at m=512 translates directly to prefill.
- Measure the iu8 route against the row4 parent **at m in the hundreds**, not at

  the test is exactness, not a KL envelope.
