# the bench runs with resident-capacity 8 and our prefill hits 877 tok/s at 512 rows

## Summary

Two open items closed from the retained refresh packet, without a rerun. The MTP clamp
looks binding rather than inert, because the benchmark itself requests eight resident
slots while the adapter allows four. And the prefill deficit is a small-batch regime
problem, not a kernel ceiling: at 512 rows we measure 877.025 tok/s, which is twice the
peer's best published cell.

## Validation

The command recorded with the packet:

  scripts/bench_chain.sh c1c8-k3 -> scripts/gguf_mtp_c1c8_server_bench.py --widths 1..8
  --resident-capacity 8 --max-tokens 24 --candidate-budget 3 ...

For the MTP clamp: the run asks for eight resident slots. The adapter sets max_requests
to min(4, max(1, capacity)) at qwen35_gguf_mtp2.py:1029, so a capacity of eight still
yields four. The clamp is therefore binding rather than harmless. Caveat kept: the
link from the --resident-capacity flag to the adapter's owner.capacity is by name and
by the resident-loop sizing at qwen35_gguf.py:5031, not by a traced assignment.

For prefill, same packet, post-promotion, medians of 2 with stdev at most 0.25 percent:

- 167.603 tok/s at 45 rows, against the peer's published C1 of 200.946, so about -17
  percent.
- 877.025 tok/s at 512 rows, against the peer's best published cell of 424.072, so
  about +107 percent.
- That is a 5.2x rate increase from 45 to 512 rows. Small row counts are where we lose
  and large row counts are where we dominate, which matches the grid finding: the down
  projection launches 107 blocks and row tiles are 256 deep, so small batches are
  under-occupied while large ones amortize.

So the prefill objective is not "make the kernel faster in general". At scale we already
beat the peer by more than 2x. The deficit is the low-row regime and the wall around it.
