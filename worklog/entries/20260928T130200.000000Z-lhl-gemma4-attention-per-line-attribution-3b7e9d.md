# per-line SASS attribution: the prologue is 24 percent of the body and none of the time

## Summary

Two source-level rewrites on the attention prefill kernel were inert, and a
whole-body instruction count could not say why: it moved by six instructions across
both. The missing information was *where* the instructions are, and `clang -g -S`
carries it -- the `.loc` directives attribute every instruction to a source line.

**The instrument works.** Attributing the strict prefill kernel's body:

| line | instrs | VALU | scalar | source |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 816 | 1329 | 56 | 1273 | `const int64_t kv_head = head / (num_heads / num_kv_heads);` |
| 1029 | 3324 | 140 | 3184 | same line, tiled kernel |
| 1027 | 1630 | 70 | 1560 | `const int64_t token = blockIdx.x / num_heads;`, tiled kernel |
| 400 | 664 | 28 | 636 | `const int64_t kv_groups = num_heads / num_kv_heads;` |
| 401 | 663 | 28 | 635 | `const int64_t kv_head = head / kv_groups;` |
| 398 | 652 | 28 | 624 | `const int64_t token = blockIdx.x / num_heads;` |
| **431** | **400** | **268** | **132** | **`float logit = -INFINITY;` -- the pass-1 loop body** |

### The prologue is a quarter of the kernel and none of its cost

Lines 398-401 and 816 are the block prologue: computing `token`, `head` and
`kv_head` from `blockIdx`. They are 3,308 of the 32,630 instructions in the body --
**24 percent of the static kernel**, and 99 percent scalar. The cause is that every
one of them is an `int64_t` divide by a *runtime* value, and a 64-bit divide has no
hardware support on this target, so each expands to a long shift-subtract sequence.

Rewriting them with 32-bit intermediates -- provably identical, since `blockIdx.x`,
`num_heads` and `num_kv_heads` are all far below 2^31 -- removes 1,622 instructions,
5 percent of the body. Tests pass, the logits are unchanged.

**The wall clock does not move: 1807.9 ms against 1807.2 ms for the 30 layers at
2048 tokens.** That is the result, not a failure of it. The prologue runs once per
block, and with 32,768 blocks per layer across 30 layers the saved instructions are
1.6e9 against a kernel that issues far more than that; at the scalar unit's issue
rate it predicts about 1.4 percent, which is inside this bench's noise. **A region
that is a quarter of the static body is not a quarter of the cost**, and the static
attribution says where instructions *are* without saying where *time* goes. The
change was reverted rather than kept: it does not earn its lines.

### What the attribution did find that matters

**Line 431 is the pass-1 loop body, and it is 400 instructions for one key.** 268
VALU and 132 scalar, against roughly 55 operations that the source's per-key work
accounts for -- 16 loads, 16 FMAs, a 7-add tree, 5 shuffles, a mask test, an LDS
store, an `fmaxf`. That is a factor of seven.

This is the first per-region number the branch has for the hot loop, and unlike the
whole-body counts it is directly actionable: the loop body is one source line, so a
change to it can be recompiled and re-attributed without any ambiguity about which
region moved. Combined with `SQ_INSTS_SALU` as the dynamic check, it gives a
two-sided instrument -- static attribution to find the region, dynamic counting to
confirm it is on the executed path -- which is what the two inert rewrites lacked.

The ratio also says the loop body is *not* dominated by address arithmetic: 132
scalar against 268 vector means the vector stream is twice the scalar one inside the
loop, so the earlier "the scalar stream is the cost" reading was about the kernel as
a whole, where the prologue's scalar divisions inflate the ratio. **Inside the hot
loop the vector stream is the larger one.**

## Next

- **Attribute the pass-1 loop body, line 431, line by line.** It is 400 instructions
  per key against about 55 accounted for, and it is one source line, so the excess is
  inside `gemma4_warp_key_logit`'s fully unrolled 16 leaves and the mask branch. The
  `.loc` directives do not separate an inlined callee's lines from the caller's, so
  the next step is to compile the helper with `__attribute__((noinline))` in a
  scratch copy purely to get its instructions attributed separately, or to expand it
  by hand in a scratch copy and re-attribute. Neither ships; both are measurements.
- Use the two-sided instrument from here: per-line static attribution to choose the
  region, `SQ_INSTS_SALU`/`SQ_INSTS_VALU` to confirm the region is executed, and the
  bench to confirm the time moves. Every inert change on this kernel so far moved the
  static count and nothing else.
- The prologue divisions are real waste but they are 1.4 percent at most and cannot
  be amortised into the hot loop. Leave them unless a different reason to touch the
  prologue appears.
