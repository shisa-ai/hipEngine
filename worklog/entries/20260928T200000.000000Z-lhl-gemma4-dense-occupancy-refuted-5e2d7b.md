# the dense prefill kernel is not occupancy-limited: four warps per CU equal eight, and sixteen is three times slower

## Summary

`20260928T194000` found the dense Q8_0 prefill kernel running **one warp per block with
`__launch_bounds__(32, 8)`** -- eight warps out of the sixty-four an RDNA3 CU holds -- and
proposed occupancy as the explanation for its 9.3 percent of fp16 peak. **It is not.**

| `__launch_bounds__(32, N)` | `4096 -> 2816` | `2816 -> 4096` | `2816 -> 2112` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| N = 4 | 1.512 ms | 1.029 ms | 0.475 ms |
| **N = 8 (shipped)** | **1.505 ms** | **1.032 ms** | **0.475 ms** |
| N = 16 | **4.514 ms** | **3.858 ms** | **1.705 ms** |

**N = 4 and N = 8 are the same number to within 0.5 percent.** Adding warps does not help
at all. **N = 16 is 3.0 to 3.7 times slower** -- forcing sixteen resident blocks makes the
compiler spill, and the spill costs far more than the occupancy gains.

## What this establishes

**The kernel is not occupancy-limited at four warps per CU.** If resident warps were the
constraint, halving them from eight to four would have cost something; it costs nothing.
**The limit is what one warp can issue**, which is consistent with the earlier
instruction-mix finding that this family's bit-manipulation ratio sits at 0.5 to 1.2 -- the
dequantization work per WMMA is the cost, and more warps do not reduce it.

**And the 152 VGPRs are necessary, not wasteful.** The N = 16 result is the evidence: the
compiler cannot fit sixteen blocks without spilling, and the spilling is catastrophic. The
shipped eight-block target is therefore at or near the right point, and the flat 4-to-8
region says the choice is not delicate.

## Consequences for the dense term

**The dense prefill's 184.2 ms -- 27.1 percent of the prefill, the largest single family --
is at its practical limit for this algorithm.** The levers that have now been tested and
closed on it:

| lever | result |
| --- | --- |
| tile `(tile_m, tile_n)` | already swept; `(32, 64)` is the measured optimum |
| route (MMQ32 against WMMA against T16) | converge to within 1 percent at production width |
| occupancy (blocks per CU) | **flat from 4 to 8, catastrophic at 16** |
| bandwidth | 10.8 to 19.4 GB/s per shape, 24.6 percent of peak combined |
| launch overhead | 18 to 20 microseconds, 28 to 77 times faster than the GPU work |
| gap time | ruled out; the step is 100 percent kernel-busy |
| packing | one kernel per call |

**The only remaining lever is algorithmic** -- fewer instructions per output, which means a
different dequantization scheme rather than a different tile, route, or launch
configuration. That is a substantial change and it is not scoped here.

## The in-situ gap, and what this does to it

`20260928T194000` named occupancy as "the only candidate left standing" for the 19 to 23
percent gap between the isolated and in-situ dense times. **That candidate is now removed
too**, and the gap is left with every physical and structural cause ruled out while the
trace confirms both measurements to 1.6 percent.

**The honest statement is that the two measurements disagree by 19 to 23 percent and no
tested mechanism explains it.** It is worth recording that this gap has now consumed four
iterations and three refuted hypotheses (gap time, host dispatch, packing) without a
positive finding, and that the dense term's *absolute* rate is the quantity that matters
for the prefill -- the gap is a discrepancy between two of our own measurements, not a
piece of the step.

## Changes

None retained. The `__launch_bounds__` experiment was reverted; the shipped kernel is
unchanged at `__launch_bounds__(32, 8)`.
