# DE-1 corrected: the decode MoE owner is `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_silu` at 63.43 GB/s

## Summary

This unit **retracts an error I introduced two commits ago** and replaces it with a correct,
reproducible localization of the MoE bandwidth gap to a single kernel.

**The error:** Addendum 1 named `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_wmma_iu8_risk_*` as the decode MoE owner at
`grid = 1280`. **It is the PREFILL owner.** All 192 of its dispatches fall **before** the measured
decode repetitions and **zero** fall inside them.

**The cause:** it was found by ranking kernels by total time across the whole 68 s capture. The
measured decode reps are only **0.68 s** of that, so a prefill-dominated ranking cannot identify a
decode owner. The decode dispatches are also **not identifiable by raw name in that file at all** —
all 5,313 decode dispatches carry renamed role names, and all 13,628 prefill dispatches carry raw
names.

- **New `scripts/qwen4exp_de1_moe_per_kernel.py`** — splits the `moe:expert_gate` family into raw

## Method

Two artifacts are joined:

- `rocprof/x4full_kernel_trace.csv` — the harness renames every **decode** dispatch to its semantic
  role, so this file carries the attribution but not the raw kernel name.
- `rocprof/x4full_results.db` — `rocpd_info_kernel_symbol` maps `Kernel_Id` back to the mangled raw
  kernel name.

The decode window comes from the `qwen4exp_decode_live4097_rep{0,1,2}` ROCTX ranges in the marker
trace; every figure is divided by the three reps.

**The join reproduces the family total exactly — 24.550 ms/step — and the published kernel sum:
162.654 ms over three reps = 54.218 ms/step.** That is the check that it is correct rather than
merely plausible.

## Two findings that change the target

**1. The family figure is a blend.** The 81.73 GB/s attributed to the whole block mixes kernels
running at **63.43 / 113.06 / 184.66 GB/s**. The gate+up GEMV is simultaneously the largest owner
(**57.1%** of the family) and the least efficient (**28.7%** of roof).

**2. The family model's router term is wrong.** `qwen35_router_select_kernel` is the **top-k selection
over 512 logits**, not the router GEMV: it takes 0.331 ms/step, so charging it the router's 251.66 MB
would imply **760 GB/s, 3.4x the documented roof** — an impossible rate the family-level model
absorbed because it charges router bytes to the family rather than to a kernel. The real router GEMV
is `dense_gemv_out_kernel<float,float,false>`, the only fp32 dense GEMV in the block, which matches
`ffn_gate_inp` exactly (5,242,880 B/layer = 512 x 2560 at 4 B/weight). At 1.363 ms/step it reaches
**184.66 GB/s = 83.5% of roof** — a **second comparator inside the same block**, and a stronger one
than `linear:root.lm_head` because it is the same role family, the same step, and the same shape class.

## Headroom, now localized

At the `linear:root.lm_head` comparator rate (226.67 GB/s) the gate+up GEMV's 888.83 MB would take
**3.921 ms** instead of 14.013 ms — a **10.091 ms/step** saving, **18.6% of the 54.218 ms kernel
sum**.

**That is larger than the 15.70 ms/step the family-level gate attributed to the whole block**, because
the family figure was diluted by the router's impossible bytes. The target is now a single kernel,
not a family.

## Confidence

- **Gate+up and down mappings: high.** The kernel names carry both the quant (`q4_k`, `q5_1`) and the
  role (`selected_dual_silu`, `selected_weighted_sum`), and the per-step dispatch counts (47 and 43
  over 48 layers) match one dispatch per layer, with the shortfall expected from this model's
  **dynamic** quantization (layer tensor sizes are not uniform — confirmed by summing exact GGUF
  payloads, which do not scale uniformly).
- **Router-GEMV identification: medium.** It rests on the kernel being the only fp32 dense GEMV in the
  block and on `ffn_gate_inp` being exactly fp32, **not on a name match**. Recorded as medium in both
  the artifact and the ledger.
