# the dense gemv is at 87 percent of peak not 48 and the decode headroom is the non-bandwidth work

## Summary

`20260928T072823` concluded the dense Q8 path runs at 48.5% of DRAM peak and was the
decode target. **That is wrong.** The 48.5% came from dividing the census's *rows=1* byte
total by the `no_dense_q8_both` saving -- but that arm skips **two** kernels, and the
route probe shows the dense path runs two different ones.

## The route probe's answer

```
615  hipengine_gguf_q8_0_pack8_gemv_bf16_bf16_out            (rows=1, a GEMV)
205  hipengine_gguf_q8_0_exact_prefill_tile8x4_bf16_bf16_out (rows=8, a tiled matmul)
  4  hipengine_gguf_q8_0_pack8_gemv_bf16_f32_out             (lm_head)
```

`no_dense_q8_bf16` skips **only** `pack8_gemv_bf16_bf16_out`. So its 7.85 ms covers the
**rows=1** dense shapes alone, which the census totals at 1748.4 MB/step. `no_lm_head_q8_f32`
is the separate `_f32_out` symbol. Attributing the whole dense byte count to either arm
was the error.

## Every weight-moving family is already near peak

| family | bytes | cost | GB/s | % of DRAM peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense rows=1 GEMV | 1748.4 MB | 7.85 ms | 222.7 | **87.0%** |
| lm_head | 784.3 MB | 3.86 ms | 203.2 | **79.4%** |
| experts | 1324.8 MB | 5.65 ms | 234.5 | **91.6%** |

**The kernels that read weights are at 79-92% of DRAM peak.** There is no large
bandwidth headroom left in them, and the "dense is at 48.5%" claim is retracted.

## So where the step actually goes

| arm | ms | share | weight bytes moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense rows=1 GEMV | 7.85 | 22.2% | 1748.4 MB |
| experts | 5.65 | 16.0% | 1324.8 MB |
| norm / rope | 4.05 | 11.5% | ~none |
| lm_head | 3.86 | 10.9% | 784.3 MB |
| moe elementwise | 3.13 | 8.9% | ~none |
| router | 2.35 | 6.6% | ~none |
| attention | 0.84 | 2.4% | ~none |

The four arms that move almost no weight bytes -- norm/rope, moe elementwise, router,
attention -- total **10.37 ms, 29.3% of the step.** They are not bandwidth work at all.

The GEMV arms account for 17.36 ms; the non-bandwidth arms for 10.37 ms; together 27.73 ms
against a 35.35 ms step. The residual is the `exact_prefill_tile8x4` kernel, which no arm
isolates, plus arm overlap.

## The decode target, restated

**The step is not bandwidth-starved -- it is spending about 29% of its time in elementwise
and control work that moves no weights.** That is where a 2x has to come from, and it is a
different problem from the one `20260928T072823` named. Making the dense GEMV faster cannot
help: at 87% of peak it has ~13% of headroom on 22% of the step, or about 1 ms.

This is a correction to my own immediately preceding entry, and the error was avoidable:
the route probe that answers the question already existed, and running it was one command.
