# the decode decomposition sets the plan, and MTP is the larger lever

## Summary

`docs/campaigns/GEMMA4-26B-A4B-OPTIMIZATION.md` carried two detailed gfx1151
**prefill** status sections and no decode status at all, so the campaign's decode
plan was being re-derived every session. This unit adds a
`Current decode status — 2026-09-28, gfx1151` section recording the attributed
budget, what it rules out, what has landed, and where the remaining work is.

The section's load-bearing claims, all measured this session on this host:

- One decode step is 43.88 ms, 3219.6 MB of weight traffic and **825.6 kernel
  launches**, so it runs at **29% of the 256 GB/s this host reaches on a pack8
  `Q8_0` GEMV in isolation**.
- The same-host llama.cpp reference is 41.92 tok/s, which is 135 GB/s on the same
  traffic — **53% of peak, not peak**. So the remaining 1.84x is a gap in
  achieved bandwidth between two implementations that are both short of peak,
  and "reach peak" is not the plan.
- The host floor is 8.12 ms and is hidden (9.8 us of host per launch against
  53 us of device per launch), so removing host cost is worth at most the 3.5 ms
  of measurement slack.
- The lm head is one `Q8_0` GEMV of 784 MB at **195 GB/s** while the 218.7
  per-layer dense GEMVs run at **135 GB/s** on the same kernel family. The
  difference is launch count, which makes this a fewer-and-larger-launches
  problem rather than a faster-kernel problem.
- The landed bit-exact route change is **22.32 -> 22.80 tok/s (+2.2%)**, and its
  gain is small for the same reason.

## Next

The two open objective items are unchanged and both are now precisely scoped in
the document:

1. **MTP.** The head artifact is on disk, its hyperparameters and 49 tensors are
   recorded, the llama.cpp reference algorithm is recorded, and the loader
   contract landed in b42a56c20. Missing: the forward (4 dense blocks at width
   1024 with `nextn` pre/post projections and no `attn_k`/`attn_v`), the
   draft/verify loop, and the adapter registration that would let
   `supports_speculative_mtp` become true. The one open design question is which
   backbone layers' KV the four head blocks attend against — llama.cpp reads
   `attention.shared_kv_layers` in `load_arch_hparams` and never uses it in
   `load_arch_tensors`, and the head creates no `wk`/`wv`, so the answer is in
   the graph context's cache binding rather than in the model loader.
2. **The 1.84x decode gap.** Fewer, larger dense launches is the evidenced
   direction. Two registered Q8_0 pair leaves already exist
   (`linear/gguf_q8_0/pack8_dual_gate_up_gemv_decode_bf16_bf16_out` and
   `linear_pair/gguf_q8_0/rowvec8_dual_split_gemv_decode_bf16_bf16_out`) and the
   dense MLP gate/up is two launches sharing one input, but neither leaf is
   bit-exact with the single pack8 GEMV: `rowvec8` walks eight *consecutive* k
   per lane where the incumbent walks `k = tid; k += blockDim.x`, so it
   reassociates and needs the `docs/EXECUTION-PROFILES.md` production gate rather
   than a route change.
