# Full-attention decode-context kernel: 81% of it in one launch, 7707x K/V re-read

## Summary

`qwen35_paged_full_attn_decode_context_tensor_batch_kernel` is 801.4 ms at 1x -
**56.4% of the `qsa_attention` family** and 5.0% of the whole prefill. It runs at
**3.2% of FP32 peak** while demanding **968 GB/s of L1-level K/V load traffic
against 100.7 MB of unique data, a 7,707x re-read**, because every `(head, row)`
block rescans the entire context. In the launch carrying 81% of its time it also
runs at **25% occupancy**, LDS-limited.

## The task description was wrong on both counts

It said "72 launches over 24 full-attention layers". Measured: **36 launches over
12 layers**. The authority is `role-analysis.json` `exact_role_kernels` - exactly
12 entries, `qsa_prefill:layers.{3,7,11,15,19,23,27,31,35,39,43,47}.attn_q`,
`rows=3` each, 66.70-66.84 ms each. 48/`full_attention_interval=4` = 12, and
`compress_ratios` has a nonzero every fourth position. 72 was the raw 2-pass
count; 24 was 48/2.

Not compute-bound, and not DRAM-bound - 968 GB/s is L1/L2-level demand, and DRAM
could serve the 100.7 MB of unique data almost free. The kernel is bound by a
redundant load stream.

## Structure

One block per `(query head, query row)`: grid (24, 1024), 24576 blocks, 256
threads, 8 warps.

- **No cross-row reuse** - `row = blockIdx.y`, so every row rescans the context.
- **No GQA blocking** - `24/2 = 12` heads share each kv_head, and they are
  separate blocks that each re-read it.
- **QK phase**: warp-per-token. 256 dims / 32 lanes = **8 FMA per lane to produce
  one score**, then 5 shuffle + 5 add to reduce. **18 lane-instructions per 8
  useful FMAs - 125% reduction overhead.**
- **PV phase**: `value_groups == 1` (`threads=256 < 512`). Each of 256 threads
  holds `VALUE_VECTOR=2` dims and serially scans all `context_len` tokens.
- **Three passes over the context** (max, exp/sum, PV) with the whole `scores`
  vector parked in LDS between them.

## Occupancy is LDS-limited and tightens with context

`shared_bytes = (max_context_len + num_warps + head_dim + value_groups*head_dim)*4 + 7 + max_context_len*8`

| context | `scores` | `token_offsets` (int64) | total | blocks/CU | occupancy |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1024 | 4.00 KB | 8.00 KB | 14.04 KB | 4 | 50% |
| **2048** | **8.00 KB** | **16.00 KB** | **26.04 KB** | **2** | **25%** |
| 4097 | 16.00 KB | 32.01 KB | 50.05 KB | 1 | 12% |

At the dominant launch's context, **`token_offsets` is 61% of the LDS**. The
largest offset is `4096 * 512 = 2.1e6` at a 4096-token context, so **int32 holds
any context up to about 4M tokens**.

The selected leaf is `bf16_context_batch_paged_c1_exact_spans` ->
`<true, 2>`. The `<false>` leaf at `paged_attn_decode.hip:6268` is a different
entry point and does not declare the table; its allocation formula must not
change.

## Ranked levers

1. **Narrow `token_offsets` to int32 - bit-exact.** 2048: 26.04 -> 18.04 KB,
   2 -> 3 blocks/CU, 25% -> 38%. 1024: 14.04 -> 10.04 KB, 4 -> 6 blocks/CU,
   50% -> 75%. Only an address table's width changes.
2. **Stop storing the whole scores vector in LDS - changing.** Tiling the context
   keeps scores in registers, removes the array, and collapses three passes into
   one. This *removes* the occupancy ceiling rather than moving it, but it
   changes the accumulation order and needs the production gate plus a strict
   fallback.
3. **GQA blocking, 12 heads per kv_head - bit-exact in principle.** 12x less K/V
   traffic, but scores for 12 heads x 2048 context is 96 KB and does not fit. A
   group of 2-4 heads is 16-32 KB on top of the current 26 KB, so it likely needs
   lever 2 first.
4. **Row tiling - changing unless each row keeps its own warp set.** Same LDS
   budget conflict as lever 3.
5. **Fold `inv_denom` into the PV accumulation - bit-exact.** Removes a pass over
   `context_len` in which 31 of 32 lanes idle. At most 0.5% of the kernel.

## What is not established

**The precise hardware limiter is not isolated.** LDS-limited occupancy at 25%,
the 968 GB/s redundant load stream, and the 125% shuffle overhead are each
consistent with the measurements, and **no counter in the trace separates them**.
The levers are ranked on structural argument, not a measured counter attribution.
Separating them needs a standalone microbenchmark of the 54.30 ms launch with
one-factor-at-a-time variants, which this pass did not run. No per-lever prize is
estimated for that reason.
