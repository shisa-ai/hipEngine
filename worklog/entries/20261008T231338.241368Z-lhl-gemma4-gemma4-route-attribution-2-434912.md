# Correct the correction: the launcher's route counter is per-call and flash serves short context

## Summary

My previous entry
(`20261008T231224.628004Z-lhl-gemma4-gemma4-route-attribution-b5ad1f.md`) claimed
two published statements were wrong. One of them was not, and the claim I made
against it was itself wrong. Recording both halves, because the wrong half
matters more than the right one.

**What stands from that entry.** The scoped decode *selection* is guarded by the
resident LDS bound: `_select_exact_decode_attention` returns the plan's variant
only for a one-token untrimmed global call past
`gemma4_attention_resident_overflows(head_dim=512, keys)`, which is False at
4,096, 8,191 and 15,616 keys and True at 15,617. The `docs/KERNELS.md` row that
dropped that bound was wrong and its replacement is right.

**What does not stand.** I said the sliding-band gate's "122,695 of 122,695
decode launches were flash across both head dims" over-claims its head_dim-512
launches, on the reasoning that the counter may not have been set by the call I
read it after. That reasoning is wrong, and the count was right:

- `attention._launch_prefill` is the single funnel for decode symbols, and the C
  launcher writes the counter on **every** branch it can take (`3` flash, `4`,
  `2` two-phase split, `1` key-class, `0` block). The observer wraps that funnel,
  so a reading taken after a call is that call's selection.
- Below the bound the native path runs flash **by itself**. The condition is
  `flash = admitted and decode_slices(key_count, head_dim) > 1 and (prefer_flash
  or not global_logits)`, so a row that fits the resident budget still takes
  flash whenever the split policy asks for more than one slice -- from about
  1,024 keys. `prefer_flash`, which the production plan's selection supplies, is
  what carries a row *past* the bound. The source comment says the same thing:
  "``prefer_flash`` is what lets a long *global* row make the request ... a
  production plan selects the flash variant explicitly."

So flash serves one-token decode at short context without any plan involvement,
which is what the gate measured and what task 53's premise said all along.

**And the A/B rows are explained by that, not by the scoped route being
inactive.** At 2,049 and 8,193 keys all three arms report resolution route
`exact` and produce bit-identical logits because the plan-variant override cannot
change anything there -- every arm's native path was already running flash. My
previous entry read those identical logits as "the scoped variants were
inactive", which is true of the *plan* and false of the *kernel*.

The staged arm's 15.52 ms against plain's 12.40 ms at the same route and the same
logits remains unexplained, and is still not claimed as a route difference.
