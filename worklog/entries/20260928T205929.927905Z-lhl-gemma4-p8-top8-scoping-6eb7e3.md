# Gemma 4 P8: the top-8 fusion is 6.9% of its family — scope it out

## Summary

P8's last open item — fuse softmax + top-8 + renormalization + per-expert
scale into one kernel the way llama.cpp's `topk_moe_cuda` does — was profiled
before any of it was built, and **it does not pay**. Across a full
prefill+decode trace, the chain the punchlist names accounts for **6.9% of
`moe.route_glue`**: `qwen35_router_select` at 5.766 ms / 450 launches and
`gemma4_expert_weight_scale` at 0.994 ms / 450, against a family total of
97.215 ms. Eliminating both outright would move roughly 2 ms of a 469 ms
prefill. P8's final item is therefore closed as **measured and scoped**, not
built, and the punchlist row carries the numbers so nobody re-derives them.

The measurement also found what `route_glue` is actually made of, and it is
not the chain the estimate was about:

| kernel | ms | launches | us each | share |

| `qwen35_moe_group_compact_active` | 68.905 | 450 | 153.1 | **71.1%** |

**One kernel owns 71% of the family**: `qwen35_moe_group_compact_active`
costs 153.1 us per launch, roughly nine times the top-8 select it was never
being compared against. That, not the fusion, is the route_glue target.

## Measurement

Trace: `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` around `gemma4_family_census.py drive
--prompt 1024 --prefill-repeats 2 --decode 1`, run through the snapshot's own
`cached_build_env(1, ...)`, 18015 kernel rows. Per-kernel totals are the sum
of `End_Timestamp - Start_Timestamp` filtered to `moe.route_glue`'s own regex
(`qwen35_moe_|gemma4_moe_|gemma4_expert_weight_scale|qwen35_router_select`),
so the split is by construction inside the family the punchlist quotes.

Absolute figures here cover two prefill repeats plus decode and so are larger
than the census's single-prefill rollup (28.254 ms at 1024); the proportions
are what the decision rests on.

**Why the 0.22 ms estimate misled.** It is llama.cpp's `topk_moe_cuda`
figure — a measurement of *their* chain, and a fair number for it. Our
equivalent, `qwen35_router_select`, already runs at 12.8 us per launch, and
`gemma4_expert_weight_scale` at 2.2 us. The reference was never a profile of
hipEngine's chain, and the family the estimate was attached to turns out to
be mostly expert grouping and accumulation machinery that a select-merge
would not touch.

**A note on method.** The first attempt at this trace hung for twenty minutes
under rocprofv3 and left a python process holding 19.2 GB of GPU 1, which the
next attempt then failed on with `HIP error 2: out of memory`. This is the
exact deadlock the snapshot documents (`HIPENGINE_REQUIRE_CACHED_BUILD`,
`HIPENGINE_COMPILER_VERSION_FILE`) and the reason `cached_build_env` exists;
running `drive` bare under rocprofv3 triggers the clang probe from inside the
traced process. Anyone re-running this should launch it through
`gemma4_scoreboard_snapshot.cached_build_env`, and should not use
`pkill -f` with a pattern that appears in their own command line.
