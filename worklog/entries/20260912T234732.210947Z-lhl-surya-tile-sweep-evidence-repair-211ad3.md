# Surya tile sweep: reproducible landscape, and a diagnostic that lied

## Summary

Follow-up to `20260912T204843.734713Z-lhl-surya-tile-shape-envelope-1d7c05`.
The shape envelope stands unchanged. Two things came out of re-checking its
evidence.

**The landscape is reproducible.** A second full sweep with the plans re-run in
reverse order on a verified-quiet GPU agrees with the first to a median 0.31%
over all 67 rows (max 5.5%, on 27-29 ms `page_small` rows where 1.5 ms of noise
is 5%). The tool's protocol is sound: a fresh runner per shape and one runner
with a mutated budget agree within 0.5%, and one timed forward and seven timed
forwards agree within 2%.

**A diagnostic of mine measured the wrong thing and nearly produced a false
correction.** A re-check of the 4096-patch shapes reported 192 rows at 873 ms
and 512 rows at 888 ms, against 1094 ms and 1690 ms in the sweep, and the flat
spread of that result read like the sweep's rows had been inflated by the
concurrent GPU job that overlapped it. It had not. The re-check never lifted
`SHAPE_TILE_ROWS`/`SHAPE_TILE_DIVISOR`, so `plan_score_tiles` capped every
requested block at `max(128, 4096/32) == 128`: all four "shapes" ran a 128-row
tile. The give-away is that the re-check's 128-row row (885.88 ms) and its
192-row row (885.95 ms) agree to 0.01%, and that its `page_long` rows -- where
the cap is 200 rows, so 96/128/192 pass through -- agree with every other
measurement. The sweep's `--blocks` path does the lift, so its rows are real.

## Results

The 4096-patch curve is non-monotone, reproducibly

The most useful thing the re-check produced is the shape of the curve it was
aiming at, now confirmed by four independent measurements (two sweeps, one
one-runner probe, one fresh-runner probe), all on a quiet GPU, all with the
block asserted:

| rows | 16 | 32 | 64 | 96 | 128 | 160 | 192 | 256 | 341 | 512 | 1024 | 2048 | 2730 | 4096 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ms | 1575 | 1089 | 939 | 894 | 881 | 909 | 1102 | 1168 | 1175 | 1667 | 1327 | 1137 | 1118 | 1040 |

Two things follow. The budget-derived shape really is a bad pick there (2730
rows, 1118 ms, against 881 ms at 128, 1.27x), which is what the envelope fixes.
But the penalty is not monotone in width, and it is not the wavefront multiple:
192/256/341/512/1024 rows are 24-89% slower than 128, and 512 -- a multiple of
32 -- is the worst shape on the grid, 1.6x the dense tile. So the A4 separation
in the earlier entry justifies *rounding down to a multiple of 32* as a
memory-neutral tie-break; it does not make a multiple sufficient, and the
constant comment now says so. The floor of 128 happens to land on a
spike-free value at 4096 patches, which is luck rather than design: nothing
measured explains where the spikes fall.

## Changes

- `scripts/surya_vision_tiling_cost.py`: `--passes 2` re-runs the plans in
  reverse order and records `median_ms_pass2`/`pass_ratio` per row plus an
  artifact-level `pass_check`, warning on any row outside tolerance. A row's own
  spread cannot see a disturbance that lasts the whole row, so a reversed
  second pass is the only in-tool check for one. The docstring now names both
  failure modes (a concurrent job, and an external shape probe that forgets the
  envelope lift), because both produce a plausible-looking curve rather than an
  error.
- The tolerance started at 5% and was relaxed to `max(10%, 2 ms)` after it
  flagged three `page_small` rows on 1.8-2.2 ms swings (rows that cost 27-35 ms,
  where 5% is 1.4 ms). Recomputing the retained artifact's own rows under the
  retained tolerance leaves 67/67 within tolerance; the artifact was generated
  before the change, so its `pass_check` string still carries the old count and
  its per-row `pass_ratio`/`median_ms_pass2` are what to recompute from.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-09-13-gfx1151-surya-vision-tiling-cost-v2.json`:
  the two-pass quiet re-measurement, retained as the reproducibility evidence.
- No change to `plan_score_tiles`. `max(128, rows/32)` rounded to 32 stays: it
  is optimal at 4096 patches, within 0.7% of the best in-budget shape at 1024
  and 34320, within 3% at 256, and 10% off at 6400 where a 96-row tile wins.
  Capturing 6400 would need a divisor near 64 with a lower floor, which would
  cost 1.5% at 4096 and 2.7% at 1024 and is fitted to one grid.

## Validation

- `python3 scripts/surya_vision_tiling_cost.py --pages page_small.png
  --blocks 128 --with-budgets --reps 1 --passes 2` exercises the new pass
  plumbing (5/5 rows within `max(10%, 2 ms)`).
- `tests/test_surya_generation_contract.py` 42 tests, 0 failures.
- The landscape claims in `benchmarks/README.md`, `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md` and
  `docs/MODEL-SURYA.md` cite the first sweep's rows; the second sweep agrees
  with them to a median 0.31%, so they stand as written.

## Next

- The 4096-patch spikes are unexplained and are now recorded in
  `docs/REFACTOR.md`. Nothing about them is specific to the A4 grid, so a future
  sweep should look for a mechanism (rocBLAS kernel selection at particular
  `(M, N, K)` triples is the leading candidate) rather than more shape rules.
- `--passes 2` doubles the sweep's runtime; it is worth it for a retained
  artifact and not for exploratory sweeps.
