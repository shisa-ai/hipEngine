# VibeVoice Q4 decode-speed: pack8 prototype 5.7x over naive blocks

## Summary

The correctness-first Q4 runner's naive per-column GEMV decode is
slower than dense bf16 (114 vs 66 ms/token); the pack8 repack route is
measured 5.7x faster per GEMV and bit-accurate, and is the identified
fast-decode path.

## Changes

- None landed yet; prototype measurements only (this entry is the
  design + evidence record for the next unit).

## Validation

- Measured on blk.0.ffn_gate (Q4_K, 3584->18944): naive
  gguf_q4_k_gemv_bf16_f32_out 1.54 ms vs
  gguf_q4_k_pack8_gemv_bf16_f32_out 0.27 ms (5.7x); both rel-max ~3e-7
  vs the dequantized reference (lossless repack confirmed).
- repack_gguf_q4_k_pack8 costs ~1.1 s per gate-sized tensor on host
  (one-time at load; ~2-3 min for 196 weights).
- Full Q4 decode today: 122.6 ms/token incl. lm_head (114.1 layers)
  vs dense bf16 66.0 - the naive dequant kernels are compute-bound,
  same lesson as the earlier WMMA-GEMV decode revert.

## Next

- pack8 integration unit: loader repacks Q4_K weights to pack8
  (qweight/scales/mins triples, ~1 byte/value resident), q4 wrappers
  route rows==1 decode to the pack8 gemv family via a
  ptr->(qw,qs,qm) registry mirroring GGUF_WEIGHT_TYPES.
- Q5_K/Q6_K weights (attn_v/ffn_down on half the layers) have no pack8;
  options: t16 repack + t16 gemv, or re-quantize those tensors to Q4_K
  (llama-quantize --tensor-type) so one fast path covers everything -
  the latter is simpler; quality gate decides.
- Then: matched-protocol speed comparison and the WER artifact
  (50-clip Q4 gate in flight at time of writing).
