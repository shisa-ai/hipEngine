# C8 iter32: dp4a schedule screen wins 3/3 — grouped dp4a kernel is the candidate

## Summary

Screened the reduced-dequantization axis named by iteration 31. The
registered `q8_1_dp4a` Q6 schedule (integer decode, u32 record loads) was
timed against the BF16 rowtile owner at rows=4 on actual Qwen3.8 weights,
with the q8_1 quantize launch included in the candidate arm. **The dp4a
schedule wins 3/3**: FFN-down 5120×17408 at 0.5785× the control wall
(0.1323 → 0.0765 ms = **1.73× faster including quantize**), recurrent-QKV
5120×10240 at 0.7245×, attention-V 5120×1024 at 0.9686× (launch-bound small
shape). The axis is validated as the concrete path to closing the 30.353
ms/cycle grouped-R8 pool.

## Structural gap and the required realization

- The registered dp4a kernel caps at **rows 1–4** (`rows > 4 → invalid`);
  the production grouped-R8 route runs rows 8–32. A production candidate
  needs a **new grouped dp4a kernel** (row-grouped grid, ROW_TILE 8, same
  q8_1 input contract).
- The arms have different arithmetic (BF16 decode vs q8_1-quantized dp4a):
  15,954/65,536 FFN output elements differ at rows=4, informational KL
  recorded. Any production realization is changed arithmetic → **L4 is
  mandatory** (strict-teacher mean/tail/max KL, top-1 by category, task
  gates) per the campaign ladder; this screen makes no quality claim.
- The rows=4 comparison is a lower bound on the dp4a advantage at rows=8+
  (fewer rows per weight byte shifts both arms toward memory). If the
  rows=4 ratios held at R32, the pool would drop ~12 ms/cycle (~55%) — a
  projection from the screen, not a measured e2e claim.
