# The dense GEMV family plateaus at ~40-43% of peak bandwidth; the diffusion stage inherits it

## Summary

The diffusion head's GEMVs run at **24-38% of the ~209 GB/s this host sustains**,
and probing the kernel shows that is not a shape or scheduling artifact: the
`dense_gemv_out_bf16` family **plateaus at about 40-43% of peak regardless of N or
K**. Scaling the workgroup count from 512 to 9216 and K from 512 to 8960 never
breaks 43%.

This closes the question the previous entry opened. The diffusion stage's 45%
figure is not a stage-level inefficiency that a better launch configuration would
fix; it is the kernel family's ceiling showing through.

## Measurements

Rows=2 (the CFG batch the diffusion head uses), threads=64, flush written and
synchronized before timing, median of 15, against a measured 209 GB/s peak:

| K | N | workgroups | us | MB | GB/s | % peak |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1536 | 512 | 512 | 47.9 | 1.57 | 32.9 | 15.7% |
| 1536 | 1536 | 1536 | 77.2 | 4.72 | 61.2 | 29.3% |
| 1536 | 4608 | 4608 | 175.4 | 14.16 | 80.7 | 38.6% |
| 1536 | 9216 | 9216 | 328.6 | 28.31 | 86.1 | 41.2% |
| 4608 | 4608 | 4608 | 576.5 | 42.47 | 73.7 | 35.2% |
| 4608 | 9216 | 9216 | 937.5 | 84.93 | 90.6 | **43.3%** |
| 8960 | 4608 | 4608 | 939.5 | 82.54 | 87.9 | 42.1% |

Holding N=4608 and scaling K from 512 to 8960 moves 25.5% -> 42.1% and then
flattens. More workgroups at fixed K moves 15.7% -> 41.2% and then flattens. Both
axes saturate.

The diffusion head's own shapes sit at the low end of that plateau: 37.4% for
gate/up `(2,1536,4608)`, 38.0% for down `(2,4608,1536)`, 29.7% for adaLN
`(2,1536,1536)`, and 2.5% for `noisy_proj` `(2,64,1536)`, which is latency
dominated at 0.20 MB.

## Why this is a ceiling and not a fixable inefficiency

Each workgroup owns one output column and reads a K-element weight row -- 3 KB at
K=1536 -- with 64 threads. That is 48 bytes per thread, or three 8-wide vector
loads, followed by a 6-round shared-memory tree reduction. The reduction's cost is
fixed while the payload is tiny, and widening the payload is exactly what the
shape sweep shows does not help beyond ~43%.

The two routes out are both closed and both recorded:

- Changing the thread count moves the reduction width and therefore the summation
  order. `threads=64` reaches 81% of peak on the LM shape but moves 5 of 17920
  outputs by one ulp and drops the ten-seed quality suite from 58/60 to 57/60.
- Staging the weight row through shared memory to widen the loads bit-exactly
  measured **0.99x**, because the kernel is latency-bound rather than
  load-width-limited.

## What this means for the objective

The objective anticipates 2-10x over the torch lane. Every stage of this model is
weight streaming, the kernel family that does the streaming tops out near 43% of
peak, and the gap to a better figure is arithmetic that this model's generated
audio rejects. The accepted semantic win (1.108 s -> 0.781 s) is the exception
that proves the rule: it changed the column tile and left the accumulation order
alone.

Reaching 2x over torch would need the *traffic* reduced rather than the kernel
improved -- lower-precision diffusion weights are the obvious candidate, since the
head alone streams 123 GB per request -- and that is a quality candidate the
generated-audio suite has to justify, not a kernel optimisation.

## Next

The only remaining lever with real headroom is reducing the diffusion head's
weight precision, which halves or quarters the 123 GB. It needs the generated-audio
suite to justify and is substantial new kernel work, so it should be scoped as a
quality candidate rather than attempted as an optimisation.
