# DE-2 bounded: the authoritative family split puts shared+router+misc at 5.093 ms/step

## Summary

The per-kernel join built for `DE-1` also yields the **authoritative family split** of the decode
capture. It sums exactly to the published kernel sum, it corrects an attribution mistake in my own
earlier draft, and it **bounds `DE-2`'s shared-expert cost from above** - below the row's recorded
figure.

## Changes

- **Addendum 5 appended** to
  `benchmarks/results/2026-09-29-qwen4exp-de1-moe-bandwidth-gate/README.md`.
- **Ledger `DE-2` updated**; CHANGELOG entry added.
- No code, kernel, dispatch or default changed. **No GPU execution.**

**Sums exactly to 54.218 ms/step**, matching the published figure.

## A correction this forces

`gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel<float,float,8>` totals **23.997 ms/step over 497 kernels/step**, of
which **22.184 ms/step is the `linear` family** and only 1.812 ms/step is inside `moe`.

**So it is predominantly DE-3's owner, not a shared-expert kernel.** An earlier draft of the artifact
listed it as a shared-expert candidate; the family split excludes that. This is the second time in
this campaign that a plausible-by-name attribution was overturned by the actual join.

## The shared-expert upper bound

Inside `moe`, the two routed owners are named and charged to exact GGUF payloads:

- routed gate+up `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_silu_logical128_t64` — **14.013 ms/step** (888.83 MB)
- routed down `q5_1_selected_weighted_sum_logical256_t64` — **5.444 ms/step** (615.42 MB)

**The entire remainder of the moe family — shared expert gate/up/down AND the router GEMV AND the
top-k select AND the combine/norm/misc kernels — is 5.093 ms/step.** The shared expert moves
250.75 MB/step and the router 251.66 MB/step, so that remainder moves **~502.41 MB in ≤5.093 ms →
≥98.6 GB/s**.

**DE-2 records the shared block alone at 6.19 ms/token, which is larger than the measured cost of
shared + router + every other non-routed kernel in the block.**

## What that does and does not mean

**Does not prove the record wrong.** The two figures come from different days, models and
compositions; the record may be from a different configuration entirely.

**Does mean the record cannot be used as-is to size a screen** - it cannot be charged against a
budget that measurement shows is smaller than the figure itself.

## What is still missing for DE-2

**Which specific kernels serve `ffn_gate_shexp` / `ffn_up_shexp` / `ffn_down_shexp` versus the
router.** The per-kernel names do not say, and the candidate kernels
(`gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel` 1.812, `gguf_k_selected_prefill_out_kernel` 0.871 + 0.474,
`dense_gemv_out_kernel` 1.363) are not resolvable by name alone.

**A name-to-tensor mapping, or a scoped marker around the shared-expert call, is what the row still
needs before its action can be screened.** The row's action already anticipates this: *"Attribute
gate/up/down and shared-input preparation separately in `X-4`"*.

## Partial closure of the marking gap

Shared work **is** present in the capture: `shared_gate_combine_out_kernel` runs **48 times/step
(1 per layer) at 0.067 ms/step**, inside `moe:layers.N.expert_gate`.

The earlier note that the shared expert *"has NO role in the decode capture at all"* is correct about
**role names** - there are indeed zero `shared_*` entries among the 740 roles - but **too strong as a
conclusion**: the work is inside the `moe` family, and it is now bounded above.

## Validation

- The family split reproduces the published total exactly (54.218 ms/step over 1,771 kernels/step).
- The `linear` composition was read per raw kernel: 22.184 + 0.727 + 0.255 = 23.166.
- The raw-name total for `gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel` (23.997 ms/step, 497 kernels/step) equals
  22.184 (linear) + 1.812 (moe), confirming the split rather than asserting it.
- The routed traffic figures come from summing exact GGUF payloads, not bits-per-weight estimates.
- `scripts/sync_benchmark_readme.py --check`: synchronized.
