# Tighten the flash route's f32 bound to its measured agreement, and correct the flip-rate claim

## Summary

Two things: the battery's f32 tolerance was 100x looser than the agreement the
route actually delivers, so it is now set from measurement; and one number in
`worklog/entries/20261008T175944.497327Z-lhl-gemma4-gemma4-teacher-forced-sensitivity-545402.md`
was stated more strongly than the data supports.

**The bound.** `test_flash_matches_single_kernel_to_float_precision` and
`test_flash_ignores_poisoned_workspace` asserted `rtol=atol=1e-4` for f32. Over
the battery's own 22-case f32 matrix, the worst per-element deviation is:

| bound | worst ratio | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1e-6 | 0.985 | holds with no margin |
| 3e-6 | 0.328 | 3x margin |
| **1e-5** | **0.099** | **10x margin -- now asserted** |
| 1e-4 (was) | 0.010 | 100x margin |

bf16 is unchanged at 6e-3: that is one storage quantum with head (both arms
round to bf16, so a boundary flip is legitimate), and the measured worst is
0.139 of it.

**Why the bound is where it is, and what it does not protect.** The deviation is
**flat in the slice count** -- 9.3e-7 at 2 slices, 7.7e-7 at 64, 6.6e-7 at 256
for sliding/head_dim 256/keys 2,047, and similarly at 8,191 keys. That is not
what a reassociation bound would look like, and the reason is in the kernel:
phase A reproduces the class kernel's warp-tree logits by contract, so the two
arms agree on every logit and differ only in the softmax weighting and the
combine. The bound therefore pins the *weighting*, not the fold depth, and
tightening it is a statement about that. A realistic precision regression
(coarser partials, bf16 accumulation) jumps to 1e-3 or worse and was already
caught by 1e-4; the class this tightening actually guards is a 10x-or-more
growth in the weighting deviation. Stated plainly because the bound now looks
tighter than the protection it buys.

**The correction.** The entry above says the measured bf16 flip rate
"1.2e-4 .. 4.9e-4" matches the arithmetic prediction 1.8e-4 "within a factor of
~2". Over the wider sweep run here, 13 of 16 bf16 rows flip **zero** elements
and the rest flip one, so the honest statement is: the rate is 0 to 4.9e-4 with
a median of zero, at the low end of what a root-mean-square deviation over one
bf16 ULP predicts (~1e-4), not a match to it. The mechanism is unaffected -- a
flip is still a full-ULP event, and the tail shape of the model-level divergence
(p95 2.3e-3 against p99 0.19) still needs a discrete crossing to explain it --
but the *rate* is lower than that entry implies, so the amplification argument
should not be read as resting on a high event rate.

## Changes

- `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_flash_decode.py` -- f32 tolerance 1e-4 -> 1e-5 in both
  places that assert it, with the measured margins and the flat-in-slices
  finding in the comment.
- `scratch/gemma4_flash_bound_probe.py` -- runs the battery's own case matrix and
  reports, per case, the ratio `max(|cand - ref| / (atol + rtol*|ref|))` for
  several candidate bounds instead of asserting. This is what chose 1e-5.
- `scratch/gemma4_flash_slice_sweep.py` -- deviation and flip count against slice
  count 2 .. 256 at both packings, both dtypes, keys 2,047 and 8,191.

## Validation

- Full flash battery on HEAD with the tightened bound: **60 passed**, exit 0.
- The probe's worst ratio at 1e-5 is 0.099 over 22 f32 cases (10x margin) and at
  1e-6 is 0.985, so 1e-5 is inside the measured envelope with room and 1e-6 is
  not.
- The sweep is the evidence for flatness: 8 slice counts x 2 packings x 2 dtypes
  x 2 key counts.
- No RED case is recorded for the tightened bound, and that is deliberate rather
  than skipped: the sweep shows the one legitimate knob does not move the
  deviation, so no realistic perturbation lands in the 1e-6 .. 1e-4 window this
  bound newly covers. The bound's job is a 10x tripwire, and it is documented as
  one.
