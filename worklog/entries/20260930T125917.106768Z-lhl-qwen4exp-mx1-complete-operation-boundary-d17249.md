# MX-1's complete-operation boundary is priced at 7.75% of prefill, and it is a ceiling rather than a net prize

## Summary

MX-1 is the largest remaining open candidate in the ledger: F16 routed experts
with no repair, whose operand admissibility is already measured for all 144
expert tensors and whose gate/up coverage gap is closed. Its headline is
*"Removes the 1239.8 ms repair if the replacement passes"*, and `EG-3` hands the
repair to it as the sole path ("None queued. Either `MX-1` removes it or a
measured mechanism ... reopens it"). `MR-4` is blocked on it.

Its own action says to "include packing, repair removal, map and epilogue costs".
That pricing had not been done, so the 1239.8 ms headline was standing in for a
net prize. This unit prices the complete-operation boundary before the build
starts, so the build has a target instead of a hope.

**It is a ceiling.** The repair is 7.75% of the current prefill, and the route
must reach GEMM parity *while doubling its LDS weight footprint* to collect it.

## Changes

No code changed. The deliverable is a priced boundary recorded on the `MX-1` row
and in `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md`, derived from the X-3 authority
(`role-analysis.json` in `benchmarks/results/2026-09-28-qwen4exp-current-attribution/`,
`2789c19d6`) rather than from the 2026-09-17 repair packet.

The 1239.8 ms figure belongs to a **2026-09-17** capture of a 16,733.4 ms
prefill. The current X-3 capture is a 15,741.963 ms kernel union in a
16,319.888 ms request, 100% attributed across 2,288 kernel rows, and it is the
anchor the ledger's §1.3 already uses. Pricing from the wrong capture would have
been a 1.6% error in the headline and, more importantly, would have priced the
boundary against a prefill that no longer exists.

## Validation

MoE family 6,427.626 ms of 15,741.963 ms = 40.83%. Within it, from the role
analysis:

| bucket | ms | share of union | note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| gate/up iu8-risk (Q4_K + Q5_K) | 2,779.713 | 17.66% | 47 + 1 launches |
| down iu8-risk (Q5_1) | 1,501.188 | 9.54% | 43 launches |
| **repair (4 kernels)** | **1,219.978** | **7.75%** | 96 launches |
| shared router / grouping / epilogue | 821.872 | 5.22% | unchanged by MX-1 |

**Repair composition** (all four kernels, current capture):
`gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_sparse_exact_repair_bf16_kernel` 535.932 (47 launches),
`q5_1_selected_sparse_exact_repair_row_publish_kernel` 522.992 (43),
`q8_0_selected_sparse_repair_kernel` 101.065 (5),
`gguf_q5_k_selected_dual_sparse_exact_repair_bf16_kernel` 59.989 (1).

**The boundary:** MX-1 replaces **4,280.901 ms** of iu8-risk expert GEMM
(gate/up 2,779.713 + down 1,501.188) and removes **1,219.978 ms** of repair.
Packing, map and epilogue **cancel rather than add** — the route changes operand
type, not routing — so the row's instruction to "include" them is satisfied by
showing they are unchanged. Net prize = `1,219.978 - delta_GEMM`, and the
build's acceptance target is **F16 gate/up+down <= 4,280.901 ms**.

**Why the ceiling is not a net prize, quantified rather than assumed.** The iu8
route stages 8-bit operands in LDS; MX-1 stages 16-bit F16. That doubles the LDS
weight footprint per element at the same MMA shape, forcing a smaller tile or a
halved K-slice — itself a cost. And there is no headroom to borrow from a better
GEMM: `ED-4` measured the iu8 owner as the **fastest of three registered owners**
(median -50.4% main-kernel against the runner-up, same host, 12/12 cases), so
MX-1 cannot win on the GEMM and must take its entire value from the repair.

**Ranking.** 7.75% beats `GD-4`'s ~5.5% ceiling and DP-8's measured 0.6%, making
MX-1 the largest remaining single lever and the only one whose
operand-admissibility budget is already complete.

**Efficiency framing, which is what the build actually buys:** the repair spends
**28.5% of the full GEMM's time to correct 0.712% of elements**, and is **22.2%**
of the routed-expert total (5,500.879 ms = 4,280.901 GEMM + 1,219.978 repair).

## Next

The pricing does not decide whether to build — it decides what the build must
beat. Open on `MX-1`:

- The build itself, now with a target: F16 gate/up+down at or below 4,280.901 ms
  with the same routing, packing and epilogue.
- Whether an F16 expert GEMM can reach iu8 parity at double the LDS footprint.
  This is the build's central risk and it is not resolvable by reading; it needs
  the kernel.
- `MR-4` becomes reachable only through this route, because the F16 down output
  is what makes the folded combine read a tensor it must read anyway.

## Limits

Not a promotion and not a speed claim: no kernel, default, flag or dispatch
changed, and the operand-error budget does not replace the production
mean/p95/p99/max KL, top-1, determinism, isolation or task gate. One capture, one
case (`code-p4096`), one host (`gfx1151`, not the default W7900), one model, one
quant, one profile. The bucket split is a roctx-role attribution, and the ledger
already records that the symbol-family buckets and the role buckets do not sum
identically across anchors, so the four numbers above are quoted from one
authority and not mixed with the §1.3 family table.
