# the down projection's 3 TMAC/s ceiling is compute, proven by quadrupling its weight traffic

## Summary

The four iterations before this one optimized the gate/up Q4_K MMQ32 leaf. The prefill floor
entry names **two** expert leaves, and the down projection's fetch count (37,535 MB) is
**larger** than gate/up's (29,080 MB), so this entry measures the down leaf first and asks what
its ceiling actually is. Two answers, one confirming and one that closes a lever.

## Both expert leaves sit at the same ceiling

| leaf | quant | rate |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| gate/up, `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_fused_prefill_compact32` | Q4_K | 3.00 TMAC/s |
| down, `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill` | Q5_1 | **2.95 TMAC/s** |

`scripts/gguf_q5_1_down_mmq_microbench.py` at its own production defaults (128 experts,
2816 out, 704 in, 32 rows per expert) reports **2946 GMAC/s**. Two different quants and two
different kernels land within 2 percent of each other, so the ceiling is a property of the MMQ
design rather than of either leaf.

## The traffic lever is dead, and the experiment is direct

The same harness reports `weight_bytes_x_passes`, the weight traffic the run actually causes.
Sweeping rows per expert quadruples it:

| rows per expert | compact rows | weight traffic | median | GMAC/s |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 32 | 4096 | 24.4 GB | 2.67 ms | 3043 |
| 64 | 8192 | 48.7 GB | 5.25 ms | 3095 |
| 128 | 16384 | 97.4 GB | 10.31 ms | **3151** |

**Four times the weight traffic changes the rate by 3 percent, and the time scales linearly
with rows.** `row_passes_at_32` goes 128 to 512 over the sweep, so the re-reads are real and
they cost nothing.

**That kills the obvious next lever.** The natural reading of `weight_bytes_x_passes` is that
the down projection re-reads its weights once per 32-row tile and that a wider row tile would
amortize them. It would -- and it would not help, because the kernel is not waiting on them.
This also settles the open question in `20260928T135000`: the 66.6 GB of L2-to-L1 fetches
against a 13.2 GB weight set **cost nothing measurable**, so it does not matter whether the
counter is L2 or DRAM.

## Where the 1.36x actually lives

`20260928T133000` resolves the objective: five of six items are done and **prefill is the only
open one**, at 746.6 against llama.cpp's 1012.98 tok/s. Its census puts routed experts at 320.25
ms of the 512-token step (44.0%), dense projections and lm head at 184.85 (25.4%), and prefill
attention at 173.97 (23.9%), with no single term accounting for the 122.8 ms gap.

**So the target was right and the lever was wrong.** Matching llama.cpp on the routed experts
alone would be worth roughly 0.36 x 320 ms = 115 ms of that 122.8 ms gap, which is why the
expert MMQ is still the right place to work. What is wrong is the assumption that the
difference is memory behaviour.

The named compute lever is **pre-aligned nibbles**. The inner loop currently unpacks each
weight qword:

```cpp
const uint32_t q4_word =
    (weight_cache[cr].qs[qword >> 1] >> ((qword & 1) * 4)) & 0x0F0F0F0Fu;
dot = gguf_q4_k_mmq_dp4a(q4_word, activation_cache.qs[qword], dot);
```

That is a shift and an and for every one of the eight dp4a calls per output element, and
llama.cpp's MMQ layout has the nibbles already byte-aligned so its inner loop is the dot alone.
hipEngine has the machinery -- `repack_gguf_q4_k_tile16` and `repack_gguf_q4_k_x8` -- and the
measured value of the pre-aligned route where it exists is **3.30 against 2.84 TMAC/s on
gate/up, about 16 percent.**

**The down projection has no such route.** `gguf_q5_1_mmq_selected_prefill.py` exports exactly
one symbol, `hipengine_q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_bf16_bf16_out`, and
`hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_experts.py:879` binds the down projection to it.
A Q5 T16 path is recorded as dormant in `docs/REFACTOR.md`.

## Next

- **Port the pre-aligned nibble layout to the Q5_1 down projection.** It is the larger fetch
  term, it has no such route, and the layout is worth about 16 percent where it exists. The
  cost is a load-time repack, which `docs/REFACTOR.md` already prices for the Q4_K case.
- **Do not widen the row tile to save weight traffic.** It is measured free; the 4x sweep is
  the evidence and it should not be re-derived.
- **Gate any change here on the census terms, not on a leaf rate.** The leaf rate can move
  without the 320.25 ms moving, which is what the last four iterations showed.
