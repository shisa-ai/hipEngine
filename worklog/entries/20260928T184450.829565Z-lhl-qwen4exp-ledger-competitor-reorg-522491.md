# Reorganize the Qwen4Exp ledger by phase and block, add competitor review and tried registry

## Summary

`docs/QWEN4EXP-OPTIMIZATION-LEDGER.md` is rewritten in place. It now has
separate prefill, decode and MTP sections, organizes open work and the
tried registry by functional block (dense, routed experts, router/reduce,
hyper-connections, attention, GDN, PLE, runtime), and adds a source review of
the engines that currently beat us on this model file.

The main new fact is that **gufo** (MIT, `gufo-org/gufo` `fee9d2a`), a
dedicated Strix Halo engine, reports 1628.52 tok/s pp2048 and 25.87 tok/s AR
decode on the same UD-Q4_K_XL file. Its own profile puts prefill at 681 ms
kernel time per 1K tokens against our ~4168, **3-9x behind in every family**.
halo-box master reports 1312.51 at pp4096 and open PR #91 1636.6. All of
these are author-reported, not reproduced here.

Every fast engine runs reduced-precision operands on every large GEMM at
every layer: F16 dequant-in-LDS dense and routed experts with no repair
(gufo), BF16 MMB (halo-box, pwilkin), int8-activation W8A8 elsewhere, and
F16 KV. Our September 14 accuracy recovery cost −41/−42/−55% PP and TG
18.1 → 11.4 at 4K on the same host. The ledger therefore splits work into
Track A (kernel technology at our arithmetic) and Track B (arithmetic class).
Track B's first step is `X-1`: score the competitors' own outputs against our
strict teacher before building another arithmetic-changing route.

## Changes

- Ledger §1: one rates table with every engine, each row labeled as measured
  here or author-reported, plus a per-1K-token family comparison with gufo.
- Ledger §2: an arithmetic-class table (gufo / halo-box / pwilkin / hipEngine)
  read from gufo `executor.cpp` / `kernels.hip.cpp` and halo-box PRs #63,
  #87, #91 and #104.
- Ledger §3: new cross-cutting rows `X-1` (competitors on our gate), `X-2`
  (same-host reproduction of gufo and halo-box master / PR #91), `X-3`
  (fresh attribution), `X-4` (decode attribution prerequisite) and `X-5`
  (scratch-throttle audit, from halo-box PR #87's 3.3x private-segment
  finding).
- Ledger §4-§6: prefill, decode and MTP open rows by block, including new
  rows `DP-5` to `DP-8`, `MX-1`, `MR-4`, `HC-6`, `QA-4`, `GD-3`, `GD-4`,
  `DE-1` to `DE-8` and `MT-1` to `MT-7`.
- Ledger §7: registry of every Qwen4Exp mechanism tried and not retained
  since 2026-08-27, classified as accuracy / perf / state / withdrawn / dead /
  blocked, each with its worklog timestamp prefix and reopen condition.
  Built from a sweep of about 250 decision entries.
- Corrections to the previous revision (`b49fd2e92`), which contradicted its
  own sources:
  - `EG-4` (expert-population tiles) was listed open, but
    `20260927T081954` measured it dead. It is now in §7B.
  - `QA-2` ranked K/V reuse first, but `20260927T090500` measured K/V reuse
    and LDS staging dead (ceiling ~8%). Tensor cores are now the only live
    QSA row.
  - `ED-2` (BF16 weights) was listed as a prerequisite for `ED-1`, but the
    LDS staging path in `20260927T080534` is bit-exact and needs no BF16.
  - `HC-1` proposed a larger row batch, but `20260906T075517` and
    `20260906T080204` already measured constant-accumulator row retiles.
    The remaining lever is explicit LDS weight sharing.
  - The "~9.5 TFLOP/s FP32 peak" cited in `QA-2` contradicts the measured
    ~28.5 TFLOP/s dual-issue ceiling (`20260915T182229`). That entry also
    overturned the "dense projection cannot be tuned" conclusion (16.1% of
    its mix ceiling), which is now open row `DP-8`.
- No code, kernel, runtime default or benchmark artifact changed.
