# The expert-population sweep closes clean: no decision-bearing row used the 203 figure, and three independent measurements agree

## Summary

Iteration 153 found that the campaign carries two expert-population figures — **~335 of 512**
measured on 12 real canonical cases (`20260910T111500`) and **203 of 512** measured on synthetic
token IDs (`20260927T081954`) — and that the later, wrong one was the one put into use. This
iteration swept every remaining ledger row that cites an expert-population quantity, to find out
whether any *decision* turns on the wrong figure.

**None does.** The ledger has 8 rows citing such a quantity. Four were checked in iteration 153

### The notable positive finding: MX-1 was already on the right basis

MX-1's admissibility screen reads, in full: *"Real natural-input `general_en-p4096` capture, all 48
`expert_down` layers (10240 rows, K640, N2560...)"* with **310 of 512 experts** and a measured
`swiglu_error_f16_vs_f32` rms_abs of 1.0677e-05 against a BF16 floor of 6.7016e-05.

That is **a third independent measurement in the correct regime**, alongside `20260910T111500`'s
335 and this session's 341.54–387.92. So the campaign did not uniformly use 203: **it had the
right figure in at least three separate places, and the synthetic 203 was the outlier — used
specifically in the population-*replay* fixtures**, which is why ED-1's replay and EG-4's skew
figure inherited it.

That reframes the defect precisely, and more usefully than "the campaign used a wrong number":
the error was **scoped to the replay fixture**, not to the campaign's reasoning. ED-1 and EG-4 are
the two rows that consumed the fixture; both were checked in iteration 153 and neither is
invalidated — ED-1 is reinforced by a flatter distribution, and EG-4's conclusion holds more
strongly at the campaign's primary target case.

### Hypothesis outcome

I predicted at least one more closed row would cite a population quantity and be quantitatively
wrong. **Half confirmed, half refuted**: four more rows do cite one, but **none is wrong** — the
one that looked most at risk (MX-1) turns out to have used the correct regime and a real
natural-input capture. The falsification branch ("if every remaining row either predates the
correction's relevance or is stated as a measured replay... then the sweep closes clean and the
ledger's population question is fully settled") is the branch that fired.
