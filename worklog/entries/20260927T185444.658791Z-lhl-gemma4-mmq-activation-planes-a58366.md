# The MMQ gate/up min correction was applied once per activation plane

## Summary

The DS4 MMQ32 fused gate/up leaf applied the Q4_K min-offset correction
`-dmin * xsum` once per activation plane. That term is a property of the
activation, and only the first plane stores the activation's own sum: later
planes store their residuals' sums, because the pack spends them on error
feedback. Accumulating it per plane over-counted it by about the size of the
error the extra planes exist to remove, so the three-plane route measured 1.08x
better than one plane instead of the 22x its pack reconstruction predicts.

The correction is now taken from the first plane only. The one-plane route is
bit-identical before and after the change, and the three-plane route moves from
0.655 percent to 0.030 percent relative error against the fp32 grouped arm at 64
ids. `_MMQ_ACTIVATION_PASSES` is raised to 3, so the default route now consumes
the planes it pays for.

This closes the loop on the previous two entries. The first found that the fused
leaf hardcoded `ACTIVATION_PASSES = 1` and the launcher did not forward it. The
second found a live multi-pass path in the body and left open why it measured as
a no-op. The answer is that the path was live and did consume the extra planes -
it was the min term that cancelled the gain.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/quant/gguf_q4_k_q8_1_selected_prefill.hip`:
  in the f16 DS4 MMQ32 body, apply `-dmin * xsum` only when
  `activation_pass == 0`. The pass loop is a `#pragma unroll` over a template
  constant, so the guard resolves at compile time and the one-plane
  instantiation emits the original expression.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_experts.py`: import the `d4x3`
  pack and the `ds4x3` fused leaf, raise `_MMQ_ACTIVATION_PASSES` to 3, and
  replace the "BLOCKED AT 1" note with the two defects that had to clear and
  their measurements.
- `tests/test_unit_gemma4_expert_route.py`: add
  `test_fused_gate_up_mmq_route_takes_its_min_correction_from_one_plane`, which
  runs the three-plane route twice - once with the pack's natural stored sums and
  once with the later planes' sums blanked - and requires the outputs to agree
  bit-for-bit. This is the contract that broke: the later planes' sums are not an
  input.

## Validation

RED first. With the fix absent, blanking planes 1 and 2's stored sums changed 58
of 1280 output elements of the three-plane route, so the leaf was demonstrably
reading them. After the fix the two runs are bit-identical.

The one-plane route is bit-identical before and after, which is the regression
check that matters because it is the arithmetic the route has shipped. Running a
fixed synthetic one-plane forward against the pre-fix and post-fix kernels gives
the same SHA-256 over the output bits both times
(`6a3f884259148eb8e131a7652a2eacc75e0719af0b0e8e2e89c040b537f3dac4`), which is
expected: with `ACTIVATION_PASSES == 1` the loop body runs once with
`activation_pass == 0`, so the guard emits `acc += ...; acc -= dmin * xsum` -
the same two operations in the same order as the original single expression.

Gate/up error against the fp32 grouped arm, same layer and input, first layer of
the real artifact (`/tmp/mmq_realerr.py`):

| ids | rows | mean abs | relative | ratio vs one plane |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 | 512 | 0.00024462 | 0.0297% | 0.0454x |
| 96 | 768 | 0.00023541 | 0.0305% | 0.0458x |

Before the fix the same two lengths measured 0.005386 and 0.005144 (0.655% and
0.666%), a ratio of 1.08x. The four-arm decomposition confirms the attribution:
at 64 ids the one-plane arm, the three-plane arm, the arm with planes 1 and 2
zeroed, and the arm with only their stored sums blanked give 0.005386, 0.000245,
0.005386 and 0.000245 - the quants of planes 1 and 2 carry no signal (they are
residuals of an exact reconstruction) while their stored sums were the entire
defect.

`tests/test_unit_gemma4_expert_route.py` passes in full (49 tests).

## Cost

The three planes are not free, and the price is prefill throughput. Measured
with `scripts/gemma4_campaign_bench.py --output 4 --samples 3 --warmup 1`, the
same artifact and host, three samples per point:

| prompt | one plane | three planes | delta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 512 | 1.90s (270 tok/s) | 2.09s (244 tok/s) | +10.0% |
| 1024 | 4.11s (249 tok/s) | 4.57s (224 tok/s) | +11.2% |

Decode is unaffected (21.0 tok/s at 512, 19.8 at 1024, both arms). The cost is
confined to prefill, where the leaf's pass loop sits inside the k32 loop and so
trips the gate/up dot-product work.

That cost is accepted here because the plane count was never an accuracy choice:
the route shipped at one plane as a workaround for the two defects above, and
three planes are the design the pack's error feedback exists to feed. The
constant is one line to lower if prefill throughput is preferred, and the
one-plane route is bit-identical to what it was before this change.

## Next

The named clearing condition for this route is
`scripts/gemma4_mmq_prefill_length_probe.py` exiting 0 at every length. It still
does not, and the plane-count change shows why the condition is not measuring
what it claims:

| ids | one plane | three planes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | 2.774e-02 (pass) | 2.049e-01 (over the 0.05 bar) |
| 64 | 1.620e+00 (flip) | 1.289e+00 (flip) |
| 96 | 1.495e-06 | 1.966e-06 |

The three-plane route is 22x more accurate and scores *worse* at 16 ids, while
the 64-id case flips the same token in both arms. The probe is measuring the
model's decision margin, which is not monotone in the route's error, so it cannot
discriminate between the two. The condition should be restated against the
gate/up projection error, which is what the route actually controls.

Two cheap follow-ups the cost measurement opens:

- A two-plane point. The f32 pack has `d4`, `d4x2` and `d4x3` symbols but the
  bf16 pack only has `d4` and `d4x3`, so a two-plane route needs one more C++
  instantiation and export. If the cost is linear in the plane count, two planes
  buy 256x over one for roughly half the prefill penalty.
- The prefill cost is attributed to the leaf's pass loop by structure, not by
  measurement. A profile of the fused leaf at one and three planes would say
  whether the penalty is really the extra dot work or a register-pressure effect
  from the loop, and the second would be worth attacking.
