# VibeVoice-TTS two-speaker diffusion replay is chaos-limited, not a parity defect

## Summary

`test_unit_vibevoice_tts_diffusion.py::test_diffusion_replay_matches_fixture_chain[two]`
failed at "eps step 19 off by 0.366 of peak" against a 0.08 gate. It is not a
defect in the CPU reference. The two-speaker request's first diffusion call is a
**chaotic trajectory**: a **one bf16-ULP** change to its recorded initial noise
moves the final latent by 3.3 relative, and both our CPU reference and our
device head do it. The gate was measuring chaos, not correctness. The test now
measures each trajectory's own sensitivity band and uses it as the limit where
it exceeds the nominal envelope; every nominal gate keeps its full force on the
single-speaker call.

## How it was localized

The failing test replays the fixture's own `call0_condition`,
`call0_neg_condition`, `call0_cfg_scale` and `call0_initial_noise`, so no
prompt or encoder work is involved. Each step below rules out one candidate:

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Per-step error shape | steps 0-5 are as accurate as `single` (eps 0.004-0.008), divergence starts at step 6, peaks 0.65 at step 10 |
| Eps magnitude (relative-metric inflation?) | oracle `|eps|` peaks are 2.14 (`single`) and 2.25 (`two`), so no |
| Our solver driven by the **oracle's** eps | final latent 0.016 for both fixtures; the update rule is correct |
| CPU head, stateless, on the oracle's own pre-step speech | eps error <= 0.023 across all 20 steps for **both** fixtures |
| Device head, same stateless test | <= 0.031 for both; the two heads agree with each other to 0.031 |
| Local gain (eps response to a 0.1 speech perturbation) | identical for both heads at every step (e.g. 0.262 vs 0.270 at step 6) |
| Closed loop, CPU vs device head | final latent 0.274 vs 0.036, with per-step eps 0.65 vs 0.109 at step 10 |

The last row is the anomaly: two implementations with the same local gain and
the same stateless accuracy land 8x apart. The resolution is that the
trajectory is chaotic, so "which side of the instability you land on" is decided
by the alignment of a sub-ULP input difference, not by implementation quality.

The index convention matters for the stateless test: `collect` records the
speech **after** each step, so `call0_eps[i]` was computed from
`call0_speech[i-1]` (and from `call0_initial_noise` at `i = 0`). Comparing
`call0_eps[i]` against a head call on `call0_speech[i]` reports a spurious
0.4-1.4 error on both fixtures.

## The chaos measurement

One bf16-grid step is 2**-7 relative. `call0_initial_noise` is not itself on the
bf16 grid, so a float32-ULP change rounds away and the perturbation must be
taken on the grid the solver consumes (`bf16_round(q * (1 + ulps * 2**-7))`).

Sensitivity band: max deviation of a +/-1-ULP replay from the unperturbed run.

| Quantity | single | two |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| eps, worst step | 0.044 | 1.485 |
| speech, worst step | 0.026 | 0.642 |
| pooled speech RMS | 0.010 | 0.661 |
| final latent | 0.019 | 3.337 |
| scaled latent | 0.020 | 3.383 |

Per step, the two-speaker band is 0.008-0.020 through step 3, 0.047-0.086 at
steps 4-5, then 0.17, 0.41, 0.97, 1.88, 2.56, 2.99, 3.13, 3.08, 2.92, 2.69,
2.43, 2.08, 1.58, 0.89. The final latent across the three variants is 0.274,
3.337 and 0.183 relative, so the unperturbed 0.274 sits inside a band that spans
18x.

The band is measured against the same implementation's unperturbed run rather
than against the oracle, so it isolates the trajectory's amplification from this
implementation's bias. A stable trajectory reports a band below the nominal
envelope and the nominal gate keeps its force; a chaotic one reports a band
above it. For `single` every band is below its nominal gate, so **no single
gate changed**: 0.08 eps, 0.05 speech, 0.02 pooled, 0.03 latent, 0.03 scaled all
still apply there.

## Changes

- `tests/test_unit_vibevoice_tts_diffusion.py`: added `SensitivityBand`,
  `_bf16_ulp_variant`, `_sensitivity_band` and a module-scoped `chains` fixture
  that replays each trajectory once with its band; the replay test gates at
  `max(nominal, band)`; a new
  `test_fixture_chaos_band_is_measured_not_assumed` asserts the band is present
  for `two`, absent for `single`, and load-bearing (the unperturbed `two` replay
  still exceeds the nominal 0.08 eps gate on its own, so the band is not a
  decorative widening). Module docstring updated.
- `docs/MODEL-VIBEVOICE-TTS.md`: new "Trajectory sensitivity in the frozen
  fixtures" subsection under the scheduler contract, plus a caveat on the
  two-speaker status paragraph.

## Next

- **The open question this raises for the two-speaker chain.** The session feeds
  each diffusion frame's latent back into the LM as an acoustic plus semantic
  embedding, and this request's first diffusion call is chaotic. If the
  feedback embedding is reproducible only to the trajectory's band, the first
  span's end may be inherently unreproducible rather than fixable by accuracy
  work. Decisive test: run the two-speaker chain with the fixture's recorded
  `callN_speech_latent` fed back in place of the session's own diffusion output.
  If the chain becomes exact, the gap is diffusion-chaos-driven and the
  encoder/connector accuracy work is the wrong lever; if it stays broken, the
  prompt rows remain the cause as the previous entry concluded.
- The band is a fixture property, so a regenerated two-speaker fixture may not
  be chaotic. `test_fixture_chaos_band_is_measured_not_assumed` is what turns
  that into a test failure instead of a silently wider gate.
- The single-speaker band sits at 0.044 on eps against a 0.08 gate, so the
  nominal eps gate is already close to that trajectory's sensitivity. It is not
  worth tightening without regenerating the fixture at higher precision.
