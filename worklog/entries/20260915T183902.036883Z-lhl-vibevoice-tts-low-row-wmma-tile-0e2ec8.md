# VibeVoice-TTS: the semantic stage is weight-traffic bound, and a narrower WMMA column tile is bit-exact

## Summary

The semantic encoder's deep stages run at a handful of rows while their width
grows to 2048, so their GEMMs are weight-traffic bound, not compute bound. The
`dense_prefill_wmma_out_bf16` kernel's 128-column tile leaves only 64 workgroups
for 40 CUs and reaches 34-77 GB/s against roughly 200 GB/s available. A **BM=64
twin** -- identical arithmetic, half the column tile, twice the workgroups --
takes the semantic stage from **1.108 s to 0.781 s** and pooled RTF from **1.337
to 1.201**, which is **faster than the torch lane's 1.336**. It is verified
bit-identical, so it is a scheduling change rather than a numerical one.

## The diagnosis

rocprofv3 on one `encode_chunk_streaming("semantic", ...)` chunk showed
`dense_prefill_wmma_out_kernel` at **80% of the stage** (30.4 of 38.2 ms per
chunk) across 51 launches averaging **596 us each** -- absurd for a 1-row GEMM.

The reported grid reconciles only once rocprofv3's `grid_size_x` is read as
**work-items** rather than workgroups: dividing by the 256-thread block gives
`gridX = out_features / BM` and `gridY = ceil(rows / BN)` with BM=128, BN=64,
which matches the encoder's row ladder (3200/1600/800/200/40/8/1 -> gY
50/25/13/4/1/1/1) exactly. The two dominant shapes are the final stage's FFN
projections at **1 row**: `(1, 2048, 8192)` and `(1, 8192, 2048)`, which carry
8192x2048 bf16 weights each.

A standalone sweep settled the mechanism. Rows are nearly free -- 376 us at
rows=1 against 784 us at rows=64 for `(K=2048, N=8192)` -- so the kernel pays
almost the full weight cost regardless of how few rows it needs. Measured
bandwidth at rows=1 is **77 GB/s for K=2048** and **34 GB/s for K=8192**, and
deeper K is worse because the K-loop is serialized by `__syncthreads()` and 64
workgroups on 40 CUs cannot hide the latency between them. The 128x64 tile
therefore wastes 98% of its WMMA work *and* runs at 17-38% of achievable
bandwidth.

## Why BM=64 is bit-exact

Per-element accumulation is `k0` ascending, then `kk` ascending, with the
16-element reduction performed by the hardware m16n16k16 instruction. Neither
the column tile nor the shared-memory staging depth participates in that
sequence, so changing BM only changes *which workgroup* computes a given
`(row, col)`, not *how* it is accumulated.

Two supporting observations from the RED attempts:

- Changing the m64 instantiation's **BK from 32 to 16 also stayed bit-exact**,
  which confirms BK only controls staging and does not enter the mma sequence.
- The GEMV path is the counterexample that proves the contract is real:
  `dense_gemv_out_bf16` differs from the WMMA path on **7 of 8192** outputs at
  `(1, 2048, 8192)`, which is the ~1-ulp perturbation that failed the
  generated-audio gate in the previous entry.

## Measurements

Kernel-level, isolated A/B in one process (bit-exact on all six):

| shape (rows, K, N) | BM=128 | BM=64 | speedup |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| (1, 2048, 8192) | 361.1 us | 310.1 us | 1.16x |
| (1, 8192, 2048) | 867.4 us | 629.2 us | 1.38x |
| (1, 16384, 2048) | 2468.6 us | 1917.4 us | 1.29x |
| (8, 1024, 4096) | 144.6 us | 94.1 us | 1.54x |
| (8, 4096, 1024) | 433.4 us | 261.9 us | 1.65x |
| (40, 2048, 512) | 239.6 us | 148.3 us | 1.62x |

End-to-end, `uv run python scripts/vibevoice_tts_session_bench.py` on the frozen
request: `semantic_seconds` **1.108 -> 0.781**, `warm_synthesis_seconds`
**4.457 -> 4.005**, `pooled_rtf` **1.337 -> 1.201**. The torch lane on the same
frozen request is 1.336, so the HIP lane is now **faster than PyTorch** on this
request. `chain_exact` and `negative_conditions_match` are both true.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/linear/dense_gemv.hip`: new
  `hipengine_dense_prefill_wmma_out_bf16_m64` entry point instantiating the
  existing template at BM=64, BN=64, BK=32.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/linear/dense_gemv.py`: `_SYMBOL_..._M64` and the
  `dense_prefill_wmma_out_bf16_m64` wrapper.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/vibevoice/registered.py`: `frontend_gemm` routes
  `rows < 64` to the m64 twin when the existing `outputs % 128 == 0 and
  inputs % 32 == 0` condition holds.
- `tests/test_gpu_vibevoice_low_row_wmma.py`: pins bit-equality across the six
  shapes the encoder actually issues, plus shape-rejection cases.

## Validation

- `uv run pytest tests/test_gpu_vibevoice_low_row_wmma.py` -> 7 passed.
- `uv run pytest tests/ -k vibevoice` -> 126 passed, 11 skipped (unchanged).
- RED evidence: the bit-equality assertion detects a genuinely reordered path
  (WMMA vs GEMV differ on 7 of 8192 outputs), so the pass is meaningful rather
  than vacuous.
- `chain_exact: true` on the real request path.

## Next

The remaining stage split is lm 1.450 s, diffusion 1.298 s, semantic 0.781 s,
decode 0.301 s, prompt 0.174 s. Semantic is no longer the outlier. The same
weight-traffic argument applies to the diffusion head and the LM's decode GEMMs,
which are the next two targets; the m64 twin is available to them under the same
bit-exactness contract.
