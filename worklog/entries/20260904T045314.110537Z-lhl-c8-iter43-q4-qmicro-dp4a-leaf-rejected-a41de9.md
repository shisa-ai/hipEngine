# C8 iter43: Q4 grouped dp4a rejected 0/12 at leaf screen

## Summary

Screened the grouped dp4a mechanism on the Q4_K pool — the third K-quant
family after the Q6 (9/9, retained +1.65% e2e) and Q5 (9/9, retained
+0.44% engaged e2e) wins. **Rejected decisively: 0/12 weight x rows
cells, candidate 1.205-1.638x SLOWER** than the retained grouped
rowtile16-w2 owner on actual Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M weights (ffn_down
[5120,17408], attn_qkv [10240,5120], attn_v/attn_q [1024/4096,5120] at
rows 8/24/32, operation-complete with the shared plain q8_1 producer).

## Why it loses where Q5/Q6 won

1. **Smallest byte advantage:** Q4_K carries 4-bit codes — the dp4a
   mechanism's encoded-byte reduction versus BF16 decode is minimal, and
   the retained owner's BF16 decode is already cheap.
2. **Load granularity:** the qmicro q section is
   [subblock][k][col-pair] with an 8-byte stride per k row, so each
   (subblock, quartet, 4-k pack) needs four 2-byte u16 loads (16 per
   subblock per 8-col tile) — the owner reads wide vectorized T16 tiles.
   The load pattern defeats coalescing; the ALU saving does not repay it.

Two decode bugs were caught by the RED contract before any timing: the
Q4 qmicro meta is half-major like Q5 (empirically verified: min(sb=0,
col0) at meta byte 96), and the quartet bytes are NOT contiguous across
k rows (the initial two-u32-load design was wrong; per-k-row u16 loads
are required). Final contract: 8/8 tests — q8-oracle rtol 2e-2 at rows
8/16/24/32 (and 6e-5 max abs at the 5120-deep shape), outer KL/top-1
floor at 512, determinism, registry, wrapper validation. At in=5120 the
plain-q8 class's own KL vs exact dominates (0.32 for BOTH the oracle and
the candidate), so the KL floor is asserted only at the 512-deep shape,
matching the Q6 test's convention.
