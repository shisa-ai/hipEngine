# A narrow FP32 kernel for the F32 projections of a prompt block

## Summary

The controller, sink, router and convolution projections of a prompt block have 24 to 128 outputs
from 2,304 or 4,608 inputs. rocBLAS ran them at 1 to 5 TFLOP/s: 111 ms of the prefill at 2,057
prompt tokens and 32 ms at 575. A new kernel does them in 51 and 17 ms, and the first token
arrives 3.3% sooner at 2,057 prompt tokens, 2.5% sooner at 575 and 9.1% sooner at 47, where the
same projections run on the whole 47-row prompt. The products and sums stay FP32; only the order of
the sum over the inputs differs from rocBLAS, and the 128 greedy tokens are those of the committed
tree at every depth.

### The kernel

A wave32 holds two rows a lane and splits its work two ways: its lanes cover S slices of the
inputs and MP parts of the outputs, a lane keeps FP32 partial sums for its slice, and the slices
are added in a fixed xor tree. The weights of a chunk are read once by a block of eight waves into
shared memory and read back in 16-byte pieces, with the next chunk fetched under the products of
the current one. A block covers 16 rows: 24 and 32 outputs in one part of 32 slices, 64 in four
parts of 16 with two pieces of inputs a chunk, 128 in four parts of 32.

### Why the weights are staged

The first form had each wave read the weights itself. In a microbenchmark it was twice as fast as
rocBLAS (188 against 91 us for 1,024 rows by 4,608 to 64), but in the prefill it took 203 us a
launch against rocBLAS's 262: the microbenchmark keeps the weights in L2 across its repetitions
and the prefill does not. With L2 flushed before each launch the same kernel takes 1,650 us
against rocBLAS's 417: every block of four rows re-read the 1.18 MB of weights from DRAM. The
staged kernel reads them once per 16 rows: 170 us with L2 flushed and 131 with it hot, in the same
run as 148 and 795 for the unstaged form.

**On Strix Halo (gfx1151)**, the kernel against rocBLAS with L2 flushed: 403 against 459 us for
1,024 rows by 4,608 to 64, 199 against 335 at 575 rows, 160 against 242 at 9 rows, 264 against
301 for 2,304 to 128, 124 against 159 for 2,304 to 24. The model was not run on that GPU.

### Variants that did not make it

- Four rows a lane, or four waves a block: 383 and 334 us hot for 1,024 rows by 4,608 to 64,
  against 131 for eight waves and two rows.
- A longer chunk or a two-stage pipeline in the unstaged form: 300 to 1,000 us, on register
  spills.
- Eight or sixteen waves a block in the unstaged form: no change. The weights were not the limit
  while they stayed in L2.

### The rows it starts at

Below nine rows a row at a time takes the matrix-vector product for 64 and 128 outputs (two rows:
31 us against 114 for the kernel with its weights hot in L2), and for 24 and 32 outputs the kernel
is faster from two rows (three rows: 29 against 38 us). One row keeps the matrix-vector product
under every profile. `strict` keeps rocBLAS.
