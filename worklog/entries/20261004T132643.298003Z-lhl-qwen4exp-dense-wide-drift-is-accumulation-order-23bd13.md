# The dense-wide drift is accumulation order, verified - the 8-47 scope lever is blocked

## Summary

**The dense-wide route's numerical drift is accumulation-order error, and that is
verified rather than inferred. Extending the route to layers 8-47 is therefore
blocked by the arithmetic itself, not by a missing optimization.**

The route is worth **+8.52% prefill** at 8-47 but fails the production gate. The
passing frontier is exactly 13-47. This entry records why the remaining five layers
cannot be recovered by making the route more accurate.

## The mechanism, established by reading both kernels

Both the wide route and the coltile parent it replaces use **f16 operands with f32
accumulation** and the same `wmma_f32_16x16x16_f16` intrinsic:

| | wide (`gguf_q8_0_dense_wide.hip`) | parent (`gguf_q8_0_t16_prefill.hip`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dequant | `static_cast<half_t>(d0 * float(q))`, `d0` converted f16 to **f32** | `d_h[tm] * static_cast<half_t>(float(q))`, `d_h` stays **f16** |
| product | computed in **f32**, rounded once to f16 | computed in **f16**, rounded once |
| accumulator | `float` (f32) | `float` (f32) |
| K order | `kk = 0,16,32,48` per 64-wide stage | `kt` over 2 tiles per 32-wide Q8_0 block |
| K tiling | `WIDE_BK = 64` staged in LDS | `K_TILE = 16`, `K_TILES_PER_BLOCK = 2` |

**The two dequant paths produce identical f16 values.** The wide route's product is
computed in f32 and rounded once; the parent's is computed in f16 and rounded once.
Both are a single correctly-rounded operation on the same exact product - and that
product is exactly representable in f32, because f16's 11 significand bits times
int8's 7 needs at most 18, well inside f32's 24. `(float)q` to f16 is likewise
lossless since int8 fits f16 exactly. **So the weight rounding is shared, not the
difference.** The K order is also identical (sequential in steps of 16).

## The verification that makes this conclusive

`tests/test_gpu_gguf_q8_0_dense_wide.py::test_dense_wide_matches_the_f16_reference`
already asserts exactly this bound, and its docstring names the mechanism:

> Accumulation-order error only: f32 accumulation over K terms with f16 operands. A
> structural bug misses by the f16 rounding scale instead.

It asserts `max|out - f16_ref| <= 1e-3 * max|f16_ref|` for every variant, with a
second outer gate at `1e-2` against the exact reference. **20 tests pass, exit 0.**

**That is the conclusive evidence.** A dequant or precision defect would miss at the
f16 rounding scale; the route instead lands inside accumulation-order error. So the
route implements the arithmetic it claims, and the residual difference from the
coltile parent is pure reassociation.

## Why there is no more-accurate accumulation to find

**WMMA on RDNA3 has no f32-operand form.** The available operand formats are f16
(11 significand bits) and bf16 (8). f16 is the most precise one, so the route is
already at the accuracy floor of the formulation that gives it its speed. Using
bf16 would be strictly worse. The only way to reduce the drift is to change the
accumulation order to match the parent exactly, which is the tiling the wide route
exists to avoid.

This also explains the earlier refutation recorded in the 13-47 promotion entry: the
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_Q8_DENSE_WIDE_F16_ACT` knob is a no-op at these shapes because the
binder already resolves to the f32-input owner, so there is no activation-precision
trade to make either.

## Next

**The scope lever is closed; the remaining prefill gap must come from elsewhere.**
The route is promoted at 13-47 and yields +3.0% at `code-p4096`. Reaching 8-47 would
add roughly another 5.5% but is gated by accumulation-order error that cannot be
reduced within the f16-operand formulation.

The open prefill question is therefore the one the campaign already localised: the
gap to llama.cpp is a **uniform 1.45-1.55x per-token deficit rather than setup
cost**, so the next unit should price the per-token work of the promoted path - the
launch breakdown and the kernel-family time for a single prefill token - rather than
the layer scope. 12-47 failing top-1 by **one row** is worth remembering as the
closest point: if a future change moves the numerical envelope at all, that is the
cheapest scope to re-test.
