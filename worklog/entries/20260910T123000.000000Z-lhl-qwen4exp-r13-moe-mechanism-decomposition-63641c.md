# R13 step 1-3: MoE prefill mechanism decomposition, routing distribution, tile-policy derivation

## Summary

R13 steps 1-3 decompose the 6x MoE prefill gap into measurable factors. At
code-p4096 the MoE block costs 6,013.6 ms, split across expert gate/up (52.8%),
expert down (21.1%), iu8 repair (12.5%), router (10.6%) and scatter/gather/combine
(2.7%). Routing is bottom-heavy: per-expert row counts p50=12, p75=31, p90=68,
p99=238, max=1024, and fixed 16-row expert tiles already waste **32.75%** of
compacted rows (32 would waste 74.7%, 48 would waste 121%, so the
waste-minimizing policy over {16,32,48} always picks 16).

Four factors explain the gap against the external routed 745 ms + reduce 200 ms:
the three-plane iu8 exactness tax (~3x compute on gate/up), the 32.75% fixed-16
padding waste, 754 ms of iu8 repair passes, and ~964 ms running the legacy compact
kernel on the non-iu8 layers. A 1-plane, zero-waste gate/up would run ~1.06 s,
matching the external 745 ms within overheads.

## Routing distribution (12 canonical cases, 1,152 layer-chunk captures, 10.81M pairs)

Per-expert row counts: **p50=12, p75=31, p90=68, p99=238, max=1024**;
the instance histogram is bottom-heavy (count 1: 40,330 instances down
to count 15: 6,723). Padding waste at fixed 16-row expert tiles:
**32.75%** of all compacted rows. Fixed 32 wastes 74.7%, fixed 48

derived waste-minimizing policy over {16,32,48} always picks 16.

## The 6x gap decomposes (external routed 745 ms + reduce 200 ms vs our 6,014 ms)

| Factor | effect | evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| three-plane iu8 exactness tax (gate/up) | ~3x compute | IU8_ACT_PLANES=3 in the kernel; same structure R12 measured as +1.4x per plane pair removed |

| legacy compact layers | ~964 ms on the slow path | dispatch counts |

A 1-plane, zero-waste gate/up would run ~1.06 s - matching the external
745 ms within overheads. The external's 48/32/16 tile selection is a
row-count choice over the GEMM M dimension of their compacted-row

their per-expert-style larger tiles would waste more, so our advantage
must come from plane count and small-expert handling, not bigger tiles.

## Validation

- Subowner split is R2e kernel-level attribution at code-p4096, total 6,013.6 ms,
  with dispatch counts per kernel (`dual_wmma_iu8_risk` 1470 ms x26,
  `compact_legacy` 964 ms x8, `dual_wmma_iu8` 676 ms x13,
  `router_logits_f32_token_tile` 617 ms, and so on).
- Routing distribution measured over 12 canonical cases: 1,152 layer-chunk
  captures and 10.81M pairs. Per-expert row counts p50=12, p75=31, p90=68,
  p99=238, max=1024; the instance histogram is bottom-heavy (count 1: 40,330
  instances down to count 15: 6,723).
- Padding waste at fixed tiles is computed from that distribution: 16-row 32.75%,
  32-row 74.7%, 48-row 121%; the derived waste-minimizing policy over {16,32,48}
  always picks 16.
- The external comparison (routed 745 ms + reduce 200 ms) is a **different-machine**
  figure used as a target, not a same-host baseline.
- Stated inference, labelled: the ~1.06 s "1-plane, zero-waste gate/up" figure is a
  projection built from the R12 +1.4x-per-plane-pair measurement, not a
  measurement of a built kernel.
- Stated finding: the external's 48/32/16 tile selection is a row-count choice
  over the GEMM M dimension of their compacted-row layout, not per-expert padding
  tiles. On our measured distribution their per-expert-style larger tiles would
  waste more, so our advantage must come from plane count and small-expert
  handling rather than bigger tiles.

## Experiment ladder (next window)

1. **MoE gate/up 2-plane variant** (IU8_ACT_PLANES 3->2): +1.4x per leg
   by the R12 measurement; needs its own envelope qualification - the
   MoE drift structure (10 experts weighted-summed per token) differs
   from the dense legs R12 rejected. Reuse the subprocess-per-arm probe.
2. **Small-expert fast path**: most experts have 1-15 rows (mode 1-2).
   WMMA cannot pack different experts into one A-fragment (B differs per
   expert), so the options are a dp4a sub-16 path (T1: accumulation
   order; the iu8 risk/repair machinery may cover it) or tighter
   fragment utilization. Recoverable: up to the full 32.75% waste.
3. **Hot-expert path** (count >= 48: p90+ experts hold most pairs):
   multi-fragment blocks with better weight L2 reuse (T1: reduction
   order changes).
4. **Router**: 617 ms for 107 GFLOP (0.17 TF/s) - the router logits
   write 512 F32 per token then a separate select; a fused
   projection+top-10 (the P3 counter-last variant was rejected for
   prefill speed; revisit at MoE scope) is a separate bounded item.
5. Repair elimination is not a lever while the iu8 chain stays (it IS
   the exactness guarantee); it collapses only if the plane-count route
   is admitted.
