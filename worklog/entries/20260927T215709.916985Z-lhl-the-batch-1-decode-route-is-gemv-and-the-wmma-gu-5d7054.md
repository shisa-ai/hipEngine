# The batch-1 decode route is GEMV, and the WMMA guards confirm it
## Summary
Two things came out of one read of the dispatch conditions, and both matter for
the 2X decode target that the previous entry localised to the weight path.
## 1. The withdrawn profile is corroborated as contaminated
Every WMMA path in `hipengine/runtime/gguf_linear.py` is guarded by `rows > 1`:
```
6966:    if use_wmma and rows > 1:
7046:            if _dispatch_can_use_wmma_prefill(d, rows=rows, ...):
7185:    if use_wmma and rows > 1 and (
```
Decode is `rows == 1`, so **no WMMA kernel runs during decode at all**. The
withdrawn trace nonetheless ranked `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma<32,32>` at 6.4 percent
and `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma<64,32>` at 3.0 percent inside what it called the
decode window. Those dispatches can only be prefill work, which is independent
confirmation that the window was contaminated and that the correction in
`worklog/entries/20260927T215124.493173Z-lhl-the-decode-profile-was-traced-cold-and-its-numbe-573e4b.md`
was right. It also confirms the flat-in-context result's reading, since the same
grouping error would have put prefill work into the decode share.
## 2. The decode route is the GEMV family
At `rows == 1` the dense projections and the expert projections take the GEMV
routes, not the tiled or WMMA routes. That is the expected and correct dispatch.
It also names the mechanism behind the ~50 GB/s reading: a GEMV at one row reads
an entire weight matrix to produce a single row of output, so its bandwidth
utilisation depends entirely on how many independent blocks are in flight. That
is the classic shape for reaching a fraction of peak, and it is a plausible
explanation for 50 GB/s against llama.cpp's 94 — but it is a **hypothesis about a
mechanism, not a measurement**, and this entry does not claim it as the cause.
## What this does not establish
That the GEMV routes are the reason decode reaches 50 GB/s rather than 94. It
establishes which route runs and that no WMMA runs in decode. The next step is
still an ablation on `decode_tps`, not an inference from kernel names.
