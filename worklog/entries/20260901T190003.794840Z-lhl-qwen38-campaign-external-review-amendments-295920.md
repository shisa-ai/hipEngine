# Campaign amendments from Pat1entZ3r0 source review

## Summary

Second-pass review of
`Pat1entZ3r0/strix-qwen-next-flash-optimization@413c33c` after survey section
6.7 landed in `f8fa29642`, folding the transferable material into the
performance campaign as executable punchlist items ahead of a coder handoff.
Nothing here is a measurement. Every added item is an untested hypothesis with
a named falsification, and three externally published claims are recorded as
non-reproducing or non-transferable so no future unit is planned or rejected on
their authority.

The repository is a single-commit consolidation of the three forks the campaign
already tracks (`SOURCE_LOCK.json` pins base `c589f0ed1` + #27879, apepojken
`843d575`, Nathan `ad914eb`, EngramHalo `1423f689`). No rate in it binds:
different quant, K/V, backend, and host instance. Two evidence defects were
localized beyond section 6.7: its `pr-27742-035e227` baseline predates #27879,
so the 2.5-3x headline denominator still carries the QSA block-selection bug
that the program's own patch `0001` fixes; and its `MODEL_LOCK.json` pins this
campaign's exact UD-Q4_K_XL shards (revision `c8b5954a`, four shards
LFS-verified) while publishing no row for them.

Two reframes against section 6.7's phrasing. Its Nathan criticism is better
stated as a design gap than an application failure: a single-capture-A vs
single-capture-B gate structurally cannot separate "B is wrong" from "B is
nondeterministic", so the defect is the missing repeat arm, not an unfired
gate. And the near-tie tolerance and the position-50 rule deserve opposite
verdicts: the 0.04-nat margin classifier is sound, while classification by
output index is not, since rejection-triggered state corruption surfaces
wherever the rejection happens. The program's own EXP-016 bug is the
counterexample to its own rule.
