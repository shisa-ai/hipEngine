## The metric finding, which is the more useful half

`results.jsonl` holds 107 iterations with **0 keeps and 0 reverts**, and metric
values spanning **0.00038 to 2245** - shares, percentages, milliseconds, byte
counts and candidate counts interleaved, because `multiloop_measure` was called
with whatever that iteration happened to measure. The recorded baseline 10 /
current 6 / best 10 are therefore not a baseline, and keep/revert has never been
meaningful for this loop.

The census is offered as the loop's real metric: the count of actionable rows,
25 as of this date, which can only fall when a row closes. **This is a definition
change, not a regression against 6** - the previous 6 was not the same quantity,
and reporting 25 without saying so would look like a 4x regression.

## Why the census matters more than it looks

The count of rows is not a count of prizes. The 25 actionable rows include X-1
("informational, not a prerequisite"), X-8 ("separate lane"), X-9
("maintenance"), DP-2 ("low value"), MR-3 ("low value"), DE-6 ("low"), DE-8
("lower priority"), DE-9 ("separate serving lane") and QA-4 ("long-context
lane"). **The rows carrying measured millisecond-scale value are already closed**,
including the largest identified decode prize (DE-1, 10.09 ms/step of headroom,
closed by its own scope-space exhaustion). A reader who took "25 remaining" as
"25 prizes" would mis-plan the campaign.
