# the expert arm was hooked to the wrong entry point and fixing it makes the two instruments agree

## Summary

`20260928T072701` proved the ablation's `no_experts` arm was broken -- 0.54 ms for
1324.8 MB implies 958% of DRAM peak. This finds the bug, fixes it, and re-runs. The
expert share goes from **1.5% to 16.0%**, and the census's bandwidth check now passes.

## The bug

The census hooks three expert entry points. The ablation hooked only the first:

```
ex.gemma4_project_experts_selected          <- the ONLY one the ablation hooked
ex.gemma4_project_experts_gate_up_mmq       <- census hooks this, ablation did not
ex.gemma4_project_experts_down_mmq          <- census hooks this, ablation did not
```

The census's own labels say which ones run: `expert-mmq gate_up gguf_q4_k` at 30.93
launches/step and `expert-mmq down gguf_q5_1` at 30.93 launches/step. **The decode route
does not go through `_selected`.** The arm was skipping a function that moved no bytes,
which is exactly why its saving was impossible for the bytes the census attributes to the
expert path.

Fix, in `scripts/gemma4_decode_route_ablation.py`: capture and spy
`gemma4_project_experts_gate_up_mmq` and `gemma4_project_experts_down_mmq` in the
`experts` mode alongside `_selected`.

## Before and after

| arm | before | after | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `no_experts` | 0.54 ms (1.5%), **0.65 sigma** | **5.65 ms (16.0%), 11.47 sigma** | 10.5x |
| `no_attention` | 2.06 ms (5.8%) | **0.84 ms (2.4%)**, 2.69 sigma | also changed |
| `no_dense_q8_both` | 15.23 ms (42.9%) | 15.56 ms (44.0%), 48.90 sigma | stable |
| baseline | 35.53 ms, 28.14 tok/s | 35.35 ms, 28.29 tok/s | stable |

`no_attention` moved too, which is worth noting: the broken expert arm was also perturbing
the attention number, so `20260928T072450`'s 5.8% was not just the expert figure being
wrong.

## The validation that matters

The fix is confirmed by the **independent** instrument rather than by the new number
being more agreeable:

| | bytes | cost | bandwidth | % of peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| experts, before | 1324.8 MB | 0.54 ms | 2453 GB/s | **958% -- impossible** |
| **experts, after** | 1324.8 MB | **5.65 ms** | **234.5 GB/s** | **91.6% -- plausible** |
| dense Q8 | 1930.9 MB | 15.56 ms | 124.1 GB/s | 48.5% |
| **whole step** | 3255.7 MB | 35.35 ms | 92.1 GB/s | 36.0% |

**91.6% of DRAM peak is a physically possible number for a GEMV**, and the two
instruments now agree on the same route. Before the fix they could not both be right;
that disagreement is what located the bug.

## The decode picture now

| arm | share | sigma | efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense Q8 | **44.0%** | 48.90 | **48.5% of peak** |
| experts | **16.0%** | 11.47 | **91.6% of peak** |
| norm / rope | 11.5% | 16.41 | -- |
| lm_head | 10.9% | 16.42 | -- |
| moe elementwise | 8.9% | 14.78 | -- |
| router | 6.6% | 4.66 | -- |
| attention | 2.4% | 2.69 | -- |

Every arm is now at least 2.69 sigma; the table is resolvable end to end for the first
time.

**The two large terms have opposite headroom.** The expert path at 91.6% of DRAM peak has
essentially none -- it is done. The **dense Q8 path at 48.5% is the target**: at the
efficiency the expert path already demonstrates, its 1930.9 MB would take **8.23 ms
instead of 15.56 ms, saving 7.33 ms** and taking the step from 35.35 to 28.02 ms, or
**28.29 -> 35.7 tok/s**.

The whole-step floor remains 3255.7 MB / 256 GB/s = 12.72 ms = 78.6 tok/s, so the
objective's 41.9 tok/s still has room after that.
