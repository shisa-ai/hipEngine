# YuE2 stage comparisons corrected to matched steady-state work

## Summary

The review asked for the NAR and VAE comparisons to run on identical conditioning,
noise or latents, and that request exposed two defects in how this campaign has been
reporting against the pinned upstream. First, the solver comparison was comparing two
different trajectories: our seeded noise draw is not the reference's, and nothing
checked. Second, the decoder comparison put our warm run against the reference's
recorded product run, and the reference's first decode in a process costs **163.1 s
against its own 19.6 s steady state** on this device. On identical inputs and steady
state the three stages are: AR decode 58.30 ms per step against 30.87 (**reference
1.89x faster**), acoustic solver 25.87 s against 20.12 s (**reference 1.29x faster**)
and FP32 decode 20.01 s against 19.60 s for 1 297 frames (**level**). So this runtime is
behind on two stages and level on the third, and the product-level advantages reported
earlier come from the reference's per-request overheads rather than from faster math on
our side.

## Changes

- `scripts/yue2_stage_matched_timing.py` (new): matched `nar` and `vae` timing for the
  torch-free runtime, with `--compare` and `--merge` modes. It compares warm passes and
  prints the first pass alongside, refuses to compare sides whose case, step count,
  prefix or code digests differ, and for `nar` takes `--noise` so it can consume the
  reference's own whole-song draw.
- `scripts/yue2_reference_stage_timing.py` (new, oracle only): the same protocol on the
  reference's own `CachedNAR.solve` and `YuE2VAE.decode`/`decode_tiled`, and it writes
  the whole-song noise it drew next to its JSON for the torch-free side to consume.
- `benchmarks/results/yue2_nar_matched_timing_20260917.json`,
  `benchmarks/results/yue2_vae_matched_timing_20260917.json` (new artifacts),
  `benchmarks/README.md` (a matched stage table, plus corrections on the two earlier
  tables), `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md`.
- No runtime change: this is a measurement unit.

## Validation

- Commands, run sequentially so the two sides never contend for the GPU:
  `PYTHONPATH=~/yue2-shootout/shared/upstream ~/venvs/vibevoice-tts-oracle/bin/python
  scripts/yue2_reference_stage_timing.py --stage nar --case mandarin-off-s1234 --repeats 2 --json /tmp/yue2_nar_ref2.json`,
  then
  `python3 scripts/yue2_stage_matched_timing.py --stage nar --case mandarin-off-s1234 --repeats 2 --noise /tmp/yue2_nar_ref2.json.noise.npy --json /tmp/yue2_nar_hip2.json`,
  then the same pair for `--stage vae`.
- Protocol identity is enforced in code. The solver run reports
  `initial noise digest matches: 7c3a6164aaa7bdd4`, so both sides solved the same
  trajectory; the earlier run of this comparison reported a mismatch and its timing is
  discarded.
- Solver: ours 25.870 s warm (26.128 s first), reference 20.120 s warm (20.457 s first),
  both 32 steps. Latent norm 287.400 against 287.113 on the shared noise, a 0.1%
  difference consistent with the M4 gate's BF16-level agreement, and the digests differ
  as expected for BF16 rounding.
- Decoder: ours 20.013 s warm (20.107 s first), reference 19.602 s warm (**163.087 s
  first**), both 1 297 frames tiled at core 1024 / halo 16, both producing 2 490 176
  samples. Our tiled output is bit-identical to our own full decode (max abs 0.0); the
  reference's tiled and full differ by 1.40e-06, which is its own FP32 noise floor. The
  reference's full decode of these latents measured 170.6 s in this session against its
  recorded 69.09 s, so its full path is memory-bound here and is not used for the claim.
- `scripts/sync_benchmark_readme.py --check` -> blocks synchronized.

## Corrected claims

- The decoder's **6.18x** (twelve cases) and **3.44x** (one case) product-level
  advantages are not decode-math wins. On identical latents and identical tiling the two
  implementations are level (20.01 s against 19.60 s). The product difference is the
  reference's per-request decoder transfer plus first-use cost: its recorded product
  decode of these latents is 69.09 s against its own 19.60 s warm.
- The solver's product-level **1.05x faster** (26.07 s against 27.32 s) is against that
  same recorded number, which is 27.32 s against its own 20.12 s warm. On matched work
  the reference is 1.29x faster, so the honest product statement is that the two sides
  are close because the reference pays per-request overheads that we do not, not that our
  solver is ahead.
- The AR comparison from the previous unit stands: 1.89x on matched work.
