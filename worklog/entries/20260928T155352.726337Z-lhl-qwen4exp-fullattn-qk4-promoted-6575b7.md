# Promote the four-token QK unroll and reject the per-block base table

## Summary

The dense full-attention decode-context leaf gets a four-token QK unroll, taking
its largest launch from 54.611 ms to **28.563 ms (1.912x)** and p4096 prefill
from a 248.487 tok/s category mean to **255.066 (+2.65%)**. A second candidate -
replacing the per-token offset table with a per-block base table - was
implemented, measured at **1.41x slower**, and removed.

This supersedes `20260927T175027.679637Z-lhl-qwen4exp-fullattn-context-promoted-9304df.md`,
which promoted the first two levers (int32 token-offset table, two-token QK
unroll) for 1.734x on the same launch and +2.11% end to end.

## The rejected lever, and a falsified prediction

The previous entry named the per-block base table as the next lever and estimated
1.2-1.4x on top of the then-promoted route. **That estimate was wrong in sign.**
Cutting the shared-memory request from 18,471 to 10,423 bytes and doubling
residency from 3 to 6 blocks per CU made the launch 1.41x slower.

The mechanism is that the table is not overhead but a precomputed address stream:
reading the next address from shared memory lets the hardware issue independent
loads, while the incremental tracker puts the address on a serial recurrence that
every load depends on. In a latency-bound kernel that recurrence costs more than
the table ever did. The int64 variant's 1.38x regression at only one fewer block
per CU is the same effect read the other way, which is why the int32 table sits
between two worse extremes rather than at one end.

That reading also explains why the int32 lever worked so well: it kept the address
stream and halved its cost. It predicts that further occupancy work on this leaf
will not pay unless it preserves independent address generation.

## Next

- The occupancy axis is now measured, not assumed: the int32 table is at a local
  optimum, and the two neighbouring configurations are both worse. Further gains
  on this leaf have to come from reducing K/V traffic rather than from shared
  memory, for example blocking query heads so one block's K/V read serves more
  than one head.
- The leaf is a small share of prefill. The larger untouched targets remain the
  routed-MoE and dense Q8_0 prefill kernels identified earlier in this campaign.
