# the expert down projection cannot take a Q4T16 route: its tensor is Q5_1, and a 704-wide K is not a whole number of superblocks

## Summary

The unit was to mirror `_gemma4_project_experts_gate_up_wmma_t16` onto the expert
down projection. **It cannot be built, for two independent reasons, and both are
structural rather than a missing artifact.**

1. **The down tensor is not Q4_K.** `ffn_down_exps.weight` in
   `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-UD-Q4_K_XL.gguf` is **Q5_1 on 29 layers and Q8_0 on the
   thirtieth**. There is no Q4_K expert down tensor in the artifact, so there is
   nothing to repack into a Q4T16 tile, and the loader's tile branch never fires
   for it.
2. **The down projection's K is not tileable in this format even if it were.**
   The down projection reduces a 704-wide intermediate. A Q4T16 tile is one
   16-column tile per **256-element** K superblock, and no K-family quant can
   store a 704-wide row at all. So a Q4_K down tensor is not representable in
   GGUF at this model's geometry, in this or any artifact.

The loader comment that asserted the opposite is corrected with this unit.

**And the mirror was worth about 95 ms, not the 256 ms the campaign needs.** The
down block is 918 ms, but the DS4 DP4A consumer already runs at 58.6 GB/s against
the gate/up T16 leaf's 65.4 GB/s on the traffic that dominates both — a 1.12x
per-byte gap. The T16 route's headline advantage, skipping the activation
pack, is 22.08 ms of 930.52 ms here (2.4 percent). So even a down route that hit
the gate/up T16 leaf's exact rate would land near 835 ms: about 95 ms, roughly 2.2
percent of the step. That is a real result and it is worth recording, but it is
not the lever the 0.940x target needs.

## Changes

- `hipengine/loading/gemma4_gguf_device.py`: corrected the rank-3 Q4_K tile

## Validation

### The premise, checked three ways

`ffn_down_exps` and `ffn_gate_up_exps` ggml types from the GGUF header
(`hipengine.loading.gguf.scan_gguf`):

| tensor | ggml type | shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `blk.0.ffn_down_exps.weight` | **Q5_1** (29 layers) / **Q8_0** (1 layer) | `(128, 2816, 704)` |
| `blk.0.ffn_gate_up_exps.weight` | Q4_K (29) / Q5_K (1) | `(128, 1408, 2816)` |

The resident weight objects on the **loaded model**, reached through
`LLM.generate_detailed`'s own generator (not a stub):

```
layer  0 experts_gate_up_proj  quant_key=gguf_q4_k  resident=['raw','t16_gate','t16_up'] t16_tiles_ready=True
layer  0 experts_down_proj     quant_key=gguf_q5_1  resident=['raw']                     t16_tiles_ready=False
layer 29 experts_gate_up_proj  quant_key=gguf_q5_k  resident=['raw']                     t16_tiles_ready=False
layer 29 experts_down_proj     quant_key=gguf_q8_0  resident=['raw']                     t16_tiles_ready=False
```

`_gemma4_t16_tiles_ready` is the exact predicate the gate/up route gates on. It is
**False for both down weights**, so a down T16 route would decline every layer of
this artifact before it read a single weight.

The loader plan agrees and the repack refuses:

```
ffn_down_exps.weight:     quant_key=gguf_q5_1 allocation_names=('raw',)
ffn_gate_up_exps.weight:  quant_key=gguf_q4_k allocation_names=('raw','t16_gate','t16_up')

down row 704 elements = 528 bytes; 528 % 144 = 96
  repack_gguf_q4_k_tile16 -> ValueError: bytes_per_row must be a positive multiple of 144
gate/up row 2816 elements = 1584 bytes; 1584 % 144 = 0
  repack_gguf_q4_k_tile16 -> accepted, tiles shape (1, 1, 11, 2368)
```

The geometry reason is independent of the quant type. `QK_K = 256`, and
`quant_shape_to_byte_shape` raises when `shape[-1] % block_size` is nonzero, so a
704-element row cannot be a Q4_K row in GGUF at all — 704 is 2.75 superblocks.
Every K-family tensor in the artifact has a row that is a whole number of 256:
zero exceptions across all 30 of them (29 Q4_K, 1 Q5_K; the artifact has no
Q6_K). A Q4T16 tile at this
out_features would need K in {512, 768, 1024, ...}, never 704.

Two further notes, so the next reader does not have to re-derive them. The
loader's half-split (`derived_half_shape`) cuts a rank-3 tensor's output rows in
two and calls the halves `t16_gate`/`t16_up`; for a down tensor those names are
meaningless and the cut at row 1408 would be arbitrary, though the tiles
themselves would still be geometrically valid. And
`tests/test_unit_gemma4_gguf_device.py` already pins the intended policy for a
non-Q4_K rank-3 stack: "the Q8_0 down stack has no rank-3 tile route and stays
raw", asserting `("raw",)`.

### Where the down block's 918 ms actually goes

HIP event pairs around the route's two launches, same 4096-token prefill:

| leaf | calls | ms | ms/call | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `gemma4_project_experts_down_mmq` (whole block) | 240 | 930.52 | 3.877 | 21.9% |
| `gguf_q8_1_mmq_ds4_f32_pack_bf16_d4x3` (activation pack) | 240 | **22.08** | 0.092 | 0.5% |
| `gguf_q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_bf16_bf16_out` (DP4A consumer) | 232 | **860.50** | 3.709 | 20.2% |
| remainder (8 Q8_0 layer-29 calls + glue) | — | 47.94 | — | 1.1% |

So the pack is **2.4 percent** of the block and the consumer is **92.5 percent**.
The gate/up T16 route's two stated advantages are (a) no BF16 -> DS4 Q8_1 packing
pass and (b) a tile weight operand. Advantage (a) is worth 22 ms here. Advantage
(b) is the whole question, and the two routes' per-byte rates are close: the
gate/up T16 leaf moves about 328 MB per call (293.4 MB of tiles, 23.1 MB of bf16
activations, 11.5 MB of output) in 5.016 ms, **65.4 GB/s**; the down DP4A
consumer moves about 217 MB (190.3 MB of Q5_1 weights, 3.9 MB of packed
activations, 23.1 MB of output) in 3.709 ms, **58.6 GB/s**. That is **1.12x**.
Taken at face value — an inference from the two per-byte rates, not a
measurement of a kernel that does not exist — a down route that hit the T16
leaf's rate would land near 835 ms and save about 95 ms, roughly 2.2 percent of
the 4.25 s step, against the 256 ms the 0.940x target needs. The band is
narrow however the accounting is done, because the weight traffic that dominates
both blocks is already being streamed at a similar rate.

Both are far from the hardware: `docs/reference/ROOFLINE-gfx1151.md` puts the
optimistic read roof at ~221 GB/s, and the down consumer reaches 58.6 GB/s of
it. The down block is not at a bandwidth wall, and it is not at a compute wall
either — 16.2 GFLOP per call in 3.709 ms is 4.4 TFLOP/s against a 59 TOP/s int8
WMMA class. It is latency- and occupancy-bound, which is a different unit from a
layout swap.

**A prior unit already reached the same shape of conclusion from the other
direction.** `worklog/entries/20260928T081634.604650Z-lhl-gemma4-down-mmq-load-width-397816.md`
fixed the same kernel's scalar load pattern (24 scalar memory operations per
(row, sub-block) to 6) and measured the down at 35.3 -> 46.5 GB/s, leaving it
"1.29x behind its sibling per byte rather than 1.54x". Its own `Next` named the
residual as "the Q5_1 five-bit format's extra `dot_h` pass, which is real work
rather than waste, so what is left is the gate_up's LDS staging that the down
does not do". My 1.12x at 4096 against the T16 leaf is consistent with that
trajectory: the gap has been closing by fixing the consumer, and what remains is
inside the consumer — a Q5_1 `dot_h` pass and the missing LDS staging — not in
the weight layout.

## Next

The Q4T16 mirror is settled as impossible; do not re-open it. The measured facts
that should steer whatever replaces it:

1. **The lever is the DP4A consumer's occupancy, not its layout.** 860.50 ms of
   the 930.52 ms block, at 58.6 GB/s against a ~221 GB/s roof and 4.4 TFLOP/s
   against a 59 TOP/s class. The gate is `(out_tiles = 22, experts = 128)` blocks
   of 128 threads — 2816 blocks, 360448 threads, one output column each — with
   `ROWS_PER_PASS = 32` and an average of exactly 32 compact rows per expert at
   this geometry. Widening the per-thread tile (more output columns per thread,
   or a WMMA consumer over the same Q5_1 weights) is the shape that matches the
   gap; a tile *layout* for Q5_1 would only help if the consumer were rewritten
   with it, and Q5_1 is a 32-element block quant with no T16 layout today. This
   is also where the previous unit on this kernel left off: the named residual
   was the Q5_1 `dot_h` pass and the gate_up's LDS staging, both inside the
   consumer.
2. **Do not expect 256 ms from this block.** 90 ms is what matching the existing
   T16 leaf's rate buys. The remaining prefill terms in the same census are
   `gate_up` 1276 ms and dense `launch_gguf_linear` 978 ms; the gate/up T16 leaf
   is already the fastest per-byte route in the tree at 65.4 GB/s, so the same
   ~1.12x ceiling applies to anything built in its image.
3. **If a Q5_1 (or Q8_0) WMMA/iu8 consumer is built**, the oracle already exists
   and is the right one: `tests/test_unit_gemma4_expert_route.py::test_expert_down_mmq_route_holds_at_gemma4_s_own_down_geometry`
   holds the down route at 704 -> 2816 for both quants against an independent
   dequantized reference, and
   `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_expert_route_true_geometry.py` holds the same shape
   under a real-router activation profile. 704 is deliberately in the coverage
   because it is 5 x 128 + 64 and the trailing DS4 block is partial.
