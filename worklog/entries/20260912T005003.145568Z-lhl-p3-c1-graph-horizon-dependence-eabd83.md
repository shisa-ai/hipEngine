# C1 INT8 decode graph: repeatable win above the pm4 threshold, repeatable regression below it

## Summary

The single-pair result in
`worklog/entries/20260911T233045.766311Z-lhl-ikv-c2-c1-decode-graph-6dce6a.md`
left `HIPENGINE_GGUF_INT8_KV_DECODE_GRAPH` default-off because -0.53% could not be
separated from noise. Nine further pairs settle it, and the answer is
horizon-dependent rather than noisy.

**The win is real above the transport threshold.** Five pairs at 128 decode
tokens (`pm4` transport):

| pair | eager tok/s | graph tok/s | delta | ids |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fwd rep1 | 29.563 | 29.721 | -0.53% | 10/10 |
| fwd rep2 | 29.577 | 29.722 | -0.49% | 10/10 |
| fwd rep3 | 29.427 | 29.572 | -0.49% | 10/10 |
| rev1 (graph first) | 29.426 | 29.833 | -1.36% | 10/10 |
| rev2 (graph first) | 29.355 | 29.588 | -0.79% | 10/10 |

Mean -0.73%, range -1.36% to -0.49%. The three forward pairs vary by only
**0.05 pp**, so this is not run-to-run noise. 50/50 prompt-runs were
byte-identical (per-prompt sha256 over the full 129-token sequence).

**The reversed-order control matters.** In the forward pairs eager always ran
first, which could have handed the graph a drift advantage. Running the graph
first makes the win *larger*, not smaller: graph-first mean -1.08% versus
graph-second mean -0.50%. So going second is the less favourable position and
the graph wins in both orderings. The win is not an order artifact, and the
forward pairs are if anything conservative.

**The win inverts below the transport threshold.** Repeatable, byte-identical
10/10 both times:

| horizon | transport | delta | capture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128 tokens | pm4 | -0.53% / -0.49% / -0.49% | ~82 ms per request |
| 64 tokens | hipgraph | **+0.81% / +0.68%** | ~18 ms per request |
| 32 tokens | hipgraph | **+1.36% / +1.40%** | ~18 ms per request |

## Mechanism

The sign is set by transport economics, not by replay overhead:

- Under `pm4` (>=128 replay steps) the replay itself is ~2.5% faster and capture
  costs ~2.0% of the decode, netting the small win.
- Under `hipgraph` (<128 steps) the replay is roughly neutral (-0.06% at 64
  tokens, -0.29% at 32) while capture still costs 0.9-1.7%, netting the
  regression.

So the graph helps exactly where `GGUF_DECODE_GRAPH_SUBMISSION_POLICIES` already
selects `pm4` for this geometry at one row. That is a satisfying consistency:
the 128 in the transport policy is also the empirical break-even.

## Decision

**Keep the flag default-off.** The shared eligibility floor is 24, and the decode
horizon is not known at admission time, so a default flip would enable the graph
for every 24-127-step generation, where it is a repeatable regression
(+0.68% to +1.40%). That is a concrete, quantified blocker rather than a vague
"needs more evidence".

Concrete promotion path recorded in `docs/REFACTOR.md`: add an INT8-specific
capture-aware floor near 128 steps, or admit the graph only when the resolved
submission transport is `pm4`; then re-measure at 128 steps and promote with
`=0` as rollback. If no floor mechanism is wanted, delete the flag and the INT8
admission with it.

## Why the earlier floor claim did not apply

`worklog/entries/20260912T000826.517239Z-lhl-p3-c1-graph-floor-correction-5a1217.md`
corrects the claim that the scalar floor (24) disagreed with a packed floor
(128). On gfx1100 there is no packed floor policy, so both resolve to 24. The
128 is the *transport* threshold. That correction removed a wrong explanation;
this entry supplies the right one, and the numbers here show the transport
threshold is the operationally meaningful boundary.

## Honest limits

- 128, 64, and 32 tokens are three horizons, not a curve; the break-even is
  bracketed between 64 and 128 tokens but not located. The mechanism suggests it
  sits at the 128-step transport boundary, and the transport switch is a step
  function, so a smooth break-even may not exist.
- The capture costs quoted are per-request means over 10 prompts and include the
  first, cold capture (181.5 ms at 128 tokens), so they overstate the steady-state
  per-request cost slightly.
- Only one geometry and quant were measured (dense H5120 `Q4_K_M`, INT8
  `int8_per_token_head`). The gfx1151 packed floor policy differs (rows 2 -> 23)
  and was not measured.
- The reversed-order control used two pairs versus three forward pairs, so the
  -1.08% versus -0.50% split is suggestive rather than precise; it is sufficient
  to exclude an order artifact, which is all it was run for.
