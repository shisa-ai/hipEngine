# The IQ4_XS local32 codebook gather is not bank-conflicted

## Summary

Follow-up to `ud-iq4xs-local32-mlp-rejected`, which closed the wave count and
the memory-level-parallelism hypothesis and named the shared-memory codebook
lookup as the next candidate. Two more candidates were tested on the same
owner and both were rejected:

1. **Widening the payload load.** `p[0]` and `p[1]` were merged into one
   64-bit load. This is exact (same bytes, same decode, all
   `tests/test_gguf_iq_local32_rows.py` and `test_gguf_iq_local32_decode.py`
   nodes pass) but shape-inconsistent: on two interleaved runs at rows=3 it
   helped five shapes by 1.7-6.5% and *hurt* `ffn_gate` (17408, 5120), the
   largest single IQ4_XS entry at 61 calls/step, by +0.5% and +2.5%. The
   time-weighted net is about -0.08 ms/step, below the resolution of the
   verifier census. Reverted.
2. **Making the codebook gather bank-conflict-free.** The incumbent table is
   indexed `s_cb[code_byte + 256 * nib]`. Because 256 is a multiple of the
   32-dword LDS bank stride, a lane's bank is `code_byte & 31` in either
   nibble half, so two lanes collide whenever their codes agree modulo 32.
   Replacing it with a lane-transposed `s_cb[32 * v + lane]` puts lane L on
   bank L for every `v`. That is conflict-free by construction and exact, and
   it is **3.35% slower at rows=3** (interleaved min-of-9 x 20 reps over six
   real shapes: ffn_down +4.8%, ffn_gate +6.0%, attn_q +5.5%, attn_qkv -1.8%,
   attn_gate +1.4%, ssm_out -0.2%). Reverted.

The second result also corrects the reading of the first diagnostic in the
previous entry. Replacing the gather with a constant indexed only by
`(lane, j)` - which lets the compiler hoist all eight reads out of the column
loop - is 1.37x-1.60x faster. Replacing it with a data-dependent but
LDS-free `float(code_byte & 255)` is 1.24x-1.30x faster. Since a genuinely
conflict-free gather of the same instruction count buys nothing, the gap is
the **LDS instruction and its latency in the `load -> BFE -> ds_read -> mul ->
FMA` chain**, not bank serialization. One dword gather per weight element is
irreducible for a byte-indexed codebook, so this owner has no further headroom
from the lookup itself.

## Next

Do not retry the wave count, the load batching, the load widening, or the
codebook-gather layout. The remaining lever on this owner is the decode
algorithm, not the lookup: one dword gather per weight element is what the
byte-indexed IQ4_XS codebook costs, and at rows=3 the owner sits at 460-530
GB/s (48-55% of the 960 GB/s roofline). The two ways out are a wider-arithmetic
owner that does not decode per element at all (the IQ MMQ / WMMA cooperative
route, which for Q3_K was rejected on the mean-KL gate and has not been
measured for IQ4_XS), or accepting the rate and taking the remaining time from
a different family.
