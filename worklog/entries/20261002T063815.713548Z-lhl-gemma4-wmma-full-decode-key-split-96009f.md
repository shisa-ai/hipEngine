# the full-layer decode attention was waiting on itself, and dividing the walk is worth 1.51x

## Summary

The full-layer decode now divides its key walk and recombines the per-slice
softmax. Measured on one layer with identical buffers, the walk goes **58.44 to
19.04 ms at 262,144 keys (3.07x)**, 7.39 to 2.46 at 32,768 (3.01x) and 1.84 to
0.73 at 8,192 (2.52x); end to end at a 32,560-token context the decode goes
**13.25 to 20.00 tok/s (1.51x)**. The teacher-forced gate passes at `kl_max`
3.11e-4 against a 0.05 limit with 100% top-1.

The diagnosis is that the launch was short of parallelism, not of memory. A
one-token block presents `query_tiles * num_kv_heads * gqa_tiles` = 8 blocks and
each of them walks every key, so at 262,144 keys the kernel sits at 75 GB/s
where 64 blocks reach 225. The split gives the walk something to overlap; it
does not change what the walk reads.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_attention_prefill_wmma_full.hip`:
  the full-layer kernel takes an optional workspace and a slice count, divides
  its key walk along `grid.z` when the query block is a single tile, and stages
  per-slice softmax state; a new `gemma4_attention_wmma_combine_kernel` merges
  the slices with a log-sum-exp. `slices == 1` is the unsplit walk: no
  workspace, no combine launch, epilogue writes the output directly.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_attention_prefill_wmma_full.py`:
  `plan_gemma4_attention_wmma_full_slices` and
  `plan_gemma4_attention_wmma_full_scratch_bytes` are pure functions of the
  geometry, so the rule is testable without a device and the launcher only lays
  out the workspace and sizes the grid. The launcher runs the walk whole when it
  is given no workspace; the layer always has one, so a production decode always
  splits.
- `tests/test_unit_gemma4_attention_wmma_slices.py`: 12 planner cases.
- `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_attention_prefill_wmma_full.py`: 3 split tests,
  including one that runs the kernel twice on identical buffers and compares the
  split against the whole walk.
- `scripts/gemma4_teacher_forced_gate.py`: `--wmma-full-slices` pins the
  WMMA full-layer walk's key division, independently of `--slices`, and
  `observed_wmma_full_split` records that the candidate arm actually divided.

## Validation

- Kernel A/B, one layer, 7 alternating repetitions, median, identical buffers
  and arguments in both arms: 262144 keys 58.44 -> 19.04 ms (3.07x); 32768
  7.39 -> 2.46 (3.01x); 8192 1.84 -> 0.73 (2.52x). NaN placement equal,
  7638/8192 bf16 outputs bit-identical, worst element 0.007812 against a 3.7
  peak — under one bf16 ulp. Not bit-identical, and not claimed to be.
- End-to-end A/B through `LLM.generate_detailed`, arms alternated, decode
  isolated by differencing two `max_tokens` because the prefill is O(context^2)
  and hides the decode in the wall: 75.5 against 50.0 ms/token at 32,560 tokens,
  **1.510x**, generated ids equal. The 25.5 ms saved matches 5 layers x 4.93 ms
  from the kernel A/B, so the decomposition closes.
- Teacher-forced gate, 527 rows over keys 15857-16383: kl_mean 1.71e-6,
  kl_p95 7.79e-7, kl_p99 2.44e-5, kl_max 3.11e-4 against 0.05, top-1 100%, zero
  flips. `evidence_level: numerical_rows`, `promotion_qualified: false` for the
  category/isolation/task gates this evaluator does not supply.
- rocprofv3 kernel trace names both kernels, one combine per full-kernel launch.
- 12 planner unit cases, 3 new split tests and all 24 pre-existing parity tests
  pass; 59 tests across the two GPU files.

## Next

- The split gives the walk something to overlap but does not change what it
  reads. A one-token block still presents one `(query_tile, kv_head, gqa_tile)`
  plane per slice, so each of the 8 planes reads its own copy of a KV band that
  the 8 GQA heads sharing it could read once: 537 MB per layer at 262,144 keys
  where the GQA shape would move 134 MB. That is the next lever, and it is the
  change that would make a 256K end-to-end decode rate worth measuring.
- Two measurements are deliberately absent and named as such in the artifact:
  no 256K end-to-end decode rate (a 256K prefill is 998 s per arm, so the
  kernel A/B carries 262,144 keys and the end-to-end A/B carries 32,768), and
  the trace durations, which rocprofv3 perturbs by three orders of magnitude and
  which are not reported.

## Open question

- A 24-token run of the same end-to-end A/B read **0.971x**, the opposite
  direction. It is under-resolved rather than contradictory: 24 decode tokens is
  1.6 s against a 40 s prefill whose arms differed by 0.26 s, so that estimate
  carried about 11 ms per token of noise against a 25 ms effect. The 192-token
  run was made to settle it and did, in the other direction and by a margin far
  larger than the noise. Recorded because it was run; the decision rests on the
  192-token pair and the kernel A/B.
