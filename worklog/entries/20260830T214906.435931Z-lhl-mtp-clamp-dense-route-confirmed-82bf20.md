# the four-request clamp is reachable from the dense path via registry key dense_nextn

## Summary

This strengthens the previous entry and retracts a scare I had on the way to it.
I nearly discarded the four-request MTP clamp as irrelevant because
Qwen35GGUFMTP2Adapter is constructed in only one source file, and that file is the
MoE module. That reading was wrong: the model-plugin registry registers the same
class for dense models, so the clamp is on the dense path that the C1-C8 matrix
actually measures.

## Changes

- None. Read-only tracing. Supersedes the uncertainty note in the previous entry
  rather than contradicting its measurements.

## Validation

- hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf_mtp2_registry.py:63 registers
  Qwen35GGUFMTP2Adapter under the key "dense_nextn". That is the dense adapter key,
  not an MoE one, and the registry is what model plugins resolve through.
- The only direct construction site, qwen35_gguf_moe_mtp2.py:1478, is therefore an
  additional user of the same class rather than its sole route. My grep for direct
  constructions missed the registry indirection, which is how a wrong conclusion got
  this close to being recorded.
- The benchmark model is dense: benchmarks/README.md describes the matrix subject as
  Qwen3.8-27B Dense GGUF Q4_K_M. So the clamped adapter is the plausible provider for
  the explicit-K3 row, and the clamp sites listed in the previous entry apply:
  max_requests at line 1029, admitted widths at 1063, and group growth blocked at
  1392 and 1405.

Still not proven, and deliberately not claimed: that the retained C1-C8 explicit-K3
packet resolved the key "dense_nextn" specifically, and what owner.capacity was at
C5-C8. Those two facts close the argument or kill it.
