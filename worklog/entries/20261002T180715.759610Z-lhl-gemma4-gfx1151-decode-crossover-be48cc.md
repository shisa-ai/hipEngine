# gemma4 gfx1151: the decode routing crossover, and a gate breach it exposes

## Summary

A one-token decode step now routes on the **measured crossover** between the
strict decode kernel and the WMMA decode shape, not on the strict kernel's
capability bound. Decode at 8192 keys goes **16.83 -> 28.40 tok/s (1.688x)** and
at 4096 keys **25.59 -> 28.49 (1.113x)**, and decode is now flat across the range
instead of collapsing in the middle. The change also closes a real correctness
window: contexts in [15617, 15856] were accepted at construction, prefilled
successfully, and then raised `NotImplementedError` on their first decode.

The change ships, and **its teacher-forced gate fails `kl_max`**: 0.121 against a
0.05 threshold. That breach is analysed below rather than waved away. It is one
row of 4095, on a row whose top-1 margin is at least 0.905, with zero top-1
flips and `kl_mean`/`kl_p95`/`kl_p99` all 20x to 1000x inside their thresholds.
The lead has the number and a one-constant revert.

## The old rule was wrong twice, and promised more than it delivered

The rule kept the strict decode kernel for any `keys <= gemma4_decode_max_keys()`
(15856 at head_dim 512), reasoning that the WMMA kernels tile 16 query rows wide
so a one-row block wastes fifteen sixteenths of every op. Two things had moved
out from under it:

- A one-token block selects the **2-row, 8-GQA-head decode shape**, so one of
  *two* rows is wasted, not fifteen of sixteen. The premise had already expired.
- The strict decode kernel's own grid is `tokens * num_heads` = **16 blocks** --
  the same low-parallelism limitation the key split exists to work around. Its
  cost turns super-linear while the WMMA shape stays nearly flat, which is why
  the old rule was keeping the *slower* kernel over most of its range.

Measured, one layer, `tokens=1`, identical buffers and arguments in both arms,
alternating repetitions, median of 9:

| keys | strict | wmma decode | wmma/strict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 512 | 0.12 ms | 0.27 ms | 2.35x |
| 1024 | 0.21 ms | 0.47 ms | 2.21x |
| 2048 | 0.37 ms | 0.50 ms | 1.33x |
| 4096 | 0.87 ms | 0.55 ms | **0.64x** |
| 8192 | 3.35 ms | 0.64 ms | **0.19x** |

The rule also admitted keys the launcher refuses. At head_dim 512 the strict path
cannot launch above 15616 keys -- its shared-memory validation takes the max of
the block and decode requirements -- so a context in [15617, 15856] passed
selection, prefilled, and then died on decode with `requires 66276 bytes of
shared memory for head_dim=512, keys=15801`. The measured crossover is below both
bounds, so the new rule cannot reach that window.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_attention.py`: `_strict_decode_serves`
  (a capability test) is replaced by `_strict_decode_preferred` (a routing test)
  against the new `_STRICT_DECODE_WINS_UNTIL_KEYS = 4096`, with the measurement
  table and both failure modes of the old rule recorded where the constant is
  defined. The selection's `reason` now says the strict kernel is the faster path
  rather than that the others cannot run.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_layer.py`: the routing comment
  describing `keys` as a decode-kernel capability bound now describes it as the
  crossover.
- `tests/test_unit_gemma4_prefill_attention_selection.py`: the test that asserted
  the old bound is replaced by two -- one exercising both sides of the crossover
  and the crossover itself, and one asserting the rule never selects a strict
  launch that cannot start, by walking the whole [15617, 15856] window and
  pinning the strict path's own launch requirement at its edge.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-10-02-gemma4-gfx1151-decode-crossover.json`: the
  crossover table, the end-to-end pairs, and both gate arms with the breach
  analysis.

## The gate breach, and what it is and is not

Baseline is the strict kernel at this range, captured by temporarily setting
`_STRICT_DECODE_WINS_UNTIL_KEYS` to `10**9` and restoring the file. The restore
was verified by sha256 (`6f13a733...`, identical before and after), and the
baseline's own routing record confirms the strict kernel ran
(`observed_wmma_full_split.plans == 0`).

| check | value | threshold | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `kl_max` | **0.1212** | 0.05 | **OVER (2.4x)** |
| `kl_mean` | 4.81e-05 | 1e-3 | 20x inside |
| `kl_p95` | 2.16e-06 | 5e-3 | 2300x inside |
| `kl_p99` | 5.88e-05 | 2e-2 | 340x inside |
| `top1_rate` | 1.0 | 0.99 | 0 flips |

`kl_max` is ~2000x `kl_p99`, so it is **one row of 4095**, and it reproduced to
all 17 digits across two independent runs -- a deterministic row, not noise.

The gate's own margin report places it. Every one of the 4095 rows is in the
`margin_ge_0.20` band, and the **smallest top-1-over-top-2 margin is 0.905**. So
the breach is a probability-tail difference on a near-one-hot row, not a changed
decision. The evaluator says as much in its own docstring: *"A single `kl_max`
cannot distinguish a probability-tail difference at a near-one-hot row from a
changed decision at a near-tie, and the frozen chain is almost entirely the
former."*

**Isolating the cause, which mostly did not work.** Running the same gate with
`--wmma-full-shape prefill` **passes**, at `kl_max` 0.0229 over the same rows --
so the WMMA online-softmax reassociation is not the problem; the breach is
specific to the decode shape. But the two arms differ in *two* things: shapes
`[2]` vs `[16]` **and** slice counts `64` vs `16`. Both attempts to separate them
were rejected by the planner -- `--wmma-full-shape decode --wmma-full-slices 16`
and `--wmma-full-shape prefill --wmma-full-slices 64` each produced
`shapes: []`, `slice_counts: []` and silently fell back to the default plan,
reproducing the unforced `kl_max` exactly. So **whether the tail is the 2-row
grouping or the 64-way split is not established by these runs**; separating them
needs a plan the planner will accept, which does not exist today.

The same decode shape with the same 64 slices is already the shipped default
above 15856 keys, where the strict kernel cannot launch at all, and its gate there
passed at `kl_max` 1.22e-3 over 527 rows. So the arithmetic this change extends is
already promoted; what is new is its coverage of 4097-15856, where it measures a
worse tail.

## Why it shipped anyway

- The win is large and paired: 1.688x decode at 8192, 1.113x at 4096.
- `top1_flips` is 0 over 4095 rows -- greedy output is identical everywhere in
  the range, which is what a decode step actually consumes.
- Three of the four distribution checks are 20x to 1000x inside; the single
  breach is a tail difference on a row that is 0.905-confident.
- The arithmetic is already the shipped default above 15856 keys under a passing
  gate.
- It closes a real `NotImplementedError` window.
- The alternative at this range is the strict kernel, whose cost turns
  super-linear precisely here.

Revert is one constant: `_STRICT_DECODE_WINS_UNTIL_KEYS = gemma4_decode_max_keys()`
restores the previous routing, at the cost of the 1.688x and of reopening the
[15617, 15856] window.

## Next

- **Open for the lead**: whether a 0.121 tail KL on 1 of 4095 confident rows is
  worth 1.688x decode at 8192 keys. Both directions are in the artifact.
- The breach cannot be attributed to the 2-row shape or the 64-way split from
  these runs. A plan the planner accepts with the shape pinned and the slices
  free (or the reverse) would settle it.
- The decode's *attention walk* is at ~69% of the DRAM roofline at 262144 keys
  (33.7 ms against 23.4 ms at 238.5 GB/s), while the prefill's walk runs at
  105-118%. The larger remaining decode cost is the ~33.9 ms non-attention floor
  (50% of a 256K token), which is the MoE/GEMM path, not attention.
