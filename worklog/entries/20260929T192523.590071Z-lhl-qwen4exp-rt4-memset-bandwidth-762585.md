# RT-4's cheaper alternative is ruled out: 220 GB/s is the hardware memset rate

## Summary

RT-4's attribution left two possible fixes for the capacity-sensitive KV reset:

1. make the **memset** faster - no contract change, no correctness burden; or
2. clear **less** - RT-4's proposed contract change, with its poison-testing,
   shorter-after-longer, sparse-tail, rollback, cancellation and c2 burden.

**Route 1 is closed by measurement.**

## Result

`hipMemset` over a capacity ladder, 7 reps after 2 warmups, device-synchronized:

| size | median | effective |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 MiB | 0.011 ms | 96.2 GB/s |
| 64 MiB | 0.307 ms | 218.3 GB/s |
| 768 MiB | 3.658 ms | 220.1 GB/s |
| **6 GiB** | **29.456 ms** | **218.7 GB/s** |
| 12 GiB | 58.531 ms | 220.1 GB/s |

**Flat at ~220 GB/s across a 12,000x size range.** The only deviation is at 1 MiB, where
launch overhead dominates and the rate is *lower*. The 6 GiB point - the same volume RT-4
clears at 128K - reproduces RT-4's recorded **220.77 GB/s to within 0.3%**; the best measured
rate is **0.997x** it.

## What this settles

220 GB/s is this part's achievable `hipMemset` rate, **not a property of how the reset calls
it**, so there is no memset-speed headroom. **Clearing less is the only lever**, meaning RT-4's
correctness burden is not avoidable by a cheaper alternative and the row's remaining work is
what it already says it is.

It also makes **29.182 ms at 128K a floor for the full-clear design**, not an artifact of a
suboptimal call. A future claim that the reset "should" be faster needs a different mechanism
than a faster memset.

## Validation

- The comparison is against RT-4's **published** figures read from its committed artifact, not
  a re-derivation.
- The size ladder is the control: a rate that varied with size would indicate a
  call-path problem rather than a hardware limit, and it does not vary above 64 MiB.
- `hipMemset`'s return code is checked on every sample; the device is synchronized on both
  sides of every timing.
- **Limits recorded**: one part; `hipMemset` only, so a custom kernel, a different memset
  implementation or a DMA path was **not** tested, and the claim is precisely *"the `hipMemset`
  the runner uses is at this part's achievable rate"* rather than *"no memset-like operation
  can exceed 220 GB/s"*; the reset path was not timed end to end, so RT-4's boundary stands;
  allocated buffers, no model loaded.
- **No runtime change, no promotion, and no claim about the contract change's value.**

## Next

RT-4 stands as attributed with a bounded prize and a named correctness burden. Implementing
the contract change is the only remaining route and it is an implementation-plus-gate task,
not a measurement.
