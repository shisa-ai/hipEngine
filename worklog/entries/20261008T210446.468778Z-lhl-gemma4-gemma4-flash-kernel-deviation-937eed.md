# Calibrate the decode route's footprint against the shipped quantization

## Summary

The open question is whether the decode route's numerical footprint matters.
Two committed measurements answer it with numbers rather than intuition, and
neither was in the record when the route was reverted.

**The route is an order of magnitude below the quantization the product already
carries.** `benchmarks/results/2026-10-02-gemma4-d12-int8-kv-teacher-forced.json`
measures int8 KV against bf16 KV on real prompt categories: kl_mean **0.1426**,
kl_p99 2.43, **top1_rate 0.875**. The flash route measures kl_mean **5.4e-3**,
kl_p99 0.19, **top1_rate 0.993**. So the route's footprint is ~27x smaller in
mean KL and 12.5 points better in top-1 agreement than an int8 KV choice.
Caveat, and it is a real one: the two numbers come from different corpora and
different baselines -- int8 KV against a bf16 teacher on real prompts, the route
against the exact chain on a synthetic frozen corpus -- so this is a scale
reference, not a controlled comparison. The ratio is large enough that the
qualitative reading survives the mismatch.

**The bars fail both of them.** int8 KV (0.1426 mean, 87.5% top-1) fails
`kl_mean`/`kl_p99`/`kl_max`/`top1_rate`; the flash route fails the same three KL
bars and passes top-1. A gate that fails a 1.4e-1 quantization-grade change and
a 5.4e-3 reordering-grade change alike is not discriminating between them at
this band on this model state.

**Bar-by-bar, as written** (thresholds mean 1e-3, p95 5e-3, p99 2e-2, max 5e-2,
top-1 99%):

| arm | kl_mean | kl_p95 | kl_p99 | kl_max | top-1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shipped default routes, HEAD | FAIL 5.4e-3 | pass 2.3e-3 | FAIL 0.19 | FAIL 0.64 | pass 99.32% |
| flash on the 256-dim layers, HEAD | FAIL 1.2e-2 | pass 4.0e-3 | FAIL 0.17 | FAIL 5.9 | pass 99.71% |
| the same shipped routes, 2026-09-30 tree | pass 7.8e-6 | pass 3.5e-6 | pass 3.0e-5 | pass 6.7e-3 | pass 100% |

The fourth name in each `failed` list, `no_wmma_full_launches_observed`, is a
route-coverage note rather than a numeric bar: it fires when the WMMA-full split
geometry never reached the kernel, so it says the run cannot speak to that
split, not that anything exceeded a limit.

**Precedent for the tail bar being binding.** `2026-09-24-gemma4-26b-a4b-attention-split-rejected.json`
records a reassociating attention candidate at kl_mean **8.6e-4** (inside the
mean bar) and top1_rate 99.9% that was **rejected on kl_max alone** (0.3036
against 0.05), with the reason "bit-exactness unattainable under re-grouped
reduction with bf16 KV feedback". That is a candidate numerically *better* than
the flash route rejected on the same tail bar, deliberately, at a time when the
incumbent passed. The tail bar has been the intended constraint; what changed is
that the incumbent no longer passes it either.

**Timeline refinement, independent of the bit-identity result.** On 2026-10-03,
`2026-10-03-gemma4-attention-production-quality-failed.json` measured the
production attention path at kl_mean 3.2e-3 against a bf16 teacher -- the same
order as today's readings, five days before this unit's tuning commits. That is
a different instrument (production against a teacher, not a route against the
exact chain), so it cannot be chained to the 09-30 datum rigorously; it does
corroborate that the elevated readings are not attributable to the 10-08
tuning work.
