# Census rule fixed, and my own DP-6 "defect" is retracted as a parsing artefact

## Summary

Iteration 156 re-ran the ledger's open-candidate census and reported two defects in §11: the
end-of-cell rule misclassifies DE-1, and DP-6 was "unclassifiable — its entire status cell is 29
characters with no state token". This iteration fixed both. **One was real. The second was mine.**

## Changes

1. **§11's rule corrected** — *a row is closed if a terminal marker appears anywhere in its status
   cell, with the end-of-cell read used only to resolve contradictions between a stale prefix and
   a later conclusion.* The old rule read the end of the cell deliberately, to avoid stale opening
   prefixes, and justified it by citing DE-1 — but **DE-1 is the row that defeats it**: its cell
   *opens* with `CLOSED — BOTH RUNTIME ROUTES AND BOTH FLAGS DELETED` while its tail is a
   limits-and-evidence statement carrying no state token. A tail-reading classifier therefore calls
   a row whose first word is `CLOSED` "actionable". **This defect is real and verified by reading
   the cell head directly.**

2. **DP-5's and DP-6's missing trailing pipes added** — a cosmetic repair, not a defect.

3. **The three-speed-candidate finding strengthened with DP-6's real figure.** Iteration 156
   reported the three actionable speed candidates as "low-value, demoted, or undocumented". With
   DP-6 read correctly, all three are now **priced**: DP-2 at 7.478 ms, QA-4 at a measured 3.02%
   ceiling at 64K, **DP-6 at a 1.090% scope (85.791 ms ceiling)**. The conclusion holds and is
   cleaner: every remaining speed candidate on the punchlist has a measured sub-2% ceiling.

## The retraction

Iteration 156 stated: *"DP-6's entire status cell is 29 characters — `A (for layers already on
F16)` — with no state token at all; unclassifiable by any rule."*

**That is wrong.** DP-6's row has **six content cells, the same as its table header**, and aligns
correctly:

```
| DP-6 | Mechanism | Opportunity | First bounded experiment | A (for layers already on F16) | PARTIALLY PRICED (20260929T)... |
```

The `A (...)` cell is **Track**; the **State** is `PARTIALLY PRICED (20260929T)`. `DP-5` and
`DP-6` were the only two rows in §7A **missing their trailing pipe**, and the classifier indexed
`cells[-2]` — which, under a missing trailing pipe, reads the *Track* cell as the *State* cell.
The defect was in my parser, not in the ledger.

**This is the fifth time this campaign that a conclusion was drawn before the source was read.**
The pattern in all five is the same: a plausible structural inference (a register budget that adds
up, a skew that should shrink, a status cell that looks short) taken as evidence about the artefact,
when reading the artefact directly would have settled it in one step. The rule this adds is narrow
and checkable: **before reporting a structure as defective, compare it against its own neighbours
or header.** Here that comparison — `DP-5` and `DP-6` at 7 split parts against `DP-1`/`DP-7`/`DP-8`
at 8 — was available in the same output that produced the wrong claim.

## Validation

- The DE-1 defect was verified by reading the status cell head, not inferred: it begins
  `CLOSED — BOTH RUNTIME ROUTES AND BOTH FLAGS DELETED`.
- The DP-6 retraction was verified by printing the row's cells against the table header's cell
  labels and confirming `Track` and `State` land on the right content, then re-checking after the
  trailing pipe was added.
- Both repairs are text-only. The census counts from iteration 156 are **unchanged** — DP-6 was
  already counted as actionable, and it still is; only the reason recorded for its low value
  changed.

**Limits.** Ledger text only. No engine code, no GPU run. The corrected rule is stated but has not
been re-applied to all 72 rows, which is the follow-up below.
