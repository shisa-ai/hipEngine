# correction: the down projection is at its designed optimum, and the 171 passes were an arithmetic slip

**This entry corrects the headline of
`20260928T141000.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-down-projection-fixed-cost-8e2d4a.md`.**

## Summary

That entry says the down projection "moves the same 33 GB whether it has 8 rows per
expert or 32" and calls that 171 passes over the weight set. **The first is a real
measurement and stands. The 171 is wrong**, and reading the kernel's own source shows
the behaviour is the design working as documented rather than a defect.

### The arithmetic slip

33,254 MB was the **total across 30 dispatches** -- 25 timed iterations plus 5 warmup.
Per dispatch it is **1,108 MB**, against a 190.3 MB weight set: **5.8x, not 171x.**

The same slip ran the other way for the harness's model. `weight_bytes_x_passes` is
24.36 GB **per call**, so against the measured 1.108 GB per call it is **22x above**,
not the "1.37x low" the entry claimed. I compared a per-call model against a 30-call
total.

### What the source says

`ROWS_PER_PASS` is 32, and the constant carries its own rationale:

> The weight side of the dot product is unpacked once per pass, so this is the
> weight-reuse factor: a pass reads every weight row it covers once, and a row pass
> costs the same whether it is one row or `ROWS_PER_PASS`. An expert averaging 32 rows
> therefore reads its weights twice at 16 and once at 32.

**That is exactly the measurement.** At 32 rows per expert there is one pass and the
weight stream is read once. At 8 rows per expert there is still one pass -- the pass
holds up to 32 rows and gets 8 -- so the weight stream is *also* read once, for a
quarter of the arithmetic. The fetch is therefore equal at both shapes **because the
kernel is reading its weights once in both**, and the production shape is the good
case, not the bad one.

The measured per-call fetch of 1,108 MB against a 190.3 MB weight set is not
explained by re-reading. `FETCH_SIZE` counts L2-to-CU traffic, and a line read by
several CUs is counted for each, so a broadcast weight row can account for a multiple
without any DRAM or L2 re-read. Nothing in this measurement establishes a re-read and
the entry's reading of it as one was unsupported.

## What stands

- **The per-call fetch is equal at 8 and 32 rows per expert**: 1,083 MB against
  1,108 MB, 2 percent apart, while the arithmetic differs by 4x. Measured, and the
  source explains it.
- **The time is 1.82x for 4x the work** (1.604 against 2.925 ms), so a fixed component
  exists. The fit gives `a = 1.164 ms` and `b = 0.0550 ms` per row-per-expert.
- **The fixed component is the weight stream, and it is the design's reuse factor.**
  190.3 MB over 1.164 ms is **163 GB/s**, 64 percent of the 256 GB/s peak. That is a
  plausible DRAM read of the weight set once, and it is the price of `ROWS_PER_PASS`
  being what it is.
- The rows=64 case doubles `row_passes` because 64 rows need two passes, which is why
  its time (5.267 ms) overshoots the two-point fit's 4.68 ms. Consistent with the
  documented model.

## The revised ceiling

The down projection costs 30 layers x 2.925 ms = **87.8 ms** of the 685.8 ms prefill,
12.8 percent, of which 30 x 1.164 = **34.9 ms is the weight stream**. Perfecting that
stream to DRAM peak would save about **12 ms, 1.7 percent of the prefill.** Even
making the whole kernel free is 12.8 percent, against a 1.36x gap that needs 26
percent. **The down projection cannot close the prefill gap and should not be the
next unit.**

## Next

- **The prefill gap is 26 percent and the down projection is 12.8 percent of the
  step, so the next unit has to be a term that is large or a term that is shared.**
  The gate/up kernel and the dense Q8_0 WMMA path are the other two large terms.
- **When a counter is collected over N iterations, divide by N before comparing it to
  a single-call quantity.** This is the sixth measurement error on the branch and the
  second in two iterations; the first was caught by arithmetic before a commit, this
  one by reading the source afterwards. Both were unit mismatches rather than wrong
  measurements, which is what a counter-collection CSV invites.
