# RT-1 full prefill suite: 12/12 faster at chunk 4096, and the promotion decision

## Summary

The full prefill suite decides RT-1, and it decides it clearly: **12 of 12 cases
are faster at chunk 4096, every one completely separated, `token_id` identical
12/12 - and `logits_sha256` identical in ZERO of 12.**

The promotion decision is **not a blanket default flip**, and the blocker is
concrete rather than "needs more evidence".

## Result

All 12 fixture cases (4 categories x 3 prompt sizes), three repetitions per arm,
both chunk sizes, 24 invocations all exit 0.

By size, across the four categories:

| size | speedups | peak delta |
|---|---|---:|
| p512 | -28.2, -28.9, -28.7, -29.7% | +0.77 GB |
| p1024 | -35.0, -33.4, -33.5, -34.2% | +1.24 GB |
| p4096 | -37.6, -36.5, -35.4, -37.2% | +6.20 GB |

**Monotone in prompt size, flat across category** - within-size spread under 1.6
percentage points, no category an outlier. "Separated" means the 4096 arm's
*slowest* repetition beat the 256 arm's *fastest*; all 12 pass.

Peak delta tracks `min(chunk, max_sequence_length)` exactly as the workspace model
predicts, and is identical across categories at each size.

## Numerics

`token_id` identical 12/12; `logits_sha256` differs 12/12. So this is an
**arithmetic** change in every case, not a scheduling one, and it cannot be
certified by bit-identity.

It is certified by the canonical numerical gate at chunk 4096, run over this same
12-case fixture: `canonical_numerical_qualification_claim: true`, `kl_mean
0.00038`, `kl_p95 0.00176`, `kl_p99 0.00542`, `kl_max 0.0153` against 0.001 /
0.005 / 0.02 / 0.05, `top1` above the floor.

**Stated precisely:** that artifact records `profile_qualification_claim: false`
on blockers (category-heldout, strict-teacher/BF16-relative, exact
control/isolation, task-quality, route engagement) that were **already open before
this work**. They are route-level, not introduced by the chunk size and not
cleared by it. This packet claims the canonical **numerical** qualification, not a
full profile one.

## Promotion decision

**No default changed.** The blocker is specific:

`DEFAULT_MAX_PREFILL_CHUNK_TOKENS` is **global** - it governs every model the
engine serves, and the prefill workspace is `min(chunk_size,
max_sequence_length)` for all of them. Raising it to 4096 multiplies the prefill
workspace on every model and every host, while the +6.20 GB is measured for **one
model on one host**. On a host where the model currently fits at chunk 256 with
little headroom, a blanket 4096 turns a working configuration into a possible OOM.
That is a regression risk created by generalizing past the measurement.

Two bounding facts this work establishes:

1. **The historical short-request objection is dead.** A 512-token request is
   unaffected in both time and memory, because the workspace is already
   shape-aware through `max_sequence_length`. The only remaining constraint on a
   larger chunk is **peak memory**, not short-request latency. That removes the
   reason RT-3 kept 1024 historically.
2. **There is no scoped hook to promote through.** This model has no
   geometry-keyed chunk entry (the `GGUF_PREFILL_n` pattern used for the Qwen3.5
   H2048-MoE geometry) and the runner has no memory-aware admission guard. So the
   present choice is a global flip or new mechanism, and neither is supported by
   one model's measurement.

**Concrete next step if pursued:** memory-bounded chunk selection - take the
largest chunk the host can actually admit, falling back toward 256. That captures
-28% to -38% where it fits and cannot OOM where it does not. It is a real change
with its own test and gate burden, not a constant edit.

## Limits

- Prefill only.
- Numerical qualification, not full profile qualification.
