# gemma4 gfx1151: the prefill walk's roofline percentage does not reconcile, and the doubt is recorded

## Summary

The decode walk's roofline percentages rest on a byte model, and the prefill
walk's rest on the same one. Timing the prefill kernel directly shows that model
over-counting by a factor of about four on the prefill, which means the prefill
walk's recorded "105-118% of the DRAM roofline" is not supportable and the decode
walk's absolute percentages are in doubt with it.

**The relative results stand.** The decode split and thread-count units compared
the same kernel against itself under the same model, so their gains (1.244x on
the walk, 1.052x end to end at 262,144 keys) are unaffected. What is in doubt is
every *absolute* "N% of roofline" figure.

## The measurement

`scripts/gemma4_prefill_kernel_bw.py` times the shipped prefill kernel on one
chunk, one full layer, with an all-ones mask so the byte count is unambiguous. It
reports the rate against two byte models:

- **unique**: `tokens * keys * 2 kv_heads * 512 dims * 2 bytes * 2` (K + V, once
  per key), the DRAM demand if L2 held nothing.
- **logical**: that times the number of query-head tiles that re-walk the same
  band.

| keys | tokens | ms | unique GB/s | % of 238.5 roofline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16,384 | 256 | 17.56 | 978.4 | 410% |
| 65,536 | 256 | 71.21 | 965.0 | 405% |
| 262,144 | 256 | 292.64 | 939.3 | 394% |

Scaling, at 65,536 keys:

| tokens | ms | unique GB/s | % of roofline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | 5.48 | 783.8 | 329% |
| 32 | 15.11 | 568.3 | 238% |
| 64 | 18.56 | 925.5 | 388% |
| 128 | 38.84 | 884.7 | 371% |
| 256 | 71.87 | 956.1 | 401% |
| 512 | 131.98 | 1041.3 | 437% |

Time is linear in both tokens and keys, so the demand the model describes is real
and the kernel is genuinely doing that much work. It is simply moving the counted
bytes at **three to four times** what the roofline kernel measured as this part's
DRAM ceiling.

## What was ruled out

- **Not cross-row-block L2 sharing.** At `tokens=16` there is exactly one
  16-row block per query-head tile, and it still measures 329%.
- **Not the GQA redundancy being served by L2.** The "logical" column is the
  pessimistic model and it is 8x worse, not better.
- **Not a mis-shaped grid.** The probe uses the shipped entry point and the
  shipped planner; nothing is monkeypatched.

## What this does and does not invalidate

**Does not:**

- The decode walk's split comparison (64 vs 16 vs 32 slices) and thread-count
  comparison (64 vs 128 vs 256 threads). Both compare one configuration of one
  kernel against another under one model.
- The end-to-end tok/s figures, which are wall-clock.
- The arithmetic results.

**Does:**

- `worklog/entries/20261002T191119.793388Z-lhl-gemma4-gfx1151-bringup-fa2693.md`'s
  and the long-context ladder's claims that the prefill walk runs at 105-118% of
  the DRAM roofline. Those came from the same model that is now measured to be
  four times too high on this kernel.
- The decode walk's absolute percentages -- "70.1% -> 87.2% of the DRAM
  roofline" at 262,144 keys. The direction and the relative gain are sound; the
  absolute level is not established.
- The cumulative entry's open question, which is now answered in the sense that
  the model is wrong, but not in the sense that the correct model is known.

## The anchor that suggests where the factor lives

The decode probe (`/tmp/decode_walk_roofline.py`) counts the same 4,096 bytes per
key and lands at **80.4%** of the roofline for one row. The prefill probe counts
4,096 bytes per key per row and lands at **401%** for 256 rows. The ratio between
the 16-row and 1-row configurations at 65,536 keys is **4.09x** for 16x the rows.

So 16 query rows appear to cost what about 4 rows cost, i.e. the per-row-per-key
cost is nearer **1,024 bytes** than 4,096. 1,024 bytes is one 512-dimension BF16
row -- one of K or V for one KV head. Why the kernel would need a quarter of that,
or why the model would over-count by exactly that, **is not established.**

## Changes

- None in the tree. The probe is committed as
  `scripts/gemma4_prefill_kernel_bw.py` so the numbers can be re-run.
- The claims in the two entries named above are left in place as written, since
  committed entries are immutable, and this entry is the correction of record.

## Validation

- The probe is reproducible: three runs per point, median reported, and the
  scaling table is a fresh sweep of six token counts.
- The roofline itself (238.5 GB/s) is not re-measured here. It is consistent with
  Strix Halo's LPDDR5X-8000 at 256-bit (256 GB/s peak), so it is plausible on its
  face and the doubt is placed on the byte model rather than on it.

## Next

- **Settle it with hardware counters, not another model.** `rocprofv3` with
  `--pmc` on the memory unit reports actual DRAM read bytes for this kernel. That
  is the measurement that ends the argument, and it is the only one that can.
- Until then, do not publish a "percent of DRAM roofline" figure for any
  attention kernel in this tree without saying which byte model it uses.
