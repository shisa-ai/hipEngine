# a third of the down projection's instructions expand the Q5_1 fifth bit

## Summary

Both expert MMQ kernels run at 49 GB/s of weight traffic and 2.3 to 3.0 TMAC/s while
neither DRAM nor L1 is saturated. The remaining candidate was instruction issue, and
`clang -g -S` with the `.loc` attribution answers it directly.

`q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel<false>` is **897 instructions**, and the top of
the per-line table is not the dot product:

| line | instrs | dp4a | bit-manip | source |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **77** | **204** | 0 | **192** | `return (bits4 & 1u) \| ((bits4 & 2u) << 7) \| ...` -- `expand_hi_bits` |
| **78** | **64** | 0 | **64** | the same function's closing line |
| 0 | 172 | 0 | 95 | no `.loc`: prologue |
| **47** | **68** | **64** | 0 | `return __builtin_amdgcn_sudot4(...)` -- the dp4a helper |
| 132 | 43 | 0 | 2 | the expert loop |
| 63 | 36 | 0 | 32 | `return bytes4 & 0x0F0F0F0Fu;` -- `lo_plane_nibbles` |
| **193** | **32** | 0 | 0 | `for (int r = 0; r < rcount; ++r)` -- the row loop |
| 216, 217 | 20, 20 | 0 | 0 | `acc[r] += d * xd * static_cast<float>(dot_q)` |

**`expand_hi_bits` is 268 of 897 instructions -- 30 percent of the kernel -- and it
contains no dot product at all.** Across the whole kernel the ratio is **404
bit-manipulation instructions to 64 dp4a, 6.3 to 1**; `v_and_b32` alone (100) outnumbers
`v_dot4_i32_iu8` (64). The instruction histogram is dominated by
`v_and_b32` 100, `v_mov_b32` 97, `v_lshrrev_b32` 64, `v_lshlrev_b32` 49, `v_or3_b32` 32,
`v_and_or_b32` 32, `v_bfe_u32` 29 -- every one of them a bit-field operation, and the
pattern is the nibble-plane and fifth-bit extraction the source spells out.

### Why this exists

Q5_1 stores five bits per weight as four bits plus a separate high bit, so every weight
sub-block needs its fifth bit scattered back into position before a dot product can use
it. `expand_hi_bits` does that 32 bits at a time and costs about one instruction per
output bit. The kernel calls it eight times per sub-block -- `w_hlo[w]` and `w_hhi[w]`
for `w` in 0..3 -- and there are 22 sub-blocks in a 704-wide pass, so the expansion is
roughly 2 instructions for every dp4a in the pass.

### The honest limit of this finding

**This does not prove the kernel is issue-bound.** Two instructions of expansion per
dp4a means about three instructions issued per dp4a, and at 8 percent of the dp4a peak
that is roughly a quarter of the issue rate -- not a ceiling. What the measurement does
establish is where a third of the instructions go and that the largest single block of
them is a **data-layout** cost rather than an arithmetic one.

**It also means the fix is not a kernel tweak.** The expansion exists because of how
Q5_1 is stored. Removing it means a weight layout whose five bits are contiguous, or a
different quantization for this projection -- a change to the artifact and to the loader,
not to the loop. That is a larger decision than this unit can justify on its own.

## Next

- **The Q4_K gate/up kernel is 156.5 ms against the down projection's 103.9, and Q4_K
  has its own unpacking cost.** Taking its instruction mix the same way is the obvious
  next measurement, and it is one compile: if its bit-manipulation ratio is similar,
  the 49 GB/s shared rate has a shared explanation and the two kernels are one problem.
- **The layout change is a real candidate but not a small one.** A five-bit-contiguous
  weight layout would remove `expand_hi_bits` from the hot path entirely, which is 30
  percent of the kernel's instructions and about two per dp4a. It needs a repack step
  and a loader change, so it should be evaluated as its own unit with a measured
  end-to-end number rather than folded into a kernel edit.
- **Do not re-open `ROWS_PER_PASS`, the readback drain, or the down projection's
  ceiling.** All three are measured and closed.
