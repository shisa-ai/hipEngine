# Dense-chain segment capture retired before build: interleaved tiny kernels

## Summary

Pre-build correction of the committed segment-graph plan's second rung
(capture the dense projection chain as a per-layer stateless segment). The
extraction surface shows the rung is not buildable as designed: the decode
dense projections are ~264 launches per step (about 5.5 per layer) averaging
7.5 us of kernel time each, and they interleave with the attention section's
stateful KV/cache writes rather than forming a contiguous stateless chain.
Graph-capturing individual 7.5 us kernels costs more in graph-launch overhead
(~10 us) than the launches save, and the stateful interleaving blocks grouping
them into one segment. The stateless units that are contiguous are already
captured (three GR segments plus the MoE graph per layer, all replaying in
production).

Consequence for the fix ladder: the remaining p4096 boundary-stall mass is
removable only by kernel work - fusing the attention-adjacent launch chain
(reducing the launch count itself) or a solved whole-step graph (the GDN mixer
in-place hazard) - or by the prefill-side F16 routed-GEMM build, which remains
the highest-value unit with its committed spec.

## Changes

- No code change; this unit corrects the committed plan before build effort
  is spent on it.

## Validation

- Trace evidence: dense_gemv_out 264 launches/step at 7.5 us mean kernel time
  (1.98 ms/step total), gaps concentrated around them; the runner's decode
  layer shows the projections interleaved with attention state writes, and the
  MoeGraphCache's per-key replay overhead bounds tiny-kernel captures from
  below.

## Next

- The F16 routed-GEMM kernel build (committed spec) is the next unit; the
  decode boundary-stall line waits on fusion or the whole-step graph hazard.
