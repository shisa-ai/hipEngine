# The prefill gap is flat per-token cost, not length-dependent attention

## Summary

Per-token prefill cost is flat to 512 prompt tokens and rises after that, which
confirms the campaign's attribution of the length-dependent cost to the untiled
global-layer attention - and shows that cost is not where the 3.5x gap lives.

| prompt | prefill tok/s | per-token ms | vs previous |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128 | 253.0 | 3.952 | - |
| 256 | 263.8 | 3.791 | 0.96x |
| 512 | 257.1 | 3.889 | 1.03x |
| 1024 | 230.4 | 4.341 | 1.12x |
| 2048 | 196.3 | 5.096 | 1.17x |

The flat region is flat: 3.952, 3.791 and 3.889 ms are within 4% of each other
with no monotone trend, so the length dependence starts at or above 512 and the
window sits at or below it. That is the sliding-window signature and it is what
the campaign predicted, now measured rather than assumed.

The reason this redirects the next unit is the flat part. At 512 tokens hipEngine
spends 3.889 ms per token against llama.cpp's 1.10 ms at the same length, a 3.5x
gap with no attention penalty in it at all. The length-dependent excess is 0.56 s
of the 4.445 s prefill at 1024 (12.6%) and 2.68 s of the 10.436 s at 2048 (25.7%),
so a tiled 512-wide-head kernel would be worth the 12.6% slice at 1024 and
nothing at 512. The campaign's fourth item - int8 matmuls with large tiles, where
its own note has hipEngine's MoE matmuls at about 16 TFLOP/s against llama.cpp's
26-33 - carries the whole 3.5x and should come first.

  curve, the flat-region floor, and the decomposition of the 1024 and 2048 rows
  into flat and length-dependent parts.

## Validation

- Five lengths, two timed samples each, one warmup, on a clean tree at
  `6401f2f2e`, GPU-exclusive. The same script and the same prompt corpus as the
  baseline, so the 512 and 1024 rows reproduce the baseline within noise (257.1
  against 257.2, and 230.4 against 230.0).
- The decomposition uses the flat-region minimum as the floor, which is the
  conservative choice: it makes the length-dependent slice as large as the data
  supports rather than as small, so the conclusion that the flat cost dominates
  does not depend on picking a favourable baseline.

## Next

- The flat per-token constant is the target. On this platform that means the
  matmul and layer cost, not attention, and the campaign's own numbers put the
  MoE matmuls at about 16 TFLOP/s against llama.cpp's 26-33.
- The tiled 512-wide-head attention kernel remains correct and still worth doing
  for prompts past the window, where it is 12.6% at 1024 and 25.7% at 2048. It is
  a length-specific fix rather than the main gap.
- Decode falls 21.91 to 19.57 tok/s from 128 to 2048 prompt tokens. That is
  context length, not a separate defect, but it means decode rows must state
  their prompt length to be comparable.
