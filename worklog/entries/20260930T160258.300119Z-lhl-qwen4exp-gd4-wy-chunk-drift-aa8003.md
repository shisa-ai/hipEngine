# GD-4 correct screen: the WY reassociation is numerically free in fp32 — the entire cost is the fp16 operand class, and chunking reduces it

## Summary

The retracted screen modelled gated linear attention. This replaces it with a correct one:
`scripts/qwen4exp_gdn_wy_chunk_drift_screen.py` implements the **triangular/WY form** in fp32,
verified against the committed CPU oracle at **6.4e-16** (single-chunk and multi-chunk with the
carry exercised), and measures its drift against the serial form's fp32 drift on identical
inputs, both scored against the float64 oracle.

**Two findings, and they invert GD-4's risk profile.**

## Result — seq 512, Hk16/Hv48/Dk=Dv=128, beta∈[0.1,0.9], decay∈[0.7,0.999]

| variant | out NMSE | state NMSE | vs serial fp32 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| serial fp32 (production form) | 5.359e-14 | 3.169e-15 | 1.00x |
| wy fp32 chunk 8 | 4.265e-14 | 1.116e-14 | 0.80x |
| wy fp32 chunk 16 | 3.856e-14 | 1.117e-14 | 0.72x |
| wy fp32 chunk 32 | 3.610e-14 | 1.121e-14 | 0.67x |
| wy fp32 chunk 64 | 3.477e-14 | 1.106e-14 | **0.65x** |
| serial **fp16 operands** | 1.297e-07 | 4.423e-08 | 2.4e6x |
| **wy fp16 chunk 64** | **8.637e-08** | **6.093e-10** | 1.6e6x |

## Finding 1 — the reassociation is numerically free

**In fp32 the WY form drifts at 0.65–0.80x the serial form's own fp32 drift, and it improves as
the chunk grows.** A chunk of 64 adds nothing measurable over chunk 8 or over the serial chain.

**So the WY reassociation cannot explain GPF-8's KL 0.056522.** That failure must come from
something else — most plausibly the **direct-conv Q/K/V input path** GPF-8 bundled with the
recurrence change. One-token byte-exactness argues the input path is equivalent at `t=0`, but it
does not establish equivalence over a long sequence, and the artifact never separated the two.

This withdraws the inference drawn from GPF-8 that GD-4 "carries a known in-tree precedent of
failing the KL gate via reassociation alone". It does not.

## Finding 2 — the entire cost is the fp16 operand class, and chunking *reduces* it

`serial fp16 operands` costs **1.297e-07** out NMSE. `wy fp16 chunk 64` costs **8.637e-08** —
**0.67x the serial fp16 form**. Chunking with fp16 operands is *more* accurate than the serial
chain with fp16 operands.

The state result is stronger: serial fp16 **4.423e-08** against wy fp16 chunk 64's **6.093e-10**
— **73x better**. That is structural: the WY form computes the chunk's state contribution as one
sum, where the serial chain applies 512 successive fp16 roundings to the carried state.

## What this means for the build

1. **The numerical risk GD-4 carries is not the chunking.** It is exactly the risk of running the
   recurrence with **fp16 operands** — `MX-1`'s F16-operand class, and MX-1's F16 route failed the
   `prefill_last` scope while sitting 8.0–9.5x *below* the BF16 floor already in the path.
2. **GPF-8 already demonstrated the speed at FP32**: 210.501 → 47.491 ms on the 30-layer 512 GDN
   family, **4.43x**, at **48 VGPR** against the production route's 256. The structural win —
   chunk-level matmuls, lower register pressure — bought that, not the operand format.
3. **Recommended build: the chunked WY form with FP32 operands.** It keeps GPF-8's measured
   4.43x, and this screen shows its arithmetic drift is *below* the current production path's.
   fp16 WMMA adds a speed increment whose size is unmeasured here while adding the entire
   numerical risk.
4. **Keep the existing input path.** Since the reassociation is free in fp32, GPF-8's model-gate
   failure is evidence against the direct-conv change, not against chunking.

## Validation

- **The port is verified against the committed oracle at 6.4e-16**, not merely self-consistent:
  single-chunk (n = 4/8/16/64) and multi-chunk with the carry exercised (seq 64 chunk 8,
  seq 128 chunk 16, seq 128 chunk 32). The committed oracle's own wy8-vs-serial agreement is
  1.556e-14.
- **A bug this screen's self-check caught, worth recording:** the first version used
  `np.tril(R, -1)` for the `[t,s]` causal mask. **`np.tril` on a 3-D array masks the *last two*
  axes, not the first two**, so it silently masked `[s,h]` instead — producing a
  chunk-size-independent state error of exactly 0.9263 and out errors growing with chunk size.
  Fixed with an explicit `np.where` mask on `[t,s]`.
- Whole-ledger table integrity re-checked: `GD-4` at 6 cells.

## Scope and limits

- **Synthetic inputs and a NumPy model of the arithmetic, not the real kernel.** Arithmetic drift
  only.
- **Drift is not KL.** GPF-8's own numbers are the caution: a primitive inside frozen tolerance
  still failed the model gate, and the `prefill_last` scope has now failed two non-exact routes.
  The fp16 arm must be gated, not assumed.
- **The 4.43x is GPF-8's**, on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `q4_k_m` at C=8, not on qwen4exp at this shape.
- The fp16 emulation rounds operands to fp16 and accumulates in fp32; it does not model WMMA
  fragment ordering, which a real fp16 kernel would add.
- No kernel written, no performance claim, no default changed.
