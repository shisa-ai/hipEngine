# prefix-cache serving: hit rate, miss-path cost, and retention diagnosis

## Summary

Measured the prefix cache against the live OpenAI-compatible server on the gfx1151
host with 41 multi-turn requests per arm, and diagnosed *why* the hit rate is 27%.
The reuse misses are **not** a client or transcript problem: every missed eligible
turn resent a verbatim prefix of at least 2048 tokens, the engine's own per-request
reason is the radix trie `miss` (never `state_source_unavailable`), and
`unusable_hits` is 0. Two policy defects produce the sparsity: the eligibility
threshold excludes the whole short-prompt multi-turn suite, and the snapshot
retention limit of 1 evicts the retained entry that just served a hit, so reuse
survives exactly one hand-off per conversation. With that mechanism in place the
cache still loses overall (+24.7% wall, -19.1% goodput, drift-corrected over two
arm positions), because each hit clones 63.75 MiB of state and the reused suffix
re-enters the one-token-per-step path.

### Hit rate

Identical in both repetitions of the A/B, so the sparsity is deterministic:

| slice | turns | lookups | hits | reused tokens | reused / prompt tokens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| all | 41 | 22 | 6 | 26112 | 31.0% |
| sharegpt (8 cumulative conversations) | 23 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0.0% |
| code_cumulative (3 agentic lanes) | 9 | 9 | 3 | 24576 | 31.9% |
| code_fixture (3 canonical-transcript lanes) | 9 | 9 | 3 | 27648 | 32.7% |

Reuse never happens twice in a conversation: each lane produces at most one hit,
on the turn that follows its first reusable capture. The hits are always
`source: completed_snapshot` with `snapshot_hit: true`.

### Miss-path cost

Per-turn wall delta of radix-on against radix-off for the same lane and turn
index, median over the cohort, in both arm positions:

| cohort | n | repetition 0 (off first) | repetition 1 (radix first) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no lookup (prompt <= 256) | 19 | +1797 ms | +139 ms |
| lookup, no entry (prompt > 256) | 16 | +6548 ms | +2071 ms |
| hit (prefix reused) | 6 | +3053 ms | +2351 ms |

The no-lookup cohort performs no cache work at all: `_prefix_source_for` returns
`prompt_too_short` before the flush and the match, so its +1797 ms is an ordering
artifact of that arm position, not cache cost. The lookup-miss cohort does pay
real work on every turn (flush packed owners, trie match, one 63.75 MiB snapshot
capture) and is slower in both positions. The hit cohort is also slower in both
positions: a hit costs about as much as the prefill it saves at these prompt
sizes.

### Diagnosis: policy, not structure

Per-turn instrumentation on the diagnostic run, 3 turns per lane:

| lane | turn | prompt | previous | LCP | reusable LCP | engine reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cumulative-medium_repo | 0 | 8234 | - | - | - | miss |
| cumulative-medium_repo | 1 | 8375 | 8234 | 8233 | 8192 | hit, 2048 reused |
| cumulative-medium_repo | 2 | 8530 | 8375 | 8374 | 8192 | miss |
| cumulative-small_repo | 0 | 2094 | - | - | - | miss |
| cumulative-small_repo | 1 | 2244 | 2094 | 2093 | 2048 | hit, 8192 reused |
| cumulative-small_repo | 2 | 2372 | 2244 | 2243 | 2048 | miss |
| fixture-growing_history | 1 | 2643 | 2552 | 2552 | 2304 | hit, 2048 reused |
| fixture-growing_history | 2 | 2732 | 2643 | 2643 | 2560 | miss |
| fixture-medium_repo | 2 | 8891 | 8792 | 8792 | 8704 | hit, 2560 reused |

Every missed eligible turn had at least 2048 tokens of verbatim resent prefix, so
the tokens were available to match. The engine's own reason for those turns is
`miss` (`cache.match` found no entry), never `state_source_unavailable`, and the
arm ends with `unusable_hits: 0`. The entries are gone, not unusable.

Counters from the same arm: `snapshot_limit: 1`, `snapshot_captures: 33`,
`snapshot_evictions: 32`, `snapshot_hits: 6`, `retained_snapshot_entries: 1`,
`state_clone_bytes: 66846720` per hit. A request captures twice (at its decode
boundary and again at its completion prompt boundary), and
`_capture_prefix_snapshot` evicts `next(iter(self._prefix_state_snapshots))` -
the oldest entry, retained or not - whenever the count exceeds the limit. At a
limit of 1 the second capture always destroys the entry that just served the
request, so the next request finds an empty trie. That is exactly the observed
one-hand-off-per-conversation pattern.

The second policy limit is the eligibility threshold: `len(row.prompt_ids) <= 256`
returns `prompt_too_short` before any lookup. Every ShareGPT conversation in this
suite is under that size (30-685 tokens, only four turns above 256), so 19 of 41
turns never consult the cache and 0 of 8 lookups hit.

## Validation

- `uv run pytest tests/test_unit_prefix_cache_multiturn_bench.py` — 22 passed.
- Both arms of the diagnostic run report identical engine counters for the
  measured turns (6 hits, 22 lookups, 26112 reused tokens), so the numbers are
  repeatable, not sampled.
- Correctness gates are unchanged by this unit: no engine or kernel code changed.
  The A/B artifacts carry the per-turn quality evidence from the same runs
  (`output_tokens`, generated text) and the lanes produce the same output tokens
  in both modes (5363 vs 5310 across 41 turns, the difference being the truncated
  fixture lane in one arm).

## Next

- Fix the two policy defects in `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py` (tracked in
  `docs/REFACTOR.md`): prefer unretained snapshots in eviction and size the
  retained working set above 1; decide whether the 256-token eligibility floor
  should apply to short multi-turn traffic. Both need a RED test on
  `_capture_prefix_snapshot` / `_prefix_source_for` before the change.
- Re-measure this harness after the retention fix. If the one-hand-off limit is
  the whole story, `code_cumulative` should move from 33% hit rate to ~100% on
  turns 2+ and the hit cohort's cost should fall as the clone amortizes.
- The fixture lanes regress +62% to +74% in both positions and abort as
  `invalid_tool_call` after ~26 tokens; they are not a fair cache cost sample
  until the tool-call handling in those lanes is understood.
