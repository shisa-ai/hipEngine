# What sets the routed-MoE main kernel cost: 16-row tile count, not row count

## Summary

The two routed-MoE main kernels are 25% of a `code-p4096` prefill (gate/up
2710.3 ms, down 1484.5 ms of 16673.2 ms) and both are **weight-traffic-bound at
a constant 104-126 GB/s**. Cost is `tiles x bytes_per_tile` and nothing else:
`tiles = sum over experts of ceil(rows_in_expert / 16)`, and a gate/up tile
reads 1.843 MB (1280 output columns at 1440 B/row).

Measured on the shipped kernels at the shipped geometry with
`scripts/qwen4exp_moe_main_kernel_cost.py`:

- **Row count barely moves the cost.** 1280 rows cost 8.65 ms and 5120 rows
  9.11 ms, because both need exactly 512 tiles; 40960 rows cost 45.39 ms for
  2801 tiles. 8x the rows is 5.2x the time. Bandwidth stays in 104-126 GB/s at
  every size.
- **Routing matters only through the tile count.** At 10240 rows, `uniform`
  (exactly 20 rows/expert) is the *worst* case at 1024 tiles / 17.26 ms, while
  `balanced` reads 857 tiles / 14.80 ms and `sparse`/`single` read 640 tiles /
  ~10.9 ms for identical arithmetic. The floor is 512 tiles.
- **The packet reproduces the engine.** The packet's single 10240-row launch
  takes 14.82 ms against the engine's 14.42 ms per launch (188 gate/up launches)
  and 8.90 against 8.63 ms for down (172 launches) — 2-3% apart on the same
  kernel and geometry.
- **The engine's capture agrees with the traffic model.** Its gate/up launches
  carry `grid_y = 1120` tiles (`wmma_total_rows = 17920` for 10240 compact
  rows), i.e. 2.064 GB per launch in 14.5 ms = 142 GB/s.
- **The four gate/up launches per layer are the four 1024-token chunks** of a
  4096-token prompt, each with the full `grid_y = 1120` — not four sub-chunks of
  one layer. An earlier reading of "4 launches per layer" as a chunking defect
  was wrong, and MoE chunking does not multiply weight traffic here.
- **The tile map is near its 16-row floor.** 1120 tiles for 10240 rows is 9%
  above the 1024-tile floor, so padding waste at this tile height is nearly
  exhausted.

The lever this identifies is the **M-tile height**: with ~20 rows per expert, a
32-row tile needs one tile per expert instead of two, halving traffic to 943.7 MB
per gate/up launch (512 tiles). At the measured bandwidth that is a **projected
1.7-2.3 s of the 16.67 s prefill** across 188 + 172 launches. This is arithmetic
on the measured bandwidth, not a measurement: no 32-row kernel exists yet.

## Changes

- `scripts/qwen4exp_moe_main_kernel_cost.py` (new): times the shipped
  `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill` and
  `qwen4_exp_q5_1_selected_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill` kernels with synthesized
  weights at the real shapes and byte layout, under four routing distributions
  and a row-count sweep, and reports tiles, padding, bytes read and achieved
  GB/s per launch. `max_risks=0`, so the risk queue is never written.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-09-22-moe-main-kernel-cost/` (new): `README.md`,
  `artifact.json` (row sweep), `artifact-distributions.json` (routing sweep).

## Validation

- Artifacts: `benchmarks/results/2026-09-22-moe-main-kernel-cost/artifact.json`,
  `artifact-distributions.json` (medians of 15 and 11 event-timed launches after
  two primed launches).
- Engine agreement is against
  `benchmarks/results/2026-09-17-iu8-repair-unroll/role-analysis.json`
  (188 gate/up launches at 14.42 ms, 172 down launches at 8.63 ms) and its
  `role-trace/gfx1151/2915662_kernel_trace.csv` (`grid_y = 1120`).
- The traffic model is checked arithmetically in the README: tiles x
  out_features x row_bytes / measured ms lands at 104-142 GB/s in every
  configuration, including the engine's own launches.
- Host: AMD Radeon 8060S (`gfx1151`), Strix Halo, 125 GB unified memory.
