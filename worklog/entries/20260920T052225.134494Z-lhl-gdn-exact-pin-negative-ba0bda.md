# Pinning the GDN prefill mode to exact does not align the two prefix paths

## Summary

Negative result. Pinning the GDN prefill arithmetic to the byte-equality contract
for both paths (`HIPENGINE_GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_MODE=exact`) does **not** align them:
126 `semantic_boundary` and 128 `initial_state` components still differ, and two
flips per 128 rows remain in both scopes. The numbers move, so the knob takes
effect; the route difference survives it. The GDN prefill mode is therefore not
the mechanism.

This also rules out the most attractive remaining explanation. gfx1151's
package policy resolves the dense27B `Q4_K_M` GDN geometry
`("MOSTLY_Q4_K_M", 16, 48, 128, 128)` to `chain_compact_peer_wave32` while
`GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_EXACT_MODE` is `chain_lds32_direct_nonvolatile`, so a
mode-selection split between the packed and session paths was plausible. It is
not what produces the divergence.

## Changes

- None. One measurement.

## Validation

128 teacher-forced steps per scope, the calibrated packet's commands with
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_MODE=exact`:

| scope | flips / 128 | mean KL | max KL | semantic mismatches | initial mismatches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `category-general_ja` | 2 | 0.056492 (0.022005 unpinned) | 6.602 (1.758 unpinned) | 126 | 128 |
| `heldout-code` | 2 | 0.002238 (0.024089 unpinned) | 0.109 (3.002 unpinned) | 126 | 128 |

Both scopes move, one improving and one worsening, which is the signature of a
different rounding draw rather than of a realigned route. `sched_bdry` stays True
in both, so the packed oracle still matches the candidate bit-exactly.

Source context for the ruled-out hypothesis, from
`hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1151/__init__.py:1538-1565`:
`GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_AUTO_MODE = "chain_lds32_direct_nonvolatile"`,
`GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_AUTO_MODES_BY_QUANT_SHAPE[("MOSTLY_Q4_K_M", 16, 48, 128, 128)] =
"chain_compact_peer_wave32"`, `GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_EXACT_MODE =
"chain_lds32_direct_nonvolatile"`, and `GGUF_GDN_PREFILL_COMPACT_PEER_CHUNK_ROWS
= 1024`. The comment records the compact peer-wave route as bit-exact to the
peer-wave oracle and 1.42-1.52x faster than direct LDS32 at 512/1K/4K.
