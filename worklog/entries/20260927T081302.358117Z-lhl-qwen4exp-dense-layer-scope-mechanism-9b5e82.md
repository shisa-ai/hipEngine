# Dense scope boundary at 16 is an f16 drift budget, not a tile or shape limit

## Summary

Sprint P asked for a numerically admitted scope extension or a mechanism-level
account of the boundary. This is the account, plus a measured layer split that
settles what actually runs where.

Measured on a production-default 1024-token prefill, instrumenting the three
Q8_0 dense selectors: the wide route claims 264 of 398 dispatches (0.663, against
the scope's 32/48 = 0.667) and leaves 134 on the exact coltile chain. The f16
WMMA rewrite is inactive in this lane and `HIPENGINE_QWEN4_EXP_Q8_IU8_WMM` reads
0 in this binding. The declines are the layer scope and nothing else - role,
quant, variant and shape are identical on both sides - so the boundary is purely
numerical, not a coverage or shape gap.

The boundary is a monotone drift budget, not a cliff. Across seven gated scopes
on the same fixture, mean KL against the exact chain rises smoothly with the
number of f16 layers: 5.81e-5 (32-47, 16 layers), 9.34e-5 (28-47), 2.53e-4
(20-47), 3.80e-4 (16-47, promoted), 6.47e-4 (12-47), 8.44e-4 (8-47), 1.099e-3
(0-47). The 1e-3 limit is crossed at roughly layer 11. The per-layer cost of the
arithmetic itself grows as the scope deepens (3.6e-6 per wide layer at 32-47
against 2.3e-5 at 0-47), which is amplification; a geometric model with g ~ 1.10
per downstream layer fits the 16/20/28-layer points to ~10% and over-predicts the
deepest extensions, so the shape is amplification but the model is not tight
enough to place a boundary without gating.

**Retiling cannot move it.** The 12-47 packet already records the load-bearing
fact: `dense_wide256` is a retiling of the f16 WMMA arithmetic, not a
reassociation - both emit `candidate_logits_sha256 65370710c0b9c40f` at 16-47 -
and that arithmetic is what the WMMA 0-47 arm failed at. The wide route bought
the same arithmetic at 4.65x lower per-layer cost, not more accuracy.

Prize: layers 0-15 cost 3262.5 ms (203.9 ms/layer) against the wide tile's 43.9
ms/layer over 16-47, so running 0-15 on the wide tile would be 702.1 ms and save
**2560.4 ms**, 19.7% of the 12.97 s gap. Scope tuning cannot collect it - the
reachable extension is at most a few layers, ~0.4 s, sitting at the numerical
limit with a real chance of failing its own gate.

The mechanism says what would: reduce per-layer operand rounding while keeping
the tile. The wide kernel stages f16 weights and converts activations to f16, so
the drift is operand rounding, not accumulation order. A multi-term f16 split
(hi + lo, 2-3 WMMA passes against the same LDS tile) recovers most f32 accuracy
at the same memory traffic, which is the budget the kernel is bound by. The
comparator does this: the PR #63 env capture carries `LLAMA_MMB_F32SPLIT=2`.
Untested here - that a split crosses the gate is a prediction, not a result.

## Next

If this direction is picked up, the unit is a split-precision f16 wide tile:
carry the staged weight and the converted activation as hi + lo, run the extra
WMMA passes against the same LDS tile, and gate the scope at 12-47 first because
that is the nearest failing scope (mean 6.47e-4 against a 1e-3 limit) and it
fails on one category only. Do not run a scope sweep first: the boundary is a
budget, so a scope that passes by a hair is not evidence that the arithmetic
improved.
