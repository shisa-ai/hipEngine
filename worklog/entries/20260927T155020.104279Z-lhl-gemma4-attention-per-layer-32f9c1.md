# Attention is 1.6% of the FLOP and 15% of the time

## Summary

The 30 attention launches in the 512-token trace run in layer order, so sorting
them by timestamp attributes each to one layer without instrumentation. The
result is a strict 5:1 pattern: 25 sliding layers at about 7.1 ms and 5 global
layers at about 23.5 ms.

The global layers are 3.3x slower per layer while doing identical FLOP, since
head_dim 512 with 4 heads is the same work as head_dim 256 with 8. The cause is
a gate rather than a tuning miss: the fast warp-per-key path is guarded by
`if (head_dim == 256 && blockDim.x == 256)`, so the global layers always run the
block path whose own comment calls the per-key barrier rounds "the whole cost of
this kernel". The kernel's only tuning surface is
`threads = next_pow2(head_dim)` capped at 256, so there is no tile to sweep -
which corrects the next unit I named in the previous entry, where I assumed one
existed.

**The framing that matters is FLOP share against time share.** Attention is 1.6%
of the prefill's FLOP and 15.1% of its wall time. The whole prefill runs at 6.9%
of gfx1151's roughly 30 TFLOP/s and 2.9% of DRAM bandwidth - 14.6 GB of weights
read over 1.991 s is 7.3 GB/s - so it is neither compute-bound nor
bandwidth-bound. Attention's time share is nine times its FLOP share precisely
because it is so far off peak: 1.0% for the sliding path and 0.3% for the global
one, against about 7% for the matmul side.

That bounds what attention can be worth. A perfect attention fix recovers up to
its 15% of wall time, taking the prefill from 3.54x to about 3.0x llama.cpp at
512. Worth doing, and the campaign is right to list it first, but it cannot close
the gap alone - and the same question then has to be asked of the matmul side,
which is 78% of the time at about 7% of peak with weight traffic at only 2.9% of
DRAM.

One block per (token, head) explains the memory side: each block re-reads all of
K and V for its head, 512 KiB at head_dim 256 and 2.15 GB per layer across 4096
blocks, which is 302 GB/s. Query tiling reduces that traffic and the barrier
count together, and it is the only route that helps the sliding layers, which are
59% of attention and already on the fast path.

## Validation

- Per-launch durations from the existing 512-token `rocprofv3` trace, sorted by
  start timestamp; the two groups do not overlap and the pattern is strict.
- The FLOP figures are `2 * (2 * tokens * keys * head_dim * heads)` per layer at
  the model's head counts; the weight traffic is 26e9 parameters at 4.5 bpw.
- The 256 GB/s DRAM figure is an assumption for this box, not a measurement, and
  the computed 302 GB/s exceeds it. The defensible statement is therefore
  "memory-limited rather than compute-limited", which the 1.0% and 0.3% of peak
  figures support on their own. The artifact says so explicitly.
- I printed "a perfect attention fix caps at 1.6% of prefill" while computing the
  context block, conflating FLOP share with time share. It is up to 15%. The
  error is recorded in the artifact's limits rather than dropped, because that
  distinction is the finding.
