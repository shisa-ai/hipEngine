# RT-3 strengthened: the shape policy has no benefit even where the owners genuinely differ

## Summary

RT-3 was updated earlier today with "PREMISE FALSIFIED; THE TRADE IS MEMORY-ONLY". One gap
remained in that argument, and this entry closes it.

At p512 the two chunk sizes resolved to the **same** `min(chunk, max_seq_length)` - 768 rows -
so that measurement could not separate *"the shape policy is unnecessary"* from *"the two arms
were identical"*. The A/B in
`benchmarks/results/2026-09-29-qwen4exp-rt1-same-owner-selection/` separates them: two prepared
owners whose row counts **genuinely differ** (1024 vs 4096 rows against a 4352 sequence).

## Result

The same short prompt, selector **on** (served by the 1024 owner) vs **off** (forced onto the
4096 owner), same runner, same process:

**3.2068 s vs 3.1730 s = 1.06% slower on the small owner, at 0.017x the within-arm spread**,
with **identical `logits_sha256`**. No measurable benefit in either direction.

## Why this is the stronger evidence

The workspace is **scratch**. Kernel work scales with the **actual token count**, not with the
size of the scratch handed to it, so a 512-token prefill does 512 tokens of work whichever
owner supplies the buffers. The earlier `min(chunk, max_seq_len)` observation and this A/B are
therefore **independent** routes to the same conclusion, and this one holds where the owners
are *not* identical.

So the length/capacity-bucketed policy RT-3 asks for is **not merely the wrong form for time -
it is unnecessary**. A single larger owner is at least as good, and the only real lever remains
the capacity guard RT-3 already names. The extra owner also costs **1.555 GiB** for nothing.
