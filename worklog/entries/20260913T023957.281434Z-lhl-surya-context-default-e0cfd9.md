# Surya text-context default measured: 16384 stands, 24.58 KiB/token, vision-budget bound

## Summary

`DEFAULT_MAX_SEQ = 16384` was chosen against a memory argument (it admits the
300-DPI A4 page's 8580 image tokens where 2048 rejected every real document), not
measured. The task was to measure the document-reach versus resident-memory
trade across candidate defaults and keep the value only if the evidence supports
it. The value stands, and both of its bounds are now measured rather than
argued:

* the **lower** bound is a gate that is actually run — the transcription
  acceptance suite's A4 row declares a 4000-token output budget and needs
  `121 + 8580 + 4000 = 12701` tokens, so 12288 (upstream Surya's own per-slot
  budget, and the value the generator's comment cites) cannot serve it and 13312
  is the smallest 1024-multiple that can, with 5% headroom;
* the **upper** bound is the vision time budget from the previous unit — with
  the A4's measured 2108-token output held fixed, a 16384-token context reaches
  14155 image tokens = 14.49 MP against the 120 s budget's 14.56 MP ceiling, so
  the two policy defaults agree to 0.5% and a larger context only buys pages
  whose vision forward already exceeds the time budget.

Resident is exactly linear in the context at **24.58 KiB per token** (24.0 KiB
of KV planes over the six full-attention layers plus ~9 B/token of
`KVLiveSpans` metadata), so the whole trade is a straight line and the question
is only where to stop on it.

Two findings came out of the measurement that were not part of the question.
**Prefill does not see `max_seq` at all**: at 16384 tokens the three prefills
are 17.17/17.19/17.18 s across `max_seq` 16384/20480/32768 with the same 268.4 MB
score tile and the same 3032.9 MB of non-attention scratch, because the causal
tile plan and the activations depend on the token count, not the capacity.
**Decode does, weakly**: the split chunk is derived from `max_seq` while the
split count is held at 64, so a larger context means fewer, longer producer
blocks, and at a *fixed* live context 1024 tokens decode in
18.31/18.36/18.88/18.96/19.55 ms per token for the five candidates. That is a
reason not to over-provision the default, and it is a recoverable inefficiency
rather than an intrinsic cost, so it is recorded in `docs/REFACTOR.md` with the
proposed fix.

No engine behaviour changes in this unit: `DEFAULT_MAX_SEQ` stays 16384 and only
its justification is rewritten. The measurement tooling gained a decode probe.

## Changes

- `scripts/surya_attention_memory.py`:
  - `--decode-steps N` times `N` `runner.decode_step` calls after each row's
    prefill (0 keeps the previous prefill-only behaviour) and records
    `decode_steps`, `decode_steps_requested`, `decode_seconds_runs`,
    `decode_seconds_mean`, `decode_ms_per_token` and `decode_tokens_per_s` per
    row. A step ends in `_logits_last`, which synchronizes before the logits
    D2H, so a step is launch + execution + the copy the greedy loop needs.
  - A row filled to `max_seq` has no free KV slot, so the probe stops at
    `max_seq - tokens` steps and reports `decode_steps` 0 with
    `(no decode room)` on stdout; the first attempt at `--tokens 16384` on a
    16384 context asserted instead.
  - Each budget block records the `split_plan` it ran
    (`chunk_size`/`num_splits`/`block_size`/`block_table_len`) — the decode
    timing is uninterpretable without the chunk, which is the thing that varies.
  - `protocol.decode_timing` documents the above, and the fitted-slope printout
    no longer crashes when a single token count makes `fit_linear` return
    `None`.
- `hipengine/runtime/surya.py`: `DEFAULT_MAX_SEQ` keeps its value and gains the
  measured justification — the five-candidate table, the 24.58 KiB/token
  linearity, the two reach columns, the three measured facts that fix the value,
  and the decode-chunk caveat.
- `docs/MODEL-SURYA.md`: the same trade in the "Measured memory envelope"
  section, with the reach table and the prefill/decode asymmetry.
- `docs/REFACTOR.md`: "Surya decode split chunk keyed to `max_seq`" — the
  measured 3%/4% decode cost, the host-only fix (`plan_surya_decode_splits` on
  the live count, which the ops wrapper already accepts as a `chunk_size`
  override), the one allocation that must move with it, and the numerical gate
  promotion would need.
- `benchmarks/README.md`, `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md`: retained measurement, its
  artifact, and the rollup one-liner.

## Validation

`python3 scripts/surya_attention_memory.py --max-seq 8192 12288 16384 20480
32768 --tokens 256,1024,4096,8580,12288,16384 --runs 3 --decode-steps 16`, one
fresh runner per (candidate, token count) with the allocation counter reset
after construction, three timed prefills per row, sixteen timed decode steps per
row. Measured fp32 on the Ryzen AI Max+ Pro 395 (Radeon 8060S, gfx1151) with no
other GPU job running; `--tokens` values above a candidate's `max_seq` are
skipped (8192 skips three rows, 12288 one).

Resident, exactly linear (`fitted_linear_bytes_per_token` is not fitted here —
the slope is `resident_bytes` differences, 100.7 MB per 4096 tokens):

| `max_seq` | KV planes | span metadata | resident | chunk | splits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8192 | 201.3 MB | 0.07 MB | 2885.7 MB | 128 | 64 |
| 12288 | 302.0 MB | 0.11 MB | 2986.4 MB | 192 | 64 |
| 16384 | 402.7 MB | 0.15 MB | 3087.1 MB | 256 | 64 |
| 20480 | 503.3 MB | 0.18 MB | 3187.8 MB | 320 | 64 |
| 32768 | 805.3 MB | 0.30 MB | 3489.9 MB | 512 | 64 |

Reach is `max_seq` minus the 121-token prompt minus the output budget. Two
output models are defensible and both are reported: a fixed 4000-token budget
(the largest any retained gate row declares) and the A4's measured 2108-token
output. A page's text does not grow with its resolution, so the fixed-output
column is the physical one; the third column is where that page's vision forward
lands in time under the current budget.

| `max_seq` | resident | reach @4000 | vision | reach @2108 | vision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8192 | 2886 MB | 4.2 MP | 25 s | 6.1 MP | 25 s |
| 12288 | 2986 MB | 8.4 MP | 57 s | 10.3 MP | 57 s |
| **16384** | **3087 MB** | **12.6 MP** | **102 s** | **14.5 MP** | **119 s** |
| 20480 | 3188 MB | 16.8 MP | 161 s | 18.7 MP | 161 s |
| 32768 | 3490 MB | 29.3 MP | 437 s | 31.3 MP | 437 s |

The 16384 row is the one that matters: 14155 image tokens is 14.49 MP against
the 14.56 MP ceiling of the 120 s vision budget (`vision_patch_ceiling()` from
the previous unit), i.e. the context default and the time default stop agreeing
to 0.5%. Under the *proportional* output model (output grows with page area at
the A4's measured 0.246 ratio) the same row reaches 13.4 MP / 102 s, which is
the conservative bound; either way the next 1024-multiple up (18432) buys a page
that needs 130 s of vision and is rejected on time.

Peak scratch is `max_seq`-independent, which is the clean part of the result:

| tokens | 8192 | 12288 | 16384 | 20480 | 32768 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| prefill s @4096 | 3.31 | 3.31 | 3.32 | 3.32 | 3.33 |
| prefill s @8580 | — | 7.54 | 7.56 | 7.55 | 7.57 |
| prefill s @12288 | — | 11.68 | 11.70 | 11.69 | 11.69 |
| prefill s @16384 | — | — | 17.17 | 17.19 | 17.18 |
| score tile @16384 | — | — | 268.4 MB | 268.4 MB | 268.4 MB |
| non-attention @16384 | — | — | 3032.9 MB | 3032.9 MB | 3032.9 MB |

Decode, at each candidate's own live context (ms/token, mean of 16 steps):

| live tokens | 8192 | 12288 | 16384 | 20480 | 32768 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 256 | 18.25 | 18.37 | 18.83 | 18.89 | 18.92 |
| 1024 | 18.31 | 18.36 | 18.88 | 18.96 | 19.55 |
| 4096 | 18.25 | 18.70 | 18.98 | 19.05 | 19.65 |
| 8580 | — | 19.07 | 19.22 | 19.38 | 20.00 |
| 12288 | — | — | 19.49 | 19.63 | 20.17 |
| 16384 | — | — | — | 20.16 | 20.47 |

Per-row spread over the 16 steps is 1.1-2.4%, so the mean's standard error is
0.3-0.6% and the 3% (8192 -> 16384) and 4% (16384 -> 32768) steps at a fixed
live context are ~5-10 standard errors. The effect is largest where few splits
have work (1024 live tokens is 8 active splits at chunk 128 and 2 at chunk 512),
which is what attributes it to the split plan rather than to the KV planes: the
`max_seq`-independent prefill numbers above rule out capacity as a general cost,
and the chunk column is the only thing that varies with `max_seq` in the decode
launch.

## Next

- `docs/REFACTOR.md` carries the one follow-up this opened: derive the decode
  split chunk from the live count instead of `max_seq` to recover the measured
  3-5% on short requests, which needs the production-profile numerical gate
  because it moves the online-softmax split boundaries.
- The `--decode-steps` probe is gfx1151 evidence. A gfx1100/W7900 lane should
  re-run it before its own default is justified from these rates.
- The reach table is a geometry calculation over the measured resident and the
  vision-time calibration; a real page at 12.6 MP has not been transcribed, so
  the largest *tested* page remains the 34320-patch A4 (43.4 s of vision).
