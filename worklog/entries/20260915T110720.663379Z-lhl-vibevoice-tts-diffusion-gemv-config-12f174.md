# VibeVoice-TTS diffusion head GEMV config: RTF 1.836 -> 1.642

## Summary

The diffusion head's 17 GEMVs ran at the shared 256-thread default and its ffn
used the fused dual gate/up GEMV. Both were wrong for this head's shape, which is
2 rows against 123M parameters. `threads=64` with gate and up as two single GEMVs
takes a head call from 3.33 to 2.08 ms, and on the real-request bench the whole
improvement lands in the diffusion stage: **2.114 -> 1.390 s (-34.2%)**, pooled
RTF **1.836 -> 1.642 (-10.6%)**.

## Why the shape matters

At 2 rows, a block-wide reduction dominates the call. The head's GEMVs reduce
1536-4608 values per output, so a 256-thread block spends most of its work in
the reduction tree rather than in the multiply. At 64 threads the same call
measures 2.08 ms against 3.33 ms.

The fused dual kernel was not dropped for a correctness reason and the strict
dual variant still exists in the registry. It loses here because its two-output
block costs more than the x re-read it saves: x is 2x1536 bf16 (6 KB) against
28 MB of weights per layer, so there was never meaningful traffic to save, and
the dual kernel pays for the second output in the same reduction.

## Measurements

Same host (zbook, gfx1151 / Radeon 8060S), same command, 3 repeats per arm,
`scripts/vibevoice_tts_session_bench.py --repeats 3`, on the pinned single-speaker
request (121-token prompt, 27 tokens generated, 25 diffusion frames, 20 solver
steps, CFG 1.3, 3.333 s of output audio):

| metric | before (256, fused dual) | after (64, split) | delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| pooled RTF | 1.836 | 1.642 | -10.6% |
| warm synthesis | 6.121 s | 5.474 s | -0.647 s |
| diffusion stage | 2.114 s | 1.390 s | **-34.2%** |
| LM stage | 1.525 s | 1.587 s | +0.062 s |
| decode stage | 1.291 s | 1.303 s | +0.012 s |
| semantic stage | 1.016 s | 1.013 s | -0.003 s |
| prompt stage | 0.174 s | 0.182 s | +0.008 s |
| time to first audio | 0.438 s | 0.422 s | -0.016 s |

Every stage other than diffusion is flat within its own run-to-run spread, so
the pooled delta is attributable to the head rather than to a shift in the rest
of the pipeline. The 0.647 s warm reduction is far outside the ~0.01-0.06 s
spread of the untouched stages.

## Next

Two caveats the review raised are carried in the artifact rather than fixed here,
because they change the benchmark's own definition and belong with the
benchmark-comparability work: `cold_start_seconds` is measured after a traced
synthesis so it is not a cold measurement, and the generation budget is the
oracle's expected token count (27) rather than an independently specified cap,
so EOS and truncation are not distinguished. Neither affects this A/B, since
both arms share the protocol and the budget.

The next unit is the unassisted multi-request generated-audio quality suite,
which is what replaces numerical agreement as the acceptance evidence for the
two-speaker request.
