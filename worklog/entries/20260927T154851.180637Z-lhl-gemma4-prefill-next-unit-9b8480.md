# Gemma 4 26B prefill/decode state and the next unit

## Summary

Handoff for the Gemma 4 26B-A4B prefill and decode work. Objective items 1, 2,
3 and 6 are closed with evidence; items 4 and 5 are open and this entry records
exactly what directs them, so the next unit does not have to re-derive it.

**Three measurements, and each one redirected the work.**

The llama.cpp baseline on identical prompt ids gave a prefill gap of 3.54x at
512 and 5.14x at 1024, not the 30x the objective opened with, and decode at
21.06 tok/s against llama.cpp's 36.03.

The length curve then showed per-token prefill cost is flat to 512 (3.952,
3.791, 3.889 ms) and rises after (4.341 at 1024, 5.096 at 2048). That confirmed
the sliding-window signature the campaign predicted, but the reading that
mattered was the flat part: at 512 the entire 3.5x gap carries zero attention
penalty.

The kernel trace then said what the flat part is. 2.013 s of kernel time against
1.991 s of prefill wall is 101%, so the prefill is entirely kernel-bound, and
matmuls are about 78% of it: q5_1 staged grouped 714.91 ms (35.5%, 29 calls),
q8_0 dense tiled 566.68 ms (28.1%, 205 calls), the fused MMQ32 route 189.24 ms
(9.4%) and one layer's q5_k at 110.89 ms (5.5%, a single launch).

**The correction I had to make to myself, recorded because it is the useful
part.** The length curve measures a difference, so it sizes a slope and not a
level. I read attention as being only the growth past the window, and that
pushed me to rank the campaign's fourth item above its first. Attention is
15.0% at 512 tokens, where the curve shows no penalty at all, so the campaign's
first item has more headroom than I gave it, not less.

**What is left, and the next unit.**

The two largest kernel lines are already claimed. The q5_1 down projection's
tile space is closed by the parallel workstream - six shapes and a staging
rewrite, all bit-identical and all slower - and the one faster shape found
(`K_TILE=512`, 17.28 against 20.23 ms) is not bit-exact, so that lever is the
`kl_max` decision rather than another sweep. The q8_0 dense route's tile shape
has likewise been swept.

Attention is the largest unclaimed line at 15.0% flat plus growth, and the next
unit is a tile sweep of the prefill kernel rather than the tiled 512-wide-head
rewrite the campaign proposes first, because the sweep is cheap and the rewrite
is not. The reason to expect it may pay: `gemma4_attention_prefill_kernel` has
no tile selection at all - only the decode launcher does, and its search starts
at 8, which is why its `case 16` is unreachable - and the LDS budget that the
comment cites as the reason to keep the tile small is not binding. At tile 8
the kernel uses 11264 B at head_dim 256 and 12288 B at head_dim 512 with 512
keys, 17.2% and 18.8% of the 64 KiB budget, and only 28.1% even at 2048 keys
with the larger head. Every matmul tile sweep on this project has found a
smaller tile winning, so a sweep in both directions is the cheap first test.

Layer 29 is the cleanest unclaimed unit and the smaller one: one launch at
110.89 ms against 29 calls of the q5_1 kernel at 24.65 ms each makes it about
4.5x a normal layer on its gate_up alone, and the campaign's own note is that
its Q5_K gate_up and Q8_0 down have no fast kernels. It is 5.5% of the prefill,
so it is a smaller prize than attention but a much more contained one.

Decode is unchanged at 21.06 tok/s against a target of about 42, above
llama.cpp's 36.03. The cheap levers there are closed by the parallel
workstream, so it needs the same treatment: profile first, then tune.
