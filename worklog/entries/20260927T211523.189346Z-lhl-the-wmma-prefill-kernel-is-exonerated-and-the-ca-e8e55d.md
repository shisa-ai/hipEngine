# The WMMA prefill kernel is exonerated and the cause is still open

## Summary

Checkpoint correcting
`worklog/entries/20260927T205805.397424Z-lhl-the-wmma-prefill-divergence-is-sourced-in-the-q8-fbfecc.md`,
which concluded that the divergence was a defect in
`gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel`'s arithmetic. **That conclusion is wrong.** The
kernel is correct, and the cause of the end-to-end divergence is still open.

Three things I got wrong, and what measurement says instead.

## 1. The kernel is arithmetically correct

A bare GEMM — attention, the MoE router, and every other kernel out of the
picture — puts the WMMA route and the exact tiled route on the same raw Q8_0
weight and the same bf16 activation:

| rows | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 512 | 1024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WMMA vs tiled | 0.0 | 0.0 | 7.4e-07 | 2.4e-06 | 1.1e-03 | 1.1e-03 |

Both sit ~2.5e-03 from a float64 CPU oracle, which is exactly bf16 output
rounding (2⁻⁹ = 1.95e-03). Both reproduce their own contract to the last bit the
output format can carry.

I also swept every tile and shape I could think of, because the 512-column and
2816-row shapes are all multiples of 64 and a tail defect would be invisible:

- **All six `(tile_m, tile_n)` combinations** at rows 64/128/256/512 — 24 results,
  every one between 2.2e-03 and 2.8e-03 against the oracle.
- `out_features` 104 (not a multiple of 64) and 2112; `rows` 100 and other
  non-multiples of the 32-row tile. Worst WMMA-vs-tiled across every shape:
  **1.126e-03**.

So the "a more precise f16 activation cannot produce this gap" argument was
sound as far as it went, and it pointed at the wrong conclusion. The gap was not
the kernel's.

## 2. The 10.9 KL was an input artifact

My original probe used **random token ids**, which put the model far off the
manifold its weights were trained on, where residual cancellation amplifies a
1e-3 perturbation. Rerunning the identical harness on the campaign's own frozen
corpus (`exact_prompt_ids`, the same corpus the bench and the gate use):

| rows | 4 | 16 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KL, real text | 0.027 | 0.258 | 0.185 | 0.341 | 0.088 | **3.9e-06** |
| KL, random ids | 0.658 | 0.898 | 10.88 | — | — | — |
| top-1 same | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

One to two orders of magnitude smaller, and **no top-1 ever flips**. At 512 rows
— the block size — the two routes are effectively identical.

## 3. But the gate does fail, at a shape production actually hits

Applying the gate's own methodology at a short shape rather than my logit probe:

```
scripts/gemma4_teacher_forced_gate.py capture/gate --prompt 512 --prefill 256
```

| metric | value | limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kl_mean | 0.0249 | 0.001 |
| kl_p95 | 0.0837 | 0.005 |
| kl_p99 | 0.712 | 0.02 |
| kl_max | **1.60** | 0.05 |
| top1_rate | 0.976 | 0.99 |
| **top1_flips** | **6** | 0 |

A hard fail on every axis. And a 256-row prefill is not exotic: it is what any
prompt shorter than the 512-token block produces, and what the final partial
block of a longer prompt produces. **The 1024-row pass covers the shapes the
campaign measures, not the shapes users hit.** That is the strongest argument for
keeping this reverted, and it is a better one than the logit probe I originally
used.

## What is still open

Profiling the failing shape does not move the answer. At 256 rows on real text
both arms dispatch **1558** kernels and the same three names differ as at 64
rows, with the same 205 q8_0 prefill calls. So: the kernel is correct, it is the
only difference, and the end-to-end result still differs.

**The cause is invocation-side, not arithmetic.** Remaining candidates, none
tested: a non-contiguous or padded activation stride that both routes assume away
(both index `x` as `token * in_features`), a scratch buffer the WMMA route aliases
and the tiled route does not, or a `rows` argument that differs from what the
caller believes.

## Changes

- `scripts/gguf_q8_0_prefill_ab.py` — the diagnostic, preserved. Compares the two
  routes on one raw Q8_0 weight against a float64 oracle, and sweeps tiles and
  shapes. `--tiles` runs the tile sweep.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-09-28-gemma4-wmma-dense-prefill-short-row-divergence.json`
  gains a `correction` section and the 256-row gate result.

No production code changed, so no promotion gate applies. **The revert from
iteration 57 stands**, now on the gate's own evidence rather than on mine.

## Validation

- The kernel comparison is a direct two-route measurement against an independent
  float64 oracle, not a profile inference.
- The tile and shape sweeps exist specifically because the obvious shapes are all
  multiples of 64 and would hide a tail defect.
- The on-manifold retest reuses the campaign's own corpus function, so it is the
  same input distribution the gate scores.
- The 256-row gate result is the campaign evaluator against a frozen baseline, on
  its own thresholds.
