# AR graph-captures its uploads; the MTP accept path is eager per cycle

## Summary

Cross-check of the accept-upload mechanism (`20260830T155357…`) against why AR does not
suffer it. AR pays its
pageable host-to-device copies at **graph capture time** and replays them; the MTP
accept path issues roughly
seven **per cycle, eagerly**. That is a structural difference between the two paths,
found by counting call sites
rather than by tuning a story.

## Changes

- No code change; this entry records the contrast and one consequence for how #17 is scoped.

## Counted, not inferred

- `grep` over `hipengine/generation/`: `qwen35_gguf.py` (AR) and `batch_scheduler.py` have
  **0** occurrences of
  `copy_host_to_device` or `memcpy_async`; every one of the 8 blocking and 4 async sites
  in that directory is in
  `qwen35_gguf_mtp2.py`, and ~7 of the blocking ones are the per-cycle accept uploads.
- Where AR does upload: `hipengine/runtime/gguf_packed_decode_graph.py:505-514`
  (`token_array`, `mask_array` via
  `copy_host_to_device`) inside `capture_qwen35_gguf_packed_decode_graph` - a capture
  function, with a per-capture
  `malloc` as well. `hipengine/runtime/gguf_decode_graph.py:384` is the same shape.
- Measured side of the contrast (same session, `he-uploadsplit.json`): accept uploads are
  85.4-101.6 ms per
  lane-cycle at C5-C8, 96.5% of accept, while AR cycle cost scales w^0.36 against MTP's w^0.76 with
  width-invariant acceptance.

## Inference, labelled as such

That replayed graphs carry no per-step host-blocking copy, and therefore the replay-vs-
eager difference is what
separates the two exponents, is inference from the code shape plus the measured
exponents. It is not a
measurement of AR's steady state, and it does not need to be true in detail for #17's
fix to matter: the accept
uploads are measured on the MTP path directly.

## Consequence for #17

Two shapes now, not one: (a) the pinned single-copy staging fix already specced in
`20260830T155455…`, or (b) capture the MTP cycle the way AR captures decode, which
removes the whole class of
per-cycle host copies rather than one instance. (a) is smaller and testable against the
same baseline; (b) is
what the peers effectively do and would also cover other per-cycle host work in the
cycle. Scope (a) first; do
not let it hide (b) as an option.
