# GD-3 closed: the GDN recurrence baseline is stale by 41x and the lever is bounded at 1.1% of prefill

## Summary

GD-3 was the highest-priority unstarted row in the ledger's Track A list ("OPEN,
no experiment", priority A) and it carried a concrete, quantified claim: our GDN
prefill recurrence is **3.15x slower than gufo's** - "theirs is ~65 ms per 1K
tokens against our 205" - so the row asked to "measure stall cycles in our
register-state kernel first; if latency-bound, add the windowed prefetch".

I measured all four registered recurrence routes directly instead of starting
from the row's numbers. **The row's baseline does not exist, and its comparison
is inverted.**

Per 1K tokens, gfx1100, realistic shape (Hk16/Hv48/Dk=Dv=128), 5 reps after one
warmup, median:

| route | ms per 1K | GB/s | % of 221 GB/s roof |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `tiled16` (**production**) | **4.961** | 19.9 | 9.0% |
| `register_state` | 5.687 | 17.4 | 7.9% |
| `columnwarps` | 7.610 | 13.0 | 5.9% |
| `serial_lds` | 39.628 | 2.5 | 1.1% |

- The row's "our 205" matches **no** route. The worst registered route
  (`serial_lds`) is 5.2x faster than it; the production route is **41x** faster.
- **gufo's quoted 65 ms is 13x slower than our production route** and 11.5x
  slower than `register_state`. We are ahead, not behind.
- Scaling is exactly linear in tokens (2.898 / 5.665 / 11.278 ms at 512 / 1024 /
  2048), so there is no fixed overhead to attack - the cost is per-token.

**The row's mechanism is confirmed even though its numbers are not.** Every route
sits at 1-9% of the bandwidth roof and 0.1-1.1% of the FP32 FLOP roof (traffic
floor 0.448 ms, FLOP floor 0.053 ms at 1K), so none is traffic-bound; latency,
occupancy or issue is the only remaining explanation. Two structural facts
support it: the register-state kernel holds **128 floats of state per thread**
and carries roughly **16 `__syncthreads()` per token**, while the column-warp
sibling in the same file is explicitly built to have "no `__syncthreads` in the
token loop".

## Validation

The measurement is the validation, and it is reproducible from one command:

```
python3 scripts/qwen4exp_gd3_recurrence_screen.py --tokens 512 1024 2048 --reps 5 \
  --json benchmarks/results/2026-09-30-qwen4exp-gd3-recurrence-screen/screen.json
```

Two defects found and fixed in my own harness before the numbers were used:
`hipengine.core.memory` exposes `malloc`, not `alloc`; and the first revision
reported achieved GB/s and TFLOP/s 1000x too high because it multiplied a value
already in seconds by `1e-3`. The second was caught by the absurd "8263% of
roof" output and corrected; the `x above traffic floor` column was correct
throughout and is what the argument rests on.

**The cost gate is an unconditional upper bound**, which is why this closes the
row without needing a fresh family trace: one layer's recurrence is 4.961 ms per
1K tokens, there are **9 certified GDN layers** (global 35-47 -> actual GDN
36/37/38/40/41/42/44/45/46), so ~44.6 ms per 1K = **22.7 ms at p508**, against
today's **2014 ms** p512 prefill. 22.7 / 2014 = **1.1%**. Even an *infinitely
fast* recurrence therefore recovers at most 1.1% of prefill; a realistic 2x
recovers ~0.55%. That bound does not depend on the family's current share, which
is why it is the argument I used.

## Next

- **The mechanism is redirected, not rejected.** The GDN family's cost is
  overwhelmingly *outside* the recurrence - the recurrence is ~3% of the family
  even on the last recorded 750.68 ms p508 trace - so if GDN is worth optimizing,
  the norms, gates, conv, prepare and snapshot stages are where the time is.
  That is a different row and needs its own measurement; I deliberately did
  **not** claim the family's current share of prefill, because the 750.68 ms
  figure comes from a 6.500 s prefill (2026-08-29) and today's is 2014 ms.
- **Not established:** no `rocprofv3` stall counters were taken, so
  "latency-bound" is inferred from the distance to both roofs rather than read
  from hardware counters. If the prefetch experiment is ever revisited, counters
  are the cheaper first step now that the ceiling is known to be ~1%.
- **GD-4 (chunked fp16 WMMA GDN, priority B) shares this cost gate** and should
  be screened against the same 1.1% ceiling before any build.
- The two stale-baseline defects found here (a row quoting a number no route
  produces) suggest a cheap audit: any Track A row whose baseline is not
  reproducible from a registered route should be re-measured before it is
  trusted for prioritization.
