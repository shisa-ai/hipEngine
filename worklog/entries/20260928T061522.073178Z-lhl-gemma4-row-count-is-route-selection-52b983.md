# the expert row-count divergence is route selection at the lane crossover and nothing else

## Summary

The expert forward's row-count divergence -- the defect that makes
`test_incremental_decode_matches_a_dense_prefill` fail and that a speculative
verify would have to survive -- has **one cause, and it is not an arithmetic
defect**.

`gemma4_moe_prefill_route_enabled(lanes, num_experts)` gates the fused gate/up
MMQ route on the **live lane count**:

| lanes | num_experts=4 | num_experts=128 (production) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 2, 3 | False | False |
| 4 | **True** | False |
| 128 | True | **True** |

The forward computes `lanes = tokens * top_k`. With the row-invariance test's
`top_k=2`, rows=1 gives `lanes=2` and rows=2 gives `lanes=4`, so against the
fixture's `num_experts=4` the crossover falls **exactly between the two calls
the test makes**: rows=1 runs the fp32 grouped family, rows=2 runs the int8-dp4a
MMQ route.

Those are two different execution profiles -- an int8-dp4a accumulation and an
fp32 accumulation -- so they differ by roughly a bf16 ulp. Measured at
`gate_up_out`: **4 on a scale of 596**, about 0.7%. GeGLU's `exp` then amplifies
it to about 1% in `activated`, and the down projection carries it to the output.
That is the whole divergence.

## Why this is not a defect

`docs/EXECUTION-PROFILES.md` separates the strict unfused path from production
candidates that reassociate. The grouped family is the strict path; the MMQ
route is a production candidate. **Bit-exactness between them is not a contract
they hold**, so a token's expert output is invariant across row counts *within*
a route, not *across* the lane-count-controlled boundary between routes.

The row-invariance test's two `xfail(strict=True)` cases assert the stronger
property, which is why they fail. That is a **test-framing problem**, not a
kernel problem: the property worth asserting is invariance within a route, with
the crossover handled explicitly.

## Why every earlier probe came back bit-exact

Every direct probe forced **one** route in both cases and therefore compared a
route against itself:

- `gemma4_project_experts_gate_up_mmq` called directly at two row counts
- `gemma4_project_experts_down_mmq` called directly at two row counts
- `gemma4_project_experts_rows` called directly at two row counts
- the K and fused-width sweep, which held the route fixed and varied shape

The one probe that *did* cross a family boundary -- `selected_gemv` against
`grouped_prefill` at compact 2 against compact 4 -- also agreed, which is
consistent: those are both fp32 grouped variants, i.e. the same profile.

This also explains `20260928T061202`'s "both defects are toy-shape artifacts":
both routes **are** invariant at the true geometry within themselves, and the
apparent row-count dependence was the crossover rather than the geometry. The
conclusion was right; its framing as a geometry property was not.

## A retracted finding, and the mistake behind it

Mid-investigation I reported the group-scatter as broken -- `sorted_experts` all
zeros, `sorted_lanes` not a permutation. **That was wrong, and it was my probe.**
I allocated 4 bytes per lane (`int32`) for buffers the kernel declares
`int64_t*` in `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/moe/group_scatter.hip`, so the
kernel's 8-byte writes read back as interleaved halves. With correctly-sized
buffers:

```
rows=1  selected=[0,2]        sorted_lanes=[0,1]      sorted_experts=[0,2]
rows=2  selected=[0,2,0,2]    sorted_lanes=[0,2,1,3]  sorted_experts=[0,0,2,2]
```

Both are correct. `sorted_experts` is expert-major as required, and `[0,2,1,3]`
is a valid ordering of expert 0's lanes `{0,2}` and expert 2's `{1,3}` -- lanes
within an expert's block are not required to be ascending.

This is the same class of mistake as `20260928T060700` (assuming the fixture's
down quant instead of reading it): **taking a fact from context instead of from
the authority.** The authority for a buffer's width is the kernel signature, not
another test's download call.

There was a tell in output I already had and did not check: in the `int32` read,
`counts` came back `[1,0,1,0]`, consistent with `expert_start=[0,1,1,2,2]`; in
the `int64` read it came back `[1,1,0,0]`, **contradicting**
`expert_start=[0,2,2,4,4]`. Two buffers that must agree disagreeing means at
least one is being read at the wrong width. `counts` is `int32` here and
`expert_start` is `int64`.

## What this means for production

The crossover is real at production scale. `num_experts=128`, so **decode**
(`lanes` below 128) takes the grouped family and **prefill** (`lanes` at or
above 128) takes MMQ, and the two disagree at the ulp level by design. That is
fine for correctness. It does mean the remaining MTP work **cannot assume a
bit-exact verify across the two regimes** without either forcing one route for
the verify or accepting the profile difference -- which is the same requirement
`docs/EXECUTION-PROFILES.md` already places on a changed-arithmetic path.

## Changes

None. This unit resolves and records.

## Validation

- `gemma4_moe_prefill_route_enabled` called across `lanes` at both
  `num_experts=4` and `num_experts=128`, showing the crossover at 4 and 128
  respectively.
- The forward's stage bisect at rows=1 against rows=2: `packed_hidden` agrees,
  `gate_up_out` differs by 4 on 596, `activated` by 3072 on 2.888e5.
- The scatter called directly with correctly-sized buffers at both row counts,
  producing correct `sorted_lanes` and `sorted_experts`.

## Next

Re-frame the row-invariance test: assert invariance **within** a route at two
row counts that both sit on the same side of the crossover, and cover the
crossover itself as an explicit, documented profile difference rather than as an
xfail. That test then covers what a caller can actually depend on. After that,
return to the objective's throughput items.
