# VibeVoice-TTS two-speaker chain: the diffusion condition is the fault, not the solve

## Summary

The 59-token two-speaker chain diverges at token 32 (the first span's end).
Injecting the oracle's recorded per-call diffusion **condition** and running
**this session's own** 20-step solve, decoder, semantic feedback and LM makes the
chain **exact** — 59 tokens, 55 diffusion calls. So the fault is the condition
the prefill produces, and the solver, decoder, semantic encoder, feedback path
and LM are all correct. This resolves the open question left by the previous two
entries and sets a concrete target: the condition error has to fall by about
4x, which means bringing the 208-frame voice's connected rows from 0.206 to the
70-frame voice's 0.06.

## The triage

All three runs use the same prompt, the same recorded noise, the same recorded
negative conditions, and `cfg_scale=1.3`:

| Injected | Diffusion calls | Chain |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| nothing (this session's condition and solve) | 59 | diverges at 31, `DIFF` where the oracle has `END` |
| the oracle's per-call **condition**, own solve | **55** | **exact** |
| the oracle's per-call **latent** | **55** | **exact** |

The middle row is the informative one. It keeps our 20-step DPMSolver run, our
decoder, our semantic streaming encoder, our feedback sum and our LM, and only
replaces the 1536-wide condition vector. Exactness there means none of those are
implicated, including the chaotic 20-step solve.

## The tolerance

Blending the two conditions per call as `oracle + a * (ours - oracle)`:

| `a` | Diffusion calls | Chain |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0.00 | 55 | exact |
| 0.10 | 55 | exact |
| 0.25 | 55 | exact |
| 0.50 | 55 | not exact, first mismatch 31 |
| 0.75 | 46 | not exact, first mismatch 31 |
| 1.00 | 59 | not exact, first mismatch 31 |

So a 4x reduction of the current condition error is sufficient and a 2x
reduction is not. The current error is 0.047 relative at call 0 (peak 6.94), and
it is *systematic* rather than white: at call 0, feeding our prefill hidden as
the condition moves the latent 3.573 relative, while the oracle's condition plus
`N(0, 0.33)` noise — the same peak absolute deviation — moves it 0.33. That
asymmetry is what the previous entry's chaos measurement explains: a perturbation
aligned with the trajectory's sensitive direction is amplified, and white noise
of the same size is not.

## Why this does not contradict the chaos finding

The previous entry measured that this request's first diffusion call is chaotic:
a one-bf16-ULP change to the initial noise moves the final latent from 0.274 to
3.337 relative, for both our CPU reference and our device head. That is still
true and still bounds how tightly anything downstream of a diffusion latent can
be compared. It is not the binding constraint on the chain, because the chain is
exact when the condition is right — the condition error, not the solve's
sensitivity, is what pushes the trajectory onto the wrong branch.

## Next

- The target is the connected-row error on the 208-frame voice: 0.206 worst,
  0.0067 median, against 0.06 worst for the 70-frame voice. The encoder's
  `encode0_mean` is 0.054 max / 0.0026 median on the long voice versus 0.036 /
  0.0034 on the short one, so the *median* is already comparable and the gap is
  in the tail — 18 of 208 frames exceed 1e-2. That points at a per-frame
  accumulation effect in the encoder rather than a global scaling error.
- Worth measuring before optimizing: which of those 18 frames land in the
  worst connected rows (41, 103, 127, 160-162), and whether the tail shrinks
  when the encoder runs on the batch-padded input rather than a single row. If
  the tail is an artifact of how the reference is presented, the fix is
  presentation rather than arithmetic.
- A 4x reduction is the requirement, not a nice-to-have: at 2x the chain is
  still broken.
