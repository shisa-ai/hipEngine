# Reject the Gemma flash decode promotion on its numerical gate

## Summary

Supersedes the route promotion in
`worklog/entries/20261008T162238.312050Z-lhl-gemma4-gemma4-flash-global-decode-900c2e.md`.
That entry fixed the flash kernel and selected the key-sliced variant for the
one-token global decode. The kernel fix stands; **the selection does not.** The
teacher-forced production gate rejects the candidate, so production is back on
the staged singleton and `gemma4_flash` stays registered and reachable by
explicit request.

The measured numbers are not marginal. Over the 527 teacher-forced rows at keys
15,857-16,383 (`kl(baseline || candidate)`, nearest-rank percentiles):

| metric | measured | limit | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean KL | 1.11e-2 | 1e-3 | 11x over |
| p95 | 9.18e-3 | 5e-3 | 1.8x over |
| p99 | 3.35e-1 | 2e-2 | 17x over |
| max | 1.61 | 5e-2 | 32x over |
| top-1 | 98.67% | 99% | 7 flips in 527 rows |

The flips are not tie-breaks. The evaluator's margin report bands them by the
baseline row's own p1 - p2: one flip at margin < 0.01, one in 0.01-0.05, three
in 0.05-0.20, and **three in the margin >= 0.20 band** (518 rows), whose worst
row reads KL 1.61. A token that the baseline ranked first by a fifth of the
probability mass moving is a numerical divergence, not a coin flip.

The divergence is the slice reassociation, not the kernel. Against an f64
oracle the flash arm is within 5.4e-6 at 4096 keys, and the 60-case flash
battery passes. What the gate measures is that summing per-slice partials is a
different arithmetic from one ordered pass over the keys, and at 64 slices the
difference exceeds what the production envelope allows.
