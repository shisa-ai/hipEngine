# gemma4 gfx1151: the decode split inherited the prefill shape's block target, and that was both the slowdown and the gate breach

## Summary

The full-layer decode's key split is sized by a **per-shape** block target now
instead of one constant shared by two shapes that move different amounts of
traffic. The decode shape's target is 32 blocks rather than 128, so it divides
its walk into **16 slices instead of 64**.

This fixes two things at once, and they turn out to be the same bug:

- **It is faster.** Sweeping the slice count directly, on one full layer at
  `tokens=1`: 16 slices beats 64 at every key count, by **1.578x at 16,384 keys**
  and 1.066x at 262,144, and reaches 74.7% of the DRAM roofline where 64 reaches
  70.1%. More slices is *worse*, not better -- 128 and 256 both regress.
- **It resolves the gate breach** that the previous unit shipped with. The
  teacher-forced gate at `--prompt 8192 --prefill 4096` went from `passed: false`
  with `kl_max` 0.121 to **`passed: true`** with `kl_max` **0.0229**.

And it settles the question that unit could not: **the 2-row decode shape does
not change the arithmetic at all.** At 16 slices the decode shape's KL report is
*bit-identical* to the WMMA prefill shape's -- `kl_max` 0.022933083436323335,
`kl_mean` 1.5775760632637358e-05, `kl_p95` 2.891630927655655e-06, `kl_p99`
5.33139616525737e-05, `top1_rate` 1.0, to every digit. The breach was entirely
the **64-way split**, not the shape.

## The bug: one constant, two shapes

`plan_gemma4_attention_wmma_full_slices` divided a single-query-tile walk until
it reached `TARGET_BLOCKS = 128` blocks. That constant was measured -- "8 blocks
reach 75 GB/s, 64 reach 225, 256 reach 252" -- but measured on the **prefill**
shape, whose base grid at one query tile is `1 * num_kv_heads * gqa_tiles` = 8
blocks. The split unit that established it says so: *"A one-token block presents
`query_tiles * num_kv_heads * gqa_tiles` = 8 blocks."*

The decode shape was added later, with a base grid of `1 * 2 * 1` = **2** blocks.
Against the same target of 128 that gives **64** slices, and the decode-shape unit
recorded that as observed without measuring it. The reasoning offered was: *"the
decode shape reads each band once where the prefill shape reads it four times, so
it has four times less to overlap and needs four times the slices to reach the
same grid."*

That double-counts. The shape change had already cut each block's traffic to a
quarter; multiplying the slice count by four then cut each slice's work to a
sixteenth. The sweep shows the result: 128 blocks of the decode shape is past the
point where extra blocks help, and the cost is wave quantization plus a longer
combine.

## The sweep

`/tmp/decode_walk_roofline.py`, one full layer, `tokens=1`, shipped decode shape,
identical buffers, median of 9, `plan_gemma4_attention_wmma_full_slices`
monkeypatched so the split can be swept independently of the shape -- which the
gate's `--wmma-full-slices` pin cannot do, because the launcher re-plans and both
attempts to pin shape and slices apart were rejected and silently fell back to the
default. Percentages are against the 238.5 GB/s DRAM roofline the roofline kernel
measured on this device; the walk is 1.074 GB at 262,144 keys.

| keys | 8 | **16** | 32 | 64 (was) | 128 | 256 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16,384 | 36.5% | **51.3%** | 39.7% | 32.5% | 22.1% | 11.2% |
| 65,536 | 41.9% | **66.4%** | 55.7% | 57.4% | 49.2% | 35.3% |
| 131,072 | 42.8% | **73.0%** | 60.8% | 63.3% | 60.7% | 49.3% |
| 262,144 | 43.1% | **74.7%** | 61.9% | 70.1% | 70.4% | 62.9% |

Best against the shipped 64: **1.578x** at 16,384, 1.156x at 65,536, 1.154x at
131,072, 1.066x at 262,144. The unsplit walk (`slices=1`) is 6.2% of the roofline
at 262,144, so the split is still doing its job -- it is just sized wrong.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_attention_prefill_wmma_full.py`:
  `WmmaFullShape` carries a `split_target_blocks`; `PREFILL_SHAPE` keeps 128 and
  `DECODE_SHAPE` takes the new measured `DECODE_SPLIT_TARGET_BLOCKS = 32`. The
  sweep table and the double-count it corrects are recorded at the constant and
  in the planner's docstring. Both shapes now land on 16 slices, from different
  targets and different base block counts.
- `tests/test_unit_gemma4_attention_wmma_slices.py`: the test asserting the
  decode shape divides **four times as far** as the prefill shape is replaced by
  one asserting the targets are separate measured numbers; the target-arithmetic
  tests now use each shape's own target. 23 cases.

## Validation

- `tests/test_unit_gemma4_attention_wmma_slices.py` 23/23 and
  `tests/test_unit_gemma4_prefill_attention_selection.py` 38/38: 61 passed, exit 0.
  RED first: the target test failed with `assert 16 == (((128 + 2) - 1) // 2)`.
- `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_attention_prefill_wmma_full.py`: **27/27, exit 0**,
  including the split-vs-whole-walk comparisons. The split changed, so the decode
  arithmetic moved and this is the gate that says it still matches.
- Teacher-forced gate, `--prompt 8192 --prefill 4096`, scored range 4097-8191,
  4095 rows: **`passed: true`, `failed: []`**, `kl_max` 0.0229 against 0.05,
  `top1_rate` 1.0, zero flips. `observed_wmma_full_split` confirms
  `shapes: [2]`, `slice_counts: [16]`, `divided_plans` 20475 = 4095 rows x 5 full
  layers, so the decode shape really did run the new split over the range.
- End-to-end, `scripts/gemma4_campaign_bench.py`, same device and artifact:
  **8192 keys 28.40 -> 28.95 tok/s (1.019x)**, **32768 keys 25.98 -> 26.53
  (1.021x)**. The walk is roughly a third of a decode token at these contexts, so
  a 1.07x-1.16x walk gain lands as ~2% end to end.

## This supersedes the breach analysis

`worklog/entries/20261002T180715.759610Z-lhl-gemma4-gfx1151-decode-crossover-be48cc.md`
records a `kl_max` breach of 0.121 and states that it "cannot be attributed to the
2-row shape or the 64-way split from these runs". It can now: it was the split.
The gate passes with the split fixed and nothing else changed, and the decode
shape's report is bit-identical to the prefill shape's at the same slice count.

## Next

- The decode's attention walk is now at **74.7%** of the roofline at 262,144 keys,
  up from 70.1%, and the prefill's is at 105-118%. The remaining gap is
  unmeasured; the two structural suspects are the decode shape's 64-thread blocks
  (2 warps per block, one block per CU at 32.5 KB of LDS) against the prefill's
  128 threads, and the combine pass.
- The larger remaining decode cost is the **~33.9 ms non-attention floor**, 50% of
  a 262,144-key token. That is the MoE/GEMM path, not attention, and it is now the
  biggest single number on the decode.
- The sliding layers' decode has no split at all. Their walk is ~210 MB of the
  5.58 GB at 262,144 keys, so it is ~4% of the total and was not pursued.
