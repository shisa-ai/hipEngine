# DE-1 mechanism answered: the bandwidth gap is inside the kernels, and the step wall is gap-dominated

## Summary

Addendum 1 left one question open: whether the MoE block's 2.77x bandwidth gap is **launch/tail
overhead** (583 kernels at 42.11 us each) or **per-kernel streaming inefficiency**. It is answerable
from the capture already in the tree, and the answer is **per-kernel** - launch/tail is excluded.

## Changes

- **Addendum 2 appended** to
  `benchmarks/results/2026-09-29-qwen4exp-de1-moe-bandwidth-gate/README.md`.
- **Ledger `DE-1` mechanism note added**; CHANGELOG entry added. No code, kernel, dispatch or default
  changed. **No GPU execution.**

## Method

`x4decode_marker_api_trace.csv` carries the `qwen4exp_decode_live4097_rep{0,1,2}` ROCTX ranges. Using
them to segment `x4decode_kernel_trace.csv` yields **exactly 1,771 dispatches per rep** - matching
the attributed kernels/step - and reproduces the attributed kernel sum.

| rep | kernels | kernel sum | span | total gap | **median gap** | max gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rep0 | 1,771 | 54.721 ms | 227.470 ms | 172.749 ms | **53.60 us** | 7,857.7 us |
| rep1 | 1,771 | 54.671 ms | 225.228 ms | 170.558 ms | **58.46 us** | 1,217.7 us |
| rep2 | 1,771 | 54.173 ms | 81.685 ms | 27.512 ms | **2.28 us** | 514.0 us |

## Result 1 - launch/tail is excluded

**The per-family figures are sums of kernel *durations*** - the 30 families total exactly 54.218 ms -
so **inter-kernel gaps are not in them.** The MoE block's 24.5504 ms is GPU-resident execution time,
not queueing or launch latency.

**Therefore the 2.77x bandwidth gap is inside the kernels, not between them.** `DE-1`'s structural
lever ("the structure, not the dp4a arithmetic") is aimed at the right target, and a screen should
change the *work decomposition* - not add launch batching or graph coverage.

## Result 2 - the step wall is gap-dominated and unstable

The kernel work is **stable across all three reps** (54.72 / 54.67 / 54.17 ms - a **1.0% spread**)
while the step **span** varies **2.8x** (227.5 / 225.2 / 81.7 ms). The wall varies entirely through
inter-kernel gaps, whose **median swings 23x** between reps (53.60 vs 2.28 us).

**A stable kernel sum under an unstable wall is exactly the signature identified in the previous
unit's DE-5 correction** (`benchmarks/results/2026-09-29-qwen4exp-de5-host-overhead-correction/`):
the harness's step wall is not a serving-path number, and the engine's kernel work is what it is.
This is independent corroboration from a different file and a different analysis path.

## Limits

Same profiled capture and the same one-case/one-model/one-host scope as everything above.

**The gap statistics describe this profiled harness, not the engine's steady-state scheduling.** A
median gap of 53.60 us between consecutive kernels is far above a normal device-side
kernel-to-kernel latency, and the three reps differ by 23x, so these gaps are dominated by
harness/profiler interaction rather than by engine behavior. **No gap figure here should be read as
an engine property.**

The conclusion drawn from them is only the **negative** one: gaps are *not* what the MoE block's
24.5504 ms consists of. No positive claim is made about the engine's scheduling quality.

## Validation

- Rep segmentation reproduced the attributed kernels/step **exactly** (1,771 x 3) and the attributed
  kernel sum to within 0.6% (54.173-54.721 vs the published 54.218), which is the check that the
  segmentation is correct rather than merely plausible.
- Gap statistics computed as consecutive-dispatch differences within each rep window.
- MoE host ranges (48 per rep = 48 layers) confirmed, and their total host span (10.477 / 10.690 /
  3.906 ms) is well **below** the 24.5504 ms of attributed GPU time - consistent with GPU execution
  outlasting the host range, i.e. the host ranges are not a proxy for kernel time. This is why the
  segmentation uses the rep markers and not the per-layer MoE ranges.
