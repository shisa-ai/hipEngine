# Predeclare the accuracy recalibration campaign for user decision

## Summary

Wrote `docs/PRODUCTION-ACCURACY-RECALIBRATION-PROPOSAL-2026-09-16.md`, a
predeclared campaign that implements the recommendation in
`docs/PRODUCTION-ACCURACY-POLICY-REVIEW-2026-08-31.md`. It is a proposal for a
user policy decision. It changes no threshold and admits no candidate.

The decision is whether to add a **borderline-review lane** alongside the
existing automatic-admission envelope. The lane requires more evidence rather
than waiving a failure: a candidate entering it stays rejected until the review
completes and a separately frozen policy authorizes a fresh decision.

## Why the decision exists

The current envelope applies the same point limits to scopes of very different
sizes: 450 rows overall, 100-150 per category, and **18 rows** for the prefill
transition. An 18-row mean cannot support a `0.001` mean-KL decision, because
the sampling error of an 18-row mean is comparable to the limit. The gate is
strict as a fixture-acceptance rule and weak as evidence about deployed quality.

That mismatch currently blocks measured performance. The largest directly
measured case is the layer-2 Q5_K grouped-WMMA route: +5.34% on p508 with all 20
category-balanced pairs faster, passing the aggregate envelope, every category,
determinism, state and lifecycle, and failing only the 18-row prefill mean KL at
`0.001179` against `0.001`.

## What is frozen before measuring

- **Controls.** Four from the 2026-08-16 calibration with their labels, plus a
  strict-versus-itself null, plus the layer-2 candidate as the borderline
  control. The calibration set contains one fresh positive; that is recorded as
  a limitation and a second fresh positive must be produced before any threshold
  moves.
- **Metrics.** Mean KL, p95, p99, max, overall and per-scope top-1, row count,
  and a prompt-clustered interval, per scope, all binding together.
- **One decision rule**, applied to every borderline candidate rather than to
  the largest speed win.
- **Sample-size procedure.** Estimate the between-prompt spread of per-prompt
  mean KL on the frozen controls, then choose `n` from a target interval
  half-width. The number is deliberately not fixed here, because fixing it
  before the controls are measured is how a threshold gets chosen to fit a known
  candidate. An illustrative table shows that a 10% half-width on the `0.001`
  limit needs roughly 246 prompts, so the cost of the decision is visible.
- **Prompt-clustered uncertainty** for the mean, with row-level percentiles kept
  as row statements.
- **BF16-relative comparison**, closing the gap the calibration artifact records
  as not re-estimated.
- **Task-grounded checks** for executable code, structured output, multilingual,
  retrieval/long-context and longer generation, each with a predeclared pass
  rule and each reported as unmeasured rather than passing if it cannot reach
  its sample size.
- **Predeclared sampled-generation seeds**, fixed in the artifact before any
  generation runs and shared across candidates.
- **Publish-before-change list**: controls with hashes, the estimated spread and
  implied sample size, every candidate's full metric tuple, task results, seed
  results, keep/reject outcomes, and an explicit statement of anything amended
  after measurement began.

## Sequencing

A staged path is recommended because the expanded scope dominates cost:

1. Measure the spread on the frozen controls. Small, and it decides whether the
   rest is affordable.
2. If affordable, run the expanded prefill scope and the BF16-relative
   comparison on the frozen controls only, not on candidates.
3. Freeze the review rule from those results.
4. Only then run the borderline inventory under the frozen rule.

Steps 1 and 2 are what make step 4 a calibration rather than a search for a
threshold that admits a known candidate.
