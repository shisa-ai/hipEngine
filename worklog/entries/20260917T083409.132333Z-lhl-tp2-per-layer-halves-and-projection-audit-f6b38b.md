# TP2 per-layer split: the deficit is latency-bound small kernels in the attention/GDN half, not the GEMVs

## Summary

The previous entry localized the unexplained milliseconds to a uniform ~12%
per-byte shortfall and called it "shard-route kernel efficiency". This entry
**corrects that**: it is not the GEMV kernels at all. Splitting a layer into its
two captured halves and auditing every projection alone on real weights shows
that every projection already runs at or above the resident route's blended
rate, and that what remains is **3.82 ms/token of latency-bound small-kernel
work in the attention/GDN half** (15.8% of the 24.155 ms wall), replicated on
both ranks.

Two new instruments, both committed here:

- `scripts/tp2_layer_segment_attribution.py` - captures each probe layer as two
  graphs (attention/GDN half; the rest = post-attention norm, D2D input copy, MLP
  shard chain, residual) and replays them in a **DRAM rotation** over eight
  layers, four of each type.
- `scripts/gguf_decode_projection_bandwidth_audit.py` - measures every decode
  projection alone, with the model's real weights through the production launch
  entry points, per launch, same rotation protocol, and records the resolved
  kernel key.

## What this changes

1. **The shard shapes are fine.** Every projection is at 516-694 GB/s (W7900) /
   532-813 GB/s (XTX), at or above the resident route's blended 529.3 / 637.7
   GB/s. The previous entry's "both ranks run ~12% below their own resident
   per-byte efficiency" is true as an aggregate and misleading as a diagnosis:
   the blended comparison mixes the MLP half (which is fast) with the attention
   half (which is latency-bound), and TP2's mix has more of the latter.
2. **The attention half is latency-bound.** Subtracting the measured projections
   from the attention halves leaves **89 us per full-attention layer** and
   **50 us per GDN layer**: input norm, head norm + RoPE, KV write, attention
   core + gate multiply, alpha/beta, conv, GDN recurrence. That is
   **3.82 ms/token** across 16 + 48 layers, and it is replicated work, so
   sharding attention/GDN weights would not remove it.
3. **The worst projection is the smallest.** The `attn_k`/`attn_v` pair moves
   7.2 MB in 45.8 us because a pair launch at `out=1024` is fixed-cost-bound;
   `launch_gguf_linear_triple` exists but declines Q4_K (it requires
   `gguf_q8_0_t16_v1` on all three weights), so every full-attention layer pays
   two launches where one would do. 16 layers x ~30 us recoverable ~= 0.5 ms.
4. **The 19.629 ms traffic-implied floor is not a kernel-tuning target.** It
   assumes every byte moves at the resident *blended* rate; the resident route
   reaches that rate only because 61.7% of its bytes are MLP (TP2's mix is 49.9%
   MLP, 45.1% replicated attention/GDN). The floor is a lower bound on what a
   *further byte reduction* could buy, not on what kernel work could buy.

## Measurement notes (what the instruments can and cannot say)

- **The rotation is load-bearing.** One layer's weights (61-87 MB per half) fit
  gfx1100's 96 MB Infinity Cache. A single-layer replay of the MLP half measured
  748 GB/s (an L2 rate); the same half in a rotation measures 493-504 GB/s. All
  numbers here are rotation numbers.
- **Isolated tiny-kernel times are host-submission-bound upper bounds.** The
  norms measure 20-24 us alone because the queue is host-bound for a 24 us
  launch (the TP1 eager arm's measured ~23 us per launch); in a captured graph
  the same kernels are graph nodes. The 3.82 ms/token figure therefore comes
  from *subtracting measured projections from measured graphed halves*, not from
  summing isolated small-kernel times.
- **The eager per-segment split is indicative only.** It repeats one layer (L2
  resident) and pays a submission gap per launch; its sum runs 19-50% above the
  captured-layer replay. It is retained for the intra-attention ordering
  (`qkv_head_norm_rope` ~59% of the full-attention half, `output` ~15%).
- **The whole-layer graphed replay in this script is not a layer wall.** It
  launches both ranks back to back on an otherwise idle device, so the in-graph
  exchange spin pays a full round trip the real pipeline hides (0.55-0.74 ms vs
  the real 0.345-0.350). The real loop's per-layer spans remain the wall
  reference.

## Next

1. **A Q4_K `q`+`k`+`v` triple decode fusion** (the Q8_0/T16 triple declines
   Q4_K today). Measured headroom: ~30 us in each of the 16 full-attention
   layers, ~0.5 ms/token. Needs the usual kernel gate: strict/parent-parity RED
   test, CPU-reference outer gate, `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`, registered strict
   fallback, and a shape contract that admits `(1, 5120, 12288)` plus
   `(1, 5120, 1024)` twice.
2. **Cut the attention half's launch count** (89 us/FA layer, 50 us/GDN layer):
   fuse the input norm into the q/k/v launch chain, fuse head-norm + RoPE + KV
   write, and fuse alpha/beta + conv + recurrence + norm on the GDN side. This is
   the largest remaining term and it helps the resident route equally.
3. **Then** revisit the full degree-2 shard plan (attention head sharding +
   GDN group/state sharding): it cuts replicated bytes by ~28-36% but leaves the
   latency term, so its payoff is the byte part only - worth doing after, not
   before, the fusions.
