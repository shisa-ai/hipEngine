# the attention prefill kernel is neither traffic- nor compute-bound; 75 percent is per-key loop machinery

## Summary

The 30 attention layers cost **128.8 ms at pp512** (measured, `--tokens 512 --keys 512`). Three
ablations say what that time is, and none of the obvious answers holds.

| ablation | ms | reading |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| bf16, keep-mask | 128.8 | baseline |
| f32, keep-mask | 138.0 | **ratio 1.07** |
| bf16, zero-mask | 97.1 | **removes 31.7 ms, 25 percent** |

The bench documents the dtype ablation's own reading: *"A time ratio near 2.0 means the kernel
is paying for bytes; near 1.0 means it is not."* **1.07 is not paying for bytes.** Doubling
every K/V byte costs 7 percent.

The throughput agrees from the other side: **557 GFLOP/s**. On this part that is under one
percent of bf16 peak, so the kernel is not compute-bound either.

**`--mask zero` runs the same shapes with every K/V load skipped and leaves the key loop
intact.** It removes 25 percent, so the load *and* the dot it feeds are a quarter of the time
and **the remaining 75 percent is the per-key machinery around them**: the mask test, the LDS
write of the logit, the block max, the softmax rescale, and the loop's own scalar work.

## Cost tracks the key loop, not the grid

`--keys` scales only the walk while `--tokens` scales both, so running `--tokens 512` across
key lengths separates them:

| keys | 30 layers | delta |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 128 | 52.9 ms | -- |
| 256 | 81.4 ms | +28.5 |
| 512 | 131.7 ms | +50.3 |

Roughly 26 ms per 128 keys, against a **fixed cost near 26 ms** at the extrapolated key-zero
intercept. At pp512 the loop is about 80 percent of the time. **So the grid is not the lever**,
and query tiling -- which would cut the K/V load, the 25 percent -- is capped at about 1.3x
even if the re-read went to zero.

This is consistent with `20260928T130200`, which found the pass-1 loop body at **400
instructions for roughly 55 operations of source work** and concluded the loop is
vector-dominated rather than scalar-dominated. A seven-fold instruction expansion per key is
the machinery this ablation is measuring.

## What this means for the prefill gap

Prefill is 0.65 s against llama.cpp's 0.563 s, so the remaining gap is 1.15x. Attention is
128.8 ms of that 0.65 s -- **20 percent**. A perfect attention kernel would reach 0.52 s, only
just past llama.cpp, so **the last 1.15x cannot be closed by attention alone**: the dense GEMM
(189.8 ms) and the expert MMQ kernels (260.4 ms) are the larger terms and both have their own
recorded ceilings.

## Next

- **The lever is the per-key instruction count, not the grid and not the bytes.** The strict
  kernel makes four LDS accesses per key visit (write the logit, read it for the max, write
  the weight back over it, read the weight for the PV accumulation); the tiled variant makes
  two, which is exactly the right direction, but it was measured 1.44x slower at 2048 on its
  other overheads. **A tiled variant whose only change is the LDS traffic is the unit to
  write**, and `20260928T130200`'s `.loc` attribution gives it a two-sided instrument.
- **Do not pursue query tiling as a traffic fix.** The dtype ablation caps its value at the
  25 percent the load represents.
