# the existing hipEngine WMMA route is the fastest mode, and the t16 repack is not worth doing

**This entry corrects the WMMA closure in
`20260928T160000.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-q4k-variant-screen-9c1e5a.md` and supersedes the
candidate scoped in `20260928T162000.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-t16-route-scope-4f2d8b.md`.**

## Summary

The screen in `20260928T160000` measured five harness modes and concluded the t16 variant
of the MMQ32 design had 13 percent available and that "the WMMA direction is closed for
this shape". Adding the harness's `selected-wmma` mode -- **which its own docstring
describes as "the current hipEngine Q4T16 selected-dual WMMA prefill", not a prototype**
-- changes both conclusions.

On one fixture, 128 experts, 32 rows per expert, hidden 2816:

| mode | ms | TFLOPS | TMAC/s | what it is |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **`selected-wmma`** | **18.692** | **6.79** | **3.40** | **a current hipEngine kernel** |
| `q8-1-ds4-t16-mmq32` | 19.264 | 6.59 | 3.30 | prototype |
| `q8-1-ds4-mmq32` | 22.391 | 5.67 | 2.84 | prototype |
| production gate/up, in situ | -- | -- | **3.00** | 470 G MACs / 156.537 ms |

**The fastest thing on the fixture is a kernel hipEngine already has.** Both MMQ modes --
including the t16 one the previous two entries built a candidate around -- are slower than
it. The production gate/up route's in-situ rate of 3.00 TMAC/s sits below `selected-wmma`'s
3.40 and above the plain prototype's 2.84.

## The correction

**`20260928T160000` says "the WMMA direction is closed for this shape: three modes are 1.5x
to 2.8x slower than the MMQ32 prototype on the same fixture".** That is true of the
harness's *WMMA prototypes* -- `q8-1-ds4-wmma32`, `q8-1-ds4-wmma64`, `q8-1-ds4-wmma32-lds`
at 3.80, 3.83 and 2.01 TFLOPS -- and it does **not** generalise to hipEngine's WMMA route,
which is a different implementation and the fastest mode measured. The entry named the
prototypes and then drew a conclusion about the direction; the distinction it needed is
the one this entry draws, between a prototype of a design and a shipped kernel of it.

The instruction-mix data from `20260928T154000` is consistent and was misread the same
way: the registered WMMA *kernels* in `gguf_q4_k_q8_1_selected_prefill.hip` have no dp4a
and 250-plus bit-field instructions, which is why the prototypes are slow -- but
hipEngine's `selected-wmma` route is not one of those, and its 3.40 TMAC/s is measured,
not inferred.

## The candidate that survives, and the one that does not

**The T16 repack does not.** `20260928T162000` scoped it as a session-scale unit -- repack
the expert weights into 16-wide output tiles, add a loader path, wire the selection,
validate against the strict owner -- on a 13 percent margin over the *plain prototype*.
Against `selected-wmma` that margin does not exist: 3.30 against 3.40. **A session-scale
repack to reach a rate a shipped kernel already exceeds is not worth doing**, and the
candidate is closed before any of it was started.

**What survives is a route comparison, not a repack.** hipEngine already has a WMMA
gate/up route at 3.40 TMAC/s on this fixture and runs the MMQ32 route at 3.00 in situ.
If that ordering holds on the production geometry, the gap is **13 percent of 156.5 ms =
20 ms of a 687.7 ms prefill, 3.0 percent** -- the same number the repack was scoped
around, for a route selection instead of a repack.

**But the two numbers are still from different fixtures.** 3.40 is on a 5504-wide fixture
and 3.00 is in situ at 1408 wide. The harness cannot be reduced to the production width
(`--out-features-b 0` is rejected), so the next measurement has to run the *production
symbol* on the harness fixture, or run the WMMA route in situ. Either one is a same-shape
comparison; neither has been done.
