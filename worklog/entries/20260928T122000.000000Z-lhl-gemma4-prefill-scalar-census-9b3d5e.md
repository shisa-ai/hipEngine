# the expert MMQ32 kernel is the prefill's largest scalar consumer, and the WMMA kernels show the overhead is avoidable

## Summary

The attention kernel is scalar-issue-bound, but attention is only 19.2 percent of
prefill kernel time at 512 tokens while the expert block is 46.8 percent. So the
question worth answering is whether the diagnosis generalises, and a per-kernel
scalar census of the whole production prefill answers it.

`rocprofv3` with `SQ_INSTS_SALU` and `SQ_INSTS_VALU`, no kernel filter, on
`scripts/gemma4_campaign_bench.py --prompt 512`, which is the verify metric's
shape. Summed over every dispatch in the run:

| kernel | SALU | VALU | SALU/VALU |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_fused_prefill_compact32_kernel` | **6.945e10** | 1.061e11 | **0.65** |
| `gemma4_attention_prefill_kernel` | 1.115e10 | 1.514e10 | 0.74 |
| `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel` | 1.051e10 | 2.728e10 | 0.39 |
| `gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel` | 8.682e9 | 1.630e11 | **0.05** |
| `gemma4_attention_decode_class_kernel` | 7.932e9 | 1.574e10 | 0.50 |
| `qwen35_router_logits_token_tile_kernel` | 1.372e9 | 1.684e9 | 0.81 |
| `gguf_q8_1_mmq_ds4_pack_bf16_kernel` | 1.148e9 | 1.957e9 | 0.59 |
| `gguf_q5_k_selected_dual_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill_kernel` | 5.818e8 | 8.949e9 | **0.07** |
| `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel` | 2.978e8 | 1.568e10 | **0.02** |

### The finding is the split, not any single row

**The MMQ (dp4a) kernels issue scalar instructions at 0.39 to 0.74 of their VALU
rate. The WMMA kernels issue them at 0.02 to 0.07.** That is a factor of ten to
thirty, across kernels doing the same job on the same data at the same shape, and
it says the scalar stream in the MMQ family is an artifact of how those kernels are
written rather than something the algorithm requires. `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel`
moves 1.568e10 VALU with 2.978e8 scalar instructions; the MMQ32 expert kernel moves
1.061e11 VALU with 6.945e10.

**And the largest absolute scalar consumer in the prefill is the expert gate/up
MMQ32 kernel, at 6.2 times the attention kernel's scalar stream** -- on a kernel
that is 46.8 percent of prefill kernel time at this shape, against attention's
19.2. The attention work recorded earlier in
`20260928T121300.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-attention-scalar-bound-7f2b1a.md` was aimed at
the smaller of the two terms.

### One number in this entry is not resolved, and it matters

The scalar unit's issue rate is the conversion between these counts and time, and
the counts do not reconcile with the measured durations at a rate of one scalar
instruction per cycle per CU.

Taking that rate on 32 CUs at 2 GHz gives 64e9 scalar instructions per second. The
attention kernel's 1.115e10 would then be 174 ms of scalar issue against a measured
132.07 ms -- already more than the kernel takes -- and the expert MMQ32 kernel's
6.945e10 would be 1085 ms against roughly 180 ms of measured gate/up time. Both are
impossible, so the constant is wrong and no share is quoted from it here.

The ratios above are unaffected: they are counter-to-counter within one run, so any
global replay factor cancels. The absolute magnitudes are also unaffected in their
*ordering*, which is what the finding rests on -- the expert MMQ32 kernel is the
largest scalar consumer in the prefill whichever constant converts it to time.

Two things could explain the mismatch and neither is measured: the scalar unit may
issue more than one instruction per cycle per CU on this part, and `rocprofv3`'s
counter collection may replay a dispatch more than once. **Settling it needs a
kernel whose scalar count can be predicted from its own source**, which is a
bounded piece of work and worth doing before anyone quotes a scalar share of the
prefill from these numbers.

## Next

- **Point the scalar lever at the expert gate/up MMQ32 kernel, not at attention.**
  It is the largest scalar consumer in the prefill and the largest kernel term at
  the verify shape. The WMMA rows are the existence proof that the overhead is not
  inherent: a kernel doing comparable work at this shape issues scalar instructions
  at a twentieth of the rate.
- The tree already has a measured 1.13x on this kernel's body from the T16 resident
  layout, recorded as a decision and not yet landed. Combining that with a scalar
  reduction is the natural unit, and the `SQ_INSTS_SALU` count is the mechanism
  check that says whether a change to the kernel is touching the scalar stream at
  all -- which is exactly what the two inert row-pointer hoists on attention
  failed.
- Resolve the issue-rate question first, with a kernel whose scalar instruction
  count follows from its source. Until then, use `SQ_INSTS_SALU` as a *relative*
  instrument: a change that does not move it has not touched the scalar stream, and
  that verdict does not need the constant.
