# VibeVoice-TTS diffusion-head fusion and benchmark device identity

## Summary

Two changes landed together in commit `bc581e8a7` and this entry is written
after the fact to close its bookkeeping gap. First, two bit-exact fusions in the
diffusion head. Second, a correction to the benchmark artifact's device
identity: the harness had hardcoded `W7900`/`gfx1100`, but the physical host is
an `AMD RYZEN AI MAX+ PRO 395 w/ Radeon 8060S` (`gfx1151`).

The fusion result is **negative on the metric it targeted**: it is RTF-neutral.
That is the substantive finding, and it redirects the next optimization step.

## Changes

- `kernels/hip_gfx1100/vibevoice/diffusion.hip` / `diffusion.py`: new
  `vv_diff_rmsnorm_modulate_bf16`, rmsnorm plus adaLN modulate in one launch.
  The double-rounding sequence is preserved, including the intermediate rounded
  `normed` value: dropping that round broke two-fixture replay parity at 0.0504
  against a 0.05 gate.
- `runtime/vibevoice_tts_diffusion.py`: the per-layer rmsnorm + modulate pair
  becomes one launch, and the gate/up GEMV pair becomes one
  `dense_dual_gemv_separate_out_bf16` launch. Net effect: about 9 launches
  removed per diffusion call out of roughly 35k per synthesis.
- `scripts/vibevoice_tts_session_bench.py`: the GPU name now comes from
  `rocminfo` instead of a hardcoded string.
- `benchmarks/results/vibevoice_tts_session_baseline.json`: regenerated with the
  corrected host/GPU identity.

## Validation

- Correctness: the fused path keeps the 27-token constrained chain exact and
  holds two-fixture replay parity under the 0.05 gate; the 0.0504 measurement
  above is what rejected the dropped intermediate round.
- Performance, host `zbook`, GPU `AMD RYZEN AI MAX+ PRO 395 w/ Radeon 8060S`
  (gfx1151), command `uv run python scripts/vibevoice_tts_session_bench.py`:
  warm synthesis 7.743 s, pooled RTF 2.323 after the fusion versus 2.332 before.
  That is a 0.4% change, inside the noise of the metric.
- Interpretation: removing 9 launches of roughly 35k moved pooled RTF by less
  than 0.5%. Per-step D2H serialization and small-kernel GPU execution dominate
  the diffusion stage, so launch-count reduction is not the lever here.

## Next

- Target step-batching rather than further kernel fusion: the measured
  bottleneck is per-step host/device round-trips, not launch count.
- The device identity in this entry is `gfx1151`, not the repository default
  `gfx1100`/W7900. Any RTF comparison against a W7900 lane would be invalid.
- The torch-lane RTF figure quoted in the commit message had no committed
  harness or artifact at the time; committing it is tracked separately.
