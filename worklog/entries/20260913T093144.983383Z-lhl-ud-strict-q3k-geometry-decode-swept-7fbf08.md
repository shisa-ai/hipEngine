# The strict Q3_K/IQ3_S owner is not short of parallelism, decode, or tiling

## Summary

`gguf_iq_dense_strict` is the only owner for the eleven Q3_K/IQ3_S tensors in
UD-Q4_K_M. Every one of them is 38.30 MB and either (17408, 5120) or
(5120, 17408), so the owner moves 421.3 MB/step in eleven calls. The
2026-09-13 verifier-window census puts those calls at 258.01 us/call for
`gguf_iq_dense_strict_kernel<3, true, 8, 4>` (Q3_K, 96 VGPRs, 105 calls) and
361.33 us/call for `<2, true, 8, 4>` (IQ3_S, 112 VGPRs, 60 calls), i.e.
106-148 GB/s. The t16 rowtile owners reach 600-704 GB/s on the same shapes
and the same byte counts, so the gap is structural to the owner family, not
a per-shape effect.

This unit swept every launch-geometry and scheduling knob that leaves the
per-(row, column) arithmetic untouched, and then bisected the decode by
mutation. **No candidate was retained; the shipped configuration was already
the best of every axis measured, and the residual is not in the decode.**
The value is negative evidence: it closes four hypotheses with measurements
and localizes the remaining 63% floor.

## Changes

None retained. All measurements were taken against a scratch copy of
`hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/quant/gguf_iq_dense.hip` restored byte-for-byte
afterwards (`git diff --stat` empty, verified). The scratch harness is
`/tmp/phase8/strictbench.py`, which reads the real `blk.0.ffn_up.weight`
(Q3_K, 38297600 bytes) and `blk.13.ffn_down.weight` (Q3_K) payloads straight
out of `/models/gguf/Qwen3.8-27B-UD-Q4_K_M.gguf` at their GGUF data offsets
and times `D.launch(..., quant='gguf_q3_k', output='bf16', row_batch=rb)` at
rows=3, best of 5 x 50 reps after 5 warmup launches.

## Validation

Harness command (all runs):

```
HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 /home/lhl/hipEngine-main/.venv/bin/python /tmp/phase8/strictbench.py 3
```

Isolated baseline reproduces the in-situ census: `blk.0.ffn_up.weight`
221.29 us (173.1 GB/s) and `blk.13.ffn_down.weight` 249.06 us (153.8 GB/s) at
R=4, against 258.01 us in the census window.

**Axis 1, column tile T** (`constexpr int T` in `run_rows`, line 351).
T is a pure tiling parameter: the per-(row, column) k ownership
(`k = rbase*256 + h*128 + tid`), the FMA order, the wave32 shuffle tree and
the four serial wave sums are all independent of which columns share a block,
so every T is bit-identical to T=8.

| T | ffn_up us | ffn_down us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 241.61 | 302.03 |
| 8 (shipped) | 221.29 | 249.06 |
| 16 | 297.34 | 317.79 |

More blocks (T=4 gives 4352 instead of 2176) is worse, so the owner is not
short of blocks.

**Axis 2, row slab R** (`row_batch`, the shipped `_row_batch(3) == 4`):

| R | ffn_up us | ffn_down us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 522.01 | 510.97 |
| 2 | 363.07 | 375.03 |
| 4 (shipped) | 221.29 | 249.06 |
| 8 | 303.35 | 370.22 |

R=1 moves 3x the weight bytes (one pass per row) in 2.36x the time, so the
R=1 arm runs at 220 GB/s against R=4's 173 GB/s: the row tile is a byte-count
win, not an efficiency win, and R=4 is still the right choice.

**Axis 3, phase-A/B round depth RB** (`constexpr int RB`, line 165): RB=2
217.71/252.49 us, RB=4 (shipped) 220.85/248.86 us, RB=8 248.34/278.20 us.
RB=2 and RB=4 are a tie inside run-to-run noise; RB=8 is worse.

**Axis 4, occupancy floor** (`__launch_bounds__(128, R == 1 ? 16 : 2)` ->
`(128, R == 1 ? 16 : 8)`, which asks the compiler for at most 64 VGPRs):
220.85/248.86 us, identical to shipped. Either the compiler did not comply or
occupancy is not the binding constraint; no change retained.

**Mutation bisection of the Q3_K decode** (`else` branch of the phase-B body,
the `const int kk = k & 255;` block). Each mutation was built and measured in
the same harness:

| mutation | ffn_up us | ffn_down us | implied GB/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| full decode (baseline) | 220.85 | 248.86 | 173 / 154 |
| `q = 0` (no payload loads, no bit ALU) | 138.50 | 145.06 | 276 / 264 |
| one payload byte load, `q = code_byte`, no bit ALU, no hmask load | 195.55 | 202.35 | 196 / 189 |

So the two per-element payload byte loads cost 82 us (37% of the shipped
time) while the whole bit arithmetic plus the hmask load costs only 25 us.
The decode is a **memory-access** cost, not an ALU cost, and removing it
entirely still leaves 138.50 us -- a floor that is 63% of the shipped time
and only 276 GB/s.

That floor is 1.4 waves: 2176 blocks x 128 threads = 278528 threads against
96 CUs, and the block count is fixed by N/T with T already optimal. At that
occupancy the load-use latency of a fresh 110-byte quant block per
(bi, column) is exposed with almost no other work to cover it, which is why
neither a cheaper decode nor a finer tile helps. The t16 rowtile owners do
not have this problem because they use 32-thread blocks and aligned
word-wide weight reads with each lane owning 8 contiguous k; the strict
owner's declared contract (128 strided accumulators over `k = tid + 128*i`)
forces one scalar byte per lane per payload element.

## Next

Closing the strict owner's 106-148 GB/s gap needs a change that adds
concurrency along k or removes the scalar byte loads, and both change the
declared accumulation order:

1. **Split-K** over the 20 quant blocks per column, which multiplies the
   block count by the split factor and is the same lever the plan already
   names for the Q5_K rowtile. It needs a new bit-exactness contract plus the
   full production profile gate.
2. **A tile repack** of Q3_K/IQ3_S payload bytes, matching the IQ4_XS item
   above it in `docs/UD-GFX1151-OPTIMIZE.md`, at the cost of the repacked
   resident footprint.

Do not re-run the T/R/RB/launch_bounds sweeps: they are measured and the
shipped values win. `docs/UD-GFX1151-OPTIMIZE.md` now carries this bullet.
