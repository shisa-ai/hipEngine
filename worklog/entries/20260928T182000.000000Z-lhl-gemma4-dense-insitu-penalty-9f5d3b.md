# the dense in-situ penalty is 19 to 23 percent and it is not the estimator

## Summary

`20260928T180000` found every dense shape 10 to 31 percent slower in situ than in the
isolated sweep and left two explanations open: the best-of-8 statistic, and the
environment. **A new isolated harness that reports the mean as well as the best separates
them.** The statistic is worth 6 to 11 percent and the environment is worth **19 to 23
percent, uniformly across all three shapes.** The environment is the larger lever and it is
about **38 ms of the dense term's 189.8 ms, 5.5 percent of the prefill.**

| shape, rows = 512, tile `(32, 64)` | isolated **mean** | isolated best | in situ mean | quoted sweep best-of-8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `4096 -> 2816` | **1.507** | 1.363 | 1.792 | 1.482 |
| `2816 -> 4096` | **1.030** | 0.963 | 1.229 | 0.939 |
| `2816 -> 2112` | **0.479** | 0.450 | 0.587 | 0.534 |

## The estimator is not the explanation

`mean / best` is **1.105, 1.070, 1.064** -- 6 to 11 percent, not the 10 to 31 percent the
gap needed. And the isolated means reproduce the numbers quoted in
`gguf_q8_0_prefill.py`'s docstring (1.507 against 1.482, 1.030 against 0.939, 0.479
against 0.534), so the sweep is reproducible and the quoted figures were not unusually
optimistic.

**The gap decomposes as: best -> mean, 6 to 11 percent; isolated mean -> in situ mean, 19
to 23 percent.** Both effects are real and they are additive.

## The environment penalty is uniform

```
4096 -> 2816   1.507 -> 1.792   +18.9%
2816 -> 4096   1.030 -> 1.229   +19.3%
2816 -> 2112   0.479 -> 0.587   +22.5%
```

**19 to 23 percent on three shapes of three different geometries, none of them exempt.**
That uniformity is the informative part: a shape-specific cause would not hit all three by
the same factor. A uniform factor points at a resource the shapes share -- L2 capacity or
bandwidth, DRAM bandwidth, or clock state -- and the isolated harness by construction has
none of the other kernels resident that the in-situ one does.

## The orientation effect is intrinsic

The isolated run reproduces it exactly: `4096 -> 2816` at 7.84 TFLOP/s against
`2816 -> 4096` at 11.46, a ratio of **1.46**, against 1.46 in situ. So the orientation
effect is a property of the shape that survives a change of environment, while the
environment penalty is a property of the environment that survives a change of shape. The
two are independent and they were being read as one number.

## The two prizes, separated

| effect | scope | size |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| orientation, grid shape | 64.9 ms of the dense term, three shapes | ~22 ms |
| **environment** | **all 189.8 ms of the dense term** | **~38 ms** |

**The environment is the larger and the broader of the two**, and unlike the orientation
effect it is not confined to the shapes with `in / out >= 1.45`.

## Next

- **Identify what the environment costs.** The penalty is uniform at ~20 percent, so the
  experiment is to add one resident kernel at a time to the isolated harness and see which
  one reproduces it. The candidates in launch order are the expert gate/up and down MMQ
  routes, the attention kernel, and the MoE elementwise; the expert routes are the ones
  streaming weights at 49 GB/s and so the ones most likely to be taking the bandwidth.
- **A cheaper first cut: measure the achieved DRAM bandwidth during the in-situ dense
  shapes.** If the dense shapes are bandwidth-starved in situ, the penalty is contention
  and the lever is scheduling; if they are not, the penalty is latency or clock state and
  the lever is elsewhere.
- **Do not re-derive the orientation effect or the tile.** Both are measured and closed.
