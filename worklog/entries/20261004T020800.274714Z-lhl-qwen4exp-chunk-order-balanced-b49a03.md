# Order-balanced chunk A/B refutes the p512 regression: +18.1% at p4096, no short-prompt cost

## Summary

The full-suite run (`20261004T014918`) reported a shape-dependent result: +15.6% at
p4096 but **-4.1% at p512**, and I attributed the p512 loss to a fixed allocation cost
of the larger chunk. **That attribution was wrong, and so was the loss.**

That suite measured chunk 512 for its entire first arm and chunk 2048 for its entire
second arm, which confounds the arm with its position in the session. Re-running with
**interleaved, order-balanced arms** (512, 2048, 512, 2048, fresh process each time)
gives a different answer:

| shape | chunk 512 | chunk 2048 | prefill delta | decode delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **p512** (control) | 297.03 | 299.08 | **+0.7%** | **0.0%** |
| **p4096** | 261.516 | 308.899 | **+18.1%** | **-5.5%** |

**There is no short-prompt regression.** At p512 the 2048 configuration is marginally
*faster*, and decode is unchanged to three decimals (20.354 vs 20.355).

The p4096 gain is **larger** than the sequential suite showed: **+18.1%**, reproducing
the original single-case ladder's +18.4% under order balancing. The composite is
**-9.1%** (22.179 -> 20.156 s) even counting the decode regression.

## The p512 case is a control, and it is what makes this interpretable

A 512-token prompt fits in a single prefill pass at either setting, so the
partitioning is **identical** and the only differences are allocations sized by the
configured chunk. That makes p512 the clean discriminator between the two candidate
mechanisms, and it says the following:

- **The decode regression is present exactly where the partitioning changes** (8
  passes to 2 at p4096, -5.5%) **and absent exactly where it does not** (1 pass to 1
  at p512, 0.0%). That is consistent with a KV-write-pattern or prefill-state effect.
- **The fixed-allocation hypothesis is not confirmed.** I had already sized the
  chunk-scaled allocations and they are too small to matter: the PLE staging buffer is
  `2 * (chunk * 16) * 160 * 4` bytes, i.e. **10 MiB at chunk 512 and 40 MiB at 2048**,
  a 30 MiB delta, and the scratch reservation has a **4 GiB floor** the chunk-scaled
  terms may not even reach. The p512 result now confirms that. **The allocation
  explanation should not be repeated as though it were established.**

## Methodological lesson worth keeping

The spurious regression was **negative in all four p512 cases** (-4.0% to -5.3%) and
still did not reproduce. Per-case consistency is not evidence against an arm-level
confound. Only order balancing is. A partitioning parameter measured with one arm
occupying the whole first half of a session and the other the whole second half can
produce a confident, sign-uniform, entirely spurious result.

## Validation

- Four fresh process invocations per shape, alternating 512, 2048, 512, 2048, so
  neither arm occupies only one session position. 1 warmup + 3 measured repetitions
  inside each invocation, 128 decode transitions.
- Within-run spreads are tight (CV about 0.4-0.7% at p4096), and the between-arm
  deltas are far larger than the within-run spread.
- The p4096 result reproduces the earlier ladder's independent +18.4%, which was
  measured in a different session with a different arm order.
- Cross-arm bit-identity is established for `code-p4096` (129/129 across chunks
  512/1024/2048, ladder artifact). Every run here is internally deterministic.

## Next

- **The -5.5% decode regression at p4096 is the remaining blocker** and it is now
  better characterized: it follows the prefill partition, not the configured chunk.
  Decode is a first-class target of the same objective, so it must be resolved or
  bounded before promotion. The p512 control gives the discriminating test - if the
  regression tracks the number of prefill passes rather than the chunk value, a
  configuration that keeps the pass count low without changing the KV write order
  should avoid it.
- **The other ten canonical cases were not re-measured under order balancing.** The
  sequential suite's per-category numbers for them remain unreliable and are not
  superseded by this artifact. A promotion decision needs them re-measured properly.
- **Promotion of chunk 2048 is now supported at p4096 and neutral at p512**, so the
  earlier shape-adaptive-only recommendation is no longer forced by the evidence. The
  dead-code finding stands unchanged: the profile's intended 1024 never binds, and
  the harness default of 512 is what has been measured all along.
