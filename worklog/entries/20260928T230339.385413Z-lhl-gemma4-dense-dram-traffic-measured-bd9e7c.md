# the 127 GB/s dense plateau was an artifact of a 2x tile error, and the real traffic spans 24x

## Summary

`20260928T172000` and the entry it fed attributed the prefill residual to the dense
GEMMs moving weight at a "127 GB/s plateau", against the lm head's 223.5 GB/s.
**Both numbers in that comparison are wrong and the conclusion does not survive a
measurement.** The traffic was never measured; it was inferred as `16 x weight_bytes`
and divided by time.

The multiplier is **8, not 16**. `gguf_q8_0_prefill.hip:106-107` defines
`AWQ_TILE_M` as "TM rows of out_features" and `AWQ_TILE_N` as "TN rows of `rows`
(token rows / activation rows)", and line 128 is `row_start = row_tile *
AWQ_TILE_N`. The row dimension is tiled by `tile_n` (64), not `tile_m` (32), so
rows=512 is 512/64 = **8 row-tiles**. The profiler's own grid size confirms it:
704 blocks at K=4096 is 88 out-tiles x 8 row-tiles. The write-up divided rows by
the out_features tile.

And the true multiplier is not constant anyway. Measured with
`rocprofv3 --pmc GL2C_EA_RDREQ_DRAM_sum` (L2 to memory read requests, 128 B
each), rows=512, tile (32, 64), 704 blocks, n=8:

| K | weight | 16xW claim | measured DRAM read | meas/weight | DRAM rate |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 256 | 0.77 MB | 12.3 MB | **1.06** MB | 1.4x | 10.9 GB/s |
| 2048 | 6.13 MB | 98.0 MB | **32.1** MB | 5.2x | 40.5 GB/s |
| 2816 | 8.43 MB | 134.8 MB | **16.5** MB | 2.0x | 21.2 GB/s |
| 4096 | 12.26 MB | 196.1 MB | **221.9** MB | 18.1x | 145.9 GB/s |
| 8192 | 24.51 MB | 392.2 MB | **787.9** MB | 32.2x | 257.4 GB/s |

The measured rate spans **10.9 to 257.4 GB/s, a 24x range**. There is no plateau.
The 16x prediction is 11.6x too high at K=256 and 8.1x too high at K=2816, and
0.88x and 0.50x too *low* at K=4096 and K=8192.

## Changes

### The mechanism is scheduling, not shape

Two knobs move the traffic, and both show that whether a re-read reaches memory
depends on the grid, not on the shape:

- **row-tiles** at K=4096, rows=512: 1207 MB at 32 row-tiles, 385 MB at 16, 222
  MB at 8.
- **out-tiles** at K=4096, rows=512: 12.2 MB at 352 blocks, 25.3 at 440, 52.0 at
  528, 149.5 at 616, 221.9 at 704. **At 352 blocks the traffic is exactly the
  unique bytes** -- activations once plus one weight slab per out-tile, the 8
  row-tile blocks sharing that slab perfectly through L2. The cliff is where the
  grid exceeds the resident block window, which for this kernel lies between 440
  and 528 blocks.

**And at the shipped grid the dominant term is the activation re-read, not the
weight re-read.** At rows=512, K=4096 the weight term is 8 x 12.26 = 98 MB while
the activation term is 88 out-tiles x 4.19 = 369 MB. At rows=1024 the measured
836 MB is about 90 percent of the 934 MB total demand, of which 738 MB is
activations and 196 MB is weights. **Attributing this residual to weight traffic
names the smaller term.**

### The counter was validated before it was trusted

Four cold-streaming cases where the answer is known by construction:

| case | expected | measured | error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| GEMM rows=16 K=4096 (weight + act) | 12.380 MB | 12.387 MB | +0.06% |
| GEMM out=32 K=4096 (act + weight) | 4.333 MB | 4.334 MB | +0.02% |
| T16 GEMV 4096x2816 (matrix) | 12.255 MB | 12.264 MB | +0.07% |
| T16 GEMV 2816x2816 (matrix) | 8.425 MB | 8.431 MB | +0.07% |

Corroborated by an independent memory-side counter block
(`GCEA_RDRAM_REQ_PER_CLIGRP_sum`) tracking it at a constant 25.6 B/request.

**Caveat that makes the refutation conservative**: GL2C counters sit *above* the
32 MiB MALL, so these are an upper bound on true DRAM reads. I checked whether the
MALL absorbs this traffic rather than assuming: cycling a 12.255 MB matrix through
a 24.5 MB pool that fits inside the MALL still reports 12.264 MB per launch and
takes the same time as a 74 MB pool (84.70 against 84.75 us), so for these
streaming reads the MALL is not serving them.

I also tested my own first hypothesis and it was wrong: the weights (0.77 to 24.51
MB) all fit in the 32 MiB MALL, so I expected the re-reads to be absorbed. They
are not.

### What survives, and it is not nothing

- **K=4096 and K=8192 do move real DRAM traffic**, and the implied rates are 146
  and 257 GB/s against this part's 256 GB/s LPDDR5X peak -- 57 percent and 100
  percent. **Those two shapes plausibly are memory-bound, for a different reason
  and with a different number than the plateau claim.** They are also the shapes
  the model's `o_proj` uses: `num_heads * head_dim` is 4096 on the sliding layers
  and 8192 on the full ones.
- **K=2816's speed anomaly now has a correlate**: it is the lowest-traffic shape
  (16.5 MB, 2.0x weight) as well as the fastest (10.4 TFLOP/s). The earlier
  objection -- "K=2048's weight is smaller and slower" -- does not apply, because
  traffic is not a function of weight size. K=2048 moves 32.1 MB to K=2816's 16.5.
- The model's `q`/`k`/`v` and expert gate/up projections are K=2816, the
  *lowest*-traffic shape at 21.2 GB/s. They are nowhere near the memory roof.

### Consequence for the objective

**The 4 to 6 percent prefill residual at 2048 and 4096 is not attributable to
"weight traffic at 127 GB/s" on this evidence**, and the recorded lever -- raise
the reuse of the weight re-read -- targets the smaller of the two terms. The
addressable quantity is the **activation re-read across output tiles** (369 MB
against 98 MB of weights at the shipped grid), and it is a scheduling question
with a measured threshold: the grid exceeds the resident block window between 440
and 528 blocks, and below it the traffic is the unique bytes exactly.

## Validation

```
scripts/gguf_q8_0_dense_dram_probe.py      # added, the probe behind the table
scripts/gguf_q8_0_dense_prefill_shape_time.py   # baseline reproduced: 0.101 / 0.772 / 0.769 / 1.505 / 3.073 ms
rocprofv3 --pmc GL2C_EA_RDREQ_DRAM_sum ...      # commands recorded in the session
rocminfo | grep -A4 "Cache Info"                # L2 2048 KB, L3 32768 KB
```

The kernel-source reading (8 row-tiles) and the grid-size confirmation I checked
myself; the traffic numbers are the probe's, validated against the four known
cases above.

## Next

**Do not start from "weight traffic at 127 GB/s".** The open questions are, in
order:

1. What sets the resident block window for this kernel, and can the shipped grid
   be brought under it? Below it the traffic is provably the unique bytes.
2. Why does K=2816 move 16.5 MB where K=2048 moves 32.1 MB? That is a 2x
   difference between adjacent shapes and it is unexplained.
3. Only then, whether `o_proj`'s K=4096/8192 at 57 to 100 percent of peak has
   anything left to win, and whether the upper-bound caveat means the 100 percent
   figure is really lower.
