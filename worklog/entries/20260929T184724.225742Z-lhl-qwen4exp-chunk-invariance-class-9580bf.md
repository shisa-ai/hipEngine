# The RT-1 chunk-invariance class is {1024,2048,4096}; chunk 256 is outside it

## Summary

RT-1's retained claim is that chunk size "changes scheduling, not arithmetic", evidenced by
`token_id` and `logits_sha256` being identical across its arms (1024 vs 4096). Reading the
artifacts across all three RT-1 packets shows that claim is **correct for chunks >= 1024 and
false for chunk 256.**

| comparison | cases identical | cases differing |
|---|---:|---:|
| **chunk 1024 vs 4096** | **12 of 12** | 0 |
| chunk 256 vs 4096 | 0 of 12 | **12** |
| `token_id` across all three | **12 of 12** | 0 |

Independent three-point run on `code-p4096`: 1024 = 2048 = 4096 = `7270a972da43ff46...`.

**The invariance class is exactly {1024, 2048, 4096}.**

## Why this matters

1. **RT-1's claim is scoped, not wrong.** Its arms were 1024 and 4096; those are identical
   on all 12 cases.
2. **`token_id` is invariant across all four sizes including 256**, so the 256 divergence is
   **sub-argmax** - it does not change the chosen token on these cases, but it is a real
   arithmetic difference that could change a near tie.
3. **Chunk 256 is not numerically interchangeable with 1024+.** A baseline at 256 against a
   candidate at 1024, or a gate run at 256 quoted for a 1024 deployment, compares two
   configurations - the protocol-confound class the external review checklist C1-C6 warns
   about.
4. **It predicts the X-6 gate passes at chunk 4096**: the certification is at 1024, and
   1024 vs 4096 is bit-identical on all 12 cases. The chunk-4096 gate run now in flight
   tests that prediction directly.

## Likely mechanism - explicitly a hypothesis

Chunk size sets the per-chunk row count, and several kernels are selected by row/M
thresholds (dense wide-256, the coltitle/rowbatch families, the staged routes). A threshold
between 256 and 1024 rows would select a different kernel, hence a different reduction
order, at chunk 256. **No dispatch trace was taken**; this is not established.

  three prior packets and the hashes are theirs.
- The 1024-vs-4096 identity is checked on all 12 cases, not just the one case RT-1 quoted,
  and is independently corroborated by the three-point remeasure on `code-p4096`.
- The 256 divergence is also checked on all 12 cases, so it is not a single-case fluke.
- I distinguished the two different chunk-256 packets (`rt1-256-vs-1024` and
  `rt1-serving-suite`) before comparing, having initially confused the serving-suite's
  chunk-256 artifacts with RT-1's chunk-1024 arms - the medians (23.8 s vs 15.6 s) are what
  exposed the mix-up.
