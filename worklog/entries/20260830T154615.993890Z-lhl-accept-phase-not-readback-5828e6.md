# Accept phase is not the D2H readback; it is inside the accept enqueue

## Summary

I added a counter for the D2H readback inside the MTP accept window, on the hypothesis
that a per-cycle
sync explained the near-linear scaling. **The hypothesis is dead**: the readback is 1.4-1.7 ms per
lane-cycle, **1.6% of accept** at C5-C8. The instrumented sub-parts of accept account
for about 4 ms of a
87-104 ms window, so roughly 95% of it lives in one call that is not timed at all:
`_enqueue_target_batch_accept`.

## What is and is not established

- Established: the D2H readback is not the cost. Established: accept minus its three timed
  sub-parts is
  83-99 ms per lane-cycle, and those sub-parts are the whole of what `accept_started`
  encloses except
  `_enqueue_target_batch_accept`, which has no timer.
- Not established, two candidates inside that call, both worth one counter each:
  (a) `_upload_accept_array` issues 3 `runtime.memcpy_async` H2D uploads per cycle. 3
  memcpys cannot cost
  85 ms as host work, so if they dominate it is because they are issued immediately behind
  the verify
  launch and absorb **device queue latency** - meaning accept_ms is largely device wait
  attributed to a
  host phase, and the fix is pipelining, not less Python.
  (b) the call's validation loops and tensor building (`for request_index, (result,
  candidate_count) in
  enumerate(...)`) doing real host work per row. (a) and (b) imply opposite fixes, which
  is why guessing
  further is not worth it.
- The readback counter stays in the tree regardless: it is the only thing that closed
  (a)'s weaker form,
  and it makes the accept window auditable for whoever takes #17.
