# Gemma 4 prefill: chunk policy closed, MMQ defect narrowed to the caller

## Summary

Two results, both negative and both closing a task-named item.

**The chunk policy is not a lever.** `DEFAULT_PREFILL_BLOCK` is 512 and the
source comment justifies it by scratch memory (52.68 GB at 8192 against 3.29 GB
at 512), not by measurement. The forward path documents chunking as exact, and
it is: a 1024-token prefill at block 1024 and at block 2048 is bit-identical to
block 512. It is also not faster - 5.963 s against 6.072 and 6.126, with the warm
2048 arm at 7.671 s - so the memory-driven default is already the fastest of the
three and the Qwen35 chunk-policy finding does not transfer.

**The MMQ gate/up defect is not in the leaf.** That route is the largest
remaining prefill lever (1.265x, about 0.84 s) and it is off by default for a
defect this session found: deterministic, over the bar at 7 of 13 prefill lengths
from 16 to 1024 ids, up to 2.036 KL with decision flips. Sweeping the leaf
against a dequantized reference at the model's own geometry, it is correct in
**every** configuration the harness can build - every per-expert count pattern,
128 experts, `top_k` 1 and 8, a canary tail that survives 3968 rows of tile
padding, and one scratch reused across a big-then-small sequence. So the fault is
in the `expert_start` and `packed_hidden` the group scatter builds on device, not
in the tile walk, the padding mask, the fused stride or the scratch.

## Validation

- **Chunk policy measured three ways.** Bit-exactness (0 differing logits words
  against the 512 block in both arms), speed at a 1024-token prompt, and a second
  warm run per arm to separate the first-touch cost. Each arm built its own
  runner, so the scratch was sized for that block rather than reused.
- **The leaf sweep compares against the strict owner's math**, a numpy
  dequantized reference, not against the fp32 route it replaces.
- **The canary is a real check, not a shape assertion**: a 32 x 128 row tail
  filled with a known pattern after the output buffer, read back and compared.
- **The padding-decline experiment** (temporarily declining the route whenever
  `total_rows > compact_rows`) declined at every length including 1024, which is
  what showed the padding is present even where the route measures exact; the
  canary then refuted the overflow it suggested. The edit was reverted.
- `tests/test_unit_gemma4_expert_route.py`: 47 passed.

## Next

- Instrument the handoff, not the leaf: dump `expert_start` and the first
  `packed_hidden` rows at a failing length (64 ids) and at a passing one (96),
  then run the leaf on exactly those buffers. If the leaf is right on the real
  buffers, the divergence is downstream of the projection; if it is wrong, the
  group scatter's `expert_start` is the defect.
- The route stays off by default and its recorded reason is now the measured
  short-prefill divergence, with `scripts/gemma4_mmq_prefill_length_probe.py` as
  the clearing command.
