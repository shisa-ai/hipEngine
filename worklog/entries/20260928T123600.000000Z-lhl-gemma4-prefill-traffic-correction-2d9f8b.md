# correction: the 995 GB was decode, not prefill; prefill moves 95 GB

## Summary

**This entry corrects `20260928T122800.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-prefill-fetch-census-c7e1a4.md`,
whose prefill traffic figures summed the decode phase.**

`20260928T122800.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-prefill-fetch-census-c7e1a4.md` reported that
"the two MMQ expert kernels move about 995 GB of read traffic to produce a 0.69 s
prefill from roughly 500 MB of weights". **That is wrong.** The census summed every
dispatch in the run, and `scripts/gemma4_campaign_bench.py` runs 128 decode steps
after the prefill. Splitting by dispatch order -- decode begins at the first
`gemma4_attention_decode_class_kernel` dispatch, id 1609 of 317,021:

| phase | FETCH_SIZE | share of run |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| prefill | **95,106 MB** | 5.4% |
| decode (128 steps) | 1,663,035 MB | 94.6% |

**94.6 percent of the traffic is decode.** Per token, decode moves 12,992 MB against
prefill's 186 MB -- expected, since a GEMV reads the whole weight set per token while
a prefill amortises it over 512 rows, and it means the combined totals say almost
nothing about the prefill.

The per-kernel *ordering* in the earlier entry is wrong for the same reason. Corrected,
for the prefill phase only:

| kernel | FETCH_MB | share of prefill |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel` | 37,535 | **39.5%** |
| `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_fused_prefill_compact32_kernel` | 29,080 | 30.6% |
| `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel` | 13,695 | 14.4% |
| `q8_0_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel` | 3,888 | 4.1% |
| `gemma4_attention_prefill_kernel` | 3,157 | 3.3% |

**The top prefill traffic consumer is the Q5_1 down projection, not the gate/up
projection.** And `gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel`, which the earlier entry named the
largest single consumer at 36.1 percent, is 634,592 of its 635,394 MB in the *decode*
phase -- it is a decode term and has no bearing on the prefill metric.

## What survives, and what the correction changes

**Survives.** The expert MMQ kernels are the prefill's traffic centre: 69.5 percent of
prefill read traffic between the two of them, against 3.3 percent for attention. The
counter's absolute unit is still unresolved and still not quoted. The ratios are still
counter-to-counter within one run.

**New, and it is the useful part.** Prefill moves 95,106 MB against roughly 9 GB of
expert and dense weights -- an overfetch of about 10x, concentrated in the two MMQ
expert kernels. For the gate/up alone, 29,080 MB against 272 MB of weights is 107x.

**But the overfetch is not what binds.** The T16 experiment cut this kernel's traffic
3.5x (980 MB to 281 MB) and bought 1.13x. A 3.5x traffic reduction that yields a 1.13x
speedup says the kernel is only weakly traffic-sensitive, and it is the scalar census
that named it the prefill's largest scalar consumer at 6.2x the attention kernel's
stream. Between the two instruments, **the scalar side is the better-supported target
and the traffic side is the larger number.** The earlier entry's "worth its load time"
framing for the repack leaned on traffic; on this evidence a 116.6 s load-time cost for
1.13x on 24 percent of the step is a bad trade, and the repack is not the next unit.

## Also checked

The lm head is **not** recomputing logits for all 512 prefill positions.
`hipengine/runtime/gemma4.py` documents `forward` as "return the last row's logits"
with `logits_rows` defaulting to 1, and the dense kernel's 256 largest dispatches
(grid 4,194,304, 776.82 MB each) fall in the decode phase. There is no waste there to
recover.
