# Device-aware split-K attention plan

## Summary

The split-K decode attention route took its split count from a fixed 32-token chunk
target, so the same plan ran whether the grid had to fill 40 compute units or 96. The
plan now takes the larger of that chunk target and two blocks per compute unit, which
keeps the grid wide enough for a gfx1100 (96 CUs), unchanged on gfx1151 (40 CUs), and
proportional on parts nobody has measured. The kernel receives the chunk length with
the split count, so its staged-scores footprint and its coverage are host-known
quantities instead of a fixed 256-token worst case.

## Changes

- `hipengine/core/hip.py`: `device_compute_units()` and
  `parse_rocminfo_compute_units()`. The probe reads `rocminfo` and keeps only agents
  whose name is an ISA, so the CPU agent's core count is not read as a GPU size, and
  returns values in device order. It is cached per device; the runtime calls it once.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/vibevoice/attention_spans_splitk.py`: `plan_split`
  and `SplitPlan` replace `split_count`. Constants are `MIN_CHUNK=8`,
  `CHUNK_TARGET=32`, `MAX_CHUNK=256`, `MAX_SPLITS=64`, `BLOCKS_PER_UNIT=2`,
  `DEFAULT_COMPUTE_UNITS=40`. The wrapper takes `context` instead of `splits`, plans
  against `min(context, spans.max_live_count)` (the value the kernel clamps to), and
  raises when a plan does not cover that context or asks for a chunk wider than the
  staged scores.
- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/vibevoice/attention_spans_splitk.hip`: `chunk` is a
  kernel argument. Shared memory is sized from it, the scores stride is `chunk`
  instead of `VV_SPLITK_MAX_CHUNK`, and the constant is gone.
- `hipengine/runtime/yue2_ar.py`: probes the compute-unit count once at construction
  and passes `position + 1` with it per step.
- `scripts/yue2_splitk_plan_sweep.py`: kernel-only sweep over split counts at one
  context, marking the plan's own choice, with `--json` output.
- `benchmarks/results/yue2_splitk_plan_sweep_gfx1100_20261018.json`,
  `benchmarks/README.md`, `docs/KERNELS.md`: the plan, its measurements, and a pointer
  to the artifact.

## Validation

- `tests/test_unit_yue2_attention_splitk.py`: 21 passed. The agreement sweep now also
  covers 64 splits at 997 tokens and 16 000 tokens, plus plans for 40, 96, 128, 2 and
  unknown compute units across contexts 1-16 000 and 1-8 KV heads. Plan rejections
  (under-coverage, chunk over `MAX_CHUNK`) are pinned.
- `tests/test_unit_backend_device_capability.py`: 34 passed, including the rocminfo
  parser against a two-GPU host (CPU agent's 32 excluded, order preserved).
- `tests/test_unit_yue2_ar_runtime.py`, `test_unit_yue2_session.py`,
  `test_unit_yue2_song_generator.py`, `test_unit_yue2_product.py`: 82 passed.
- AR replay through the product path (`scripts/yue2_ar_replay.py`, `attention=splitk`
  reported at runtime ready): 18 cases / 576 rows, pooled mean KL **1.0096e-3**, top-8
  recall 0.9575, against 1.05e-3 before the change.
- Product decode loop, paired CFG, ~900-1 000 tokens of context: **12.464 ms** per
  step against the 12.54 ms committed before the change.
- Kernel-only sweep on the 96-CU W7900: 998 tokens floors at 24-32 splits
  (0.026 ms/call), 48 splits costs 0.032 ms, and the plan picks 32. At 128 tokens the
  plan is within 0.2% of the floor. At 4 098 tokens the plan reaches the 64-split cap
  at 0.062 ms against the 0.056 ms floor at 48 (11% over, on a call that is ~2% of the
  step).
- `python3 scripts/docs/check_all.py`: all 4 gates pass.
  `tests/test_integration_benchmark_readme_sync.py`: 11 passed.

## Next

- The 40-CU path is exercised by the plan tests, not by a gfx1151 run in this session;
  the last gfx1151 measurement used the fixed 32-split plan, which is what the new plan
  also selects at product depth there.
- `hipDeviceGetAttribute(hipDeviceAttributeMultiprocessorCount)` reports 48 on a 96-CU
  gfx1100 under ROCm 7.2 (compute units per shader engine), and no attribute ordinal
  reports the total, so the probe uses rocminfo. If a future ROCm fixes that attribute,
  it is the cheaper source and can replace the subprocess.
