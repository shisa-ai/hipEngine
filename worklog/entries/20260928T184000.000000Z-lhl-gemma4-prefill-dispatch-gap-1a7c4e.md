# the dense in-situ penalty is inter-launch gap time, not dense kernels running slower

## Summary

`20260928T182000` established that the dense shapes run 19 to 23 percent slower in situ
than in isolation and ruled the estimator out as the cause. This unit rules out the two
physical explanations and finds a third:

- **DRAM bandwidth contention is ruled out.** The dense shapes move 10.8 to 19.4 GB/s each,
  15.5 GB/s across the whole dense term, and 47.6 GB/s for the expert routes -- **24.6
  percent of the 256 GB/s peak combined.**
- **Thermal or clock drift within a run is ruled out.** First-quartile against last-quartile
  means over 400 iterations drift by only **1.4 to 4.4 percent**, not 20.
- **The penalty is inter-launch gap time being attributed to the dense calls.** The dense
  penalty is **0.169 ms per call** on the three measured shapes; the step's unaccounted time
  is **0.176 ms per launch** across all 206 dense launches. **A 4 percent match.**

Reconciling the whole dense term confirms it: **189.9 ms in situ = 158.5 ms of estimated
kernel time + 31.4 ms of gap**, against a launch-proportional share of 28.7 ms.

## The measurements

**Bandwidth, per shape in situ:**

| shape | weights | activations | output | total | in-situ GB/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `4096 -> 2816` | 12.3 MB | 4.2 MB | 2.9 MB | 19.3 MB | 10.8 |
| `2816 -> 4096` | 12.3 MB | 2.9 MB | 4.2 MB | 19.3 MB | 15.7 |
| `2816 -> 2112` | 6.3 MB | 2.9 MB | 2.2 MB | 11.4 MB | 19.4 |

Aggregate: dense 15.5 GB/s, experts 47.6 GB/s, combined **24.6 percent of peak**. The whole
step moves about 16.64 GB in 687.7 ms -- **24.2 GB/s, 9.4 percent of peak.**

**Drift, 400 iterations per shape:**

```
4096 -> 2816   first q100 1.462   last q100 1.527   drift 1.044
2816 -> 4096   first q100 1.026   last q100 1.063   drift 1.036
2816 -> 2112   first q100 0.469   last q100 0.476   drift 1.014
```

**The gap, by two independent routes:**

```
dense penalty on the three measured shapes:
  (1.792-1.507)*25 + (1.229-1.030)*25 + (0.587-0.479)*60 = 18.6 ms over 110 calls
  -> 0.169 ms/call

the step's unaccounted time:
  687.7 - (287.7 + 189.8 + 173.97) = 36.2 ms over 206 dense launches
  -> 0.176 ms/launch

whole dense term:
  in situ                       189.9 ms
  estimated isolated            158.5 ms   (mean isolated/in-situ ratio 0.832)
  difference                     31.4 ms
  launch-proportional share      28.7 ms
```

## What this means

**The dense kernels are already running at their isolated rate in situ.** There is no dense
kernel penalty to recover, and the orientation effect from `20260928T174000` remains the
only kernel-level rate gap in the term -- which is why it is worth 22 ms where the
"penalty" looked like 38.

**The 36.2 ms is a dispatch problem, and 0.176 ms per launch is far too large for kernel
launch overhead**, which is single-digit microseconds on this class of device. It is
consistent with host-side dispatch time -- Python and ctypes per-launch cost -- during which
the GPU has nothing queued. **That makes the prefill partly host-bound**, and it is the
first mechanism found in this branch that would explain why kernel-level work has not moved
the number.

**It also scales the wrong way.** Host dispatch cost is per launch, and the launch count
does not fall as the prompt grows, so a 5.3 percent share at 512 tokens should grow at
longer prompts where the kernels themselves take proportionally longer. That prediction is
untested and it is the cheapest next measurement.

## Next

- **Test the scaling prediction.** If the 36.2 ms is host dispatch it is roughly constant in
  prompt length while kernel time is not, so its share should rise from 5.3 percent at 512
  tokens. The dense probe already runs at 1024; comparing the unaccounted share at the two
  lengths is nearly free and would confirm or kill the mechanism.
- **Then attack the launch count or the dispatch path.** 206 dense launches in one 512-token
  step, at 0.176 ms of idle each. The levers are fewer launches (a fused or batched
  dispatch), a cheaper per-launch path, or graph capture -- and the choice depends on
  whether the cost is in the Python wrapper or in the driver.
- **Do not look for a dense kernel penalty.** It does not exist: the in-situ time is the
  isolated time plus a gap. `20260928T174000`'s orientation effect is the only kernel-level
  gap in the term and it is worth about 22 ms.
