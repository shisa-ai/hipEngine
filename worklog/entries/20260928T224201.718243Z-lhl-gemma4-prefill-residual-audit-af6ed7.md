# prefill is at parity except 4 to 6 percent at 2048 and 4096, and the residual is weight traffic

## Summary

The quick goal `have-working-gemma-4-26b/run-20260926-184752` asks for llama.cpp
prefill speeds. Measured against the same-host reference, prefill has gone from
3.74x / 4.21x / 4.85x slower to **1.051x / 0.984x / 0.956x / 0.943x** at 512 /
1024 / 2048 / 4096. That is parity at the short end and faster at 512, and it is
**not parity at 2048 and 4096**.

This entry exists so the residual is not mistaken for noise, and so the next unit
does not re-derive a lever that is already spent.

## Changes

### The residual is real, not noise

The reference artifact records its own spread, so the comparison can be made
properly:

| prompt | llama.cpp | sd | hipEngine | gap | significant? |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| 512 | 1012.98 | 31.24 | 1064.5 | +5.1% | we are faster |
| 1024 | 1057.48 | 0.91 | 1040.8 | -1.6% | ~18 sd, but small |
| 2048 | 1067.14 | 4.05 | 1020.1 | **-4.4%** | yes, 11 sd |
| 4096 | 1039.85 | 52.38 | 980.9 | -5.7% | ~1.1 sd, inconclusive alone |

At 2048 the gap is 47 tok/s against a reference sd of 4.05, so it is not
measurement noise. At 4096 llama.cpp's own sd is 52.38 -- 5.0 percent -- so that
single point is inconclusive on its own, but it is consistent with 2048 and with
the structural finding below.

The reference's own structural note still holds, with its magnitude collapsed:
llama.cpp prefill is flat in prompt length (1013 to 1067), while this engine
degrades (1064.5 to 980.9). The gap widens exactly where the objective's
long-prompt cases live.

### The levers that are spent

- **The Q4T16 expert gate/up route is live and already inside these numbers.**
  Instrumented on a 4096-token prefill through the public surface: 232 `t16` +
  8 `iu8` gate/up calls, being 8 chunks x 29 Q4_K layers plus the one Q5_K
  layer. Its in-situ value was measured at **1.0817x** by forcing the tiles
  unavailable in the same process (4.578 s against 4.232 s, and the fallback
  serves all 240 calls, so the required MMQ32 path still works in situ).
  Anything that starts from "the T16 gate/up route is dead code" is reading the
  tree at `02e2780fa` or earlier; `a8b59363b` wired it.
- **The T16 down route does not apply to this artifact.** Its down weights are
  Q5_1/Q8_0, not Q4_K, so the loader's `t16_gate`/`t16_up` naming for a rank-3
  Q4_K down tensor is unused here.
- **Attention is no longer the gap.** The full-layer WMMA kernel took the 4096
  step from 8.980 s to 4.189 s, and attention is now a small share of it.

### What is left, and it is one question

The recorded analysis says the expert term's **49 GB/s** and the dense term's
**127 GB/s** of weight traffic "now look like the same defect at two
magnitudes", against the **223 GB/s** the lm head proves is available for a pure
read stream. Those two terms are the large majority of the step at every prompt
length, and the same entry names the plateau -- not any particular shape -- as
"the number to attack".

So the residual is a weight-traffic-rate question, not a shape or a kernel-
selection question. The unexplained part is the K = 2816 anomaly, where the dense
GEMM exceeds 10 TFLOP/s; the recorded guess of L2 residency does not survive
contact, because K = 2048's weight is smaller (6.13 against 8.43 MB) and slower.
