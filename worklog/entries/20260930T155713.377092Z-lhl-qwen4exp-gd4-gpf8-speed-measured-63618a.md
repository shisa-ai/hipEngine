# GD-4's speed case is measured at 4.43x on the GDN family — the entire remaining risk is the KL gate

## Summary

The previous entry left one thing unresolved: GPF-8's artifact records the candidate GDN
family at **47.491 ms** but not its baseline, so it could be cited neither for nor against
the claim that a chunkwise/WY kernel beats the serial route.

**The baseline is in the prose, not the artifact.** `docs/GGUF-PREFILL-OPTIMIZATION.md`'s
GPF-8 section states the requirement as *"the complete 30-layer GDN family must fall from
**210.501 ms** to roughly 66 ms or less"* at the clean 512 trace.

**So the chunkwise/WY kernel took the GDN family from 210.501 ms to 47.491 ms — 4.43x —
beating its own predeclared 66 ms ceiling.**

That is direct in-tree evidence that the mechanism delivers on speed, and it **withdraws**
the "cannot be cited either way" caveat from the previous entry.

## The rejection was still correct, and still not about the kernel

- **Numerics:** KL 0.056522 > 0.05, top-1 445/450 = 98.889% < 99%, 5 of 18 free-running
  trajectories exact — with the kernel **byte-exact at one token** and inside frozen
  primitive tolerance at full chunks.
- **Speed:** the whole-model 512 prefill reached 2003.399 tok/s against the 2412.320
  llama.cpp HIP floor, 16.951% below (4K passed at +1.116%).

The second is a fact about an **external whole-model floor**, not about the kernel: a 4.43x
win on the GDN family did not by itself clear it.

## GD-4's prize, restated with the measured speedup

| | ms | share of p512 prefill |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| qwen4exp GDN family, measured | 194.487 | 4.97% |
| at 4.43x | 43.9 | 1.12% |
| **saving** | **150.6** | **~3.85%** |

The row's ~5.5% is the *ceiling* (family to zero, plus the separately-classified conv
0.35% and norm 0.41%). **~3.85% is what this mechanism has actually demonstrated on a
sibling model.**

## What this changes

**The entire remaining risk is numerical, not structural.** The speed question is answered
by in-tree evidence; the structure question (does the chunkwise form escape the
register-capacity bound?) is answered by GPF-8's 48 VGPR against the production route's
256. What is *not* answered is whether a correct chunkwise reassociation passes the
qwen4exp production envelope — and there is an in-tree precedent that it does not, at
FP32, with the kernel otherwise byte-exact.

That is exactly what the re-scoped decision experiment tests: port the existing CPU
oracle's arithmetic into a plain unfused FP32 GPU kernel for the qwen4exp shape and run the
production envelope on it, before writing any WMMA.

## Validation

- Baseline quoted verbatim from `docs/GGUF-PREFILL-OPTIMIZATION.md` GPF-8 section; candidate
  figure from the artifact's `synthetic_30_layer_512_gdn_family_ms.total`. Both are
  predeclared-contract values, so the comparison is against a threshold frozen before
  implementation rather than one chosen afterwards.
- Share arithmetic: 194.487 / 3914.468 = 4.968%; 194.487 / 4.43 = 43.90 ms;
  150.59 / 3914.468 = 3.847%.
- Whole-ledger table integrity re-checked: `GD-4` at 6 cells.

## Scope and limits

- **The 4.43x is on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `gguf_q4_k_m` / `qwen35_gdn_prefill_*`, not on
  qwen4exp.** The 3.85% is therefore a *transfer estimate* built from a measured sibling
  speedup applied to a separately-measured qwen4exp family share, not a measurement of
  GD-4 on this model. It must not be quoted as a qwen4exp result.
- GPF-8's `C=8` and the qwen4exp shape (Hk16/Hv48/Dk=Dv=128) differ; the achievable
  speedup at the qwen4exp shape is unmeasured.
- GPF-8 bundled the chunkwise recurrence with a direct-conv Q/K/V input path. The
  one-token byte-exactness argues the input path was not the cause of the KL, but the
  artifact does not separate the two by measurement.
- No kernel written, no performance claim, no default changed.
