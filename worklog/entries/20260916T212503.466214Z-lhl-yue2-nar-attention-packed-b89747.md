# YuE2 NAR attention packed loads and eight rows per block

## Summary

The NAR attention kernel was stalled, not bandwidth-bound. `rocprofv3 --pmc` on the
row-blocked kernel measures 3.5e8 cycles carrying 3.7e9 VALU instructions at **19%
achieved occupancy**, with 3.4e8 global and 4.3e8 shared-memory instructions. Its
own comment claimed it was against a bandwidth wall, and the previous entry's
27.9 GB of K/V traffic per call made that reading plausible; the counters say
otherwise. Two access shapes dominated: a lane owns one key, so the scalar K walk
asked the L1 for 32 sectors per request (lanes land `head_dim * 2` bytes apart) 128
times per tile, and every lane re-read the whole query row from L1 for every row it
owned.

Packing the K row to eight elements and the query row to four per instruction, plus
splitting the V walk at the AR/NAR boundary so it steps by pointer, and taking the
block from four query rows to eight, moves the kernel's own per-call time at the
production shape (1 299 rows / 2 695 keys) from **177.22 ms to 28.34 ms (6.25x)**
and raises achieved occupancy to **93%**. The NAR solve of `mandarin-off-s1234` at
2 ODE steps falls from **27.76 s to 11.03 s (2.52x)** in a same-session pair of
gate runs, and the full replay of that case from **47.89 s to 31.10 s**.

The change is bit-exact: the kernel keeps its per-row d-ascending dot product, its
tile walk and its key-ascending accumulation order, and the recorded parent-bits
fixture compares equal bit for bit. Attention is now 2.93 s of the 11.11 s solve
(26%), so the solver's remaining cost is its projection path (8.18 s of hipBLASLt
GEMMs, RoPE and norms) rather than its attention.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/yue2/nar.hip`:
  - K is read as 16-byte `uint4` (eight BF16) per lane per request instead of one
    `uint16_t`, with a scalar fallback for head dimensions that are not a multiple
    of eight, where rows are not 16-byte aligned.
  - Q is read as two `float4` per row per eight dimensions instead of eight scalars.
  - The V walk is split into its AR and NAR ranges and steps by
    `num_kv_heads * head_dim` instead of computing a 64-bit address and an
    `ar_rows` comparison per key.
  - `YUE2_NAR_ROWS_PER_BLOCK` 4 -> 8. Eight is a measured optimum, not a guess:
    33.90 ms at four rows, 44.90 ms at sixteen, 88.50 ms at thirty-two.
  - The stale "bandwidth-bound" header comment is replaced with the measured
    counter reading.
- `tests/test_unit_yue2_nar_kernels.py`: `head_dim` is now parametrized over
  {128, 100}. The 100 case is not a multiple of eight, so it exercises the new
  scalar fallback.
- `scripts/yue2_attention_bench.py`: new. Times the kernel at the production shape
  in ~3 s per measurement (the replay takes a minute per measurement and mixes in
  the decoder, which is too slow to iterate a kernel against) and re-checks the
  recorded parent bits in the same run.

## Validation

- `python3 scripts/yue2_attention_bench.py --repeat 6 --fixture-check`: 28.34 ms
  median per call at 1 299 rows / 1 396 AR rows / 2 695 keys, 16 query heads, 8 KV
  heads, head_dim 128, and **parent-bits parity bit-identical (max abs diff 0.0)**.
- `python3 -m pytest tests/test_unit_yue2_nar_kernels.py`: 15 passed.
- `python3 -m pytest tests/test_unit_yue2_*.py`: 219 passed.
- RED check on the new fallback coverage: perturbing the scalar walk's start index
  fails the `head_dim=100` cases and leaves `head_dim=128` passing, so the new
  parametrization is load-bearing.
- `rocprofv3 --pmc GRBM_COUNT SQ_WAVES_sum SQ_INSTS_VALU SQ_INSTS_LDS SQ_INSTS_FLAT
  OccupancyPercent` over the bench harness, before and after: occupancy 19.1% ->
  93.1%, global instructions per lane-tile 740 -> 385.
- Same-session, same-protocol replay pair via
  `scripts/yue2_e2e_gate.py --only mandarin-off-s1234 --steps 2 --skip-live`:
  `nar_seconds` 27.76 -> 11.03, `vae_seconds` 20.13 -> 20.07, total 47.89 -> 31.10,
  with the case's frames/samples/peak and bit-identical determinism intact and
  `torch imported: False`.
- Attention-vs-rest split for the same case (device synchronize on either side of
  the attention call): 11.11 s solve = 2.93 s attention over 112 calls (26.2 ms
  each) + 8.18 s of projections, RoPE and norms.
- Same-protocol replay at the product's 32 steps (the step count the reference's
  27.32 s figure is quoted at): solve **443.4 s -> 116.53 s (3.80x)**, replay
  465.9 s -> 136.59 s, case shape and bit-identical determinism intact. The gap to
  the pinned upstream on this host is 16.2x before and 4.27x after.
- Artifact: `benchmarks/results/yue2_m7_attention_packed_20260917.json`.

Host: zbook, AMD Radeon 8060S / gfx1151, 40 CU, unified LPDDR5X. Every figure here
is gfx1151; no gfx1100 hardware was available, so gfx1100 performance and
correctness remain unverified for this kernel.

## Next

  solve, 1 494 hipBLASLt dispatches per replay of a 32x32 macro-tile kernel. At 32
  steps the solve is 116.53 s against the reference's 27.32 s, and this is where
  most of that remaining gap sits.
- The replay is VAE-bound: 20.07 s of 31.10 s, dominated by
  `yue2_vae_conv1d_f32_kernel` and `yue2_vae_conv_transpose1d_f32_kernel`.
- Still on the table for attention, now much smaller: the tile maximum is a serial
  128-entry shared-memory walk per row per tile, and `fmax` is order-independent so
  a warp shuffle keeps it bit-exact.
