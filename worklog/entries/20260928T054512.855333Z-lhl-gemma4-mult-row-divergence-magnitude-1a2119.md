# the block-width divergence is 0.8 to 2.2 percent, five to fifty times bf16 epsilon

## Summary

`worklog/entries/20260928T054418` found that a two-or-wider block is not the
same computation as several one-row forwards and could only say the divergence
was in the hidden state, because the metric it had compared BF16 bit patterns as
integers and a near-zero element made that count meaningless. This entry
replaces the magnitude with a sound one and adds the two facts that follow from
it.

Measured on the synthetic fixture artifact, comparing a k-row block against k
one-row forwards from the same starting position:

| k | hidden bit-equal | hidden relative L2 | logits max abs diff | top-1 agrees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **[True]** | **0.0** | 0.000000 | — |
| 2 | [False, False] | [0.008416, 0.022052] | 1.623520 | [True, True] |
| 3 | [False × 3] | [0.008416, 0.022052, 0.007548] | 2.507651 | [True × 3] |
| 5 | [False × 4] | [0.008416, 0.022052, 0.007548, 0.018547] | 2.507651 | [True × 4] |

Relative L2 is ``||a - b|| / ||a||`` over the hidden vector. The element-max
relative error is also large (0.86 to 1.97) but is not quoted as the magnitude,
because a max over a few hundred elements is set by whichever element happens to
sit near zero.

## What the magnitude means

BF16 carries about eight mantissa bits, so a rounding difference between two
orders of the same summation lands near 0.4 percent, and usually well under it
once the terms are balanced. The observed 0.8 to 2.2 percent is five to fifty
times that. This is not two summation orders of one computation; it is a
different computation, and across thirty layers it compounds.

**Row 0 diverges.** Row 0 is the first token of the block, with the same
preceding context in both paths and nothing else in the block ahead of it. Its
computation cannot depend on how many rows follow unless a kernel reads the
block width as an input. It does, and that is the whole mechanism.

**Each row's divergence is identical at k=2, k=3 and k=5.** Row 0's is 0.008416
in all three; row 1's is 0.022052 in all three. So a row does not depend on its
companions -- the difference is entirely between a one-row block and a
two-or-wider one, which is a route selected by block width rather than a
per-row reduction-order effect.

## Why the fixture understates it

Top-1 agrees at every position above, which is what greedy acceptance reads and
what would make a verify pass safe in practice. That agreement is not reassuring
here: the fixture's logits are nearly all saturated at the softcap, so a 1.6 to
2.5 absolute difference lands inside a plateau and the ordering survives it. On a
real prompt with unsaturated logits the same 2 percent hidden divergence has
room to change an argmax. The fixture is evidence that the plumbing works, not
evidence that the route difference is benign.
