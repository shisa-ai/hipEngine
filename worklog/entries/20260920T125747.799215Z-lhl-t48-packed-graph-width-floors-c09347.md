# Task 48: packed-graph width floors are already correct - profiling all four graph widths adds no policy row

## Summary

Profiled every graph-bucket width (C1, C2, C4, C8) at the campaign's D24
short-horizon shape on gfx1151 and **added no policy row**, because the
measurement says there is nothing to win. C4 is the only graph width whose
scaled global floor blocks capture at D24, but its eager submission overhead is
4.54 ms of a 92.62 ms wall - not the 15.72 ms of a 97.28 ms wall that justified
the 2026-08-29 C2 admission. The 2026-08-29 verdict "do not lower thresholds for
unlisted keys" is now upheld by measurement rather than by deferral.

| W | captured | wall_ms | launches | kernel_ms | submission overhead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | no | 82.18 | 592 | 79.42 | 2.76 ms |
| 2 | **yes** | 85.87 | 840 | 81.87 | 4.01 ms |
| 4 | no | 92.62 | 852 | 88.08 | **4.54 ms** |
| 8 | **yes** | 110.02 | 872 | 106.18 | 3.84 ms |

## Why C4 needs no row

Production captures iff `remaining_decode_tokens >= floor(width)`
(`hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py:10697-10717`), where the floor is the
backend policy row if present and `ceil(128 / width)` otherwise:

- C2 floor 23 (policy row) -> captures at D24
- C8 floor 16 (scaled global) -> captures at D24
- C4 floor 32 (scaled global) -> **eager at D24**
- C1 floor 128 (scalar path) -> eager, and cheapest at 2.76 ms

C4's ceiling for improvement is bounded by its own eager overhead: 4.54 ms of
92.62 ms is 4.9%, and that is if capture eliminated submission cost entirely.
The captured reference widths show it does not - C2 sits at 4.01 ms and C8 at
3.84 ms - so the realistic gain is about 0.7 ms, or **0.75%**. That is inside
run-to-run noise and far below what justifies a policy row plus its exactness
gate.

## The sweep also re-measured the C2 admission from the other side

Width 2 was run first, deliberately, to validate the invocation against a known
row before the never-profiled widths were trusted:

| C2 at D24 | wall_ms | kernel_ms | launches | overhead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pre-admission (2026-08-29 artifact) | 97.28 | 81.56 | 880 | 15.72 ms |
| post-admission (this run) | 85.87 | 81.87 | 840 | 4.01 ms |

Kernel time is unchanged (81.56 -> 81.87 ms) while 11.71 ms of submission
overhead disappears. That independently confirms capture acts only on the
submission layer - which is exactly what makes C4's 4.54 ms the correct ceiling
on any gain from lowering its floor, rather than an assumption.

## Counterfactual attempted and not required

I tried to profile C4 with its floor temporarily lowered to 23 rather than infer
the result. `scripts/gguf_continuous_owner_rocprof.py` refuses a dirty source
root (`RocprofCacheError: source root must be clean`) and has no opt-out flag,
so the edit could not be profiled in place. The temporary edit was reverted and
the tree verified clean before anything else.

Not completing it is acceptable here because the eager-side measurement already
bounds the answer from above (4.54 ms) and the two captured reference widths
bound it from below (~0.7 ms). Both bounds are measurements from this run, not
inferences about unmeasured behavior.

## Method notes worth carrying forward

- **Each width needs its own build cache.** The harness's build child compiles
  only the artifacts its own route touches, so a cache built for packed C2 lacks
  the serial-C1 kernels (`gguf_q6_q4_pair`) and the cache-only warm child fails
  with `FileNotFoundError: cached build artifact missing`. Since `--rebuild`
  refuses a non-empty cache root, every width gets a fresh one. Two sweep
  attempts were lost to this before it was understood.
- **The shared `~/.cache/hipengine` cannot be reused.** The harness sets
  `HIPENGINE_REQUIRE_CACHED_BUILD` for its warm child and the shared cache does
  not carry this source revision's artifact hashes.
- **The marker name lives in the marker trace's `Function` column**, not a
  `Marker_Name` column. Reading the wrong column yields a `None` window, and the
  extractor then counts every dispatch in the process - which is how an 85 ms
  wall first came back reporting 2.3 s of kernel time and 21,425 "launches". The
  tell was the arithmetic being impossible; the corrected extractor returns 840
  launches in the marker window.
