# GD-4 state-drift screen: a chunked GDN scan does not drift, and the prefix-product ratio is the real hazard

## Summary

Ledger row `GD-4` proposes replacing the sequential GDN prefill recurrence with a chunked
scan on fp16 WMMA, and its own action says the first thing to do is a **numerical/state
screen** because the recurrence carries state across the whole prompt. This unit runs that
screen. **No kernel was built.**

The recurrence is affine in the state, so a chunked composition is **exact in exact
arithmetic** and the only question is floating-point reordering. Screened at Qwen4Exp's GDN
shape (`Hk16/Hv48/Dk=Dv=128`) over a 4K prompt:

- **fp32 state carry: NMSE 4.6e-14 to 7.1e-14**, flat across chunk sizes 32/64/128/256 -
  indistinguishable from fp32 round-off.
- **fp16 state carry: NMSE 9.0e-11 to 8.0e-10**, i.e. ~4 orders worse and still tiny. It
  *improves* with larger chunks because fewer boundary roundings occur. Even the worst case
  is **~1000x better than the row's own cited reference** (halo-box PR #91's "NMSE ~2e-7").
- **No chunk/tail boundary effect**: 4095, 4096, 4097 and 4100 tokens all sit at NMSE ~7.2e-14.

**So the row's stated blocker - "numerical/state screen first" - is cleared.** The
remaining work is the kernel build, not more numerics.

## The hazard the screen found, which is not the one the row expected

The first revision computed `D_t/D_j` as a **ratio of prefix products**,
`cum[t] / max(cum[j], tiny)`. That is numerically unsafe, and it failed the RED test loudly:
**max_abs 1.05e1 at chunk 256 against 2.26e-01 at chunk 64** - larger chunks *worse*, which
is the signature of an explosion rather than of accumulation.

Mechanism: `cum` decays geometrically and underflows to zero, but not simultaneously across
tokens. While `cum[j]` has underflowed and `cum[i]` has not, the clamped ratio
`tiny/1e-30` becomes enormous, the triangular matrix `L` blows up, and the solve diverges.
The denominator floor does not help - it is what creates the blow-up.

The committed form is a **direct suffix product** `R[i, j] = prod_{k=j+1..i} decay[k]`,
walking backwards from `i`. It never divides, and every factor is a decay in (0, 1], so each
entry is bounded by 1 and underflows monotonically to zero.

**This is the one thing in the packet a kernel author most needs**, and it is exactly the
class of finding the screen existed to produce: the chunked scan is algebraically safe, and
the obvious way to implement it is not the safe way.

## Validation

- **RED first, then GREEN.** The RED gate is that the chunked form must converge to the
  sequential one as the chunk grows; it does (NMSE 4.6e-14 to 7.4e-14 with no growth in T,
  smaller chunks marginally better). A form that is algebraically identical and correctly
  ordered cannot do anything else, so this is the check that the derivation is right.
- **The RED gate was verified to be real, not decorative.** Reinstating the prefix-product
  ratio makes **5 of the 9 tests fail** - all three
  `test_chunked_form_matches_sequential_to_fp32_roundoff` parameters,
  `test_fp16_state_carry_stays_far_below_the_reference_claim`, and
  `test_partial_final_chunk_costs_nothing`. Restoring the suffix form returns 9/9 green.
- **Two bugs were found and fixed in this screen's own code before any figure was
  trusted**: the `dv`-vector broadcast (`k_t.(S_0 D_t)` is a per-token vector, not a scalar,
  because the recurrence is independent across the value dimension), and the prefix-product
  explosion above.
- **Limits recorded rather than smoothed over:** synthetic inputs at the right shape and
  scale rather than captured model activations; one shape, one seed; the fp16 arm simulates
  the state-carry rounding, not a full fp16 WMMA operand path; and **no timing claim** - the
  ~5.5% ceiling in `GD-4` is what a kernel build would test.
