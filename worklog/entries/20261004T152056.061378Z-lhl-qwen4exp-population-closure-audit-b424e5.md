# The population correction: what it invalidates, what it confirms, and the figure it re-derives that was already measured

## Summary

Iterations 151–152 corrected the campaign's MoE expert-population model from the synthetic
probe's **203 of 512 (39.6%)** to a natural **368 of 512 (72.0%)**. This iteration asked the
follow-on question those units did not: **does the correction invalidate any already-closed
row, and was the corrected picture already in the tree?** The answer is no invalidation, and
yes — twice over.

### 1. The 335-of-512 figure was already measured on real cases, three weeks earlier

Ledger line 766, `Hot-expert weight reuse; next-K pipelining | dead`, reads: *"Routing diffuse
(**~335 of 512 experts per layer-chunk**; top-16 cover 25.3%)"*, entry `20260910T111500`.
That entry's own text is stronger than the ledger row:

> **Routing is diffuse**: ~335/512 experts (~65%) selected per (layer, chunk); **~1.05 GB of
> expert weights streamed per capture — the layer nearly fully streams its expert pool every
> chunk regardless of scheduling.**

and its method line records **"12 canonical cases (prefill chunks plus 48 decode steps each)"** —
**real canonical cases, not synthetic token IDs**.

So the campaign measured ~335 of 512 on real inputs on 2026-09-10, and then measured 203 of 512
on synthetic IDs on 2026-09-27 and used *that* figure. My natural measurement —
**341.54 to 387.92 across four categories** — brackets the earlier 335 and shows the later 203
was the anomaly. The right number was in the tree before the wrong one replaced it.

### 2. My iterations 151–152's "inference" was already a measurement

Both units labelled the traffic consequence *"an inference, not a measurement"*: that at ~72%
of experts active, expert-weight traffic behind the selected MoE kernels is close to the full
matrix. `20260910T111500` had already **measured** it — "~1.05 GB of expert weights streamed per
capture — the layer nearly fully streams its expert pool every chunk" — and drawn the same
conclusion, which is why its hot-expert route was closed as `dead`.

This is the third time this session that a hypothesis was formed before the tree was read. The
durable rule is already recorded in the other lane's loop state and applies here unchanged:
**read the row and its entry before forming the hypothesis, not after.** The population
*numbers* from 151–152 stand and are the per-category detail the earlier unit did not have; what
does not stand is presenting their consequence as newly inferred.

### 3. EG-4's closure: the cited number is token-count-dependent, the conclusion survives

EG-4 (`Expert-population-aware tiles`, `dead`) was closed on: *"Real skew moves the tile count
6.6% from the balanced model; the tile count is not the constraint."*

Recomputing that metric — `tile_count(t) = sum over experts of ceil(count_e / t)`, compared
against the same assignments spread evenly across the *active* experts — on both populations:

| arm | active experts | t8 | **t16** | t32 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| synthetic (recorded 6.6%) | 203.0 | +1.82% | **+7.53%** | +20.41% |
| `code-p1024` | 385.5 | −1.20% | **+11.77%** | +47.79% |
| `general_en-p1024` | 359.1 | −1.42% | **+11.31%** | +41.17% |
| `general_ja-p1024` | 341.5 | +0.58% | **+11.31%** | +37.28% |
| `mixed_ja_en-p1024` | 387.9 | −2.58% | **+10.84%** | +46.68% |
| `code-p4096` | 446.5 | +0.17% | **−0.11%** | +6.57% |

The metric is validated: it reproduces the recovered fixture's recorded `mean_tile16` of
**772.9167 exactly**. My reconstruction of the *balanced* reference gives +7.53% for the
synthetic where the ledger records 6.6%, so absolute levels differ by ~1 point; the **ordering**
is what is robust.

**What this says:** the skew's tile-count effect is **token-count dependent**, which the single
6.6% figure does not capture — roughly **+11% at 1,024 tokens and ~0% at 4,096**. At the
campaign's primary target case (`code-p4096`) the deviation is essentially zero, so EG-4's
conclusion — *the tile count is not the constraint* — **stands, and stands more strongly there
than the recorded figure implied**. EG-4 is **not reopened**; its cited figure should be read as
token-count-dependent.

**Hypothesis outcome, stated against what was predicted:** I predicted the natural skew would
move the tile count *less* than the synthetic 6.6%. **Refuted at 1,024 tokens** (natural is
+10.8 to +11.8%, i.e. 1.4–1.6x more) and **confirmed at 4,096 tokens** (−0.11%). The prediction
was half wrong because it assumed the skew effect is a property of the distribution; it is
primarily a property of the token count.

## Changes

No engine, kernel, dispatch or default change. Measurement and audit unit; nothing to keep or
revert. Ledger ED-3's row gains the 335 cross-reference so the two population figures are
connected rather than one silently replacing the other.

## Validation

The metric reproduces the recovered fixture's recorded `mean_tile16` (772.9167) exactly, which
is the check that it is the campaign's metric and not a new one. The synthetic counts are read
from `benchmarks/results/2026-09-29-qwen4exp-population-recovery/populations.json`; the natural
counts from `benchmarks/results/2026-10-04-qwen4exp-natural-expert-population/populations.json`.
The `20260910T111500` figures are quoted from that entry and its ledger row, not recomputed.

**Limits.** The balanced-model reference is a reconstruction, so absolute deltas carry ~1 point
of method uncertainty — the ordering across arms and token counts does not. One model, one
quant, one host (gfx1151). Counts, not throughput measurements.
