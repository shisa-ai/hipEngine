# The per-step token readback costs nothing; the decode non-kernel remainder grows 7x with depth

## Summary

The decode host/device attribution published in `20261004T073816` is **withdrawn
and replaced**. Its measurement is reproduced; its interpretation was an
artifact of where the loop was split.

**`device_drain_ms = 0.0` does not mean host-bound.** `runner.step()` ends with
a **blocking** device-to-host copy of the argmax result (`_finish_lm_head` ->
`copy_device_to_host`), so the probe's "host enqueue" window already contains the
device wait and a zero trailing drain is a tautology. Two checks:

- The canonical bench's `--capture-mode compact` removes the explicit per-step
  `device_synchronize()` and still reports `decode_device_drain_ms_per_step =
  0.000`, because the blocking readback supplies the synchronization instead.
- Removing the readback makes the drain meaningful, and then it is not zero.

**The readback costs nothing.** The first attempt at pricing it was confounded:
its two arms ran at different sequence positions on one growing sequence, so
they differed in KV state and in which PLE rows had to be faulted in, and the
"23.8 ms/step readback cost" it reported was that difference. A counter-rotated
A/B on **one generator with `reset()` + `prefill()` before every pass**, so both
arms start from the identical position, KV state and PLE row set, four passes
per case:

| case | arm | wall ms/step | tok/s | trailing drain / 128 steps |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| code-p512 | readback | 54.687 / 54.670 | 18.286 / 18.292 | 0.000 ms/step |
| code-p512 | no_readback | 55.230 / 55.091 | 18.106 / 18.152 | **0.343 ms/step** |
| code-p4096 | readback | 84.618 / 84.588 | 11.818 / 11.822 | 0.000 ms/step |
| code-p4096 | no_readback | 85.761 / 85.789 | 11.660 / 11.657 | **0.575 ms/step** |

**Delta: -0.482 ms/step (-0.87%) at p512 and -1.172 ms/step (-1.37%) at
p4096** - dropping the blocking readback makes the loop *slower*, by exactly the
extra trailing drain the readback was hiding. Within-arm repeatability is 0.03%
and 0.04%. The readback is inside `max(host enqueue, device work)`, not on top
of it.

**What actually limits the step, measured in the bench rather than the probe:**

| case | decode wall | profiled kernel sum | wall - kernel sum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| code-p512 | 47.66 ms/step | 46.19 ms/step | **1.47 ms/step** |
| code-p4096 | 57.69 ms/step | 47.13 ms/step | **10.56 ms/step** |

The wall grows **10.04 ms/step (+21%)** where the kernel sum grows 0.94 ms/step
(+2.0%), and the non-kernel remainder grows **7x** with depth. Two independent
checks say that remainder is host work and not device stalls: removing 2.3
ms/step of host work (the companion memo unit) moves p512 **+0.02%** but p4096
**+2.53%** against a 4.0% prediction; and the counter-rotated A/B's 0.343 / 0.575
ms/step drain says host enqueue and device execution are within ~1% of each
other.

**Consequence for the campaign: the loop wall is `max(host enqueue, device
work)` with the two nearly equal, so a host-only or device-only change is capped
at roughly half its nominal saving.** At p512 there is essentially no non-kernel
remainder to attack (1.47 ms/step) and the step is device-limited; at p4096 the
remainder is 10.56 ms/step and is the target, with the ~1729 launches per step
as one candidate.

## Validation

Hardware: AMD Radeon 8060S / `gfx1151` (Strix Halo Framework host), machine-id
`55ea6c509d0b49eea8de7094a1023668`, ROCm 7.15.26333,
`HIPENGINE_HIP_ARCH=gfx1151`. Model: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next
`unsloth-...-UD-Q4_K_XL`, production profile, BF16 KV, greedy AR, chunk 1024,
128 transitions.

- Counter-rotated A/B: `bash /tmp/readback_ab.sh` - `/tmp/readback_ab.py
  --model-root <UD-Q4_K_XL> --case-id code-p512 --case-id code-p4096
  --transitions 128 --passes 4 --prefill-chunk-size 1024 --execution-profile
  production --compiler-version-file /tmp/hipengine-hipcc-version.txt
  --require-cached-build --output .../counter-rotated.json`. Arms alternate
  readback / no_readback / readback / no_readback; each pass is
  `runner.reset()` + `runner.prefill(...)` + 128 steps, so both arms share
  position, KV state and PLE rows.
- The instrument is proven sensitive: within-arm repeats agree to 0.03% and
  0.04%, so it resolves well below the effects it was built to test.
- Wall/kernel-sum table: bench rows from
  `benchmarks/results/2026-10-04-qwen4exp-thermal-protocol-and-llamacpp-basis/{p512-c1024,p4096-c1024}.json`
  (47.656 and 57.694 ms/step); kernel sums from that directory's
  `bench-decode-rep-trace.json` (46.1927 and 47.1298 ms/step), whose own note
  records that the profiler serializes launches and inflates absolute times.
- D2H copy micro-benchmark, same host, to rule out the copy call itself as the
  cost: `hipMemcpy` 8 B D2H **0.009 ms**, pinned 0.008 ms, `memcpy_async` +
  sync 0.015 ms, `device_synchronize` alone 0.000 ms.
- `uv run pytest tests/test_unit_qwen4exp_canonical_ar_bench.py -q`: 15 passed.

## Next

- **The decode target at depth is the 10.56 ms/step non-kernel remainder at
  p4096.** Host profiling of the bench's own decode loop (not the probe's) is
  the next unit: attribute the remainder before choosing between launch
  consolidation, per-step host work, or a deeper cause.
- The probe's absolute levels remain unexplained against the bench's (54.7 vs
  47.7 ms/step at p512). It never warms the PLE page cache and runs both cases
  in one process; a one-case-per-process variant with a PLE warm pass would
  settle it. It is not on the critical path now that the bench measures the
  remainder directly.
- `scripts/qwen4exp_decode_host_bound_probe.py` still reports `host_bound: true`
  from the zero drain. It should either drop that field or remove the readback
  before claiming a split; recorded in `docs/REFACTOR.md`.
