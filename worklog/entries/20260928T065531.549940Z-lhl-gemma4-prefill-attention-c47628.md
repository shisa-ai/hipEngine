# doubling the bytes costs 2 to 13 percent so bytes are nearly free and flash tiling is ruled out

## Summary

A clean traffic ablation, unlike the confounded one two entries ago: run the **same
arithmetic** over the **same shapes** in bf16 and in f32. Only the bytes moved
change. Doubling them costs **2.4%** on the sliding case and **13.0%** on the full
one.

If this kernel were DRAM-traffic-bound, doubling its bytes would cost close to 2x.
It costs 1.02x to 1.13x. **Bytes are nearly free here**, and the traffic hypothesis
is refuted by an unconfounded measurement.

## Measured

`tokens=2048`, `keys=2048`, `iters=5`, `mask=keep`, gfx1151:

| case | bf16 | f32 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| swa 256d 16q/8kv window=1024 | 62.39 ms | 63.89 ms | **1.024** |
| full 512d 16q/2kv window=0 | 129.87 ms | 146.80 ms | **1.130** |

## Why this ablation is the one to believe

`20260928T065333` used `--mask zero`, which skips the branch containing
`gemma4_warp_key_logit` -- both the `k_row` load and the multiplies and reduction.
It removed traffic and arithmetic together and could not separate them.

bf16 against f32 separates them: identical loop structure, identical key count,
identical FLOPs, identical mask, twice the bytes. It is the ablation the previous
entry said was needed and thought required a kernel change; it did not, because the
kernel already exports both entry points.

## What this resolves

The `keys`-scaling data in `20260928T065448` showed time growing faster than work
(4.27x work against 6.49x time from 256 to 2048 keys) and I attributed that to a
cache effect -- K at 1 MB fitting and K at 8.4 MB not. **With bytes shown to be
nearly free, that attribution is wrong.** The superlinear growth must come from
something else that varies with `keys`: wave quantization, or per-key loop overhead
that grows with the key count. That is now the open question, and it is the same
question the kernel's own comment points at.

It also re-closes a door I re-opened: **token-tiled flash attention is ruled out**
for the reason it was proposed -- reducing K re-reads. The re-reads are not what the
kernel is paying for.

## Caveat, stated because it limits the claim

f32 also doubles **shared-memory** traffic and register pressure, not only DRAM. So
the 13.0% on the full case may be LDS or occupancy rather than DRAM bytes. The
conclusion that holds either way is that **doubling every byte the kernel touches
costs at most 13%**, so no change whose benefit is measured in bytes moved can be
worth more than that here. That is enough to rule out flash tiling as a route to the
1.74x the prefill needs, which is the decision this was for.
