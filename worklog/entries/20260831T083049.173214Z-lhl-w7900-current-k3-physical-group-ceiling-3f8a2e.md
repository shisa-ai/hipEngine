# W7900 current K3 is capped by serial physical groups of four

## Summary

A clean current-source D1/D24 packet pair on the W7900 settles P13-D/#23. The
post-grouping K3 deficit is **not acceptance** and is not slower size-4 arithmetic
at wider logical widths. The dense adapter resolves `max_requests=4` under an
owner with capacity 8; EngineLoop then executes physical subgroups serially in
one scheduler tick. All ten C1/C3/C5/C8 waves observe exactly `[1]`, `[3]`,
`[4,1]`, and `[4,4]`. A size-4 subgroup has the same named-stage cost at C5 and
C8 (146.097 vs 146.340 ms/cycle), so C8 pays essentially the same subgroup twice.

The dominant per-subgroup boundary is accept/commit/readback: the two C8 groups
sum to 207.488 ms accept out of 292.681 ms named stages (70.9%), with selected
commit plus blocking readback accounting for 207.060 ms. `target_readback_ms` is
a dependency wait that retires queued verifier/accept/commit work, not pure D2H
transfer cost. Direct target submit is 67.692 ms across the two groups and is
secondary in the host-timer decomposition. D1-D24 decode-only MTP/AR is 1.722x,
1.146x, 0.611x, and 0.603x at C1/C3/C5/C8.

This corrects immutable entry
`20260830T165257.727025Z-lhl-k3-cycles-width-invariant-f23dfc.md`: constant
per-request cycles/token cannot rule out subgroup splitting, because every
request still participates in one speculative cycle per logical tick. It also
narrows `20260830T163534.455602Z-lhl-accept-per-batch-readback-f11c82.md`:
accept/readback is batched once **per physical subgroup**, not once across a
logical C5-C8 wave.
