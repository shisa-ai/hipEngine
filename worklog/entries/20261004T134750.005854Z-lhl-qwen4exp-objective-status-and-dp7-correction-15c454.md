## Correction: DP-7 measured the production TFLOP/s three days earlier

`20261004T132957.520268Z` recorded that the ledger's §4A figure of 2.573 ms /
20.9 TFLOP/s "is stale" and that the production figure is 40.65 TFLOP/s. **The
number is right and the novelty claim is wrong.**

**`DP-7` recorded it on 2026-09-28:** *"Historical 20.9 TFLOP/s at 1024 predates
the F16-input path. Current packet measures attn_qkv F16-input at
**40.54**/41.32/41.66 TFLOP/s for 1024/2048/4096 rows"* - and it added the
guidance that follows: *"The historical 30-TFLOP/s threshold is already exceeded
in this packet. Do not justify tile work using the old gap."* `DP-7` also
recorded zero numerical difference between the F32 and F16 input paths across 36
cases, which agrees with the `max abs diff` exactly 0 measured here.

**So my 40.65 confirms `DP-7` rather than discovering it**, and the ledger edit I
made repeated reasoning `DP-7` had already closed. §4A now cites `DP-7` and keeps
only what is actually new.

**What survives as new:** the four tile families had never been compared against
each other at one shape (`DP-7` measured one tile at three row counts), the hoist
had never been priced end to end (`RT-1` measured chunk 4096 but not this knob),
and the symbol census had not been recorded. So the **tile ordering** (128x256 >
128x128 > 64x256 > 64x128) and the **+4.09%** are new; the 40.65 figure and the
bit-identity are confirmations.

**Why this happened, recorded because it is the session's fourth instance.** I
read §4A, saw a number that disagreed with my measurement, and treated the
disagreement as a finding about the ledger. The artifact that resolved it was
`DP-7`, in the same file, forty lines away. **Reading a section is not reading its
block.**

## Which levers remain, from the ledger's own census

The census (`§11`) classifies all 71 rows by their own declared state. Reading it
against what has since been measured:

- **The prefill blocks are exhausted.** Dense: the scope lever is closed by
  verified accumulation-order drift, and the route is already ahead of the
  reference on throughput, leaving `DP-2` (7.478 ms) and `DP-6` (an 85.791 ms
  ceiling). Routed MoE: `MX-1` is refuted on both halves - it fails the numerics
  **and** its most favourable case is +53.9% slower, exceeding the entire 1,219.978
  ms prize - the repair is load-bearing (`prefill_last` fails at 2.159e-3 against a
  1e-3 limit when dropped), and `EG-3` closed repair efficiency by falsifying
  registers, instruction count and occupancy. Hyper-connections, GDN, attention and
  router rows are closed.
- **The actionable rows are evidence refresh (`X-*`), decode (`DE-*`), serving and
  depth (`RT-*`), and MTP (`MT-1`, `MT-8`).**
- **`MT-1` is the single concrete MTP item:** its body pass is found - rowbatch 2-8
  is width-invariant at 7/7 widths and sub-linear at 8x rows costing 2.42x - and
  what remains is composing it into a verifier body pass and measuring the complete
  pass.

**The binding constraint on the largest remaining prefill lever is numerical, not
mechanical.** Both the dense-scope lever (+18.876%) and the MoE-repair lever
(7.75%) are blocked by per-scope KL envelopes, and in both cases the blocking scope
is one the campaign's own rows already flagged as a threshold question. That is a
campaign-level decision, not a kernel one.
