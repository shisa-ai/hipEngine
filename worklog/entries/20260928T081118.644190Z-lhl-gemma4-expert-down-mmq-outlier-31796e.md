# the expert down projection runs at half its sibling's rate and the WMMA expert route is slower than dp4a

## Summary

Follow-up to `20260928T080550.751446Z-lhl-gemma4-prefill-operating-point-1a6631.md`,
which split the expert block into its kernels and found both GEMM families at
about 5 TFLOP/s. This unit reads the geometry from the GGUF tensor table instead
of estimating it, traces both expert kernels' launch configurations, and measures
the expert block at three prompt sizes.

**The headline is a single kernel.** The Q5_1 expert down projection runs at
**3.01 TFLOP/s** while the Q4_K gate_up projection it sits beside runs at
**6.19 TFLOP/s** -- same rows, same 128-thread workgroups, same wall time, half
the FLOPs and two thirds the bytes. It is 156.3 ms of an 865 ms prefill (18.1%)
at half its sibling's rate. Matching the sibling would save about 80 ms, 9.3
percent of the step.

The metric is unchanged: prefill at 512 tokens is 0.859-0.866 s (592-596 tok/s).

## Ground truth from the tensor table, not from estimates

The previous entry's dense figure was derived from an assumed geometry. Reading
`gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-UD-Q4_K_XL.gguf`'s own tensor table gives the exact split.
Per layer, expert tensors only:

| tensor | quant | n | GB | GFLOP at 512 (top-8 of 128) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ffn_gate_up_exps` | Q4_K | 29 | 8.279 | 941.9 |
| `ffn_gate_up_exps` | Q5_K | 1 | 0.349 | 32.5 |
| `ffn_down_exps` | Q5_1 | 29 | 5.519 | 471.0 |
| `ffn_down_exps` | Q8_0 | 1 | 0.270 | 16.2 |
| **total** | | 90 | **14.416** | **1461.6** |

and over the whole model: 1.180 GB of attention projections (1136.8 GFLOP at 512),
0.569 GB of dense FFN (548.1 GFLOP), 0.784 GB of embedding and head.

**So the prefill's quantized GEMM work is 3.15 TFLOP** -- 1.46 experts, 1.14
attention projections, 0.55 dense FFN -- and against the measured blocks that is
**4.9 TFLOP/s** (641.6 ms for experts plus `launch_gguf_linear`), against
llama.cpp's pp512 of 0.505 s at the same work, which is 6.2 TFLOP/s. The previous
entry's conclusion stands and is now on exact numbers: the 1.3x gap is uniform
across the GEMMs rather than concentrated in one route.

Two corrections to that entry, from the same table: the dense block is **1.69
TFLOP in 273.8 ms = 6.15 TFLOP/s** (it says 1.79 TFLOP and 6.35 TFLOP/s, and its
281.8 ms is the route A/B's dense-block figure rather than the census's
`launch_gguf_linear` leaf, which is 273.8 ms), and the dense route is **9.5x**
less weight-hungry per FLOP than the expert route rather than 8x. The weight
figure it quotes, 1.75 GB, is exact: 0.569 + 1.180 GB.

## The down projection is the outlier

Timed in place by `scripts/gemma4_expert_mmq_wrapper_split.py` at 512 tokens,
29 layers each:

| kernel | bytes | GFLOP | ms | GB/s | TFLOP/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q4_K gate_up MMQ (`mmq32`) | 8.279 GB | 941.9 | 152.1 | **54.4** | **6.19** |
| Q5_1 down MMQ | 5.519 GB | 471.0 | 156.3 | **35.3** | **3.01** |

The down reads 2/3 the bytes and does 1/2 the FLOPs, and takes 1.03x the time.
**It is 2.06x less efficient per FLOP and 1.54x less efficient per byte.**

The kernel trace shows the two are structurally different, not merely tuned
differently. `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` (which works here; `--pmc` does not):

| kernel | workgroup | grid | LDS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_...` | 128 | 5632 x 188 | 1792 B |
| `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel<...>` | 128 | 2816 x 128 | **0 B** |

Both launch 128 threads. The gate_up stages a 32-row weight tile and a 32-row
activation tile through LDS -- 32 x 20 plus 32 x 36 is exactly its 1792 bytes --
and its grid Y is the tile count. The down stages nothing: it is
`__launch_bounds__(128, 4)` with one thread per output column, walking its
expert's rows in `ROWS_PER_PASS = 16` groups with `float acc[16]` in registers,
and its grid Y is the expert index. **So the down is a rank-1-per-thread kernel
and the gate_up is a tiled one**, which is the most likely home of the 2x, and
which of the two structural differences carries it was not separated here.

## The obvious explanation is wrong, and the measurement says so

`ROWS_PER_PASS = 16` with `acc[16]` reads as a weight re-read: a thread walks its
own weight column once per 16-row pass, so an expert with 32 rows would read it
twice and one with 64 rows four times. That predicts the down's cost scaling with
rows per expert. It does not:

| prompt | rows/expert | gate_up ms/layer | down ms/layer | down / gate_up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 256 | 16 | 3.449 | 3.094 | 0.897 |
| 512 | 32 | 5.246 | 5.391 | 1.028 |
| 1024 | 64 | 5.244 | 5.347 | 1.020 |

**Both kernels are flat from 32 to 64 rows per expert**, which a per-pass re-read
would double, and the down is the *faster* of the two at 16 rows. So the re-read
is not happening -- the loop's own comment about the trailing partial DS4 group
suggests the compiler is handling the pass structure differently than the source
reads -- and the 2x is inside the per-row work rather than in repeated weight
traffic.

**The other result in that table is worth its own line: the expert block does not
get more expensive per token as the batch grows.** From 512 to 1024 tokens the
expert rows double, the tile count grows by about 1.36x, and both kernels are
within 0.1 percent of their 512-token time. So the expert block's 370 ms is set
by touching each selected expert's matrix, not by the token count, and a
per-token throughput argument does not describe it.

## The WMMA expert route is slower than the scalar one

The Q4_K selected-prefill file implements, exports and registers int8 matrix-core
variants of exactly this kernel -- `selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_wmma32_prefill_compact32`
and `..._wmma64_...`, using `__builtin_amdgcn_wmma_i32_16x16x16_iu8_w32` -- and
the gemma4 expert path does not use them. It hard-codes the scalar dp4a symbol
(`gguf_q4_k_mmq_dp4a`). The Q5_K branch one screen below takes a WMMA iu8 route
instead, which is the natural experiment: the same route family, the same
geometry, one layer.

Layer 29's Q5_K experts, from the same probe:

| kernel | bytes | ms | GB/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `q5_k_selected_dual_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill` | 349 MB | 9.956 | 35.0 |
| `q5_k_selected_dual_sparse_exact_repair` | -- | 3.546 | -- |
| **total** | | **13.50** | **25.8** |

Against the Q4_K dp4a route's 5.246 ms for 285.6 MB at 54.4 GB/s, the WMMA route
is **2.1x slower per byte** even counting only its fast pass, and 2.6x slower
counting the repair. **Wiring the Q4_K WMMA variants into the gemma4 expert path
is therefore not the lever it looks like**, and that is worth the ten minutes it
took rather than the day it would have taken to wire and test. The comparison is
across quants -- Q5_K dequant is not Q4_K dequant -- so it bounds the idea rather
than proving the instruction set is the cause; an A/B on the same quant would be
the clean form, and the evidence does not justify building one.

## Next

1. **The down kernel's 2x.** It is 18.1 percent of the step and the only measured
   route running at half a sibling's rate on the same rows. The structural
   difference is rank-1-per-thread with no LDS against a tiled kernel with LDS;
   the next step is to separate the tile shape from the staging, most cheaply by
   giving the down a 32-row tile in the same style as the gate_up.
2. **Whether the gate_up's own ceiling is the tile or the dequant.** It is flat in
   rows from 32 to 64, so it is not row-bound, and 54.4 GB/s is under half of what
   the dense family reaches; the `mmq32` body dequantizes Q4_K per qword with
   `gguf_q4_k_mmq_dp4a`, and whether that or the 32-wide tile is the limit was not
   separated.
3. The pass-1 `#pragma unroll 2` that the compiler refuses, still open from the
   previous entry, and still the only attention thread with a named mechanism.
