# YuE2 solver stage breakdown: device-bound, projections then attention

## Summary

The tensor-core attention took the 32-step solve of `mandarin-off-s1234` to 32.22 s
against the pinned upstream's 27.32 s. This unit answers where the remaining 4.9 s
sits, because the two candidate explanations — the attention kernel's 1.67x gap to
the reference's kernel, and the projections' GEMM rate — call for very different
work.

The answer: the projections are the largest single item (14.7 s, 46%), the attention
is 8.7 s (27%), the step-independent conditioning is 3.44 s (11%) and everything else
is 3.4 s (11%). The solver is **device-bound**: host enqueue for one evaluation is
28 ms against 419 ms of device time.

## Changes

- `scripts/yue2_solver_stage_profile.py` (new): one velocity evaluation timed with
  HIP events, with `--skip none|attention|gemm` to suppress a stage. Reports the
  device time, the wall time, the host enqueue time, the launch counts and the
  projection shapes.
- `scripts/yue2_gemm_rate.py` (new): hipBLASLt's best algorithm per YuE2 projection
  shape at rows 1299, with the zero-workspace candidates reported separately.
- `benchmarks/results/yue2_solver_stage_profile_20260917.json` (new): the measured
  split, both traps, the library's own rates and the two remaining gaps.
- `benchmarks/README.md`: the solver section now carries the measured table and
  replaces the synchronize-wrapped two-step split, which understated the GEMMs and
  overstated the elementwise remainder. `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md` gets the dated row.

## Method, and two traps

`scripts/yue2_solver_stage_profile.py` times one velocity evaluation with HIP events
and splits it by **suppressing one launch at a time** (`--skip none|attention|gemm`).
Two obvious approaches do not work, and both cost time here:

- **rocprofv3 --kernel-trace** reports 40.22 s of kernel time for a 27.3 s replay.
  Its per-dispatch overhead is comparable to the elementwise kernels it measures, so
  it cannot split a mix that is 55% GEMM, 32% attention and 13% small kernels. It
  remains the right tool for naming kernels and reading their geometry (it gave the
  VGPR count and shared-memory size below).
- **A synchronize around each kernel** destroys the pipeline. With 200 projection
  launches per evaluation the sync cost alone (0.3 ms each) exceeds the kernel time
  being measured; an earlier reading of 660 ms "of projections" was almost entirely
  sync. The same trap explains a false "host-bound" conclusion: an enqueue-only
  measurement of 433 ms looked like host cost, but it is the command queue throttling
  the host once the device falls behind. With HIP events the host is 28 ms and the
  device is 419 ms.

## Measured split

One velocity evaluation, 28 layers, 1 299 rows, 2 695 keys:

| | Device time | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| full | 418.9 ms | |
| without the attention launch | 282.9 ms | attention **136 ms (32%)** |
| without the projection launches | 189.5 ms | projections **229 ms (55%)** |
| neither | 53.5 ms | everything else **53 ms (13%)** |

Projections: 200 launches, 3 662 GFLOP, i.e. **16.0 TFLOP/s**. Host enqueue 28 ms.

At the product's 32 steps (64 evaluations at 418.9 ms) plus 3.44 s of conditioning
— of which 2.94 s is the AR prefill attention over 28 calls — that projects to 30.2 s
against the measured 32.22 s, the difference being the between-evaluation state
updates.

## Validation

- The three ablation runs are internally consistent: full 418.9 ms = attention
  136 ms + projections 229 ms + everything else 53.5 ms.
- The split reproduces the end-to-end measurement: 64 evaluations at 418.9 ms plus
  3.44 s of conditioning projects to 30.2 s against the 32.22 s the e2e gate reports
  for the same case, the difference being the between-evaluation state updates.
- The GEMM probe agrees with the in-place rate to within the expected in-place
  penalty: 19.4 TFLOP/s back to back against 16.0 TFLOP/s inside the solve.
- `python3 scripts/sync_benchmark_readme.py --check` passes after the README edit.

## What each remaining gap costs to close

**Projections (14.7 s).** `scripts/yue2_gemm_rate.py` measures hipBLASLt's own best
algorithm per shape at rows 1299: k/v 30.2 TFLOP/s, q 28.9, o 28.7, gate/up 20.0,
down 20.1, and a 4 096³ square GEMM 21.0. That is 19.4 TFLOP/s across the mix and
169 ms per evaluation, against the 229 ms the solve actually takes — **74% of the
library's own in-place rate**, and the library's algorithm list contains nothing
faster than the index the runtime already picks (the fastest zero-workspace
candidate). Closing the in-place gap is worth at most 3.9 s; going past ~20 TFLOP/s
needs a hand-written GEMM, since that is the library's ceiling on this device against
a ~51 TFLOP/s fp16 peak for 40 CUs.

**NAR attention (8.7 s).** 28.7 GFLOP per call in 3.85 ms is 7.5 TFLOP/s, 15% of the
device's fp16 peak, against the reference kernel's 12.4 TFLOP/s. The kernel is not
WMMA-throughput-bound: it uses 248 VGPRs, which caps it at two blocks of 128 threads
per CU (12.5% of the 2 048-thread limit) with 34 816 B of shared memory, and its K/V
staging branches between the AR and NAR buffers for every 16-byte pack. Matching the
reference's 2.31 ms per call would take 8.7 s to 5.2 s.

## Next

The attention is the better-understood lever and the one with a concrete mechanism
(occupancy from register pressure, and the per-pack gather in the staging loop). The
projections are close to a ceiling that only a hand-written GEMM would move, so they
are not worth attention until the attention is at the reference's rate.
