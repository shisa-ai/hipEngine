# Surya vision-attention tiling wall-clock cost

## Summary

The query-row tiling was retained on memory and exactness evidence — it made the
300-DPI A4 grid admissible at 535 MB instead of 56.5 GB — but its wall-clock cost
had never been measured. It is now, on the grids that run both ways.

The page-scale grids cannot be measured against dense at all: the dense score
matrix is 56.5 GB at 34320 patches and 206 GB at 65536, so admission rejected
them outright and there is no "before" to time. The two grids below are the ones
that fit densely, so they bound the tiling's cost rather than the page case.

## Results

gfx1151 (Radeon 8060S, Ryzen AI Max+ Pro 395), fp32, one runner per budget, one
discarded warm-up `vision_forward`, median of 7 wall-clock timings around
`vision_forward` only (preprocessing and scratch allocation excluded). `None`
disables the budget and runs one dense tile.

| page | patches | budget | query block | tiles | score scratch | median | vs dense |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| page_dense | 1024 | dense | 1024 | 1 | 48.0 MiB | 192.27 ms | 1.000x |
| page_dense | 1024 | 512 MiB | 1024 | 1 | 48.0 MiB | 190.33 ms | 1.010x |
| page_dense | 1024 | 64 MiB | 1024 | 1 | 48.0 MiB | 193.69 ms | 0.993x |
| page_dense | 1024 | 8 MiB | 170 | 7 | 8.0 MiB | **138.26 ms** | **1.391x** |
| page_ja_fit | 4096 | dense | 4096 | 1 | 768.0 MiB | 1045.58 ms | 1.000x |
| page_ja_fit | 4096 | 512 MiB | 2730 | 2 | 511.9 MiB | 1118.47 ms | 0.935x |
| page_ja_fit | 4096 | 64 MiB | 341 | 13 | 63.9 MiB | 1177.79 ms | 0.888x |
| page_ja_fit | 4096 | 8 MiB | 42 | 98 | 7.9 MiB | 1129.09 ms | 0.926x |

Two findings worth keeping:

1. **Tiling is faster at 1024 patches.** An 8 MiB budget splits the 1024-patch
   grid into 7 tiles of 170 query rows and runs **1.391x faster** than the single
   1024x1024 tile, while cutting score scratch 48.0 -> 8.0 MiB. A 1024x1024
   score tile per head is a poor shape for the batched GEMM; the smaller tile
   occupies the device better.
2. **The cost is not monotonic in tile count.** At 4096 patches, 98 tiles of 42
   rows (1129.09 ms) beat 13 tiles of 341 rows (1177.79 ms) and nearly match the
   2-tile 512 MiB default (1118.47 ms). Tile *shape* matters more than tile
   count, so a budget chosen only to hit a memory target can land on a worse
   plan than a tighter one.

The default 512 MiB budget costs 6.5% at 4096 patches and is a no-op below
~512 MB of dense score (i.e. up to ~4000 patches at 8 heads), so the default
trades a bounded 7-11% at 4096 patches for a 106x-386x score reduction at page
scale. Both numbers are now in the artifact, so the trade is explicit.

## Validation

- The measurement is deterministic in ordering: an order-controlled re-check
  (`8 MiB, None, 64 MiB, 512 MiB, 8 MiB, None`) reproduced every row within
  0.5% and in both orders, so the 1.391x at 1024 patches is not a clock-ramp
  artifact.
- `python3 scripts/sync_benchmark_readme.py --check` — synchronized.
- The tiling's arithmetic is separately gated: the query-block plan is
  bit-identical to dense over 4562 teacher-forced rows
  (`benchmarks/results/2026-09-12-gfx1151-surya-numerical-gate.json`).
