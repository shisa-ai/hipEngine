## Target A: decode

**Measured baseline: 35.35 ms/token, 28.29 tok/s** (route ablation, `20260928T072823`).
The step moves **3255.7 MB**, which is 36.0% of DRAM peak; the floor for those bytes is
12.72 ms = 78.6 tok/s. The objective's 41.9 tok/s needs **53.3% of DRAM peak**.

**Excluded, with the evidence:**

| hypothesis | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| weight-moving kernels are slow | **no** -- dense rows=1 GEMV at 87.0% of peak, lm_head 79.4%, experts 91.6% | `20260928T072916` |
| too many norms per layer | **no** -- the 5 are the dual dense/MoE branch structure, architecturally required | `20260928T073020` |
| launch overhead dominates | **no** -- ~6-8 us of the 23.1 us; fusion ceiling is 2.7 ms of 10.37 | `20260928T073101` |

**What remains: the dependency chain.** 448 of 762.7 launches per step move zero weight
bytes (58.7%, 14 per layer) and cost **10.37 ms = 29.3% of the step** at **23.1 us per
launch**, which is execution and dependency latency, not queue overhead. The fix is a
shorter chain or overlap -- more work per kernel -- not fewer launches.

**Two instruments must be used together and they already disagree usefully once:** the
route ablation (`scripts/gemma4_decode_route_ablation.py`) and the traffic census
(`scripts/gemma4_decode_traffic_census.py`). Cross-checking them located a real bug
(`20260928T072823`). **Check any new arm's saving against the census's bytes for that
route before believing it** -- that is the check that would have caught the broken expert
arm a day earlier.

**Known gaps in the ablation, both still open:**
- `exact_prefill_tile8x4` runs on the dense path at rows=8 and **no arm isolates it**;
- the arms overlap, so individual savings are not additive.

## Target B: prefill

**584 vs 1013 tok/s.** Fully diagnosed across eleven entries: the attention kernel holds
one logit per live key in LDS, which forces three LDS round trips per key visit (the
latency chain), caps token tiling at TQ ~ 4, and caps the context at 15872/15616 keys.
**An online-softmax kernel removes all three.** Acceptance test: exact equality against
`gemma4_attention_prefill_*` on the same device. Bench:
`scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py`.

**The kernel is unwritten.** It is the highest-value single action on the objective.

## Method note

Across this stretch the decisive evidence came from reading a docstring, bisecting a
function, three greps, one diff, and two small probes -- **not from any benchmark
invented for the purpose**. Five separate times a cheap independent check overturned a
plausible-looking number I was about to build on: the `us/head` figure, a path-based "not
mine" claim, an expert share below its own noise floor, a dense efficiency derived from a
byte total covering a different kernel, and the norm-count hypothesis.

**The recurring error is accepting a number at the resolution it is printed rather than
the resolution it was measured at.** Every instance was caught by one command, and in
three cases the command used an artifact already loaded.
