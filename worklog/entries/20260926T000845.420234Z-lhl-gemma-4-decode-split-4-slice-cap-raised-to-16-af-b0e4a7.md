# Gemma 4 decode split: 4-slice cap raised to 16 after measurement (+5.3%), and the teacher-forced gate's split blind spot found

## Summary

`decode_slices` capped the two-phase decode split at 4 slices on the strength
of an argument rather than a measurement; measured properly, 16 is optimal or
within 4% at every point tested. Raising the cap moves the campaign's metric row
**45.8427 -> 48.2630 tok/s (+5.3%)** and the 4096p row 42.7662 -> 45.9349
(+7.4%). The same investigation found that the campaign's teacher-forced gate
never exercises the decode split at all, which corrects a standing claim.

## Changes

`decode_slices` (`hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_attention.py`)
capped the split at 4 slices. Its docstring said more slices is better until the
cap and never said why the cap was 4. Paired interleaved arms, one pass per arm
per run, us per launch on GPU1:

| geometry | keys | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| sliding | 1024 | 192.0 | 168.0 | **159.3** | 165.6 |
| sliding | 2048 | 322.5 | 288.5 | **264.3** | 265.7 |
| sliding | 8192 | 865.9 | 708.6 | **617.4** | 632.0 |
| full | 1024 | 227.9 | 189.8 | **197.6** | 197.8 |
| full | 2048 | 357.4 | 297.9 | **292.4** | 290.6 |
| full | 8192 | 1043.4 | 667.5 | 658.7 | **609.9** |

16 is optimal or within 4% at every point but full/8192, where 32 is 8% better.
The per-geometry refinement is deliberately not taken: iteration 35 had just
been reverted for extrapolating a one-shape microbenchmark win to the whole
model, and one number is one thing to justify. Both geometries now take 16
slices above the 1024-key entry.

The 512-keys-per-slice doubling rule is retired. It returned 2 slices at exactly
1024 keys, where the table shows 64 keys per slice to be the fastest
configuration measured, so the floor it encoded was simply wrong. `decode_slices`
no longer takes `head_dim`; nothing above the threshold depends on it, and it had
one production caller.

Campaign rows, incumbent -> candidate: **1024p 45.8427 -> 48.2630 tok/s
(+5.3%)**, 4096p 42.7662 -> **45.9349 (+7.4%)**, 512p 47.9667 -> 48.0076 and
128p 51.7789 -> 52.0185 flat. The two flat rows are below the split's entry
threshold, so they take the single-kernel path with byte-identical arguments and
are untouched by construction rather than merely unregressed. The metric row is
+200% over the 16.0931 baseline and 60.2% of the campaign's 70%-of-Qwen stop
condition.

## The gate does not cover the split

The teacher-forced evaluator was run and reports `kl_max 0.0` exactly with zero
top-1 flips over all 1023 rows. That is not a strong result, it is a blind spot:
`capture_chain` in `scripts/gemma4_teacher_forced_gate.py` feeds the prompt one
token at a time from an empty cache (`runner.forward([ids[position]])`), so key
counts run 1, 2, 3, ... 1023 - one token below the split's 1024-key entry
threshold - and `decode_slices` returns 1 for every row. Instrumented: 30 calls
per forward (one per layer), keys `[1]` at position 0 through `[5]` at position
4, `slices=1` at every one. After a 600-token prefill the same instrumentation
reports keys 601, 602, 603, 604, so prefill-then-decode does engage the split.

Consequence: the 2026-09-25 split gate result (`kl_max 0.006746`) measured the
sliding read range, not the split, and that claim is corrected rather than left
standing. Until the fix lands, the split's production-profile status rests on
kernel-level parity and on the identical greedy tokens above, not on the KL gate.
