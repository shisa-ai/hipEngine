# at the production width the three gate/up designs converge, and the 13 percent was a fixture artifact

## Summary

The Q4_K screen's 13 percent margin came from a 5504-wide fixture. Narrowing it toward
the gate/up projection's real 1408 columns collapses the gap:

| fixture | `selected-wmma` | `t16-mmq32` | `mmq32` | spread |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5504 wide | 3.40 | 3.30 | 2.84 | **1.20x** |
| **1440 wide** | **3.12** | **3.14** | **2.71** | **1.16x** |
| production, in situ at 1408 | -- | -- | **3.00** | |

(TMAC/s. The 1440-wide runs are `--out-features-a 1408 --out-features-b 32`; the harness
rejects `--out-features-b 0` and the MMQ32 modes require multiples of 32, so 1440 is the
closest fixture available against production's 1408.)

**Two things change when the width narrows.** `selected-wmma` falls from 3.40 to 3.12 --
so the rate is a property of the shape, not only of the design -- and the three designs
converge to within 1 percent of each other at the top. The production route's in-situ
3.00 is then within **4 percent of the best design measured on a comparable fixture.**

## The candidate is closed

**There is no 13 percent available from route selection.** The best case is 3.14 against
3.00, about **4 percent of 156.5 ms = 6.3 ms of a 687.7 ms prefill, 0.9 percent** -- and
that comparison still crosses a fixture boundary (1440 against 1408) and an environment
boundary (the harness times a bare kernel loop; the in-situ number includes the launch and
the wrapper). Against the noise this branch has already measured on this kernel family, a
0.9 percent expected gain is not a candidate.

**And it closes the repack with it.** The T16 layout reached 3.14 at the production width,
against a shipped kernel's 3.12. `20260928T164000` closed the repack because a shipped
kernel already beat it at the wide fixture; this entry closes it at the production width
too, where the two are within 1 percent. Both the route switch and the repack are now
measured shut.

## The hypothesis this unit started with was wrong

The plan was to reach the WMMA route by extending
`_gemma4_project_experts_gate_up_wmma_iu8`'s quant-key condition from `gguf_q5_k` to
`gguf_q4_k`. **That function is Q5_K-specific**: it imports
`build_gguf_q5_k_q8_1_selected_prefill` and `gguf_q5_k_selected_dual_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill_bf16_bf16_out`,
and its docstring describes a 3-plane residual int8 path with risk repair. It is not a
generic WMMA entry point and not a drop-in for a q4_k weight. The fixture-narrowing
measurement above reaches the same answer without a code change, which is the better path
and the one taken.

## What this leaves for the prefill

**The gate/up kernel is at its design's practical rate at this shape, and the route choice
is not where the remaining 1.36x lives.** At the production width every design measured
lands at 2.7 to 3.1 TMAC/s, which is a narrow band for three materially different
implementations -- the same convergence the shared 49 GB/s showed across two quantizations
in `20260928T144500`. Two independent measurements now say the limit is not the design.

The prefill's remaining terms are the 173.97 ms of attention and the 184.85 ms of dense
projections and lm head, neither of which has been priced against a floor the way the
expert routes now have.
