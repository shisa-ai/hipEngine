# The q5_k iu8 route is measured prior art, and the prize is 2-3% not 5%

## Summary

Supersedes `worklog/entries/20260927T155730.440545Z-lhl-gemma4-layer29-q5k-route-c7ab1d.md` on one point and adds the reconnaissance that
entry lacked. It said wiring the q5_k iu8 route was worth about 100 ms, 5% of
prefill, by reading the 17x gap between a q4_k layer's gate_up and layer 29's as
what the fast route would recover. That reading was wrong.

**The route is measured prior art in this repository.** `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md`
records, for Qwen, that the iu8-WMMA + Kahan-risk + sparse pair2-exact repair
route is **bit-identical to the exact pair2 parent in all 32 screen cases** and
**1.50-2.00x operation-complete at m=1..8**, and that promoting it improved every
prefill case with PP512/1024/4096 at **+7.602%/+8.842%/+8.361%**.

Two things follow. The win is bounded by that 1.50-2.00x, so layer 29's 110.89 ms
becomes roughly 55-74 ms and the saving is **37-56 ms, 1.9-2.8% of prefill** - not
5%. And because the route is exact rather than approximate, the gate it needs is
bit-exactness against the strict row4 parent rather than a KL envelope, which is
much cheaper to run than the q4_k route's numerical gate.

**The 17x is still unexplained, and that is the more interesting question.** Even
after a 1.5-2x speedup, layer 29's gate_up would sit an order of magnitude above a
q4_k layer's 6.53 ms. Either the 30 MoE layers do not share expert shapes - the
launch counts (29 plus 1) and Gemma 4 26B-A4B's uniform MoE design argue they do,
but I have not read the shapes from the artifact - or the q4_k fused MMQ32 route
is doing something the q5_k path has no equivalent of. Resolving that decides
whether the ceiling here is 2% or much more, and it is cheaper than the port.
