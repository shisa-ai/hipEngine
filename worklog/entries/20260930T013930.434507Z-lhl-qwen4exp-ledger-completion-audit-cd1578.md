# qwen4exp ledger completion audit: both phases are 81-88% MoE + dense linear

## Summary

The objective was to work through every remaining candidate in
`docs/QWEN4EXP-OPTIMIZATION-LEDGER.md`, keep what improves performance, and update the
results. The ledger has 60 rows and 26 are open or blocked. Rather than re-litigate each
row's argument, this unit builds the missing artifact: a **cost gate**. Both phases now
have a measured per-family breakdown, so every open row can be charged against the slice
it can actually move.

The result is that the two phases have the same shape, and it is not where most of the
open rows are aimed. **MoE + dense linear is 81.6% of prefill and 88.3% of decode.** A
lever that helps one of those two families helps both phases; a lever aimed anywhere else
competes for a remainder of at most ~5%.

Three findings came out of it that the ledger did not previously contain:

1. **`DP-9` is re-ranked UP, not down.** Filed as "measured-owner prerequisite", its
   target turns out to be ~35% of decode. Five of the decode `linear` family's six largest
   launch shapes share one signature (`wg=128, VGPR=48, LDS=512`) and serve both `linear:*`
   and `moe:*` roles.
2. **The comparator is inside the same capture.** `linear:root.lm_head` reaches
   227.8 GB/s = 103% of the 221 GB/s roof while the routed-expert owner of the same era
   measured 63.43 GB/s = 28.7% - a 3.6x spread at the same block width.
3. **Two of the four giant prefill kernels were already on their optimal path**, verified
   from the trace rather than the manifest: the 18.05% kernel is *already* the two-stage
   K-pipelined form, and the 21.77% one is the fastest registered owner per `ED-4`.

## Validation

- **The decode windowing is validated against a committed measurement, not assumed.** The
  steady-state repetition (`rep2`) reproduces the previously recorded DE-2 breakdown
  exactly: moe **583 kernels**, linear **545 kernels**; 24.733 vs 24.550 ms/step and 23.121
  vs 23.166 ms/step. The X-4 packet's own README independently records 1,771 kernels/step
  and a 54.218 ms sum; this measurement finds 1,771 kernels and 54.173 ms.
- **A bug was found and fixed in the audit's own tooling.** An earlier revision mixed
  `x4full` marker timestamps with `x4decode` kernel timestamps. Separate `rocprofv3` runs
  have separate clocks, so that join was meaningless; both now come from one stem.
- **A classifier error was found, fixed, and recorded.** `scripts/qwen4exp_family_share.py`
  matched its `qsa` pattern against any name containing `sparse`, sweeping in
  `*_selected_sparse_exact_repair_*` - MoE expert-repair kernels, where "sparse" means
  sparse *expert selection*, not sparse attention. QSA was overstated as 7.90%; corrected
  it is **0.22%** and MoE rises 39.70% -> 47.39%. This is the single most consequential
  correction in the unit, because it cost-gates `QA-2`'s Tier-B tensor-core build out of
  its priority slot.
- **Limits recorded rather than smoothed over:** the shape split is by launch signature and
  a signature is not unique (both `q5_1_selected_sparse_exact_repair_row_publish` and
  `gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel` carry the same one); decode kernel names are
  unavailable by construction because `--kernel-rename` replaces them with ROCTX roles, so
  the *slot* attribution is exact while *kernel identity* is inferred. The ranking does not
  depend on that inference.
- `python3 scripts/worklog.py check` clean; ledger rows re-parsed at their expected cell
  counts.
