# Route the Gemma 4 expert gate/up through the four-wave Q8T16 schedule

## Summary

The mid-band census named `gguf_q8_0_t16_prefill_wmma_nwave` (the expert
gate/up Q8_0 GEMM) the largest mid-band prefill kernel at 19-24% of every
8192-32768 step. The four-wave LCP-3 schedule for that kernel family exists
and the gemma4 registration already binds the auto-4wave wrapper, but the
policy gate `out_features >= 2048` -- written for other models' shapes --
excluded the gemma4 expert gate/up width 1408 (= 2 x 704), so the nwave
kernel served it.

An in-process A/B at the exact shape (1024 routed rows x 2816 -> 1408, real
kernel launches, seven-rep medians): four-wave 6.83 ms vs nwave 7.72 ms,
**+13.2%**, 2-wave unchanged. 1408 is a whole multiple of the schedule's
128-column tile, so it pays no tail waste. The policy now covers the measured
width only (`out_features == 1408` joins `>= 2048`); every other sub-2048
width keeps the previous behavior, pinned by RED/GREEN tests, and
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_Q8_T16_PREFILL_2WAVE=0` remains the rollback for the whole
wide-wave family.

End-to-end through the real artifact, back-to-back rounds at the campaign's
8192 shape (samples 2, warmup 1): four-wave on 1166.9 / 1166.1 tok/s vs
off 1126.2 / 1122.2 -- **+3.6% and +3.9%**, replicating across rounds, both
arms status ok with public parity True, decode unchanged. The end-to-end gain
matches the microbench arithmetic (+13.2% on a 23.7% share ~ +3%). The 8192
ladder row moves from 0.88x to ~0.91x of llama.cpp.

Fixture honesty note: the row-count sweep below 1024 rows crashed the auto
kernel on a synthetic tile layout -- the loader packs Q8 tiles in a layout
my fixture did not reproduce, so sub-1024-row microbench numbers are not
evidence here; the real artifact's router distribution (buckets of ~8-80 rows
plus the 1024-row worst case) is covered by the end-to-end A/B instead.
