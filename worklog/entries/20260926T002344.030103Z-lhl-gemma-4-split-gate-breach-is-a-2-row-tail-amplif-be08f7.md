# Gemma 4 split gate breach is a 2-row tail amplification on a cycled prompt, not a broad drift

## Summary

Diagnostic, no product change. The two-phase decode split breaches the
production `kl_max` bar on 1-2 of 1023 teacher-forced rows. Row-level analysis
shows this is a tail amplification on a small number of peaked rows, not a broad
arithmetic drift: the median row KL is 2.592e-08 and the 99th percentile is
3.955e-05, both orders of magnitude inside their bars, and top-1 never flips on
any row. It also shows that the earlier reading of `max|delta logit|` = 18-20 as
the explanation was wrong.

## Changes

None. This iteration is measurement only; no product path, kernel or policy
changed. The campaign document carries the numbers and the decision they leave
open.

## Validation

Against the frozen strict baseline (prompt 2048, prefill 1024, keys 1025-2047,
1023 paired rows), shipped 4-slice split:

| statistic | p50 | p90 | p99 | p99.9 | max |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| row KL | 2.592e-08 | 1.040e-06 | 3.955e-05 | 3.611e-02 | 5.559e-02 |
| max\|delta logit\| | 1.085 | 2.797 | 7.803 | 22.586 | 22.586 |

- A one-logit difference is the **median**, not the exception: 571 of 1023 rows
  exceed 1, 37 exceed 5, 5 exceed 10. It is almost always invisible in KL because
  it sits in the tail - the row with the largest difference of all (row 863,
  22.586) has KL 0.0000 - and `corr(kl, max|delta logit|)` is only 0.288. The two
  are largely independent; the rejected candidate's record tied them together
  and that reading is withdrawn here.
- Rows over 1e-3 KL: **2 of 1023**. Over 0.05: 1. The breach is a tail of the
  distribution, not a drift.
- Top-1 never flips. On the outlier rows the candidate is *sharper*
  (p_top1 0.995465 -> 0.999999), so its top logits differ from strict's by 5-15,
  far above f32 association error - that is what collapses the tail.
- The KL is carried by mid-rank tokens: on row 864 the largest single
  contribution is at rank 7220 of 262144, and the top-1 token contributes a
  *negative* 8% of the total.
- The divergence is largely shared across slice counts - 4 and 16 slices differ
  from strict comparably and in the same direction, and the two split arms differ
  from each other by far less than either differs from strict. A difference that
  is mostly identical at 4 and 16 slices must come from the code the two share:
  phase 1, which the design claims is the incumbent kernel's own passes 1 and 2
  and which nothing has verified bit-for-bit, or the combine's division by
  phase 1's denominator.
- The frozen prompt is cycled prose: `exact_prompt_ids` repeats a six-sentence
  corpus to reach 2048 tokens, so the breaching rows are repeated-context rows
  where the model is legitimately near-certain. `kl_max` is a single order
  statistic and on a peaked reference it is dominated by the residual tail.

- `python3 scripts/docs/check_all.py` -> all gates pass except the pre-existing,
  unrelated `published command drift` failure
- `python3 scripts/worklog.py check` -> clean

## Next

- **Next experiment, specified:** verify phase 1 bit-for-bit. Compare the split's
  phase-1 per-key weights and denominator against the single kernel's own pass-2
  values, at the rows that diverge, instead of comparing end-to-end logits. If
  they differ, that is the defect and the fix is in phase 1; if they are
  identical, the divergence is the combine's association order and the question
  becomes whether an absolute `kl_max` bar is the right binding criterion for a
  greedy workload on a peaked reference.
- **Decision owed to the lead**, recorded rather than taken: make phase 1
  bit-identical, re-derive the `kl_max` bar's applicability to peaked references,
  or return the strict path as the default. `kl_mean`, `kl_p95`, `kl_p99` and
  top-1 - the bars a greedy workload depends on - pass in every arm with room to
  spare. The reverted slice-count table is worth +5.3% at the metric row and
  +7.4% at 4096p whenever this is resolved.
