# the gemma4 context length is hard capped at 15616 by the resident logit design

## Summary

The last unknown in this chain is resolved, and the limit is **live and
user-visible**.

`capacity` is the runner's context length. The field's own docstring
(`hipengine/runtime/gemma4.py:468`) says so: *"`capacity` is the number of positions
the cache holds."* It is a required dataclass field with no default, so every caller
supplies it, and the callers that matter supply it from configuration --
`engine_loop.py:488` passes `capacity=configured_capacity`.

The construction path then validates it against the attention kernel's LDS
requirement:

```python
for attention in config.attention:
    gemma4_attention_shared_bytes(head_dim=attention.head_dim, keys=self.capacity)
```

which raises above 15,872 keys at `head_dim=256` and **15,616 at `head_dim=512`**.

**So: a Gemma 4 runner configured with a context longer than 15,616 tokens cannot be
constructed.** The model's context length is capped by a shared-memory budget in one
kernel, and the cap is hit at configuration time rather than at request time.

## What is correct here

The refusal is loud and named, raised during construction with the geometry and the
byte count. Nothing is silently truncated and no context is silently dropped. The
defect is the cap's existence, not its manner.

## What I have not done

  to watch it raise. The claim rests on reading the construction path, the field's
  docstring, and one configured call site, and it is stated as a code reading rather
  than a measurement.
- I did not check whether any caller clamps `configured_capacity` to the ceiling
  before passing it, which would make the cap unreachable in practice. That is the
  one remaining way this could be latent rather than live, and it is one grep.
- I did not check what the tested configuration used. The objective's numbers (584
  tok/s prefill, 27.31 tok/s decode) came from runs that obviously constructed
  successfully, so their capacity was at or below the ceiling.
