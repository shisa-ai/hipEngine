# Wire the wide dense route's F16 activation hoist at launch time

## Summary

The promoted wide-row Q8_0 dense prefill route converts its activation from f32
to f16 inside the K loop, once per column block, and re-reads the f32 activation
the same number of times. The measured composite (conversion pass then
f16-input tile, no sharing) is worth **553.9 ms over the route's 1056 launches**
in a 4096-token prefill, bit-identical output, and nothing wired it: the route
shipped on the f32-input owner.

This unit wires it: a bounded runner-owned workspace, one conversion pass per
launch on the consumer stream, dispatch to the registered `*_f16in_*` sibling,
and the f32-input owner kept as the fallback. **The engine measurement is not
done yet** (see Blockers); what is validated here is the mechanism, on the GPU,
including the failure mode that matters.

## The bug this unit found, and why the first census failed

The first census of the shipped default after wiring showed **792 launches of
`..._dense_wide256_f32_f32_out` and none of the sibling** — the swap was inert,
including with the env override forced. The cause: the sibling's registry key is
`KernelKey("hip_gfx1151", ...)` (the package backend) while
`register_gguf_q8_0_dense_wide_kernels` registers under `hip_gfx1100`, so a bare
`is_registered` on the sibling key is False until
`_ensure_linear_kernel_registered` loads the backend package. The dispatch path
already does that; the launch-time lookup did not. Both the helper test and the
call-site test now pin the population step, and the call-site test is the one
that would have caught it.

## Validation

- 232 unit tests pass across the launch dispatch, the hoist contract, the route
  gate and the dispatch tests.
- 7 GPU tests pass under `/tmp/run-gpu.sh -m pytest
  tests/test_gpu_qwen4exp_dense_wide_f16_activation_hoist.py`.
- Bit identity is exact (`np.array_equal`, not a tolerance) between the
  f32-input owner, the hoisted composite, and the fallback path.

## Reset and graph behavior (reasoned, not measured)

- `reset()` documents "without freeing weights or scratch", so the workspace
  pointer stays valid across a reset; a new session allocates its own buffer and
  binds its own workspace.
- `MoeGraphCache._capture` replays the captured closure once and **rejects the
  graph unless the replay is bit-identical to the eager reference**. A capture
  that happens while the workspace is bound therefore validates the hoisted
  conversion plus tile by the engine's own gate, and the two ABIs are
  bit-identical anyway.
- A graph captured without a workspace keeps the f32-input kernel; replays do
  not re-dispatch, so a later bound workspace cannot change what a captured
  graph runs.
- `close()` frees graphs and buffers without executing a graph in between.

## Blockers

The engine measurement is blocked on host memory, not on the change: a
long-running `hipengine serve` process for qwen3.8-27b holds **100.9 GB of GTT**
(`/proc/4175913/fdinfo/17: drm-total-gtt`), and the Qwen4Exp model needs 95.9 GB
including reserve against 22-25 GB available. That process is the user's; it was
not stopped. The census and the route A/B are scripted and ready to run the
moment memory is free.

## What this does not establish

- **No engine wall-time saving is claimed yet.** The 553.9 ms is a
  kernel-and-launch-count aggregate over the route's launches, not a prefill
  wall. The interleaved route A/B is the measurement that decides it.
- **No qualification run.** The change is arithmetic-preserving (bit-identical
  on the kernel path), so the applicable gate is the profile's own rather than a
  new calibration, but the full qualification has not been run.
- **No claim about the cross-consumer reuse.** This unit converts once per
  launch. Sharing one conversion across the consumers of the same activation is
  worth ~7.5 ms by the packet's own accounting, which is why no cache and no
  invalidation machinery was built.
- **The workspace bound is 1024 rows.** A prefill chunk larger than that keeps
  the f32-input owner, which costs the saving and not correctness. The canonical
  chunk is 1024.
