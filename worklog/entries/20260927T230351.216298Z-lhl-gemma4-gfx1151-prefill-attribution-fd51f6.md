# prefill attention and the routed experts are 71 percent of gfx1151 prefill

## Summary

The objective's prefill item had a ratio (3.74x to the same-host llama.cpp
reference at 512 tokens, 4.85x at 2048) and no target. The campaign's existing
prefill attribution is gfx1100 and does not carry over. This unit attributes the
gfx1151 prefill and isolates the larger of its two costs.

At 2048 tokens, over a 7.536 s / 271.8 tok/s baseline:

| route | saves | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| routed experts | 3.070 s | **40.7%** |
| prefill attention | 2.295 s | **30.5%** |
| router | -0.028 s | ~0 |
| normalization + rotary | -0.001 s | ~0 |
| MoE elementwise | -0.011 s | ~0 |

**Two routes are the prefill.** Everything the earlier gfx1100 attribution spent
its time on outside those two is free here.

Attention is the better-scoped of the two, so it was isolated:
`scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py` runs the kernel alone at the
artifact's own geometry and reports **89.99 ms per launch for the 25
sliding-window layers, 2249.8 ms total**, which reproduces the ablation's
2295 ms and confirms the arm measures the kernel rather than a wrapper. The rate
is **286 to 363 GFLOP/s**, under 1.3% of this part's 29.7 TFLOP/s fp32 FMA peak.

The mechanism is visible in the launcher: the grid is `tokens * num_heads`
(32768 blocks at 2048 tokens and 16 heads), one block per query row, and each
block walks the keys for its own row. K and V are therefore read once per query
row instead of once per query tile. The windowed K/V working set at 2048 tokens
is about 16.8 MB, which fits this part's L2, so the re-reads are L2-bound rather
than DRAM-bound and they still cost. That is exactly the shape flash-attention
tiling removes.

## Changes

- `scripts/gemma4_prefill_route_ablation.py` (new): the prefill twin of
  `scripts/gemma4_decode_route_ablation.py`. Each arm skips one route and times
  the remaining prefill with the benchmark's own wall clock, because
  `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` serializes dispatches on this host.
- `scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py` (new): times
  `gemma4_attention_prefill_bf16` in isolation at the artifact's real geometry
  (16 query heads; 8 KV heads and head_dim 256 on the 25 sliding-window layers
  with window 1024; 2 KV heads and head_dim 512 on the 5 full layers), and
  reports GFLOP/s against the causal pair count.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-09-28-gemma4-gfx1151-prefill-route-attribution.json`:
  the artifact, including the caveat below.

No production code changed and no performance claim is made.

## Validation

```
env -u HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES ROCR_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 PYTHONPATH=. \
  python3 scripts/gemma4_prefill_route_ablation.py --prompt 2048 --repeats 2
# baseline prefill 7.536 s  271.8 tok/s
# no_experts 4.465 s (saves 3.070 s, 40.7%)
# no_attention 5.241 s (saves 2.295 s, 30.5%)

env -u HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES ROCR_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 PYTHONPATH=. \
  python3 scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py --tokens 2048
# swa  256d 16q/8kv window=1024   89.99 ms   286.4 GFLOP/s   2249.8 ms
# swa  256d 16q/8kv window=0     109.30 ms   314.5 GFLOP/s   2732.4 ms
# full 512d 16q/2kv window=0     189.24 ms   363.3 GFLOP/s    946.2 ms
```

The isolation reproduces the ablation for the sliding-window layers (2249.8
against 2295 ms), which is what makes the ablation's attention share trustworthy.
The geometry is the artifact's own, read from its header:
`head_count 16`, `head_count_kv [8, ..., 8, 2, ...]`, `key_length 512`,
`key_length_swa 256`, `sliding_window 1024`, and a 5-SWA-then-1-full pattern.

**One arm is wrong and the artifact says so.** `no_dense_q8` and
`no_all_gemv` both save about -1%, which cannot be right: `no_experts` alone
saves 40.7% and the expert projections are GEMVs, so skipping every GEMV must
save at least that much. Both arms hook `gguf_k_gemv._launch` and
`gguf_q4_k_gemv._launch_selected`, and the prefill leaves for the dense Q8_0
route and the grouped expert route go through neither. The dense share of
prefill is unmeasured here, not zero.

## Next

Prefill attention is the better-scoped target: one kernel, geometry fixed by the
artifact, and a missing query tile rather than a missing route. A kernel that
processes a query tile against a K/V tile, reading K and V once per tile, is the
standard fix and would be measured against the 2249.8 ms recorded here.

Two things are not established and should not be assumed from this run. The split
between the K/V re-read cost and the per-key reduction cost inside the attention
kernel: the kernel's own comment records the reduction tree as the *pre-fix*
bottleneck and the register-tree rewrite as the fix, and 286 GFLOP/s is the
post-fix number, so a profile is needed to say which dominates now. And the
mechanism behind the experts' 40.7%, which this run measured as a share only.
