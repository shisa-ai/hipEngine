# The ledger's decode section now budgets the growing non-kernel remainder (DE-11)

## Summary

The ledger's section 5 priced ten decode rows against device kernel families but
had no row for the quantity that actually moves the decode rate with depth. This
adds **DE-11** and a budget paragraph, and records the two retractions the row
depends on.

**The budget:** the canonical bench's decode wall is 47.656 ms/step at p512 and
57.694 at p4096; the profiled kernel sum is 46.1927 and 47.1298 (+2.0%). The
wall's growth across 8x more context is **10.04 ms/step (+21%)** while device
kernels grow 0.94 ms/step, and the non-kernel remainder grows **1.47 -> 10.56
ms/step (7x)**. Host enqueue and device execution are within ~1% of each other,
so the step is `max(host, device)` and a host-only or device-only win is capped
at roughly half its nominal saving.

**DE-11's row** carries the remainder, the host-side attribution (the memo A/B
at +0.02% / +2.53% against a 4.0% prediction; the counter-rotated readback A/B
at -0.87% / -1.37% with a 0.343 / 0.575 ms/step drain), the action (host-profile
the bench's own decode loop at p4096 and attribute the 10.56 ms/step before
choosing a lever), and the limits.

**Two retractions are recorded in the row because DE-11 supersedes them.**
`device_drain_ms = 0.0` does not mean host-bound — the step's blocking token
readback supplies the synchronization, so a zero drain is a tautology.
And the 23.8 ms/step readback cost was a position confound between two arms that
ran at different sequence positions.

Rows DE-1 through DE-10 are explicitly marked as still valid: they price device
kernel families, and none of them is the growing remainder. That distinction
matters because the previous day's reading ("decode is host-enqueue bound") would
have redirected all of them.
