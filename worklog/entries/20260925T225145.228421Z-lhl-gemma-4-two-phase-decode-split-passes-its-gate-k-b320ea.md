# Gemma 4 two-phase decode split passes its gate (kl_max 0.0067, top-1 1.0) and ships on

## Summary

The two-phase decode split's execution-profile gate **passed**, so the split is now the
default decode path above 512 keys and the precondition constant is retired. Over all
1023 teacher-forced rows of the frozen chain: **kl_max 0.006746** against the 0.05 bar,
kl_mean 9.03e-06 (0.001), kl_p95 5.25e-06 (0.005), kl_p99 6.12e-05 (0.02), and **top-1
rate 1.0 with zero flips** against 0.99. That is 45x under the rejected per-slice-max
variant's 0.304, which is what the two-phase design was for: with no per-slice max there
is no rescale to amplify bf16 error, so the only change left is the association order of
an f32 sum.

## The gate ran on GPU0 (W7900), not GPU1, and why that is sound

Three attempts on GPU1 died in `hipMalloc`: a peer worker's job holds ~6.9-7.5 GiB of its
24 GiB, which leaves less than the model's own footprint. The same OOM reproduces at
context 4096, so the weights rather than the KV cache are what do not fit - the split's
own workspace is under 1 MiB. GPU0 is a W7900: the same gfx1100 ISA and the same compiled
kernel, so the arithmetic is identical and a numerics verdict transfers. Performance does
*not* transfer (different clocks and bandwidth), so the campaign's timing rows still have
to come from GPU1.
