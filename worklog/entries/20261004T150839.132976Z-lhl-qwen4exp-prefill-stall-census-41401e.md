# Prefill stall census: the gap is not uniform, HC-1 is the only LDS-stalled family, and GDN prefill is register-bound at the 256-VGPR maximum

## Summary

The ledger's §1.2.1 reads the prefill gap against gufo as *uniform* across families.
A prefill-only `rocprofv3` capture refutes that at the hardware level: the two largest
prefill kernels have opposite stall profiles. HC-1 `gr_up_sigmoid_mean` (18.26% of
prefill device time) sits at **30.82% LDS wait** while every other top-ten prefill
kernel is at **≤0.35%** — a ~90x spread. The wall-time ratios remain correct as ratios;
what does not survive is reading them as one shared per-kernel deficit.

Two levers come out of the ranking, one of them new:

1. **HC-1** (18.26%, the only LDS-stalled kernel) — already blocked on mean KL, and its
   width direction is closed by measurement.
2. **GDN prefill register→LDS state** (5.93%, **new**) — `qwen4_exp_gdn_prefill_f32_kernel`
   runs at **9.6 waves per busy CU cycle and 0.92 instructions per CU-cycle**, ~23% of the
   issue ceiling and the worst utilization of any large prefill kernel. Its resource record
   explains it exactly: `VGPR_Count` **256 (the hardware maximum)**, `LDS_Block_Size` **0**,
   128-thread workgroups, 6,144 grid blocks. The grid is not the constraint, so this is
   register pressure — and the kernel uses no LDS at all. Bounded at ~4.6% of the prefill
   window; not measured, not built.

The exact chain (16.57%) and qsa attention (3.31%) are at 3.76 and 4.03 inst/CU-cycle,
near the issue ceiling — **not** the lever.

**Stated limit:** `SQ_WAIT_INST_ANY` is accepted by `rocprofv3 --pmc` and silently dropped
on this build, and there is no VMEM wait counter, so the non-LDS stall cannot be named.
The ranking rests on LDS wait, issue rates, occupancy, and resource metadata only.

## Changes

The ledger's §1.2.1 reads the gap against gufo as *uniform* — "dense projections 6.93x,
routed MoE 5.59x, attention/GDN/router/other 4.61x... the difference is per-kernel
throughput, so no single family is the answer and no single port closes it". That is a
wall-time reading with no stall attribution behind it. Every prefill lever this campaign
has judged was judged by wall time or by a microbenchmark; the only PMC capture in the
tree (`20261004T140327.256898Z`) is decode-dominated, because the canonical bench runs
128 decode transitions.

So: capture prefill-only, and ask whether the families stall the same way.

### Protocol

`scripts/qwen4exp_profile_gap.py --mode prefill --case-id code-p4096 --prefill-chunk-size
4096 --execution-profile production`, under `rocprofv3 --pmc`, with the HARNESSES.md
recipe (compiler-version file captured outside the profiler + `--require-cached-build`,
prewarmed). 5,840 kernel rows, 27.102 s device time.

Two captures were needed. The first requested `SQ_WAIT_INST_ANY` / `SQ_WAIT_INST_VMEM` /
`SQ_INSTS_VMEM` and got **only** `SQ_WAIT_INST_LDS`, `SQ_WAVE_CYCLES`, `SQ_INSTS_VALU`,
`SQ_INSTS_LDS`, `SQ_INSTS_SALU`. The second, after `rocprofv3 --list-avail`, requested
`SQ_WAIT_INST_ANY_sum` explicitly — it is accepted on the command line and **silently
dropped from the collected set** on this build (0.00% for every kernel, absent from the
counter list). There is no VMEM wait counter at all.

**Limit, stated up front: the non-LDS stall component cannot be named on this build.** The
attribution below rests on LDS wait, instruction-issue rates, occupancy, and grid/resource
metadata, not on a full wait breakdown.

### Finding 1 — the prefill is NOT uniformly stalled

| kernel | share | LDS wait | VALU issue | waves/busy | inst/CU-cycle |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `q8_0_gr_up_sigmoid_mean_coltile2_branch4_rowbatch4` (HC-1) | **18.26%** | **30.82%** | 2.34% | 55.3 | 2.45 |
| `gguf_k_prefill_out_coltile_rowbatch_pipelined` (DP-8 exact) | 16.57% | 0.03% | 7.76% | 36.2 | **3.76** |
| `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill` (MoE gate/up) | 16.38% | 0.15% | 9.49% | 23.9 | 2.67 |
| `q5_1_selected_wmma_iu8_risk_prefill_kernel<16>` (MoE down) | 8.92% | 0.10% | 7.33% | 31.4 | 2.75 |
| `qwen4_exp_gdn_prefill_f32_kernel<true,true>` | 5.93% | 0.01% | 6.99% | **9.6** | **0.92** |
| `qwen35_router_logits_f32_token_tile_dense_exact` | 4.55% | 0.35% | 1.30% | 60.1 | 1.92 |
| `q5_1_selected_sparse_exact_repair_row_publish` | 3.88% | 0.03% | 1.77% | 53.9 | 3.00 |
| `qsa_sparse_attention_h256_wave_rows_f32` | 3.31% | 0.21% | 8.26% | 40.6 | **4.03** |

HC-1 sits at **30.82% LDS wait**; every other top-ten prefill kernel is at **≤0.35%**. That
is a ~90x spread. The two largest kernels in the prefill — HC-1 at 18.26% and the exact
chain at 16.57% — have *opposite* stall profiles. The uniform-throughput reading is a
wall-time artifact of summing differently-shaped work.

`inst/CU-cycle` is `(SQ_WAVE_CYCLES/SQ_BUSY_CYCLES) x (VALU+SALU+LDS instructions)/SQ_WAVE_CYCLES`,
against a ~4-per-cycle issue ceiling on a 4-SIMD CU. It is an estimate, not a measured
issue rate, and it assumes issue is the binding constraint — which is exactly what is in
question for a bandwidth-bound kernel. It is used here only to rank, not to conclude.

### Finding 2 — HC-1 is the only localized lever, and it is already blocked

This is the second independent confirmation that HC-1 is where the prefill's stall lives:
18.26% of device time, the only kernel with a material LDS stall, and the kernel whose
staging screen (HC-1) reached 2.388x. The route remains blocked on mean KL, and the
kernel's own MEASURED DEAD note closes the width direction (ROW_BATCH widening is
occupancy-bound at 72 VGPR / 28 waves per CU; 130 VGPR crosses the 128 granule).

### Finding 3 — GDN prefill is register-bound at the hardware maximum

`qwen4_exp_gdn_prefill_f32_kernel<true,true>` is the fifth-largest prefill kernel (5.93%,
1.607 s) and the **worst-utilized large kernel in the whole prefill**: 9.6 waves per busy
CU cycle and 0.92 instructions per CU-cycle, ~23% of the issue ceiling. Its kernel-trace
resource record explains it exactly:

| | GDN prefill | HC-1 | exact chain | router |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `VGPR_Count` | **256** | 72 | 80 | 32 |
| `LDS_Block_Size` | **0** | 512 | 512 | 0 |
| workgroup | 128 | 128 | 128 | 256 |
| grid blocks | 6144 | 163840 | 5120 | 131072 |

256 VGPR is the hardware maximum. With 128 threads per block that is ~7-9 waves resident
per CU, and the grid (6144 blocks over 96 CUs) is not the constraint — so this is a
register-pressure limit, not a grid limit. **It also uses no LDS at all.** A chunked
linear-attention prefill keeps its running state matrix in registers; moving that state to
LDS is the obvious occupancy route, and it is a route no campaign unit has named.

Not a claim of a win: whether the state fits in LDS, whether the LDS traffic is cheaper
than the register pressure, and whether the arithmetic survives the production envelope
are all unmeasured. It is named as the next candidate, ranked below HC-1 only because
5.93% x 77% = ~4.6% of the prefill window bounds it.

### Finding 4 — the router is latency-bound, not occupancy-bound

`qwen35_router_logits_f32_token_tile_dense_exact` (4.55%) has 32 VGPR, 256 threads per
block, 131,072 grid blocks and **60.1 waves resident** — the highest occupancy of any large
prefill kernel — yet issues only 1.30% VALU and 0.243% FLAT loads. It has all the waves it
could want and still cannot issue, so the constraint is memory latency, not parallelism.
Its flat-load rate is 3x HC-1's and 3x the MoE's while its VALU rate is a third of theirs.

## Validation

1. **HC-1** (18.26%, only LDS-stalled kernel) — blocked on mean KL; width direction closed
   by measurement.
2. **GDN prefill register→LDS state** (5.93%, 0.92 inst/CU-cycle, 256 VGPR, zero LDS) —
   new, unblocked, bounded at ~4.6% of the prefill window.
3. **Router latency** (4.55%, 60 waves and still not issuing) — unblocked, unattributed
   (needs a VMEM wait counter this build does not expose).
4. Exact chain (16.57%) and qsa attention (3.31%) are at 3.76 and 4.03 inst/CU-cycle —
   near the issue ceiling, **not** the lever.
