# one kernel per dense call, the dispatch counts are cheap, and the dense kernel runs at 12.5 percent occupancy

## Summary

Three things come out of the existing trace's dispatch counts.

**The dense funnel launches exactly one kernel per call** -- 205 `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma`
dispatches against the dense probe's 206 `launch_gguf_linear` calls. The pack hypothesis
from `20260928T192000` is dead: the isolated harness and the in-situ path run the same work.

**The dispatch counts are cheap.** 452 `copyBuffer` dispatches total **1.2 ms (0.2 percent)**
and 301 rmsnorm-family dispatches total **11.3 ms (1.7 percent)**. **785 of the 1592
dispatches -- 49 percent -- are families that together cost 7.3 ms, 1.1 percent of the step.**
Launch count is not a problem here and the step's time is concentrated: four families are
**86.8 percent** of it.

**And the dense kernel is the narrowest in the step.** It is the only one that runs one warp
per block.

## The occupancy, and why it matters

`__launch_bounds__(32, 8)` with `dim3 block(32)` is one warp per block and eight blocks per
CU -- **8 warps out of the 64 an RDNA3 CU can hold, 12.5 percent thread occupancy.** The
kernel also carries **152 VGPRs**, which is high and is what caps the block count.

**The measured rates are near neither roofline.** `2816 -> 4096` at rows = 512 is 11.8 GFLOP
in 1.2495 ms = **9.45 TFLOP/s** against a gfx1151 fp16 WMMA peak of about **102 TFLOP/s** --
**9.3 percent of compute peak.** Its arithmetic intensity is **611 FLOP per byte**, so it is
compute-bound by construction and not traffic-bound. The whole step moves 16.64 GB in 679.2 ms
= **24.5 GB/s, 9.6 percent of DRAM peak.**

**So the prefill's matrix kernels are at roughly a tenth of both rooflines**, and the shape
that is worst at it is the one with the lowest occupancy. **Connecting those two is a
hypothesis, not a measurement** -- 152 VGPRs and one warp per block is a real constraint, but
the trace does not prove that raising either would raise the rate.

## Next

- **The occupancy hypothesis is the one to test, and it is testable cheaply.** The kernel's
  tile is already swept, but the sweep varied `tile_m` and `tile_n` -- the *workgroup* size
  is fixed at 32. A variant with two or four warps per block on the same tile would separate
  "the tile is wrong" from "the block is too narrow", and `(32, 64)` was chosen from a sweep
  that never changed that.
- **Then the MoE plumbing, 51.6 ms.** Router logits at 14.7 ms over 26 layers is 0.57 ms per
  layer for a projection the size of a small dense layer; group compact plus gather is
  another 21 ms of index work. This is the largest block outside the matrix families.
- **Do not re-open the dense in-situ gap as a measurement artifact.** The trace confirms the
  probe to 1.6 percent, the funnel launches one kernel per call, and every physical cause --
  bandwidth, drift, launch overhead, gap time, readback, packing -- is now ruled out. The
  gap is real kernel time and the occupancy is the only candidate left standing.
