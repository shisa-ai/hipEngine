# Qwen3.8 scaling campaign extension W: dataflow-wall analysis and successor punchlist

## Summary

Opened extension W in `docs/QWEN38-GFX1151-SCALING-CAMPAIGN.md` (new section 8)
as the successor to the closed C6/C8 K1 ten-iteration loop
(`benchmarks/results/2026-09-01-gfx1151-qwen38-c6c8-k1-ten-iteration-closeout.json`,
named blocker: multi-family packed-verifier dataflow wall). This is a
planning/analysis unit only — no code, kernel, or measured W result yet.

The analysis converts the blocker into economics: at the clean endpoint,
C8/K1 commits ~1.956 tokens/request/cycle (95.65% acceptance) but runs at
0.9192x own AR, so each speculative cycle costs ~2.13 matched-AR-step
equivalents (C6: ~2.11); K1's arithmetic ceiling is ~1.96x and reaching 1.15x
needs cycle cost <= 1.70 AR-steps — the closeout's 19-20% wall gap. The
stronger lever is committed tokens per weight sweep: K3 at the historical
78.894% acceptance would commit ~27 tokens/cycle at C8 (vs K1's ~15.7) but is
blocked because R20-R32 verify loses every R<=16 owner and wide proposal cost
balloons. External evidence (same host/model/protocol, survey matrix): stock
HIP llama.cpp K3 C8 = 56.222 tok/s = 1.854x own AR, above our 54.326 C8
target — reached with a row-invariant general-GEMM verifier, an all-slot
batched drafter, and token-budget caps (X1). Requirement derived: (1)
row-invariant verify cost R8-R32 per family, (2) proposal small vs a target
sweep at wide rows/depth, (3) no separate accept/commit stage. C6 has no
external absolute existence proof (externals top out at 37.154); it rests on
ratio evidence plus tokens-per-sweep arithmetic — stated in the doc.
