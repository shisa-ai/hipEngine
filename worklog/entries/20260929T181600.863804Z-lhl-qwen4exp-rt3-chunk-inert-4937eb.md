# RT-3 premise falsified: the chunk setting is inert when the prompt fits in the chunk

## Summary

RT-3's rationale is "the September 15 4096 option improved 4K but **short-request
costs kept 1024 default**". Measured: that short-request cost is **not
reproducible today**. The chunk setting has no effect - on time or memory - for any
prompt that fits inside the chunk.

That **simplifies** RT-3: the shape-based *time* policy the row asks for is not the
right form, and the lever is a capacity guard.

## The mechanism

Scratch is sized `min(chunk_size, max_sequence_length)`, and the harness sets
`max_sequence_length = max(768, prompt_tokens + 1)`. So the chunk setting only
changes anything once the prompt exceeds the chunk:

| prompt | rows at chunk 1024 | rows at chunk 4096 | differs? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 512 | 768 | 768 | no |
| 1024 | 1024 | 1025 | +1 row |
| 4096 | 1024 | 4096 | 4x |

That model predicts all three measurements below, including two I ran before
stating it.

## Measurements

`code-p1024`, three reps per arm, production profile, cached build, one process per
arm, chunk size the only difference:

| chunk | wall (3 reps) | median | peak allocation |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1024 | 3.6975 / 3.7710 / 3.7671 s | **3.7671 s** | 84,337,158,344 B |
| 4096 | 3.7459 / 3.8207 / 3.8196 s | **3.8196 s** | 84,338,566,196 B |
| **delta** | | **+0.0525 s (+1.39%)** | **+1,407,852 B (+0.00167%)** |

`token_id` (248068) and `logits_sha256` identical, `fell_back_to_strict=False`.

**The +1.39% is not an effect.** The within-arm ranges are 0.0735 s and 0.0748 s;
the median gap of 0.0525 s is *smaller* than either range, and the arms overlap. I
am not reporting it as a slowdown.

## Across all three points

| prompt | wall at 4096 vs 1024 | allocation at 4096 vs 1024 |
|---|---|---|
| 512 | indistinguishable | **byte-identical** |
| 1024 | indistinguishable | **+1.34 MiB** |
| 4096 | **-4.94%** | **+4.96 GB** |

The transition is sharp and lands exactly where the mechanism says it should.

## What this changes about RT-3

**Falsified:** the short-request cost. At p512 the settings are byte-identical in
allocation and indistinguishable in time; at p1024 they differ by 1.34 MiB and
overlap in time. A blanket chunk 4096 costs short requests **nothing measurable**.

**The trade is memory admission at long prompts only**, proportional to
`min(chunk_size, prompt_tokens)` - a request pays for the scratch it can use, not
for the setting.

So the length-bucketed policy the row asks for is not needed: there is nothing to
balance below the chunk size. The lever is a **capacity guard** - take the larger
chunk when the allocation is admissible, fall back when it is not. That is simpler
than what the row specified, and it is what the evidence supports.

## What I did not do

The row asks for substantially more, and these gaps are real, not pedantic:

- **No 2051/2052 case.** The canonical fixture has p512/p1024/p4096 only, so the
  exact boundary RT-3 names was **not measured**. The model predicts ~2x scratch
  there (2049 vs 1024 rows) but that is a prediction and I have labelled it as one.
- **No separate accounting** of repair queues, F16 workspace, or c2 peak - only
  aggregate `peak_allocated_bytes`.
- **No peer-fairness measurement.**
- **No policy implemented; nothing promoted; the default is unchanged.**
- Ragged final chunks not specifically exercised. One prompt family, one host.

## Validation

- The mechanism was stated as a prediction, then tested at a point that
  discriminates it (p1024: predicted +1 row, measured +1.34 MiB) - it was not
  fitted after the fact to the p4096 number.
- Arithmetic invariance re-confirmed: `token_id` and `logits_sha256` identical,
  `fell_back_to_strict=False`, `named_profile_intact=True`.
- Noise handled explicitly: the +1.39% wall difference is smaller than the
  within-arm range, so it is reported as indistinguishable rather than as a result.
- Ledger IDs unchanged (70).
