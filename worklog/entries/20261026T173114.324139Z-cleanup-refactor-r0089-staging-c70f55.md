# Own DFlash2 staging arrays and audit remaining upload callers

## Summary

DFlash2 retains its persistent staging array through synchronous uploads,
including each chunk of larger inputs.

## Changes

- Upload contiguous byte views of the existing persistent staging allocation
  through the owning helper, keeping destination offsets and chunk lengths.
- Remove the redundant cached staging pointer. Bare destination addresses
  remain caller-owned; local views cannot prove the caller's allocation size.

## Validation

- Real-device readbacks passed for empty, small, exact-capacity and multi-chunk
  noncontiguous inputs using `/tmp/r0089-dflash2-readback.py` through the
  isolated ROCm environment. All three composite forward/selector GPU tests
  passed (bg-190); focused staging unit tests passed.
- AST census of `hipengine/` finds no remaining NumPy raw-address synchronous
  uploads outside the owning helper after this migration. The remaining
  direct calls use named ctypes payloads in Laguna/Gemma attention, or a
  named file-read `bytes` object in Gemma KV restoration, retained through
  synchronous copies; they do not meet the NumPy helper's array protocol.
- `_copy_array_to_tensor` in `runtime/gguf_native_spec_cycle.py` has no extra
  synchronization. A census of copy/upload-named functions found no
  `device_synchronize()` calls. Asynchronous staging is intentionally unchanged.

- Full CPU deterministic bundle passed (bg-191). Documentation, audit and
  focused staging gates passed. No public DFlash2 generator is registered;
  the native composite forward/selector is the available integration surface.
- Closed R0089 and removed its completed ledger section. The API contract
  remains in `hipengine/core/memory.py`; individual migration worklogs retain
  validation evidence and hardware/model coverage limitations.
