# Q4 decode: raw-block pack8 routing gives 2.48x (122.6 -> 49.4 ms/token), now 1.34x faster than dense bf16

## Summary

Q4_K_M decode now routes rows=1 through the **raw-block pack8 decode GEMV
families**, which read the unmodified GGUF Q4_K/Q6_K block bytes on device.
No repack, no sidecar, no extra resident memory — the earlier plan (repack to
pack8 at load, ~1.1 s per weight, plus resident overhead) was unnecessary.

Measured integrated decode on gfx1151: **49.4 ms/token** (44.4 layers + 6.6
lm_head), stable at 49.4/50.1/51.1 over three trials, versus the recorded
naive **122.6 ms/token** — **2.48x** — and versus dense bf16's 66.0 ms —
**1.34x faster, while using 2.7x less resident weight memory (6.10 GB vs
16.66 GB)**. This is the first retained speed win in the Q4 workstream;
decode was previously a 1.7x regression against bf16.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/vibevoice/q4.py`:
  - `_PACK8_DECODE` maps `(GGML type, bf16_out)` to the raw-block pack8
    decode entry points; `_pick_gemv` takes `rows` and selects pack8 at
    rows=1 for bf16 activations. Q5_K has no dense decode entry point in
    that family and falls through to the raw-block GEMV.
  - The dual gate/up GEMV at rows=1 now issues two pack8 `bf16_bf16` GEMVs
    straight into the fused dual layout, removing the f32 scratch and the
    two cast passes from the hot path. The f32 fallback (rows>1) keeps the
    caller-owned scratch.
  - `q4_dense_gemv_f32_bf16w_f32_out` (o_proj) takes f32 activations, which
    the pack8 families do not accept. At rows=1 it narrows the activation
    once into a caller-owned bf16 scratch and runs the pack8 decode GEMV on
    that; the extra pass is over `in_features` elements and buys the whole
    GEMV its bandwidth wall. This is the only new allocation, and it is
    per-call (no process-global state).

## Validation

- `tests/test_gpu_vibevoice_qwen2_q4_runner.py`: **7/7**, including the
  greedy chain 16/16 vs the torch fixture and first-position logits top-1,
  so the fast path reproduces the validated token sequence.
- Per-GEMM measurements on real blocks (rows=1, 7 reps) established the
  selection: raw-block GEMVs run at 18-60 GB/s, pack8 decode at 107-132
  GB/s. Dense bf16 streams 14.15 GB/token in 66 ms = 214 GB/s, so pack8 is
  at the wall for these shapes and there is no further 2x hiding in the
  GEMV itself.
- Integrated decode, three trials of 40 steps after 3 warmup steps:
  49.4 / 50.1 / 51.1 ms/token. Layers-only loop: 44.4 ms; lm_head 6.6 ms.
- Artifact: `benchmarks/results/2026-09-14-gfx1151-vibevoice-q4-pack8-decode.json`.
- Quality re-check: a 20-clip Q4 WER lane was launched with the new path;
  the retained 50-clip gate is 1.978% == bf16 1.978%.

## Next

- **Correction to the previous entry.** That entry said the 50-clip subset is
  "~1.5k words, so the gate is comparing 0-1 errors against 0-1 errors".
  Wrong twice: the subset is **1112 normalized words**, and at 1.978% that is
  **~22 word errors** (torch's 4.586% is ~51). The "0-1 errors" figure was a
  residual of the fraction/percentage confusion the entry was written to
  correct. The gate can therefore separate 1.978% from 4.586%; what it cannot
  resolve is a delta of a few errors, which is the real reason to scale up.
- **The projection overshoot is the review's point in numbers.** The
  microbenchmark predicted 39.1 ms/token and the integrated runtime measures
  49.4 — a 1.26x optimism factor from the per-token non-GEMM work (196 GEMM
  launches plus ~336 norm/rotary/KV-write/attention/residual/silu launches)
  and context-dependent attention. Single-kernel ratios must not be quoted as
  model ratios.
- **Prefill is now the dominant cost and the top remaining lever.** The
  unchanged row-by-row path measured 3.80 s for 89 rows (~43 ms/row), i.e.
  ~6 s for a 146-row request, against dense bf16's ~0.30 s. This is where the
  next unit should go: the existing Q4_K/Q6_K batched prefill and WMMA
  families for these shapes, not another decode tweak. The reviewer's
  prioritization is right.
- lm_head is 1.09 GB dense bf16 and 13% of decode time at ~210 GB/s (already
  at the wall); quantizing it is a further ~0.8 GB/token but is
  quality-sensitive and needs its own gate.
