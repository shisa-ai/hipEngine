# Split-K follow-ups: reducer skip, c1-graph audit, measurement verdicts

## Summary

Worked the three "worth doing" follow-ups to the empty-split elimination
(`20261108T152858...-empty-split-elimination-1fe2c7.md`). Outcomes:

1. **End-to-end measurement: HTTP A/B attempted and abandoned as confounded;
   rocprof breakdown captured instead.** Served Qwen3.8-27B-UD-Q4_K_M from a
   detached pre-fix worktree at `c62d367a3` (main had moved to audit-only
   commits since the branch base) and ran the mixed-length streaming protocol
   twice (`scripts/measure_mixed_length_itl.py`, ~128K prompt + 3 short
   requests). The signal is unusable: SSE chunks arrive in bursts (20 of 63
   inter-chunk intervals <1 ms while the mean is ~180-340 ms), the ~128K
   prefill dominates wall time (~16 min at the default route), MTP serving is
   enabled by default, and decode ran at an anomalously slow ~340 ms/token on
   the untuned default profile. No before/after ITL claim is made from these
   runs; raw artifacts stayed in `/tmp/pr29510-itl-before*.json`. The rigorous
   evidence remains the kernel-level A/B probe (committed artifacts).
   The clean replacement measurement is a rocprofv3 kernel-trace breakdown of
   the post-fix split-K attention (`scripts/probe_splitk_rocprof_breakdown.py`,
   artifact `2026-11-08-pr29510-splitk-context-vs-reducer-breakdown-diagnostic.json`):
   at 128K/512 splits the **context kernel is 92.7-92.8%** of attention time
   (2592-2641 us/call) and the **gate reducer 7.2-7.3%** (203-206 us/call);
   mixed vs long-only totals now differ by <2%.

2. **Reducer parallelization: skip.** The batch reducer's thread-0 serial
   cross-split merge is real, but the reducer is only 7.3% of attention.
   Even an optimistic 2x reducer win is ~3.6% of attention; with attention
   ~51% of a 128K decode step (recorded July profile), the end-to-end ceiling
   is ~1-2%, below the noise floor of the serving measurement above, and it
   would risk reduction-order changes requiring profile gates. The context
   kernel (92.7%) is bandwidth-bound KV reads; its lever is KV storage size
   (INT8 KV), which is the separate quality-gated workstream in
   `docs/reference/KVCACHE.md`, not a scheduling optimization.

3. **c1/graph-replay audit: pattern exists, magnitude <1%, no action.**
   Correction to the mid-audit hypothesis: the c1 decode graph does NOT flow
   through the fixed batch wrapper. `qwen35_gguf.py:6542` captures with
   `attention_max_context_len = position + remaining` (the full output
   budget); `qwen35_gguf_layer_attn.py:1043-1078` sizes `num_splits` from that
   cap and dispatches the c1 split-K wrappers
   (`..._split_k_gqa_gate_bf16_spans` / warp / generic via
   `policy.full_attention_split_gate_fn`), whose non-batch reducers were not
   modified. Early replays therefore still zero/reduce empty splits. But the
   magnitude is small: probe-derived per-empty-block cost is ~39 ns, and a
   single row's average slack is `remaining/2/256` splits — ~0.3 us/layer
   zeroing at 2048 outputs, ~1.2 us/layer at 8192, plus 64-256 KiB/layer of
   reducer reads; across 16 layers that is <1% of a ~20 ms short-context
   step. Extending the bounds ABI to the c1 reducers is not worth the change
   surface at this size. Recorded as a known minor inefficiency.
