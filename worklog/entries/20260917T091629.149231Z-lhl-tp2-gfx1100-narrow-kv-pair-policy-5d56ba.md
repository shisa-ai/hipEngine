# gfx1100 admits the narrow K/V pair: a policy miss, not a kernel miss (TP2 p50 24.155 -> 24.025 ms)

## Summary

The previous entry named the `attn_k`/`attn_v` pair as the worst projection in
the model - 7.2 MB in 45.8 us (158 GB/s), 16 layers, 0.73 ms/token - and
attributed it to a fixed-cost-bound launch at `out=1024`, with a Q4_K
`q`+`k`+`v` triple as the proposed fix. The attribution was half right: the
launch is fixed-cost-bound, but **the kernel already exists and was already
registered on gfx1100**. What was missing was two policy declarations.

The real model's pair is the **planar** kind, not the Q4/Q4 one: K is Q4_K and
V is planar-Q6 in this quantization, so the composed kernel is
`narrow_col4_planar_pair_bf16_bf16_out` over a Q4_K col4 operand and a planar-Q6
operand. The projection audit's first pass labelled the row from operand A's
quant alone and therefore named the wrong pair; the audit now records the
launcher's own classifier answer as `pair_kind`, and the e2e arm record carries
`attention_kv_pair_route` for the same reason. Both pair kinds are admitted by
this change and both are bit-exact-gated at the production shape.

`_resolve_gguf_linear_pair_kind` returns `"none"` for `(1, 5120, 1024, 1024)`
on gfx1100 because:

1. `GGUF_NARROW_KV_PAIR_DECODE_SHAPES` was declared only on gfx1151, so the
   narrow-pair branch was never entered; and
2. even with the shape declared, the classifier requires both operands' c1
   resolution to be `dense_single_col4_bf16_bf16_out`, and the gfx1100
   `GGUF_T16_C1_VARIANTS_BY_QUANT_SHAPE` table had no `gguf_q4_k_t16_v1` entry,
   so the Q4_K K/V singletons stayed on `dense_single_local32_bf16_bf16_out`.

Both kernels - the col4 singleton and `narrow_col4_pair_bf16_bf16_out` - were
already registered on gfx1100 and already covered by the gfx1151 device gate.
Declaring the two policies routes the shape to one fused launch per
full-attention layer.

## Measured

16 full-attention layers (`scripts/gguf_decode_projection_bandwidth_audit.py`,
which now records the *effective* owner and the pair route):

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| W7900 | 45.8 us, 158 GB/s (two singletons) | **32.8 us, 221 GB/s** (fused planar pair) | 7.2 MB |
| RX 7900 XTX | 46.7 us, 155 GB/s (two singletons) | **30.6 us, 237 GB/s** (fused planar pair) | 7.2 MB |

Every other projection row is unchanged (attn_q 59.6/66.8, attn_output
34.2/33.0, qkv_gate 91.7/81.4, ssm_out 40.1/33.5, ffn_gate 86.9/68.6, ffn_up
86.8/68.6, ffn_down 106.1/90.7 us), so there is no collateral route change.

Same revision, same session, matched cell (`scripts/tp2_mlp_generate_e2e.py`,
4 prompts x 16 greedy decode transitions, three repeats):

| repeat set | TP1 W7900 | TP1 XTX | TP2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| before (3 repeats) | 31.753-31.764 | 26.320-26.355 | 24.155-24.167 |
| after, first harness revision (3) | 31.672-31.676 | 26.231-26.273 | 23.979-24.020 |
| after, route-recording revision (3) | 31.708-31.730 | 26.250-26.256 | 23.995-24.077 |

Every one of the six after-runs is below every before-run on all three arms, so
the shift is not noise. On the retained artifact run: **TP2 24.155 -> 24.025 ms
(-0.130 ms, -0.54%)**, **TP1 W7900 31.753 -> 31.713 (-0.13%)**, **TP1 XTX
26.329 -> 26.256 (-0.28%)**; best-of-six TP2 is 23.979 (-0.73%). All three arms
improve; nothing regresses.

Two independent audit runs measured the fused pair at 33.0/28.6 us and
32.8/30.6 us (W7900/XTX); the retained artifact holds the second. The XTX
spread is the larger of the two and still 16 us below its unfused cost.

The wall delta is smaller than the isolated one because the isolated audit
measures host-submission-bound eager launches (the norms alone measure 20-24 us
at 10 KB, which is a launch floor, not device time): the graphed TP2 schedule
and TP1's pipelined submission already hide part of that host cost, so the wall
keeps the device-side node saving rather than the full launch removal.

## Correctness

The pair kernel's contract is that it preserves each qualified singleton's
arithmetic tree, and the c1 swap it depends on must itself be exact. Both are
gated in `tests/test_gpu_qwen38_narrow_kv_pair.py`, now at the **production
shape** as well as the small one:

- `col4 == local32` bit-exact at `(1, 5120, 1024)` - the new contract this
  change introduces, since the c1 table now routes that shape to col4.
- `pair == two primitive projections` bit-exact at `(1, 5120, 1024, 1024)` for
  both pair kinds (`q4_q4_t16_narrow_col4` and `q4_q6_t16_narrow_col4_planar`),
  against the col4 and planar-Q6 singleton controls.
- CPU-reference KL <= 0.05 and top-1 agreement on the concatenated K/V logits.
- The routing test is parametrized over both backends and asserts the whole
  shape set on each, so a peer backend cannot declare the capability without a
  device gate.

The end-to-end gates are unchanged to the digit - mean KL 3.945e-04, max KL
2.365e-03, top-1 100% against both TP1 teachers - and the session reports
`logits_bit_identical=True` against the preceding cell, which is the strongest
available statement that the fused pair is the same arithmetic in the real
model.

The test's KL helper was rewritten in log-softmax space. The ratio form
`sum(p * log(p / q))` returned NaN at the production shape: with 5120 summed
terms the logit spread underflows most of the 2048 columns to probability zero
and `log(0/0)` is NaN. The log-softmax form is the project's established
convention (`tests/test_gpu_dms_compact_attn_decode_hip.py`) and is exact for
underflowed reference entries, which contribute zero to KL by definition.

## Next

1. The remaining K/V cost is launch-floor, not bandwidth: `attn_q` already runs
   at 594 GB/s and the fused pair's marginal rate is close to it, so a Q4_K
   `q`+`k`+`v` triple mostly *moves* the fixed cost instead of removing it
   (estimated ~5 us/layer against ~92.6 us now). It is no longer the lever the
   previous entry thought it was; deprioritize it below the attention half's
   small-kernel count (89 us/FA layer, 50 us/GDN layer).
2. **Check the label, not just the route.** Recording `pair_kind` from the
   launcher's own classifier caught a wrong label in this entry's first
   evidence pass (operand A's quant named the Q4/Q4 pair where the model
   composes the planar one). Anything that reports a route from a proxy
   (first-operand quant, requested flag) instead of the resolving call can be
   wrong in exactly this way.
3. The same policy pattern may be worth auditing elsewhere: a registered exact
   sibling with no policy entry is invisible to the classifier, and
   `_resolve_gguf_linear_pair_kind` returning `"none"` looks identical whether
   the kernel is missing or the policy is. `pair_route` in the projection audit
   now makes that visible per role, and the audit is cheap to re-run.
