# the dense q8_0 tile heuristic was picking a tile that is never the best and fixing it is 10 percent of prefill

## Summary

The dense Q8_0 WMMA prefill kernel's default tile was wrong for every shape it
was measured on. `_default_tiles` chose `(32, 32)` for most shapes; a sweep over
eleven shapes -- gemma4's dense projections and the qwen35moe shapes the ladder
was written for -- found `(32, 32)` best on **none** of them and `(64, 32)` best
or within run-to-run noise on all eleven.

**Measured end to end on the campaign's canonical instrument: prefill 597.2 ->
656.5 tok/s, +9.9 percent**, from a paired alternating A/B of two repetitions
with non-overlapping ranges. The kernel trace agrees in direction and rough
size: the dense Q8_0 family goes from 277.7 to 213.3 ms per prefill (-23.2
percent) and collapses from two instantiations to one.

**The change is numerically inert.** All six tiles produce bit-identical output
on three gemma4 dense shapes, so this is a dispatch choice with no arithmetic
consequence and no execution-profile gate.

## The defect

`_default_tiles` was a shape ladder tuned on gfx1100 (RX 7900 XTX) with the
qwen35moe shapes. Its branches:

| rule | shape family | old tile_m | measured best |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `out <= 512` | qwen35 k/v | 16 | **64** (0.136 against 0.260 ms) |
| `in >= 4096 and out >= 2048` | qwen35 ssm down, gemma4 o_proj | 64 | 64 |
| `in <= 2048 and out >= 4096` | qwen35 qkv and gate | 16 | **64** (2.494 against 2.755) |
| fallback | gemma4 q/k/v, dense ffn | 32 | **64** (0.634 against 1.187) |

Every branch that named a tile other than 64 named a slower one. The fallback
`(32, 32)` was the worst of the three candidates on nearly every shape: on the
dense ffn gate/up it measured 1.187 ms against `(64, 32)`'s 0.634.

## The sweep

`rows=512`, bf16 in and out, median of 5, gfx1151. Selected rows, ms:

| shape | `16x32` | `32x32` | `64x32` | best |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gemma4 swa k/v `2816->2048` | 0.798 | 0.931 | **0.459** | 64 |
| gemma4 ffn gate/up `2816->2112` | 1.163 | 1.187 | **0.634** | 64 |
| gemma4 global q_proj `2816->8192` | 3.736 | 3.828 | **2.885** | 64 |
| gemma4 swa q_proj `2816->4096` | 1.704 | 1.643 | **1.387** | 64 |
| gemma4 ffn down `2112->2816` | 0.728 | 0.829 | **0.603** | 64 |
| qwen35moe qkv `2048->8192` | 2.755 | 3.170 | **2.494** | 64 |
| qwen35moe gate `2048->4096` | 1.413 | 1.618 | **1.333** | 64 |
| qwen35moe ssm down `4096->2048` | 1.722 | 1.997 | **0.967** | 64 |
| qwen35moe k/v `2048->512` | 0.260 | 0.143 | **0.136** | 64 |
| gemma4 o_proj glob `8192->2816` | **4.958** | 5.758 | 5.015 | 16 by 1.1% |
| gemma4 o_proj swa `4096->2816` | **2.169** | 2.730 | 2.189 | 16 by 0.9% |

**The two qwen35moe shapes the ladder explicitly named as `(16, 32)` wins --
`2048->8192` and `2048->4096` -- both measure faster at `(64, 32)` here**, by
1.10x and 1.06x. That is the part of the old tuning that does not reproduce, and
it is why the replacement is a single tile rather than a repaired ladder.

The only two shapes where a narrow tile measured ahead did so by 1.1 and 0.9
percent, which is inside the spread observed for the same shape and tile across
runs (the swa o_proj measured 1.891 ms at `(64, 32)` in one sweep and 2.189 in
the next). Those two are kept as `tile_m = 64` rather than fitted, because a
branch separating 1 percent is a branch that cannot be cleared by measurement.

`tile_n` is unchanged at 32 for `rows >= 32` and 16 below. It is the token-side
tile -- a block covers `AWQ_TILE_N` tokens and the weight tile is dequantized
once per (block, weight element) -- so it is the dimension that would trade
weight traffic against registers, and 32 is the largest value whose accumulator
and activation registers fit. The trace shows both instantiations in use before
this change and only `(64, 32)` after.

## The end-to-end measurement

The canonical instrument, `scripts/gemma4_campaign_bench.py --prompt 512
--output 128`, alternating the two tiles through the module's own
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_Q8_0_WMMA_TILE_M/N` override so both arms run the same binary in
the same session:

| rep | `(32,32)` samples | mean | `(64,32)` samples | mean | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 599, 603, 599 | 600.3 | 654, 654, 658 | 655.3 | 1.092x |
| 2 | 598, 591, 593 | 594.0 | 659, 654, 660 | 657.7 | 1.107x |
| **both** | | **597.2** | | **656.5** | **1.099x** |

Ranges do not overlap: 591-603 against 654-660.

Decode is unchanged at 28.06 against 28.12 tok/s, which is expected -- decode
takes the GEMV route, not this kernel.

The kernel trace, two 512-token prefills under `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`:

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dense Q8_0 dispatches | 410 across `<32,32>` and `<64,32>` | 410, all `<64,32>` |
| dense Q8_0 ms per prefill | 277.7 | **213.3** |
| total device ms per prefill | 829.8 | 773.8 |

The trace puts the total at -6.7 percent where the bench puts the prefill step
at -9.0 percent. Both are reported rather than the flattering one; the bench is
the canonical instrument and the trace ran under profiling with a different
harness, and the two disagree by 2.3 points on a change of about 9.

## Bit-exactness across tiles

Six tiles, three gemma4 dense shapes, same inputs, `bf16` output compared as raw
bytes:

```
in=2816 out=4096: all tiles bit-identical = True
in=2816 out=2112: all tiles bit-identical = True
in=2112 out=2816: all tiles bit-identical = True
```

This is expected: the tile changes which output elements a block owns, not the
order in which any output element's K-loop accumulates. It is measured rather
than argued because it is what makes the change gate-free.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/quant/gguf_q8_0_prefill.py`: `_default_tiles`
  replaces the shape ladder with `tile_m = 64` (16 when `out_features < 64`, so
  a narrow lm_head does not launch mostly out-of-range columns) and keeps
  `tile_n = 32`/16. The docstring carries the sweep table and names the two
  shapes that measured within noise of 16.
- `tests/test_gpu_gguf_q8_0_wmma_prefill.py`: the pinning test's assertions are
  updated to the re-measured defaults, with the shapes kept identical to the old
  ladder's so the change is visible where it bites.

## Next

1. **The dense Q8_0 family is now 213.3 ms per prefill against llama.cpp's
   105.6 for the same weights -- 2.0x, down from 2.6x.** The remaining gap is
   structural: llama.cpp's RDNA3.5 MMQ config is `nthreads=256, I=128, J=128`
   against our 32-thread block and 64x32 tile, so it re-reads weights 4x less.
   Reaching J=128 needs `TN=8`, whose 8 float8 accumulators plus 8 half16
   activations do not fit in registers at `TM>=1`; `TN=4` (tile_n 64) is the
   reachable step and was not in `_ALLOWED_TILES`.
2. Attention remains 178.8 ms per prefill against llama.cpp's 28.8. The traffic
   ablation says it is **not** bandwidth-bound -- doubling every K/V byte
   (bf16 to f32) costs 2 percent on the dominant sliding case -- so the
   query-tiling direction is not the fix at these lengths and the kernel is
   latency-bound at about 11 percent of issue capacity.
3. The expert block is at parity with llama.cpp and needs nothing.
