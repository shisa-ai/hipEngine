# RT-1 decisive comparison: 256->1024 captures 93% of the win at 20% of the memory

## Summary

Both arms of the 256 -> 1024 comparison in **one run** (REFACTOR forbids cross-run
chunk comparisons), all 12 cases. **12/12 faster, 12/12 completely separated,
`token_id` identical 12/12, `logits_sha256` identical 0/12.**

The marginal trade is the finding, and it changes the recommendation: **1024 is
the promotion candidate, not 4096.**

## Result

By size, across four categories:

| size | speedups | peak delta |
|---|---|---:|
| p512 | -29.0 to -29.4% | +0.77 GB |
| p1024 | -32.9 to -34.4% | +1.24 GB |
| p4096 | -32.9 to -35.7% | +1.24 GB |

Flat across category; `token_id` identical 12/12.

## The marginal trade

| promotion | `code-p4096` speedup | peak cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| 256 -> **1024** | **-34.85%** | **+1.24 GB** |
| 256 -> 4096 | -37.61% | +6.20 GB |

The last step buys **2.76 further percentage points for 4.96 GB more**. 256 -> 1024
captures **93% of the win at 20% of the memory**.

Peak delta is flat at +1.24 GB for p1024 and p4096 because the workspace is
`min(chunk, max_sequence_length)` - once the chunk exceeds the sequence length it
stops growing.

## Chunk 1024 is the certified configuration

The X-6 production-profile gate certifies numerically **at chunk 1024**, and hashes
bit-identically at chunk 4096. So this promotion lands on the configuration the
gate actually certifies rather than one that merely matches it.

`logits_sha256` differs from chunk 256 in all 12 cases, so it is still an
**arithmetic** change relative to the current serving default.

## Promotion blocker, stated precisely

**No default changed.** The blocker is **not** the memory cost, which at +1.24 GB
is small.

`max_prefill_chunk_tokens` sits in `EngineLoopConfig` alongside
`fair_prefill_burst_chunks`, `round_prefill_token_budget` and
`round_decode_row_budget`. Its CLI help reads "Maximum prefill chunk tokens per
loop tick", and dispatch caps it again at the round budget
(`chunk_size=min(self.prefill_chunk_size, prefill_budget)`).

It is therefore a **C4 scheduler granularity knob that bounds per-tick prefill
work so decode requests are not starved** - not a model-shape parameter.

Raising it 256 -> 1024 makes each prefill tick up to 4x longer. The measured -29%
to -36% is **prefill throughput** and says nothing about **decode latency under
concurrency**, which is what the tick bound exists to protect. That is
**unmeasured**, and it is the prerequisite `docs/REFACTOR.md` already names for
this mechanism ("qualify ... c2 behavior").

So:

- **Prefill throughput for 256 -> 1024: measured**, 12/12, -29% to -36%, +0.77 to
  +1.24 GB, completely separated, on the certified configuration.
- **Serving default: blocked** on the interleaving measurement, because the knob's
  purpose is bounding per-tick work and that effect has not been measured.

I found this by reading the knob's call sites rather than assuming it was a
model-shape parameter. Treating it as one would have produced a promotion
justified by throughput evidence for a change whose actual risk is latency.
