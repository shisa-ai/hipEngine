# Guard the Q8 MMQ prefill ordering the Gemma map depends on

## Summary

Iteration 154 measured the Q8_0 MMQ chain 44% slower than the bf16 WMMA owner at
Gemma's dense shapes, which made `GEMMA4_Q8_MMQ_MIN_ROWS` look like dead weight.
It is not: its `min_rows` values were calibrated against the *exact* owner, the
route is reachable whenever WMMA prefill is off, and deleting it would remove a
working optimization for that configuration. What was actually missing is that
its calibration basis and the ordering it depends on lived only in a comment --
the map's values mean nothing against the WMMA owner, and only the position of
`_q8_mmq_prefill_dispatch` in the rewrite chain keeps them from applying there.
A reorder would have cost 44% of prefill silently.

So the map stays, its comment now states the basis, and a CPU-only test pins the
ordering.

## Changes

- `hipengine/runtime/gemma4.py`: the `GEMMA4_Q8_MMQ_*` comment records the
  calibration basis (the exact owner), the 1.44x cost against the WMMA owner,
  the guard test, and that the runner's 512-row block is the only row count the
  table can see.
- `tests/test_unit_gguf_q8_mmq_prefill_ordering.py`: four facts pinned -- the
  WMMA rewrite claims the name the MMQ gate matches on; the gate leaves a
  WMMA-claimed shape alone; the gate does take the shape when `use_wmma=False`;
  and the Gemma map admits exactly its shapes at exactly 512 rows.
- Docs: campaign iteration 154 gains the ordering paragraph.

## Validation

- `pytest tests/test_unit_gguf_q8_mmq_prefill_ordering.py` -- 10 passed, no GPU.
- `import hipengine.runtime.gemma4` and the map's contents unchanged.
- No behaviour change: the production comment edit and a new test only. The
  campaign bench rows for iteration 154 were taken before this edit and are
  unaffected.
