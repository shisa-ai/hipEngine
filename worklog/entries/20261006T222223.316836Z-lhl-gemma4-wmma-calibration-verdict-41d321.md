# Calibration packet verdict and the router mechanism measured

## Summary

The frozen 2026-10-07 protocol ran its WMMA arm over the full 18-case packet
(1,152 teacher-forced rows). **The candidate fails the frozen stable-row
bars**: stable-row mean KL 0.0025 (bar 1e-3), p95 0.0110 (bar 5e-3), p99
0.0448 (bar 2e-2), max 0.208 (bar 5e-2); stable-row top-1 passes globally at
99.37%. Tasks are non-inferior on every case (17/18 pass both arms, the same
one fails both), repeats and isolation are green, and the route check
confirms the WMMA variants ran. The protocol did its anti-gaming job: no bar
was widened and no promotion was made.

Three measurements then pinned the mechanism and the limits of any
stability-rule design:

1. **The KL failures are row-local spikes, not poisoned trajectories.** On
   `heldout_mixed_review` the worst row (KL 0.208) sits between rows at KL
   0.001: the state re-converges, and each spike is one row's own routing
   divergence (that row's strict top-1 token at p=0.62 became the candidate's
   #2 at q=0.33 -- logit differences of mean 0.24 on the 30.0 softcap scale).
2. **No strict-side row rule isolates the damage set.** The pre-registered
   output-top-2 rule classifies only 34/1152 rows unstable and misses the
   spikes. A router-level rule is no better: top-8-of-128 boundary margins
   are broadly tiny (every row of `heldout_mixed_review` has a min margin
   under 0.01), a plus/minus-one-bf16-ulp perturbation of the captured
   residual flips the top-8 set on 33-46% of rows at some layer, and yet 11
   of 12 spiked rows are NOT plus/minus-one-ulp flip-plausible: the drift
   compounds through layers, and the damage depends on which experts swap,
   not on the boundary margin. Any rule that did isolate the spikes after
   seeing these results would be candidate-fitted, so none was attempted.
3. **Strict-adjacency is not a property the accepted comparator has.** From
   the 2026-10-04 paired ladder's recorded chains: hipEngine-strict and
   llama.cpp agree on 128/128 greedy tokens at depths 512-4096 and diverge
   0/128 from the first token at depths >=8192 (llama's own repeatability
   gate also rejects its 8192/16384 rows). Cross-engine divergence at depth
   is the accepted norm on this model and quant, so KL-versus-strict cannot
   be the admission bar for depth work; the envelope decision belongs to the
   lead as a product call.

The WMMA arm's stable-row profile, for the record: code mean 0.00086 (passes
the unchanged 1e-3 bar), general_en 0.00185, general_ja 0.00281, mixed_ja_en
0.00539; stable top-1 by category: code 100%, en 99.59%, ja 99.60%, mixed
97.96%. Free-running chains equal strict on 4/4 code cases and diverge on 12
of 18 cases overall, scattered across all depths on non-code content.

The INT8-KV anchor could not be re-run: `paged_attn_prefill` for
`int8_per_token_head` is not a registered combination on this branch
(MissingKernelError, recorded), so the anchor is cited from the recorded
2026-10-02 artifact (mean KL 0.1426, p95 0.895, max 2.43, top-1 87.5%) --
no rule that classifies ~3% of rows unstable can move a mean of 0.1426 under
a 1e-3 bar, so the rejected anchor stays separated by an order of magnitude
under any honest envelope.
