# Point the campaign order at the punchlist and record the 4096-token reversal

## Summary

A concurrent agent landed `docs/campaigns/GEMMA4-26B-A4B-PUNCHLIST.md`
(`3defec128`) with a per-family device-time scoreboard from real
`rocprofv3` traces at 1024 **and** 4096 tokens, plus a candidate list and a
`scripts/gemma4_family_census.py` phase driver. It supersedes the ranking I had
just written into the campaign doc from adjusted 1024-token arithmetic, so that
section now defers to it and keeps only the history of what landed.

**The finding worth carrying: the 1024-token and 4096-token rankings are
different lists.** At 4096 tokens the two attention families are **2089 ms of a
3748 ms device-busy prefill (56%)**, against llama.cpp's 170 ms. Past the
1024-token window the sliding layers fall back to the exact class kernel, so the
long-context case is an attention problem, not a matmul problem. My adjusted
1024-token split could not see this and neither could the trace it was derived
from, which was taken at 1024.

Where the two agree: layer 29, the attention work and the MoE scheduler are the
top items. Where the punchlist corrects me:

- Its dense.q8_0 figure is 115.6 ms, not my adjusted 132.6; its device-busy
  total is 480.6 ms, not 515.8. My split was ~7% high.
- It scopes my "dense int8 does not transfer" conclusion correctly: the refuted
  route is the **guarded three-plane `d4x3` chain**, which is structurally
  different from llama.cpp's single-plane MMQ. The refutation does not extend to
  a llama.cpp-shaped kernel, which is punchlist P5.
- It adds decode, which my re-rank did not cover: four families (moe.gate_up,
  attn.sliding, moe.down, MoE routing) account for 11.3 of the 11.65 ms busy gap.
