# The newer dense Q8_0 decode decoder is performance neutral

## Summary
The decode traffic census puts dense `gguf_q8_0` at **1866.7 MB of the 3219.6 MB
a decode token moves, 58 percent**, and its largest shapes are all `rows=1`
GEMVs. A real decode resolves the **legacy** `pack8_gemv_bf16_bf16_out` for 410
of 411 dense Q8_0 resolutions. There is a newer family,
`gguf_q8_0_pack8_gemv_decode_bf16_bf16_out`, registered on the same key and
described in its own module as a drop-in replacement for the single-output
decoder, selected through `_resolve_use_gemv_decode`. That looked like a large
win sitting unselected on the majority of decode traffic.

**It is not.** Measured A/B at the 512/128 decode shape: **24.81 tok/s unset
against 24.83 tok/s set**. The newer decoder is performance-neutral here.

## The flag does change selection
The A/B alone does not separate "the flag is inert" from "the flag works and
buys nothing". Instrumenting a real `LLM.generate()` and counting resolved
`(quant, variant)` pairs separates them:

| `HIPENGINE_GGUF_GEMV_DECODE` | `pack8_gemv_bf16_bf16_out` | `pack8_gemv_decode_bf16_bf16_out` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| unset | 410 | 0 |
| `1` | 205 | 205 |

So the flag does exactly what its comment says -- it moves half the dense Q8_0
decode resolutions to the newer family -- and the throughput does not move with
it. Half and not all, because the remaining resolutions take a shape or a
sibling the newer family does not cover.

**That is what makes this a negative result rather than an untested lead.** The
58 percent of decode traffic on the legacy decoder is not limited by the
decoder's choice, so the next decode unit has to look elsewhere: launch count
(825.6 launches per step), the `rows=1` GEMV shape itself, or the expert side.

## A documentation sentence that sent the first inference the wrong way
`docs/ENVS.md` said *"The public generator passes `use_gemv_decode=True`"*. That
reads as universal. In fact only `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py` passes it;
the Gemma 4 generator never does, so Gemma 4 takes the process-wide default and
the variable is the only thing that selects the newer family for it. The row now
names the generator, records that Gemma 4 takes the default, and carries the
neutral measurement so the next reader does not re-run this A/B.
