# Sprint D: expert_down LDS weight staging is prescribed, bit-exact, and never tried

## Summary

The routed expert_down gap is 2.23 s of the 12.97 s code-p4096 gap and the
family is weight-traffic-bound: the shipped kernel gathers Q5_1 blocks per lane,
so cost is `sum_experts(ceil(rows/16)) * expert_tensor_bytes`. At the engine's
real routing (1120 tiles for 10240 compact rows) that is 1.261 GB per launch and
242 GB per prefill, a 2.19x over-read against the 0.576 GB of distinct expert
bytes, and 242 GB at the measured 104-126 GB/s reproduces the 2229.8 ms.

Three things came out of reading the source on both sides that were not in the
task framing:

1. **The comparator's move is llama.cpp's MMB, not a bespoke kernel.** PR #63
   adds `mmb.cu`/`mmb-quant.cuh`; the base tree has neither. Its tile is
   `MMB_BK=64, MMB_NT=256, MMB_LDS_STRIDE=BK+8` - **identical to our promoted
   `dense_wide256`**. We are not missing a tile geometry, we are missing the
   routing wrapper that applies it to expert weights.
2. **Part of the -64.1% is an ungated numerics trade.** MMB runs `LLAMA_MMB_BF16W=1`:
   weights dequantized to BF16 (8 significand bits) before the MMA. Our down
   kernel uses raw Q5_1 codes with no requantization error plus Kahan and
   at-risk repair, which is 721 ms of our 2389 ms. This is the same pattern the
   cross-engine comparison already flags elsewhere.
3. **The 32-row rejection does not cover the prescribed fix.** The j32 packet's
   own conclusion says the tile-count model is necessary but not sufficient
   because sharing "only pays if it is explicit", and names the fix: stage the
   chunk's quantized weights in LDS once per block instead of per-lane
   gathering. That path was never built. The rejected experiment changed
   `TILE_ROWS` and kept the gather.

The prescribed path is **bit-exact** - same Q5_1 bytes, same order, same iu8
WMMA and same risk repair, only a different address - so Sprint H's closure of
the GR family to arithmetic change does not apply to it. Feasibility checked
against the gfx11 64 KB workgroup budget: activation staging 32,384 B plus a
128col x 256K Q5_1 chunk 24,576 B is 56,960 B, fitting with 8.4 KB spare; a
128-K chunk halves the weight tile and leaves 20.4 KB for double-buffering.

Prize is arithmetic, not speculative: tiles 1120 -> ~600 at 32 rows, traffic
242 -> ~130 GB, ~1.0-1.1 s of the gap at current bandwidth, bounded at ~1.2 s by
the over-read ratio.

## Next

Build it as one unit: `TILE_ROWS=32`, cooperative LDS weight staging, the
per-lane gather replaced by an LDS read, a bit-exact parity test against the
`j16` owner on real routing, then `--pmc GCEA_RDRAM_SIZE_REQ_sum` at real expert
populations before any gate. The single question is whether the real traffic
follows the model once the sharing is explicit. If traffic halves and time does
not, the loss is the 32-row block's halved occupancy - answer that with a
smaller tile carrying the same staging, not with a rejection of the path. Do not
port MMB's BF16 operands: that is the part of the comparator's win we do not
gate, and the LDS path does not need it.
