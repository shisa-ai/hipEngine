# gemma4 decode cannot reach graph capture, and that is the largest known term

## Summary

The host floor is 9.66 ms/token, 27 percent of decode, driven by about 825 launches per step.
**Graph capture is the obvious fix, and the repo already has it -- but gemma4 cannot reach it.**

`hipengine/runtime/gguf_decode_graph.py` exports `build_qwen35_gguf_decode_graph_key` and
`capture_qwen35_gguf_decode_graph`. Every caller outside that module is qwen35's runner
(`hipengine/runtime/qwen35_gguf_runner.py:11867`). gemma4 has its own generator --
`Gemma4GGUFGenerator` in `hipengine/generation/gemma4_gguf.py` -- and never enters that path.

**So the single largest measured decode term is a plumbing gap, not a kernel problem.**

## Changes

None. This entry records reconnaissance.

## Validation

Read-only inspection of the graph-capture call graph. No run.

## What is already there

- **The enable flag defaults to ON**: `HIPENGINE_GGUF_DECODE_GRAPH`, read by
  `_gguf_decode_graph_enabled()` as `os.environ.get(ENV, "1") in {"1","true","yes","on"}`.
- `hipengine/core/hip.py` wraps `hipStreamBeginCapture`.
- `hipengine/core/pm4/` carries a graph runtime, a submission transport, and a manifest.
- `hipengine/generation/batch_scheduler.py` has a `GraphBucketCache` keyed by
  `BatchShapeKey` -- the "full batch/specdecode shape" -- with a `get_or_create` factory and
  hit/miss stats.

**The pieces are general; the wiring is not.**

## Why this is the right next change

| term | ms/token | share | reachable by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **host floor** | **9.66** | **27%** | dispatch/plumbing |
| MoE GEMV | 6.22 | 17% | kernel |
| attention | ~4.3-5.6 | 12-16% | kernel rewrite |
| dense projection | 3.88 | 11% | kernel |

**It is the largest term, it needs no kernel work, and the mechanism already exists and is
enabled by default for a sibling model.** Compare the attention rewrite rejected in
`20260929T223000`: smaller term, kernel rewrite, parity-test constraints.

## What is not claimed

- **No capture was attempted and no decode throughput was measured with a graph.** The 9.66 ms is
  the *host* floor with no launches issued; how much of it a graph would actually remove is not
  established here. A graph removes per-launch host work, but the Python call chain that builds
  the step still runs.
- **The 825 launches/step figure is from the 2026-09-25 gfx1100 census**, not re-measured on
  gfx1151. The 9.66 ms host floor is measured on gfx1151.
- **Whether gemma4's step is capturable at all was not checked** -- shape stability across steps,
  and whether any of its kernels do host-side work mid-step, are the usual blockers and neither
  was examined.

## Next

- **Establish whether gemma4's decode step is capture-compatible**: constant shapes across
  decode steps, no host round-trips inside the step. That is the precondition, and it is cheap to
  check by inspection.
- **Then extend the existing capture path to `Gemma4GGUFGenerator`** rather than writing a second
  one. The `GraphBucketCache` key is already the right shape (batch/specdecode), and the flag is
  already on by default.
- **Re-derive the MoE GEMV term on gfx1151** (6.22 ms is an inherited gfx1100 number) before
  touching it, for the reason `20260929T223000` exists.
