# The prefill shape policy is unnecessary: a short request costs the same on either workspace owner

## Summary

`docs/REFACTOR.md` named four requirements for enabling automatic prefill-workspace
selection. Three were already cleared. The fourth was a **measurement**, not a
qualification:

> "measure short/long requests on the same prepared owners"

Every prior measurement used one process per arm, so the selection path had never been
exercised end-to-end on a prepared pair. **This entry does that, and the result removes the
requirement rather than satisfying it.**

## Result

One runner, two prepared owners (base **4096** sets the staging capacity; extra **1024**,
since the selector may only choose owners at or below the base), one process. The owner that
served each request is **recorded by a probe inside `_prefill_chunked_impl`** - the function
the selector calls *after* swapping the workspace fields - so it is observed, not inferred.

| request | tokens | owner served | median | spread | logits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| short, selector **on** | 512 | **1024** | 3.2068 s | 1.99% | `fefecd92...` |
| short, selector **off** | 512 | **4096** | 3.1730 s | 0.73% | `fefecd92...` |
| long | 4096 | **4096** | 15.9833 s | 0.47% | `2cb5f393...` |

The selection works and selects correctly for both shapes. **The shape policy buys the short
request nothing**: forcing it onto the small owner is **1.06% slower** at **0.017x the
within-arm spread**. The honest statement is *no measurable benefit*. The policy is also
arithmetic-neutral - the short request's `logits_sha256` is **identical** on both owners.
The extra owner costs **1.555 GiB** and returns nothing.

## Why the premise fails

`REFACTOR.md` justifies the policy as *"preserving the smaller owner's short costs"*, which
assumes a smaller workspace makes a short request cheaper. It does not: the workspace is
**scratch**, its rows are `min(chunk_size, max_sequence_length)`, and the kernels' work scales
with the **actual token count**, not with the size of the scratch they are handed. A 512-token
prefill does 512 tokens of work whichever owner supplies the buffers.

This is the stronger version of a mechanism RT-1 already found from the other direction: there,
for prompts at or below the smaller chunk, both chunk sizes resolved to the same
`min(chunk, max_seq_len)`. Here the owners genuinely differ (1024 vs 4096 rows against a 4352
sequence) and the short request is **still** unaffected.

## What this changes

**It removes a blocker instead of clearing it.** The shape policy was the reason a blanket
chunk increase could not be promoted. If short requests are unaffected by owner size there is
nothing to preserve, so **the simpler single-owner configuration is at least as good** -
without the extra 1.555 GiB, without the borrow/restore path, and without a selector needing
qualification.

RT-1's promotion therefore does **not** need a shape policy. What remains is the
memory-admission question for a single larger owner: a capacity question, not a policy one.

## Validation

- `status: complete`; **both selection assertions pass** (`served == [expected]` for the
  short, the long, and the selector-off arm), asserted in-script rather than eyeballed.
- The served owner is probed at the point of use, not derived from timing.
- The harness imports the same production generator factory, profile registration and scratch
  breakdown the existing owned-workspace check uses.
- **The 1.06% is inside the noise**, so the entry claims *no measurable benefit* and explicitly
  declines the stronger claim that the small owner is worse. The short arm's own spread (1.99%)
  exceeds the effect.
- Limits recorded: one short case and one long case only; one host, one model, one quant, one
  profile; GTT is 120 GB here and the base owner peaks near 89 GB, so a lower-memory host could
  not prepare both owners at all; the selector was enabled only inside that process.
- **No promotion claim and no runtime default changed.**
