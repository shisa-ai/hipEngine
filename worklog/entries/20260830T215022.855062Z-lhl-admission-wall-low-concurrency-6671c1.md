# our admission wall exceeds the peer at low concurrency while our decode does not

## Summary

Task #14 asks whether the C1-C3 prefill deficit is kernel time. An existing artifact
already answers the equivalent question for AR, and the answer points away from
kernels: our admission wall is larger than the peer's at low concurrency, and once
admission is removed our decode rate is better at every width including the ones where
the published number loses.

## Changes

- None. Read from
  `benchmarks/results/2026-08-30-w7900-q4km-c1c8-admission-decomposition-post-grouping.json`,
  produced by `scripts/gguf_engine_submodule_decomposition.py`.

## Validation

AR, hipEngine versus llama.cpp current, same W7900 protocol. Rates in tok/s,
admission in ms per wave:

| C | ours admission | ours published | ours without admission | peer admission | peer published | peer without admission |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 308 | 22.20 | 31.06 | 223 | 21.72 | 27.22 |
| 2 | 594 | 32.71 | 54.97 | 375 | 35.44 | 48.99 |
| 3 | 602 | 44.33 | 70.40 | 520 | 30.79 | 39.59 |
| 5 | 650 | 62.85 | 95.33 | 695 | 36.39 | 46.11 |
| 8 | 878 | 78.27 | 121.91 | 847 | 58.74 | 79.29 |

Three things follow:

- Our admission wall is bigger than the peer's at C1 and C2: 308 versus 223 ms, and
  594 versus 375 ms. At C2 that is 219 ms, which is more than the whole published
  gap. The only AR cell where we lose is the one where our overhead is largest.
- Remove admission and we lead at every width, C2 by 12 percent and C1 by 14 percent.
  The kernels are not the problem at low concurrency.
- At C5 and C8 our admission is roughly level with the peer while our post-admission
  rate is 2.1x and 1.5x theirs, which matches AR winning those cells outright.

Read together with the four-request MTP clamp, low-concurrency wall deficits in this
engine look like control-path cost, and the C1-C3 prefill deficit should be attributed
the same way before any kernel work is justified against it.

Caveats carried from the artifact itself: one wave per width, so gaps under about 5
percent are not separable from noise, though 85 ms and 219 ms are far larger than
that; peer admission comes from peer packets under peer protocol; the MTP arm was
explicitly not recomputed, so MTP admission is still undecomposed.

## Next

1. Extend the decomposition to the prefill arm at C1-C3 with the same method
   (`scripts/gguf_engine_submodule_decomposition.py`). If our prefill admission also
   exceeds the peer's, kernel work is not the first fix.
2. Do the same for the MTP arm, which the artifact names as not recomputed. That
   would separate the four-request clamp hypothesis from plain admission overhead at
   C5-C8.
3. If the prefill admission is not the culprit, only then does the down-projection
   grid work in #11 become the leading prefill action.
