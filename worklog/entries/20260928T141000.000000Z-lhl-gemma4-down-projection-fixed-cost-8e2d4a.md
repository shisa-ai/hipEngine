# the down projection moves the same 33 GB whether it has 8 rows per expert or 32

## Summary

The Q5_1 down projection is the largest single term in a gemma4 prefill. Running its
own microbench harness at two row counts separates its cost into a part that scales
with work and a part that does not, and **the part that does not is the larger one at
the production shape.**

| rows/expert | compact rows | arithmetic | **FETCH (measured)** | median | GMAC/s |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 1,024 | 1x | **32,486 MB** | 1.604 ms | 1,322.6 |
| 16 | 2,048 | 2x | (not collected) | 1.631 ms | 2,489.0 |
| **32** | **4,096** | **4x** | **33,254 MB** | **2.925 ms** | **3,004.4** |
| 64 | 8,192 | 8x | (not collected) | 5.267 ms | 3,083.3 |

**Quadrupling the arithmetic adds 2 percent to the traffic.** The kernel moves 33 GB
of L2-to-CU fetch traffic for a weight set of **190.3 MB** -- the 128 experts'
`(2816, 704)` Q5_1 tiles, 1.487 MB each -- which is **171 passes over the weight
set**. At 8 rows per expert it does a quarter of the work for the same 33 GB.

The time shows the same shape without a counter. Against `time = a + b * rows`:

```
b = (2.925 - 1.604) / (32 - 8) = 0.0550 ms per row-per-expert
a = 1.604 - 8 * 0.0550        = 1.164 ms fixed
```

so **at the production shape 1.164 of the 2.925 ms -- 40 percent -- is a term that
does not scale with rows.** The fit is two points and the rows=64 case already
deviates from it upward (4.68 predicted against 5.267 measured), so treat the split
as indicative rather than exact; the direction is what is measured, and the direction
does not depend on the fit.

### What this does and does not say

The counter cannot be DRAM traffic. 33 GB in 1.604 ms is 19.8 TB/s, and even the
production shape's 33,254 MB in 2.925 ms is 11.1 TB/s. On a 40-CU part with a 256 GB/s
DRAM bus, that is L2-to-CU fabric traffic, which is what `FETCH_SIZE` counts. **So the
finding is not "the down projection is DRAM-bound"** -- the whole prefill's DRAM floor
is 15.83 GB read once, 23 GB/s, 9 percent of peak.

**It is that the kernel's L2-to-CU traffic is 171 times its weight set and does not
fall when the work falls.** Two readings are consistent with that and the measurement
does not separate them:

- the kernel re-reads weight tiles it does not need, in which case the fix is a
  layout or tiling change and the ceiling is large;
- the 33 GB is the kernel's own internal L2-to-CU movement through LDS at a fixed
  tile size, in which case it is a design property and the fix is a different
  instruction mix.

The 19.8 TB/s observed at 8 rows against 11.1 TB/s at 32 says the fabric has headroom
the production shape is not using -- the kernel is not pinned to the fabric's ceiling,
it is doing more fabric work than it needs to.

**The harness's own model is close and was never checked.** It computes
`weight_bytes_x_passes` = 24.36 GB at 32 rows, against 33.25 GB measured -- 1.37x low,
and it reports the same 24.36 GB at 8 rows where the measurement says 32.5. Its
docstring already flags that "the fraction that remained was attributed to activation
re-reads by elimination rather than by measurement." That elimination was wrong in
direction: the residual is not activation re-reads, because activations are 3.9 MB and
would scale with rows. It is a row-count-independent re-read of the weight stream.

  .venv/bin/python scripts/gguf_q5_1_down_mmq_microbench.py \
    --rows-per-expert $r --iters 25 --warmup 5

## Next

- **Read `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel` and find what is re-read 171 times.**
  The specific question: for a tile of rows that all belong to one expert, does the
  kernel's grid cover the expert's full out-feature range (correct, one pass over that
  expert's 1.487 MB) or the full weight set (171 passes)? The expert's weights are
  contiguous at `(expert * out + row)`, so the index arithmetic is the place to look.
- **The same test applies to the gate/up kernel**
  (`gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_fused_prefill_compact32_kernel`, 29,080 MB
  of fetches against a 7.71 GB weight set -- 3.8x). Its amplification is 45x smaller
  than the down projection's, so the down projection is the one to read first.
- **Do not collect two PMCs in one rocprofv3 request on this part.** The harness's
  docstring records that two or more fail with "Request exceeds the capabilities of
  the hardware to collect"; the two runs above are separate requests.
- **A row-count sweep with the counter is the instrument for any kernel with a
  suspected fixed cost.** One shape says what a kernel costs; two shapes say which
  part of it is worth attacking, and this one took two runs.
