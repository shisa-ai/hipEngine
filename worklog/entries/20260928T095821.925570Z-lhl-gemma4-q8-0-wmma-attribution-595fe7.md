# the q8_0 wmma prefill tile is already swept and the kernel is register limited at 22 percent occupancy

## Summary

`gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel<tt,32,64>` is the largest prefill block at 24.8
percent (180.6 ms per prefill) and the one prefill component I had not examined.
**Its tile is not a guess -- it is the winner of two recorded sweeps -- and the
kernel's limit is registers, not the tile.** The lead is closed by prior work plus
one arithmetic step, not by a new measurement.

## The launch geometry

`gguf_q8_0_prefill.hip:263` fixes the block at one wave32:

```cuda
const dim3 block(32);
const dim3 grid(
    (out_features + tile_m - 1) / tile_m,
    (rows + tile_n - 1) / tile_n);
```

At the 512-token prefill shape the trace's `grid=2816x8 blk=32` is, with the
work-item reading established in `20260928T095433`, **`88 x 8 = 704 workgroups of
32 threads`** -- 22,528 threads for a GEMM of `out=2816, rows=512`.

## What it achieves, from the module's own sweep

The module docstring records best-of-8 timings at `rows=512`, bf16 in and out.
Converting those to rates:

| shape | ms | GFLOP | TFLOP/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `(in=2816, out=4096)` dense ffn gate/up | 0.939 | 11.8 | **12.58** |
| `(in=2816, out=2816)` o proj | 0.684 | 8.1 | **11.87** |
| `(in=4096, out=2816)` dense ffn down | 1.482 | 11.8 | **7.97** |
| `(in=2816, out=2112)` swa k/v | 0.498 | 6.1 | **12.23** |

The 8060S's fp32 FMA ceiling is about 25.6 TFLOP/s (40 CU x 128 lanes x 2 x
2.5 GHz), so bf16 WMMA is roughly 51 TFLOP/s. **This kernel runs at 16 to 25 percent
of that** -- an order of magnitude better than the prefill attention kernel's 2
percent of fp32, and the reason attention is the one that needs a rewrite.

## Why the obvious lever is already spent

`_default_tiles` is the outcome of two sweeps over eleven shapes, and the recorded
numbers are in the docstring. `(32, 64)` is fastest on three of gemma4's four dense
prefill geometries and the family total drops **4.121 to 3.639 ms**; the second sweep
existed precisely because the first never reached `tile_n=64`. Six tile
configurations were measured per shape.

**The kernel is register-limited, and the sweep already traded against it.** The
docstring records `(64, 32)` at **127 VGPRs** against `(32, 64)` at **143**, and notes
that the wider token tile "was assumed unaffordable and never swept; the real
difference is 16 registers, not a resource wall". At 143 VGPRs a wave32 holds 4,576
of a CU's 65,536 VGPRs, so about **14 waves per CU -- 448 of 2,048 threads, roughly
22 percent occupancy.**

**So the kernel gives up occupancy deliberately to buy weight-tile reuse, and the
measurements say that trade wins.** Lowering registers to raise occupancy is the
lever the sweep already pulled and rejected.

## What this leaves for prefill

At 512 tokens, with each block now attributed:

| per prefill | share | kernel | status |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 180.6 ms | 24.8% | `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel<tt,32,64>` | **closed here**: tile swept, register-limited by design |
| 178.3 ms | 24.5% | `gemma4_attention_prefill_kernel` | **closed**: not traffic-bound, not unroll-bound, tiled is a wash; needs tensor-core QK/PV |
| 153.2 ms | 21.0% | MoE gate/up (MMQ32 fused) | **open**: 7568 workgroups at prefill, so the decode thin-parallelism finding does not apply |
| 115.8 ms | 15.9% | MoE down (Q5_1 DS4 MMQ) | open |

**The two MoE kernels are 36.9 percent of prefill between them and are the only
remaining unexplored blocks.** The gate/up moves 285 MB of expert weights per layer,
about 8.3 GB over 29 layers, in 153.2 ms -- **54 GB/s**, against the 199 GB/s the
dense GEMV reaches on the same box. That is the number to attack, and unlike the
decode case there are already 7,568 workgroups, so it is not a block-count problem.
