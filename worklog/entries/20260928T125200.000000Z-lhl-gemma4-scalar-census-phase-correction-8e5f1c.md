# the scalar census's 6.2x was decode; in prefill the attention kernel has the worst scalar ratio

**This entry corrects the scalar claim in
`20260928T121300.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-attention-scalar-bound-7f2b1a.md` and the
"aimed at the smaller term" framing that followed from it in two later entries.**

## Summary

The scalar census summed `SQ_INSTS_SALU` and `SQ_INSTS_VALU` over every dispatch of a
run that does a prefill and then 128 decode steps -- the same phase error the traffic
census had. The CSVs were still on disk, so re-reading them through the splitter
costs nothing and the result changes the conclusion.

| PREFILL | SALU | VALU | SALU/VALU |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `gguf_q4_k_..._mmq32_fused_prefill_compact32_kernel` | 5.946e9 | 1.492e10 | 0.40 |

### What was wrong

The scalar-bound entry reported that "the expert gate/up MMQ32 kernel is the largest
absolute scalar consumer at 6.2 times the attention kernel's stream". **In the prefill
phase it is 1.07 times, not 6.2.** The 6.2 came almost entirely from decode, where the
kernel runs 128 times and issues 6.351e10 scalar instructions against its prefill
5.946e9 -- a 10.7x ratio between the phases.

### What it means, and it reverses the practical conclusion

Two later entries concluded from that number that the attention scalar work had been
"aimed at the smaller term" and that the lever should move to the expert MMQ kernels.
On the corrected prefill numbers that is not what they show:

| kernel | prefill SALU | share of prefill time | SALU per unit of time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| expert gate/up MMQ32 | 5.946e9 | 24.0% | 24.8e9 |
| `gemma4_attention_prefill` | 5.576e9 | 19.2% | **29.0e9** |
| Q5_1 down MMQ | 1.403e9 | 16.9% | 8.3e9 |

**The attention kernel has the highest scalar density per unit of time in the prefill
and the worst scalar-to-vector ratio of any kernel in it** -- 0.74 against the gate/up's
0.40 and the down's 0.21. The expert block is still the larger term in *time* (46.8
percent against 19.2), which is what the original framing was about and which stands.
But the *scalar* lever is best aimed at attention, which is where it already was, and
the two entries that moved it away were moving it on a decode number.

**The attention scalar-bound finding itself is untouched.** SALU is 0.74 of VALU in
that kernel, the scalar stream is most of its runtime, and the five closed explanations
for its cost are unaffected. What changes is only which kernel the scalar lever should
point at, and it points back.

### Three corrections from one phase error

This is the third entry corrected by the same mistake: the traffic total, the overfetch
denominator, and now the scalar ordering. All three came from reading a counter census
that covered both phases. The splitter added in
`20260928T124400.000000Z-lhl-gemma4-rocprof-phase-split-6a4c2d.md` found all three --
the third one without a new profiling run, because it re-read CSVs that were already on
disk and the previous analysis had thrown the phase information away.
