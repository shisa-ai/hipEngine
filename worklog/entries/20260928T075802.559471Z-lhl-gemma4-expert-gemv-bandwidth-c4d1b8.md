# the expert GEMV reaches 17 to 21 percent of peak in isolation so it is kernel-limited not starved

## Summary

The isolated expert GEMV probe inverts the hypothesis I was about to test. I
expected the expert family's poor in-situ bandwidth to be a scheduling problem --
762 launches per token, each too short to reach steady state -- and that an
isolated run would show the kernel reaching a much higher rate. It does not.

`scripts/gemma4_expert_selected_bw.py --rows 8`, at the artifact's own shapes:

| kernel | us/layer | MB | GB/s | % of 256 peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gate_up_q4_k selected` | 411.15 | 17.842 | **43.4** | 17.0% |
| `gate_up_q4_k selected_pack8` | 330.13 | 17.842 | **54.0** | 21.1% |
| `down_q5_1 selected` | 247.45 | 11.895 | **48.1** | 18.8% |

The per-layer byte figures reconcile with the metadata: 8 x 2 x 2816 x 704 x
0.5625 = 17.842 MB for gate/up, and 8 x 704 x 2816 x 0.6875 = 11.895 MB for down.
So the probe is measuring the shapes the decode actually issues.

**The in-situ expert family is faster than the isolated kernels.** The census puts
the expert family at 14.04 ms per step against the pure-decode floor of 862.4 MB,
which is about **61 GB/s** -- above the 43-54 GB/s these kernels reach when run
back to back in isolation. Layer-to-layer overlap recovers some of the difference.
The isolated figures also sum to 19.75 ms per token across 30 layers (12.33
gate/up plus 7.42 down), which is more than the 14.04 ms the census attributes to
the whole family.

That is the finding: **the expert GEMV is kernel-limited, not starved.** Its
headroom is not "reach the 114 GB/s llama.cpp achieves overall" -- it is the 5.9x
between 43.4 GB/s and the 69.7 us that 17.842 MB would take at peak. Nothing about
reducing launch count or overlapping more work addresses a kernel that cannot
exceed a fifth of peak when run in a tight loop with no competition.

This also narrows what the census entry's conclusion means. "The lever is
achieved bandwidth, not peak" is right for the dense family, which reaches 135
GB/s in situ, and wrong for the expert family, where the kernel's own ceiling is
the limit. The two families need different work.

`selected_pack8` is already 1.25x faster than `selected` on the same shapes (54.0
against 43.4 GB/s), which is a hint that the access pattern is the constraint
rather than the arithmetic.
