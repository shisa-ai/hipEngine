# Qwen4Exp 4K prefill is GPU-bound at 95.7%; the llama.cpp gap is pure kernel throughput

## Summary

The objective is to match the tuned llama.cpp forks on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next prefill
and decode. On the same-file, same-host table those forks reach a weighted
**915.4** tok/s (best) against hipEngine's **274.1**. Converting to time per token
shows the shape of the gap: the forks ramp **479.4 -> 860.8 -> 1051.6** across
512/1K/4K, i.e. ~0.95 ms/token once fixed overhead amortises, while hipEngine is
**flat-to-worse at 294.5 -> 292.0 -> 259.6**, i.e. **3.40 / 3.43 / 3.85 ms/token**.

Before choosing a lever I asked whether that gap is recoverable outside the
kernels - dispatch overhead, host stalls, missing overlap, bad scheduling. **It is
not.** A `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` over the 4K prefill gives a **prefill window of
68.26 s that is 95.7% GPU-busy** (65.303 s busy, 2.957 s idle). The idle time is
**85,245 gaps under 50 ms totalling 1.364 s**, a **0.016 ms median**, plus 27 gaps
over 50 ms totalling 1.593 s. There is no idle time in the measured window to
recover and no launch-overhead tax worth attacking.

The trace does contain 49 stalls of ~1 s each, and they are a real phenomenon -
but they sit **entirely in the first 49 s of the trace, where there are zero MoE
prefill kernels at all**. That is weight upload and cache setup, adjacent to
`__amd_rocclr_copyBuffer` calls that themselves total only **5 ms across 2516
dispatches**, so the cost is host-side work waiting on those copies rather than
the copies themselves. It affects time-to-first-token and not prefill throughput,
and it is out of scope for this objective.

So the conclusion is structural: **the prefill is GPU-bound and the gap can only
be closed by making kernels faster.** I recorded the priority map so the next
units attack the right things.

**The MoE pair is 31.91%, or 38.34% including its repair pass; the top four
families are 64.08%.** The priority reading is that a 3.6x per-token improvement
cannot come from one family - it needs the top four to improve roughly in
proportion.

## Validation

- `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` over `scripts/qwen4exp_canonical_ar_bench.py` prefill
  on `code-p4096`, 85,696 launches. Both binding arms (4.0 and 1.0) were traced
  and **every family share agrees within 0.15 points**, which bounds arm-to-arm
  drift on the shares.
- Occupancy computed from merged intervals rather than raw sums, because
  overlapping dispatches make the naive sum wrong: the raw sum is 65.358 s in a
  117.441 s span, and merging is what separates the setup phase (49 s, no MoE
  kernels) from the prefill phase (68.26 s, 95.7% busy).
- The four closed levers were re-verified against their artifacts rather than
  taken from memory: HC-1 is `numerical_passed: false, task_passed: false` with
  `mixed_ja_en` mean KL 1.499e-3 against the 1e-3 limit, broad-based drift rather
  than a tail artifact, and its dense sibling failed the same way; MX-1's F16
  gate/up owner is 1.54-2.42x the production kernel so the repair it would remove
  cannot pay for it; the GR `ROW_BATCH` widening is an occupancy loss (130 VGPR
  crosses the 128 granule, halving resident waves) measuring 0.833-0.844x.
- `rocprofv3` perturbs absolute timing, so this artifact is used for **shares and
  occupancy**, not as a throughput measurement. The throughput figures in the
  comparison come from the profiler-free canonical protocol.

## Next

- **The open lever on the biggest family is the MoE per-expert tile padding**,
  measured at **1.25x** (12,800 padded rows for 10,240 real). Fully removed it is
  worth roughly **4.1%** of prefill on the gate/up half and **2.3%** on the down
  half. This is the next unit: it is the only open, measured, numerically-neutral
  lever on the 31.91% MoE pair, and the arithmetic is unchanged by construction
  because padding rows are discarded rather than summed.
- **Do not** re-open HC-1 (GR up), MX-1 (F16 gate/up owner), or the GR
  `ROW_BATCH` widening. All three are closed with recorded mechanisms, and the
  status ledger in the artifact says why so the closures are not re-litigated.
- **Do not** pursue dispatch, launch-overhead, stream-overlap or scheduling work
  for prefill throughput. The measurement above is the reason.
- Still unmeasured and worth a unit on its own: why the tuned forks ramp
  **2.2x** from 512 to 4K while hipEngine is flat. A batch-dependent kernel is
  the most likely cause and the breakdown's `ms/launch` column is where it would
  show, but nothing here attributes it.
