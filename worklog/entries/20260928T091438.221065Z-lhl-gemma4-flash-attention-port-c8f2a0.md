# the flash port needs no gather kernel but cannot parameterise to head_dim 512

## Summary

Before writing `gemma4_attention_flash.hip` I read both kernels against each other.
Two structural facts change the port plan in `20260928T052350`, one making it
smaller and one making step 1 fail as written.

**The gather kernel is unnecessary.** The QSA design is two kernels: a gather that
materialises the paged bf16 KV cache into contiguous scratch through the block
table, then flash prefill over that scratch. Gemma 4's cache is **already
contiguous** -- `gemma4_layer.py` appends this block's rotated K and normalised V
into a `(capacity, kv_heads, head_dim)` cache with no stride change, and passes
`key_cache + key_begin * kv_width * _BF16_BYTES` straight to the prefill, with the
stale slots past the live context masked out rather than read. There is no block
table on this path. So the port is the flash prefill kernel **only**, and
`qwen4_exp_qsa_gather_kv_f32_kernel` has no counterpart to port.

**head_dim 512 does not fit the QSA tiling.** The QSA shared-memory budget at its
own constants (`QSA_QT = 32`, `QSA_KT = 32`, 256 threads):

| head_dim | q_tile | k_tile | v_tile | s_p | m/l/rescale | total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 256 | 16.4 KiB | 16.4 KiB | 16.4 KiB | 4.0 KiB | 0.4 KiB | **52.4 KiB** |
| 512 | 32.8 KiB | 32.8 KiB | 32.8 KiB | 4.0 KiB | 0.4 KiB | **100.4 KiB** |

gfx1151 allows **64 KiB per workgroup**. At 256 the design fits; at 512 it is 1.57x
over and will not launch. `20260928T052350`'s step 1 says to "parameterise the QSA
structure on head_dim (256 and 512)", which reads as a constant change and is not
one -- 512 needs a different tiling, and the choices (shrink `QSA_KT`, stop staging
`v_tile` in LDS, split the dim across warps) each change which loop is unrolled and
how the accumulation cells map, so they are design decisions rather than a
parameter.

The reference kernel hard-codes 256 throughout: `q_tile[QSA_QT * 256]`, `d < 256`,
and `#pragma unroll 8 for (int d = 0; d < 256; ++d)` in the QK dot. Gemma 4 needs
both widths, and the five 512-wide layers are the ones the warp unroll already
found responsive (8.05 to 6.84 ms, `20260928T052132`).

## What the port actually has to change

1. **Block mapping.** Gemma 4 launches one block per `(token, head)`
   (`blockIdx.x / num_heads`). QSA launches one block per `(Q-tile, head)` with
   `QT = 32` query rows. Adopting the QSA mapping is what makes the key-parallel
   layout pay, and it changes the output-layout contract and the grid shape, not
   just the inner loop.
2. **Masking.** QSA masks with `positions` and an implicit causal bound. Gemma 4
   masks with `keep_mask` (a per-row byte mask covering the eviction case),
   `first_kept` from `window`/`row_offset`, and `key_begin`. The gemma4 semantics
   are the contract the geometry suites pin, so they stay.
3. **head_dim.** 256 fits the QSA tiling; 512 needs the tiling decision above.
4. **The inner loop.** This is the prize and it is unchanged from the plan: gemma4
   gives one warp per key and reduces 32 lanes with five shuffles plus a broadcast
   per key; QSA gives lane k key k, does the full 256-dim dot serially with no
   per-key shuffles, and reduces only per row for the softmax max/sum. At head_dim
   256 that is the difference between roughly 30 instructions for 8 MACs with 12
   of them in the reduction tree, and a dot with no tree at all.

## Validation

- **Gather not needed**: `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/gemma4_layer.py`
  around the prefill call -- the `_append_kv` pair writes into
  `kv.key_cache`/`kv.value_cache` at `kv.write_offset * kv_width`, and the prefill
  is handed `kv.key_cache + key_begin * kv_width * _BF16_BYTES`. Its own comment
  states the layout is `(capacity, kv_heads, head_dim)` and that the block is
  `(rows, kv_heads, head_dim)` with "no stride change".
- **LDS budget**: the shared arrays in
  `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/attention/qwen4_exp_qsa_flash.hip` are
  `q_tile[QSA_QT*256]`, `k_tile[QSA_KT*256]`, `v_tile[QSA_KT*256]` as `uint16_t`,
  plus `s_p[QSA_QT*QSA_KT]`, `m_run`, `l_run` and `rescale` as float. Summed at
  `QT = KT = 32` for both head widths, the table above.
- **The 64 KiB limit** is the gfx1151 workgroup LDS budget, which is the same
  ceiling the existing gemma4 kernel sizes against.

## Next

1. Decide the 512-wide tiling before writing the kernel, or scope the port to the
   25 sliding 256-wide layers first, where the design fits as-is and where 130.5
   of the 164.7 ms lives.
2. Then write the kernel with the QSA mapping, gemma4's mask semantics, and no
   gather; register as `gemma4_flash`; validate against `gemma4_plain` on the
   existing geometry suites; measure with `scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py`
   before any default flip.
3. Bit-identity against `gemma4_plain` remains the gate, and it is the hard part:
   the QSA accumulation order is per-row serial over keys where gemma4's is a
   warp-key tree, so matching bits means reproducing the existing reduction order
   rather than adopting QSA's.
