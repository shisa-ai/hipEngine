# decode attributes for the first time 28.9 ms of device time in 1199 launches and the moe gate up runs at a third of the dense paths bandwidth

## Summary

Decode had not been attributed before this unit. It is now: **28.90 ms of device
time per token across 1,199 kernel launches**, against a measured 35.6 ms per
token on the campaign bench. The 6.7 ms difference is launch gap, about 19
percent of the step.

Two things fall out. **The launch count is at its structural minimum** -- roughly
one launch per projection per layer, plus 204 normalisation launches -- so there
is no cheap launch to remove. And **the MoE gate_up moves 534 MB per token at 70
GB/s while the dense GEMV path moves 1.75 GB at 199 GB/s on the same device**,
which is the one place in decode where the engine is not already near the
memory ceiling.

**Every kernel in this table is named `prefill` or `decode` in a way that does
not describe when it runs.** `gguf_k_pack8_prefill_out_kernel` is the decode
GEMV and is 30 percent of decode; `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel` is the
decode down projection. The names come from the kernel family's first use, not
from the route that selects them, and reading the trace by name would mislead.

## Decode takes the prefill route on purpose

`gemma4_moe_prefill_route_enabled` (`gemma4_experts.py:240`) keys on
`scratch.tokens`, the width the *runner* was built for, not the width of the
call in flight. Its docstring gives the reason and it is a good one: the two
routes are different arithmetic -- an int8-dp4a accumulation against an fp32 one
-- so selecting by live row count would make a token's output depend on how many
tokens happened to share its call, and that difference compounds through the KV
cache. This is the batch-composition-invariance contract, and it is why the down
MMQ fix measured +1.6 percent on decode as well as +6.6 percent on prefill: a
512-token runner's decodes run the same kernel as its prefill.

So the prefill kernel work in `5363f73fd` and `2cf3a0a5f` is also decode work,
and this is not a defect to route around.

## Where decode is not near the ceiling

Weight traffic per token, and the rate the trace shows for it:

| path | bytes per token | ms | GB/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dense GEMV (`pack8<tt,8>`) | 1.749 GB | 8.78 | **199** |
| lm_head (`pack8<tf,8>`) | 0.784 GB | 3.46 | **227** |
| **MoE gate_up (`mmq32`)** | **0.534 GB** | **7.09** | **75** |
| MoE down (`q5_1`) | 0.107 GB | 2.29 | 47 |

The dense GEMV and the lm_head are at 78 and 89 percent of the 256 GB/s the part
has, which is as good as a single-token GEMV gets. **The MoE gate_up is at 75
GB/s and the MoE down at 47**, and together they are 32 percent of decode.

The gate_up figure is the one worth acting on: it is a grouped MMQ whose 28
launches per token are one per layer, so the per-expert grid is not the
overhead. At the dense path's 199 GB/s the same bytes would take 2.7 ms instead
of 7.09, which is 4.4 ms per token, or 15 percent of decode.

## The launch count is structural

1,199 launches per token, of which:

* **199** are the dense GEMV, against the 210 that 7 projections x 30 layers
  predicts -- one launch per projection per layer, so this is already minimal.
* **204** are normalisation (`gemma4_rmsnorm_kernel` 146 plus
  `gemma4_add_rmsnorm_scale_kernel` 58), about 7 per layer.
* **56** are the two MoE expert projections.
* **24** are attention.
* the remaining ~700 are the MoE bookkeeping family
  (`moe_group_compact_active`, `moe_gather_packed_hidden`,
  `moe_weighted_accumulate`, `router_logits`, `router_prescale`) at about 29
  launches each per token.

The 6.7 ms of gap over 1,199 launches is 5.6 µs each, which is ordinary launch
overhead and not a stall. Removing it means fusing launches, not retuning any
one kernel.
