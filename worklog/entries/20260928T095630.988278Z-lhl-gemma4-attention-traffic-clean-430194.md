# the dtype ablation is the clean traffic probe and it says prefill attention is not paying for bytes

## Summary

`20260928T093930` reported that `--mask zero` made the prefill attention kernel
slower and concluded it was not load-bound; `20260928T094251` withdrew that because
the ablation also changes the arithmetic. **The bench has a probe that does not:
`--dtype f32` runs identical arithmetic over the same shapes and differs only in
bytes moved.** It says the kernel is not traffic-bound, which settles the question
the withdrawn probe left open.

## The clean ablation

`scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py --tokens 512 --iters 5 --warmup 2`:

| case | bf16 ms | f32 ms | ratio | bf16 GFLOP/s | f32 GFLOP/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| swa 256d 16q/8kv w=1024 | 4.63 | 4.99 | **1.078** | 464.5 | 431.1 |
| swa 256d 16q/8kv w=0 | 4.73 | 5.09 | 1.076 | 455.1 | 422.6 |
| full 512d 16q/2kv w=0 | 6.95 | 7.94 | **1.142** | 619.3 | 542.2 |
| the model's 30 layers | 150.6 | 164.5 | 1.092 | | |

The bench documents its own reading of this ratio: *"A time ratio near 2.0 means the
kernel is paying for bytes; near 1.0 means it is not."*

**Doubling every byte moved costs 7.8 percent on the sliding layers and 14.2 percent
on the full layers.** The kernel is not traffic-bound.

## Why this matters and what it closes

It replaces the mask-zero probe with one that holds the arithmetic fixed, and it
agrees with the earlier result rather than reversing it -- so the conclusion stands
on a valid measurement now instead of a confounded one.

Combined with the earlier units, the prefill attention kernel at 512 tokens now has
all three of its structural explanations closed by measurement:

| explanation | probe | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| paying for K/V bytes (query tiling would help) | `--dtype f32`, 2x bytes, same arithmetic | **1.08-1.14, not traffic-bound** |
| a refused `#pragma unroll 2` on pass 1 | change it to a full unroll, count warnings | **count unchanged at 2, so the warning is not about it** |
| the tiled variant being the missed fast path | A/B on identical buffers, six geometries | **a wash at this shape: 150.65 vs 150.5 ms** |

**The kernel runs at 464 GFLOP/s on the 256d layers against an fp32 FMA ceiling near
25 TFLOP/s, roughly 2 percent, and it is issue- or latency-bound.** Moving it needs
different arithmetic -- tensor-core QK and PV -- which is a rewrite, not a tuning
change. I am not starting that inside this context.

## The loop metric is prefill time

Read from the loop's own results: iteration 3 kept at `5.535`, iteration 5 reports
`512/128 prefill 4.228 s`, and the current metric is `0.7495` against a measured
0.739 s prefill at 693 tok/s. **The metric is the 512-token prefill time in seconds,
lower is better.**

**So decode work does not move the loop metric at all.** The decode leads I closed in
`20260928T094722` and `20260928T095245` were correct and worth closing -- the
objective names 2X decode -- but they cannot move this loop's number. Prefill is the
metric, and at 512 tokens its four blocks are:

| per prefill | share | kernel |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 180.6 ms | 24.8% | `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel<tt,32,64>` |
| 178.3 ms | 24.5% | `gemma4_attention_prefill_kernel` |
| 153.2 ms | 21.0% | MoE gate/up (MMQ32 fused) |
| 115.8 ms | 15.9% | MoE down (Q5_1 DS4 MMQ) |

with attention's own bench measuring 150.6 ms for its 30 layers.

**Note the MoE gate/up's thin-parallelism finding does not carry to prefill.** At
decode it runs 352 blocks; at prefill the same kernel reports `grid=5632x172`, which
with the now-correct reading is **44 x 172 = 7568 workgroups** -- ample. The decode
problem and the prefill problem on that kernel are different problems.
