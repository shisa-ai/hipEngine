# The T16 consumer measurement protocol was unsound; corrected it stays slower on wide-N

## Summary

The previous entry measured the T16 decode consumer with a sequential A-then-B
protocol and reported four ratios. Re-running **unchanged** raw-kernel code under
that same protocol moved the raw owner from 711.0 to 417.2 GB/s on the same
shape. The sequential protocol was therefore not measuring the kernel, and its
four rows are withdrawn rather than restated. A paired protocol replaces it, and
under it the wide-header variant still loses on the wide-N shapes.

## Changes

- `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/quant/gguf_iq_dense.hip` — wide-header variant
  of `gguf_iq4_xs_t16_local32_gemv_kernel`: `d` and `scales_h` are read as four
  u32 each and `scales_l` as eight u32, extracted in registers, replacing three
  mixed-width loads per column. Adds `half_from_bits`, the same union conversion
  `half_load` performs, so a `d` read as half of a wide load converts identically.
- `benchmarks/results/2026-09-13-ud-gfx1100-iq4-xs-t16-local32-decode.json` —
  protocol replaced, paired cells written, the four sequential rows moved to a
  `superseded` block with the reason.

## Decision

The measurement protocol, not the kernel, was the first problem. The corrected
protocol interleaves the two owners inside one process: each round times both
back to back, 40 launches per round, 15 rounds after 8 warmup pairs, and the
reported ratio is the median of the per-round ratios. Interleaving is what makes
the ratio meaningful when the machine's absolute rate drifts by 40% between runs.

Under it, all five cells stay bit-exact and the T16 owner is slower everywhere
except the noisiest shape.

| K | N | raw ms | T16 ms | ratio median | min | max | bit-exact |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 17408 | 5120 | 0.110 | 0.138 | 0.803 | 0.646 | 0.962 | yes |
| 5120 | 17408 | 0.112 | 0.142 | 0.791 | 0.717 | 0.911 | yes |
| 5120 | 13824 | 0.099 | 0.128 | 0.773 | 0.663 | 0.883 | yes |
| 17408 | 2048 | 0.061 | 0.058 | 1.044 | 0.857 | 2.219 | yes |
| 5120 | 5120 | 0.026 | 0.041 | 0.633 | 0.483 | 1.844 | yes |

The wide-N ranges top out below 1.0 (0.962, 0.911, 0.883), so the ~0.77-0.80x
deficit is a real effect rather than drift. The narrow-N cell has a median above
1.0 but spans 0.857-2.219, which is too wide to call a win.

## Why the layout's byte saving does not pay

The T16 tile halves the payload bytes for a 256-element, 8-column block, from
2048 to 1024, and the wide-header variant removed a third of the header loads.
Neither helps the wide-N shapes, and the reason is that the raw owner's duplicate
reads were never costing DRAM traffic: a lane's 8-byte payload window is read by
two lanes at opposite nibbles, and the second read is an L1 hit. So both owners
issue the same number of load requests, the raw owner already runs at 467-711 GB/s
on those shapes, and the T16 path pays extra for eight live per-column scales.
Halving bytes that were already cached cannot win.

## Next

The T16 layout is now measured rather than assumed, and the measurement says its
byte saving is not the binding constraint on the shapes that dominate UD decode.
The remaining levers on the IQ4_XS owner are the ones the rocprofv3 breakdown
already pointed at, not this layout. If the layout is to be used at all, the
narrow-N regime is the only candidate and needs a lower-variance measurement
before it could be claimed.
