# the gemma 4 layer plan declares two parallel ffn branches as a flat serial tuple

## Summary

The decode cost is dominated by a serial chain of ~23 microsecond links: 448 zero-byte
elementwise launches costing **10.37 ms, 29.3% of the step**, where `20260928T073101` measured
that removing launches entirely has a **2.7 ms ceiling** -- the rest is per-link latency, so
"the real target is the serialization itself".

**Reading the layer plan found a declared structure that is a DAG written as a sequence.**

## The plan

`hipengine/models/gemma4.py::decode_layer_sequence` returns one flat `tuple[str, ...]` of 18
primitives per layer. **Its own docstring says:**

> Every Gemma 4 decoder layer runs both feed-forward branches in parallel and sums them after
> two separate post-norms, so ``dense_mlp`` and ``selected_expert_mlp`` are siblings rather
> than alternatives.

**The described structure is two parallel branches. The declared structure is a flat tuple.**
The feed-forward half:

```
rmsnorm, dense_mlp, gemma4_dense_branch_rmsnorm, gemma4_weightless_router_topk,
rmsnorm, selected_expert_mlp, gemma4_expert_branch_rmsnorm,
gemma4_parallel_ffn_combine, gemma4_layer_scalar, residual_add
```

**`gemma4_weightless_router_topk` depends on the `rmsnorm` before it, not on `dense_mlp`.** So
the router and expert branch is independent of the dense branch, and the critical path
through the feed-forward half is roughly **5 ops rather than 10**.

## Why it would matter if it is real

**Halving the links in the feed-forward half removes per-link latency, not just submission** --
which is the part the 2.7 ms ceiling does not cover. 18 ops per layer across 30 layers is
**540**, against the census's **762.7** launches: the same order, so the per-layer chain is
most of the launch population.

## What I verified and what I did not

**Verified:** the sequence is declared as a flat tuple; the docstring states the branches are
siblings; the router's position in the tuple puts it after the pre-branch `rmsnorm` and not
after `dense_mlp`.

**Not verified:** how the sequence is consumed at runtime. `layer_sequence` has **no consumer
anywhere in `hipengine/`** -- it is defined on models and composed within models, and the
executor is elsewhere. **So whether the runtime already overlaps the two branches, or is free
to, is unknown.**

**This is a candidate, not a defect.** The runtime may well already express the parallel
branches, in which case the flat tuple is a declaration the executor does not take literally
and there is nothing here. **The next check is the executor, and it decides the question in
one read.**
