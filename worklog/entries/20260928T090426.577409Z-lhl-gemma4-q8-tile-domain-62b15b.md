# the q8 tile is a two dimensional function of rows and out features and the existing heuristic is already near optimal

## Summary

`20260928T085917` closed by proposing a rule: eight shapes across two model
families separated cleanly at `out_features / rows = 5`, so a shape branch
looked justified and worth the gemma4 k/v projection's 7 percent.

**That hypothesis is refuted, and so are two mechanism models built to replace
it. The heuristic it would have replaced is the best of everything tested.**

The measurements, 33 cells at `rows` 64..512 and `out_features` 2048..8192:

| selector | total ms | overhead vs per-cell oracle | worst cell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **current heuristic** (`tile_n` 64 at rows>=64, `tile_m` 32) | **18.276** | **1.040x** | 1.448x |
| best fitted two-axis ladder | 18.119 | 1.031x | **1.529x** |
| `tile_n` from a `rows/4` ladder | 19.057 | 1.084x | 1.846x |
| minimum predicted traffic | 21.124 | 1.202x | 1.517x |
| maximum block count | 27.180 | 1.547x | 2.265x |
| per-cell oracle | 17.575 | 1.000x | 1.000x |

**No change is made.** The best fitted rule gains 0.86 percent on the average
by fitting 8 of 33 cells, regresses two of them, and has a *worse* worst case
than the rule it would replace. That is not a trade worth taking, and the
current rule's worst cell would remain.

## The ratio hypothesis, refuted

Holding `out_features` fixed and moving `rows` across the supposed boundary:

| `out_features` | `out/rows`=2 | 4 | 8 | 16 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2048 | (64,32) | (64,32) | (64,32) | tie |
| 4096 | (32,64) 1.38x | (32,64) 1.25x | (32,64) 1.18x | (64,32) 0.88x |
| 8192 | (32,64) 1.69x | (32,64) 1.59x | (32,64) 1.28x | -- |

At `out=2048` the narrower token tile wins at **every** `out/rows` from 2 to 8.
At `out=8192` the wider one wins at every `out/rows` from 4 to 16. **The
winner tracks `out_features`, not the ratio**, and the eight points that
separated at `out/rows = 5` did so because they varied `out_features` too.

This is the same failure as `ab8faa6f7` and `c2c1932d8`: a plausible aggregate
correlate taken for the cause. There, bandwidth; here, a ratio. **The check
that settles it is one experiment -- hold one variable and move the other --
and it cost about a minute of GPU time.** It should have come before the rule
was proposed.

## Why the mechanism models fail

The obvious model is a two-term traffic trade. Weight bytes are
`ceil(rows/tile_n) x out x K` and activation bytes are
`ceil(out/tile_m) x rows x K`; both candidate tiles have the same area, so the
total is nearly equal and only the mix moves. That predicts the ordering
correctly on one shape (`rows=512 out=2048`: predicted 144 < 210 < 236 < 288
against measured 0.426 < 0.522 < 0.937 < 1.053) and **fails on another**:
at `rows=64 out=2048` it prefers `(32,16)` at 36 units to `(16,16)` at 59,
while the measurement prefers `(16,16)` by 5 percent.

The missing term is that a block with no work still costs, and the machine
wants about 320 concurrent blocks (40 CUs x the 8 that
`__launch_bounds__(32, 8)` allows). At `rows=64 out=2048`, `(32,16)` gives
`(64, 4) = 256` blocks and `(16,16)` gives `(128, 4) = 512`. So the selector
has to satisfy a fill floor **and** minimize traffic, and neither term alone
reproduces the map -- `max blocks` alone is 1.547x off, `min traffic` alone
1.202x.

## A 215 result that did not reproduce

`20260928T085917` recorded the gemma4 k/v projection (2816x2112, rows=512) as
`(64,32)` 0.498 against `(32,64)` 0.534, and listed a shape branch as worth
7 percent there. Re-measured on the same shape and tile: `(32,64)` 0.495
against `(64,32)` 0.571. **The direction reverses and the gap widens.**

The difference is not noise alone: 2112 is not a multiple of 64, so the
`tile_m=64` grid has 33 blocks and a half-width tail, and the two runs used
different random activation data. **Either way the claim is withdrawn -- a
7 percent effect that reverses sign between runs is not a 7 percent effect.**
The cell is still in the map above with both numbers.
