# GGUF auto-context: lease, reserve, and growth-rate corrections

## Summary

Follow-up to `20260912T134710.640315Z-lhl-gguf-auto-context-sizing-d4b212.md`.
That entry shipped automatic resident context sizing and left three claims that
measurement has now corrected, plus one real defect. Two of the corrections are
about facts I asserted from indirect evidence rather than measurement, and the
defect was a genuine correctness bug on the exact hardware class the work was
for.

1. **The packed-verify workspace lease is real and eagerly allocated.** The
   earlier entry recorded it as "a reservation ... not physically backed at
   reservation time", inferred from `pool.current_bytes` (23.7 GB) exceeding
   `high_water_observed_bytes` (11.9 GB). Those are different quantities:
   `current_bytes` is capacity x page_bytes and `high_water_observed_bytes`
   counts pages that were actually leased or referenced. Whole-card measurement
   settles it: with the model loaded at the auto-selected context, GPU 0 used
   42.61 GiB against 40.25 GiB of hipEngine-tracked allocations. The no-lease
   probe peaks at 28.89 GiB, so the extra 11.12 GiB is the lease and it is real.
   Pricing it was correct.

2. **The transient coefficients are measured, not inherited.** The earlier entry
   called `HIPENGINE_GGUF_KV_TRANSIENT_KIB_PER_TOKEN` (74 KiB) and
   `HIPENGINE_GGUF_KV_TRANSIENT_FIXED_MIB` (1 GiB) "inherited values rather than
   measured ones". They come from a 2026-09-11 measurement recorded at
   `_GGUF_TRANSIENT_BYTES_PER_TOKEN_DEFAULT`: 1,073,741,824 B of live oracle
   owners (exactly 64 KiB/token) plus 293,400,616 B of hidden owners
   (~10 KiB/token), and a context-independent ~1.017 GiB packed execution
   workspace.

3. **The 512 MiB reserve did not cover device memory the model never prices.**
   This is the defect. Whole-card use minus hipEngine-tracked allocations is
   **2.66 GiB** — HIP context, JIT-compiled kernel modules, AOTriton, and the KV
   pool's pointer tables — and about 2.35 GiB of it is allocated *after* the
   free-memory reading the model prices against, so it cannot be inferred from
   that reading. The selection was safe only because the transient term is priced
   at a full-context worst case (4.22 GiB) against 0.30 GiB measured at a
   43,011-token prompt, and that ~3.9 GiB of slack absorbed the shortfall. Two
   errors cancelling is not a margin.

   The consequence is concrete: on a 24 GiB card the old arithmetic selected
   10,752 tokens whose true peak was 26.23 GiB against 23.98 GiB of VRAM — an
   OOM on the hardware class this work exists for. The 48 GiB validation run did
   not surface it because the slack was larger than the shortfall there.

## Changes

- `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py`:
  - `_GGUF_AUTO_CONTEXT_RESERVE_MIB_DEFAULT` 512 -> **3072**, with the whole-card
    measurement recorded in the source comment.
  - `Qwen35GGUFResidentModelRunner.prepare` now retries on an allocation failure.
    The earlier fallback lived only in `_construct_shared_session`, which cannot
    see the device KV pool — and the pool is the dominant allocation
    (`create_global_device_kv_pool` -> `_allocate_qwen35_gguf_kv_chunk` allocates
    `pages x page_bytes` eagerly, both planes). The retry now wraps session
    reservation and pool creation together, where both are in scope.
  - The pinned-context contract is now the same on both paths: a context that
    cannot be allocated backs off, and the warning names the size that was asked
    for (`GGUF context request: requested N tokens failed to allocate ... retrying
    at M tokens`). Previously the session constructor backed off a pinned context
    while `prepare` refused to, which is an inconsistency with no justification.
  - `HIPENGINE_GGUF_AUTO_CONTEXT=0` is now a complete rollback: it disables the
    automatic sizing *and* the backoff, restoring the historical fixed 256-token
    context and the historical hard failure.
- `scripts/gguf_capacity_probe.py`: reports `resident_gib`,
  `resident_plus_transient_peak_gib`, and `transient_peak_gib`. The probe resets
  the high-water mark after the session opens (which preserves live allocations),
  so the model-load peak no longer masks the transient peak at small contexts.
  `tracked_peak_gib` keeps its old process-wide meaning.
- `tests/test_unit_qwen35_gguf_capacity.py`: 30 tests. Adds the pool-level retry,
  the pinned-context backoff with its warning, the flag disabling both paths, the
  contextless propagation case, and a guard that fails if the reserve is lowered
  back below the measured untracked overhead.

## Validation

**Growth rate, measured against prediction.** Two Tier-1 probes, 27B `Q4_K_M`,
BF16 KV, 4 slots, 128-token prompt, identical settings apart from the declared
context. `resident_gib` is weights + KV + scratch.

| Declared context | `resident_gib` | `transient_peak_gib` |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16,384 | 21.672599 | 0.023621 |
| 45,568 | 28.825100 | 0.065695 |

Measured resident slope **263,156 B/token** against a predicted **262,979**
(KV 262,144 + scratch 835) — **+0.067%**. Backing the weights out of the 16,384
point gives 17.054 GiB. The two runs reproduce to six decimals on the shared
field: `resident_plus_transient_peak_gib` at 45,568 is 28.890794 here and
`tracked_peak_gib` was 28.890794 in the earlier run.

The transient is prompt-scaled, not context-scaled, so short-prompt probes cannot
calibrate it: 0.0657 GiB measured against 4.2158 GiB priced at the same context.

**Full-depth request at the auto-selected context.** 27B `Q4_K_M`, no sizing
flags, W7900 GPU 0, one 43,011-token prompt at the then-selected 45,568 tokens:
completed, `prefill_ms` 100,341, 169 chunks, `finish_reason=length`. Whole-card
use 42.91 GiB of 45.00, i.e. 2.09 GiB of headroom — which is how the reserve
shortfall was found.

**After the reserve fix.** Same model, no sizing flags, clean device:

| Run | Selected | `reserve` | Card use after a deep prompt | Headroom |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pre-fix | 45,568 | 0.50 GiB | 42.91 GiB | 2.09 GiB |
| Post-fix | 40,960 | 3.00 GiB | 40.65 GiB (38,495-token prompt) | **4.35 GiB** |

The context cost of a real margin is 10%. The post-fix run also verified the
backoff contract end to end: `HIPENGINE_GGUF_KV_CAPACITY_RESERVE_MIB=42003`
emulates a 24 GB card's post-weight budget on the 48 GiB card and the server
selected, allocated, and generated at the reduced context.

**Post-fix selections, measured.** All three runs are `--no-eager-load`, one
short chat request, no sizing flags, W7900 GPU 0.

| Model | Budget | Selected | `usable` | `reserve` |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Qwen3.8-27B `Q4_K_M` | 48 GiB card | 40,960 | 24.62 GiB | 3.00 GiB |
| Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `UD-Q4_K_M` | 48 GiB card | 87,040 | 20.77 GiB | 3.00 GiB |
| Qwen3.8-27B `Q4_K_M` | 24 GB-class | 3,328 | 3.60 GiB | 24.02 GiB |

The 24 GB-class row reproduces a 24 GB card's post-weight budget (6.60 GiB free
minus the 3.00 GiB reserve = 3.60 GiB usable) on the 48 GiB card via
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_KV_CAPACITY_RESERVE_MIB=24596`. An earlier attempt at 42,003 MiB
was miscalibrated — it used pre-load free memory instead of post-weight free
memory — and correctly produced
`MemoryError: automatic GGUF resident context sizing found no allocatable context tokens`
rather than allocating something that would not fit.

**The 24 GB answer is not the one the first entry reported.** 11,520 tokens
became 3,328 once the reserve was honest. With 4 resident slots a 27B `Q4_K_M`
on a 24 GB card is weight-bound: 17.05 GiB of weights plus 3.00 GiB of reserve
plus ~1.6 GiB of fixed scratch and workspace consume 21.65 GiB of 23.98 GiB
before any KV exists. The slot count is the lever — the same budget at one slot
carries roughly three times the context, because each slot reserves a
full-context KV plane. That figure is derived from the validated model, not
measured.

## Next

- The transient term is the largest remaining conservatism: 4.22 GiB priced
  against 0.30 GiB measured at a 43,011-token prompt. Tightening it would buy
  back most of the 10% context the reserve fix cost, but it must be done together
  with the reserve, because the two currently offset each other. A ladder of
  full-depth prompts (not the 128-token probes, which cannot see it) at several
  contexts is the measurement that would settle it.
- The 4-slot reservation costs about 3x the context a single slot allows. On a
  24 GB card that is the difference between ~7,000 and ~21,000 tokens. Worth
  deciding whether the default resident capacity should follow `--max-active-requests`
  more aggressively, or whether the server should report the trade at startup
  more prominently than the current warning line.
- The reserve is a flat constant measured on one host. JIT kernel module size
  will differ across models and routes; a per-host measurement at startup would
  be more honest than a constant, if it can be done without a second process.
