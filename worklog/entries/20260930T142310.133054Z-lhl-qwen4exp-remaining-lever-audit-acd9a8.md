# The two largest remaining-looking levers are not what they look like: Q8_IU8_WMM is closed and GD-4 is a multi-session build

## Summary

I went to start GD-4, the largest remaining implementable item, and stopped one
step in: **its own build plan says it is a multi-session build and warns against
starting it under a "port it" estimate.** Checking the alternative large lever
found the same shape of problem from the other direction — the biggest measured
number in the campaign's history is already closed. Both corrections are
documentation of committed evidence, and both exist to stop a future session
from spending its budget on a lever that is not there.

## GD-4: the row read as "port it", the build plan says five components

`GD-4`'s State cell ended with *"Remaining work is the kernel build, not more
numerics"* — true, and misleading, because the build plan that produced that
sentence prices the build at **five components and explicitly says it is "not a
port of `tiled16`"**:

1. an **fp16 WMMA GEMM primitive in `linear_attn/`**, which has **no precedent in
   the directory** — WMMA appears only inline inside `process_attention_tile` in
   `attention/laguna_flash_attention_prefill.hip`, and that use is
   attention-shaped rather than a callable GEMM helper;
2. the chunk-local scan (cooperative L in LDS, chunk 32, direct suffix products);
3. the chunk-axis state-propagation pass — the second kernel;
4. variant registration with `tiled16` as the strict fallback (this pattern
   exists);
5. **a second prefill test contract**, which is governed rather than local:
   `tests/test_gpu_qwen4_exp_gdn_hip.py::test_qwen4_exp_gdn_prefill_is_exact_to_serial_decode`
   holds every existing prefill route **exact to serial decode**, and a chunked
   scan **cannot** satisfy that by construction, because reassociating the
   recurrence's floating-point sums is the entire point.

The plan's verdict is *"a multi-session build, not a single iteration"*, and it
surfaced two constraints the row does not state, one of which silently destroys
the advantage:

- **L and QK are value-column independent**, so computing them per-warp instead
  of cooperatively costs **16x** — it converts the priced **1.05 TFLOP/s
  (2%-of-peak) break-even** into a **~15 TFLOP/s (25%-of-peak)** bar.
- **The `tiled16` grid is `(num_v_heads=48, col_blocks=8)`.** Collapsing to one
  block per v-head to remove the L redundancy leaves **48 blocks against 96 CUs —
  half the device idle**, which is why filling the device needs the second
  kernel.

The row now carries all of this, plus the governing operating point (**chunk
32**, not the feasibility artifact's chunk 128, which is 3-5x over the 64 KB
per-workgroup LDS limit under every staging choice — and chunk 32 has the
*lowest* break-even in that artifact's own table anyway).

**I did not start the build.** Its prerequisites are complete and its numerics
are locked, but a half-built two-kernel design with a new test contract is not a
committable unit, and the plan says so in its own words.

## `Q8_IU8_WMM`: the largest number in the campaign is already closed

The 2026-09-16 disabled-family sweep is the artifact that gets cited as an
opportunity list, and its largest entry — `Q8_IU8_WMM` at **1.388x**, +6.614 s of
a 23.648 s prefill — was recorded there as *"no current rejection recorded at
this scope; largest measured opportunity in this sweep"* and *"the natural first
target for a corrected arithmetic"*.

**That disposition is stale, and the family is closed.** The ledger records the
role-isolated evidence the rejection cell asked for: **14 of 17 roles claim the
route and all 14 fail the canonical envelope alone, zero pass.** The 1.388x is
real as a rate and unavailable as a route.

## The structural correction: that sweep measures the recovery profile's deficit

The more useful finding is about what the sweep is a measurement *of*. **Every
family in its top six is bound to `"1" if production` in the profile binder**
(`hipengine/generation/qwen4_exp_profiles.py`):

| family | binder line | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Q8_MMQ_PREFILL` | 711 | `"1" if production else "0"` |
| `GR_IU8` | 743 | `"1" if production else "0"` |
| `GR_IU8_DOWN` | 748 | `"1" if production else "0"` |
| `GDN_PEER_PREFILL` | 774 | `"1" if production else "0"` |
| `GDN_COLWARPS_PREFILL` | 817 | `"1" if production else "0"` |
| `Q4_DP4A64` | 780 | `"1" if production else "0"` |

All six are also members of `PRODUCTION_ARITHMETIC_RECOVERY_FLAGS` — the fifteen
flags the recovery pass zeroes for `gguf_ud_q4_k_xl`. So the sweep's fallback is
the **recovery profile**, and its `+1.493x` combined figure is **what the
recovery profile gives back**, not headroom sitting on the table. It is a
re-measurement of the recovery cost already recorded as
`-40.94/-41.89/-54.53%` prefill and `-12.60/-12.43/-36.88%` decode.

I did **not** claim an exact numeric reconciliation: the sweep's fallback is
173.2 tok/s at `code-p4096` (4096 / 23.648 s) against the 2026-09-14 artifact's
recovered 129.950 tok/s, which differ by a third because the two use different
shape weighting, chunking and dates. The **flag-level** fact is exact and is what
a reader needs; the rates are not comparable and the artifact now says so.

Two further rows of that table were stale: `GR_IU8` and `GR_IU8_DOWN` were
rejected at that scope and the binder now binds both ON — a rejection at one
scope does not survive a re-qualification at another, and the table did not say
so.
