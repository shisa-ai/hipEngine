# the MTP2 ceiling is four by the clamp itself, not via resident-capacity

## Summary

The previous entry derived the four-request ceiling from the benchmark asking for eight
resident slots. That derivation is wrong. The flag feeds a different field. The ceiling
still holds, by a stronger and simpler route: the clamp caps at four no matter what
capacity is, and the resident runner's own default capacity is also four.

## Changes

- None. Read-only tracing. Replaces the reasoning in the previous entry; the
  conclusion about the ceiling is unchanged and better supported.

## Validation

What the flag actually does:

- `scripts/gguf_mtp_c1c8_server_bench.py:697,724` passes the parsed value as
  `max_active_requests=resident_capacity`. It is not passed as capacity.
- `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py:835` shows the related engine field is
  `server_plain_ar_max_active_requests`, a plain-AR limit. Different subsystem.

What the adapter actually reads:

- `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py:6101` passes `self` as the adapter owner, where
  `self` is `Qwen35GGUFResidentModelRunner` (class at :5009).
- That runner stores `self.capacity` at :5036 from a `capacity` argument that defaults
  to `_GGUF_RESIDENT_MODEL_LOOP_DEFAULT_CAPACITY = 4` (:136), and
  `create_resident_model_runner` at :1322-1325 resolves a None capacity back to that
  same 4.
- The adapter reads `getattr(self.owner, "capacity", ...)` at :463, :523, :586 and :732,
  and at :1029 turns it into max_requests via min(4, max(1, capacity)).

So max_requests is at most four under either reading: capped by the clamp if capacity is
large, or already four if capacity comes from the default. The earlier claim that eight
slots were being throttled down to four is not supported; the honest statement is that
the ceiling is four and the serving layer may admit eight.

Not established: whether any caller passes an explicit capacity above four. The direct
call site of create_resident_model_runner was not located within my budget, so the
value in the retained run is still unconfirmed. That does not change the ceiling, since
the clamp caps at four regardless.
