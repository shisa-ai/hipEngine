# Reconcile the staged exchange's timing on matching samples and correct its claims

## Summary

Acted on the five findings in the staged-exchange review
(`worklog/entries/20260914T192208.687013Z-tp-review-staged-exchange-review-76e836.md`)
without changing the exchange's implementation. The batched orchestration keeps
its measured 70.3 -> 40.9 us per-reduction result. What changes is what the
numbers are allowed to claim: the model figures are now labelled conditional
projections, the transport floor and the native ceiling are withdrawn, the
timing is reconciled on one population instead of across two, and the ownership
tests model asynchronous execution instead of assuming it.

## Changes

### Claims corrected (review P1)

`benchmarks/README.md`, `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md` and
`docs/QWEN38-27B-GFX1100-TP2.md` no longer call the projection a qualified win.
They state that the break-even artifact records `certified: false` with no
shard-kernel evidence, and that no local shard kernel, state trajectory,
production numerical gate or end-to-end TP latency has been measured. The 30.8 us
transport floor, the "a device signal can only remove 9.4 us" inference, the
`42.9 < 30.8 + 26.4` overlap argument and the "1.3x is unreachable through
transport work" conclusion are all withdrawn as unestablished. So is the claimed
native ceiling of 10.2 us submission plus 6.5 us accumulation. The MLP-only
sharding item now states that it restores the full attention and GDN work on each
rank relative to full TP, that the slower rank can set that segment's completion
time, and that single-owner attention needs a result broadcast rather than being
free.

### Timing reconciled on matching samples (review P1)

- Performance arms are uninstrumented: `staged_exchange_host_sync`,
  `staged_exchange_batched_return_wait` and `staged_exchange_batched` run with no
  `perf_counter` in their hot path. One new `staged_exchange_instrumented` arm
  keeps the counters, so attribution and performance are separate measurements.
- The counters now wrap the whole per-rank block, so `d2h_scope_us` and
  `h2d_scope_us` name device-context entry and exit. That term is **15.2 us per
  reduction**, larger than either copy submission, and the earlier counters
  omitted it entirely - which is why no native ceiling could be derived from
  them.
- `loop_us` times the same steps the phases cover, so the residual is a
  reconciliation: **41.9 us accounted against 44.3 us of loop wall time, leaving
  2.3 us** of helper, pointer-preparation and loop overhead. The instrumented
  arm's own ladder slope (43.1 us) agrees with its loop wall time within 1.2 us.
- A third arm supplies the causal split the review asked for. Rank batching is
  **17.7 us** (70.3 -> 52.6) and dropping the return wait is **11.6 us**
  (52.6 -> 40.9); they sum to the 29.3 us host-orchestration term.
- The idle single-copy probe is joined by a prequeued probe: 16 copies submitted
  back to back between one event pair, divided by the count, which removes the
  host from the interval. Per rank and direction, single-copy observes
  13.6-15.1 us and prequeued 8.2-9.9 us. Both are labelled probe observations
  rather than a DMA critical path, and rank 1 is consistently slower, which is
  why samples are no longer pooled.

### Asynchronous ownership tests (review P2)

`_ShadowChainRuntime` now queues every copy and executes it only when its stream
is synchronized. Nothing moves at submission time, so a host-to-device read that
is still queued observes the slot's contents at drain time - a premature host
write into a slot whose read is outstanding corrupts the device value instead of
passing silently. Per-stream queues also model rank skew for free, and the shadow
collective drains its ranks' streams before reading, because a real collective
runs after everything already enqueued on them. The copy stub is width-aware
(it previously wrote and read only element zero), which is what makes vector
ownership testable at all.

New tests: the oracle's discriminating power (a queued read consumes a
post-submission host write), per-stream skew (one rank's queue survives the other
rank's sync), odd and even depths with 5x slot reuse and full drain, mid-chain
failure releasing every host registration and stream, and the full-vector check.
`scripts/tp_collective_bench.py` gained that check: the batched protocol at the
maximum timed depth with a distinct seed per rank and a bounded recurrence (the
host writes `sum / world + 1`, so the expected value is
`mean_r(seed_r[j]) + depth`, exact and informative at any depth, unlike
`seed * world ** depth` which saturates fp32). On hardware: **5120 elements,
exact, both ranks agree, rank seeds differ, at depth 128**.

  --devices 0,1 --ops all_reduce --rows 1 --dtypes fp32 --iterations 30
