# the MMQ hooks reconcile with the selected route to 0.25 percent and the census averages prefill rows

## Summary

The census now accounts for the MMQ expert route. `spy_mmq` sums bytes and
`gemma4_project_experts_down_mmq` is hooked for the first time, so the two
families that carry this artifact's expert traffic are both visible:

```
dense gguf_q8_0                218.67   1866.7 MB
expert-mmq gate_up gguf_q4_k    30.93    795.4 MB
expert-mmq down   gguf_q5_1     30.93    529.4 MB
expert-mmq gate_up gguf_q5_k     1.07     33.5 MB
expert-mmq down   gguf_q8_0      1.07     25.9 MB
TOTAL                                   3255.7 MB/step
launches per step: 762.7
```

**The new formula is validated by an independent one.** Before the route changed,
the same artifact's expert traffic was measured through the *selected* entry
point and `spy_selected` reported **793.4 MB** of q4_k gate/up and **528.9 MB** of
q5_1 down. The MMQ hooks now report **795.4** and **529.4** -- agreement to
**0.25%** between two different code paths, two different spies and two different
byte formulas. That is a cross-check, not a self-consistency check, and it is
what makes both figures trustworthy.

**My "1.56x inflation" claim in `1fe45f53c` is wrong, and the floor is right.**
That entry compared the census's 1348.1 MB of expert traffic against a computed
862.4 MB floor and called the census inflated. The census is not inflated; it is
**averaging prefill rows into a per-decode-step figure**. The expert byte formula
scales with `compact_rows`, and the run is `--prompt 8 --output 16`, so the
average over 15 forwards mixes one 8-token prefill forward with 14 single-token
decode forwards:

```
(64 prefill compact rows + 14 x 8 decode rows) / 15 = 11.73 compact rows
535.3 MB x 11.73 / 8                                = 784.9 MB  (reported 795.4)
```

The 1.49x gate/up and 1.62x down ratios are that effect, and the two differ
because the prefill's expert selection spreads differently from decode's. The
862.4 MB floor is the **pure decode** value and remains the correct denominator
for a bandwidth comparison; 3255.7 MB is not a decode-step total.

## Changes

- `scripts/gemma4_decode_traffic_census.py`: `spy_mmq` now sums weight, activation
  and output bytes instead of only counting calls, and `spy_mmq_down` hooks
  `gemma4_project_experts_down_mmq`, which no spy covered. Both read their
  dimensions from their real signatures -- `(weight, x_ptr, out_ptr,
  expert_start, compact_rows, num_experts, in_features, intermediate)` for gate/up
  and `... out_features` for down -- so `compact_rows, in_features, <width>` are
  `a[3], a[5], a[6]`. The stale comment above `spy_selected`, which documented an
  argument order with `x` first and `qweight` fourth, is corrected; it is what
  caused me to misread that spy as broken in `20260928T064512`.

env -u HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES ROCR_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 HIPENGINE_HIP_ARCH=gfx1151 \
  PYTHONPATH=. python3 scripts/gemma4_decode_traffic_census.py --prompt 8 --output 16

Exit 0 against the real 17 GB `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-UD-Q4_K_XL.gguf`, resolving to
`hip_gfx1151` / `gguf_q4_k_m`. The oracle for the new formula is the pre-route
measurement: `spy_selected`'s 793.4 MB and 528.9 MB from `20260927T225558`,
computed by a different byte formula against a different entry point, which the
MMQ hooks reproduce at 795.4 and 529.4 MB. Two independent formulas agreeing on
the same traffic is the check; the floor comparison alone would not have been,
since it was the floor that was being misapplied.

Launch count is 762.7 per step, up from 730.7 in the un-hooked re-run. That rise
is the two new spies counting launches the earlier run could not see, not a
regression.
