# Correct the fixed-share claim and make the native A/B matched and verifiable

## Summary

Matched, balanced, in one session, on W7900 + XTX (both gfx1100, visible as
`['AMD Radeon Pro W7900', 'AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX']`):

| Arm | Ladder slope, depths 1-128 | Total at depth 128 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Python host loop | 40.00 us/reduction | 5185.5 us |
| Native C++ loop | **20.45 us/reduction** | **2623.1 us** |

**1.956x**, down from the artifact-imported 1.97x - the in-session rerun is what
settled it, not the artifact. Native phases at depth 128: submit D2H 1.51 us,
wait 14.90 us, host reduce 1.97 us, submit H2D 1.82 us.

Projection with the matched figure (`benchmarks/results/tp2_break_even_native_staged_exchange.json`):
1.328x / 1.240x / 1.163x / 1.095x at 0/10/20/30% fixed share. Still
`certified: false`, and the 1.3x target remains an aspiration.

## Changes

Review of the native staged-exchange result and the TP1 segment attribution
returned three findings. All three were valid, and each one changed a claim
rather than only a caveat.

### The TP1 attribution does not establish a fixed-cost share (withdrawn)

`benchmarks/results/2026-09-14-w7900-xtx-tp1-segment-attribution.json` previously
said attention + GDN + sampler + copy at 1.578 ms/step (W7900) and 1.441 ms/step
(XTX) meant the fixed share was "small" and the projection's 20-30% rows were
unsupported.

That inference does not hold. The harness's own contract
(`scripts/gguf_c1_decode_attribution.py:1209`) states that per-family shares are
shares of *kernel time*, not of the device union, because dispatch intervals on
this route overlap; the artifact records a `dispatch_overlap_ratio` of 1.916.
Summed family durations therefore exceed the measured window (about 30 ms of
family totals plus 19.2 ms of interior gap inside a 34.9 ms window), and the
quantities are not additive. Dividing them by a *different protocol's* token time
cannot produce a critical-path share. The projection's fixed term also stands for
launch and scheduling overhead, which the family table does not measure at all.

The artifact's `findings` were rewritten, `does_not_establish` and `limitations`
were added, and the README, `benchmarks/CHANGELOG.md` and
`docs/QWEN38-27B-GFX1100-TP2.md` now present the trace as a **device-time profile
that leaves the 0-30% sensitivity range unchanged**. What survives is the profile
itself (weight reads 25.95/21.77 ms per step, the 1.19x kernel ratio matching the
1.20x token-rate ratio), the ~811 launches per step, and the interior-gap split
recorded as a candidate missing term.

### Native verification was narrower than reported (fixed)

The runner verified a separate `step_bounded()` implementation, read back rank 0
only, and accumulated error with `std::max`, which cannot see NaN because every
comparison against NaN is false. The timed `step()` output was never checked.

Now: `step()` takes the recurrence as a parameter, so the timed and verified
passes share one implementation; both ranks are read back over the whole
5120-element vector; a nonfinite observed value is counted and fails the run
explicitly; and the runner exits non-zero when either check fails. The timed
recurrence is the one the Python arm measures, and its closed form
`seed * 2 ** depth` is checked by exact-representability round trip rather than a
2**24 integer bound, so it verifies exactly at depths 1-64 and is reported as
saturating fp32 at 128 instead of being passed.

Evidence that the gate is real: with the rank-1 addend removed from the reduction
the runner exits 1 and reports `exact: false` on both recurrences, with a
`max_abs_error` of 65535 for the sum recurrence - the signature of a chain that
stayed at its seed instead of compounding.

### The A/B imported its Python arm from an artifact (fixed)

`scripts/tp_staged_exchange_native_ab.py` now reruns **both** arms in one session,
each over the whole depth ladder, with the order alternating per repetition. The
comparison is reported as `matched` only when both arms agree on count, payload
bytes, depth ladder, protocol, physical devices, the Python arm's dependency
verdict and the native arm's verification, and when every depth has exactly one
sample per arm. Otherwise the report is labelled `provisional` with the failing
conditions named, and `scripts/tp_break_even.py` carries that flag into the
collective source record.

Two bugs surfaced while making the run matched, both worth recording:

* The native runner failed at depths 32 and 64 with exit 1 because the sum
  recurrence's informativeness was tested against a 2**24 integer bound. Powers of
  two are exactly representable in fp32 well past that, so a chain that had
  compounded correctly was being reported as unverified. Fixed by the
  round-trip exactness test above.
* The driver called the Python arm once per depth, which made the ladder-level
  dependency verdict report against a single point. Depth 1 can never demonstrate
  compounding (its value *is* the single-step value), so the per-depth form
  produced false failures. The arm now runs the ladder in one call.

## Next

Stop settling TP2 from a global fixed-share model. Build the shard-shaped
execution slice: rank-local materialization of one representative MLP from the
byte-preserving shard planner, complete TP1 and local-shard MLP segment walls on
both cards, the shard outputs connected through the native exchange into the next
consumer, validated against the CPU split oracle under the production numerical
contract, and repeated across the distinct MLP geometries and quant combinations
before MLP-only versus full TP is selected. The Q4_K gate/up + Q6_K down geometry
at hidden 5120 / intermediate 17408 makes the MLP shard's reduction payload
exactly the 20,480 bytes the transport screen already measured.
