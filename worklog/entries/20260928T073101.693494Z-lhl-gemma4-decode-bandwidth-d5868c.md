# one elementwise launch costs 6 to 8 microseconds so fusing 448 of them saves at most 2.7 ms of the 10.37

## Summary

`20260928T072951` and `20260928T073020` both left the same open question: the 448
zero-byte launches cost 23.1 us each, and whether that is launch overhead or real work
decides between fusing launches and making the kernels faster. **It is mostly not
overhead.** Fusion is the wrong fix.

## The measurement

New probe `scripts/gemma4_launch_cost_probe.py` launches one production elementwise
kernel -- `gemma4_rmsnorm_f32w_bf16`, the family that accounts for 160 of the 448 -- at
the production decode shape (rows=1, hidden=2816), in a tight loop:

```
shape rows=1 hidden=2816   iters=2000
  no sync in loop          8.24 us/launch
  sync every launch       19.00 us/launch
  sync every 32            5.95 us/launch

  census's elementwise average:    23.10 us/launch
```

## What the three numbers mean

- **~6-8 us is pure submission.** `sync every 32` at 5.95 us is the cleanest figure: the
  queue is allowed to fill, so submission is fully pipelined. (`no sync` is *slower* at
  8.24 us because an unbounded queue eventually blocks submission.)
- **A synchronize round trip adds about 11 us.** `sync every launch` at 19.00 us is
  submission plus the full latency of executing the kernel and waiting for it.
- **The census's 23.1 us sits near the sync-every-launch figure, not the pipelined one.**
  That is the expected shape: in the real step each launch depends on the previous one's
  output, so it cannot be pipelined freely the way the probe's independent launches can.

## The conclusion, and it is a negative one for fusion

**448 launches at ~6 us of unavoidable submission is about 2.7 ms.** That is the ceiling
on what removing launches can recover, against the 10.37 ms the elementwise arms cost.

**The other ~7.7 ms is execution and dependency latency** -- small kernels waiting on each
other, not queue overhead. So:

- **Fusing launches is worth at most 2.7 ms of the 10.37**, and only if fusion is free,
  which it is not.
- **The real target is the serialization itself**: 448 kernels that each depend on the
  last one, at roughly 23 us per link in the chain.

The fix that follows from this is overlap or a shorter dependency chain -- doing more work
per kernel so the chain has fewer links -- rather than merely fewer launches.

## Caveat

The probe measures one kernel in isolation with no dependencies. The real step's launches
are dependency-ordered, which is why the real average (23.1 us) exceeds the pipelined
submission cost. The probe does not reproduce the dependency structure, so it bounds the
overhead component rather than measuring the chain.
