# gfx1151 35B-A3B: gufo-method reconciliation, host power validation, AR stage attribution

## Summary

Reconciles our published gfx1151 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B rows against the gufo
`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Q6dense` scoreboard, validates the host power state that gates
every measurement on this machine, and replaces the earlier kernel-share reading
with a stage-level byte budget on the shipping AR route.

**The published gufo comparison is not a like-for-like.** Their depth `d` is a
*cached conversation prefix* (a ~d-token user turn plus an 8-token reply) and the
timed request is a new ~2048-token turn plus 128 output tokens; the prefix is
never timed on either side, so the cache changes what is *paid for*, not what is
*measured*. Their `pp` column is new-tokens/s at depth `d`; ours is the average
over a 0->D ramp. Correcting our average to their marginal basis moves our 4K
number the wrong way: the ladder fit gives ~1380 tok/s marginal at depth 4096
against our published 1430 average, widening the prefill gap from 2.16x to
~2.24x. Prefix caching is a TTFT lever (F5: 249.269 -> 21.188 ms, 11.765x), not
a pp-rate lever.

**The host was contaminating every gfx1151 number.** Activating the
`accelerator-performance` profile removes the DE-5 post-prefill transient
entirely (first-30/last-30 decode step ratio 0.996x today against 3.3x in DE-5),
and lifts the published rows 1.5-2.0% (prefill) and 2.7-4.5% (decode). A
methodology correction falls out of the same probe: 2000 MHz is this APU's
*idle parking* frequency, not a load clamp, so DE-5's "all cores pinned at base"
reading needs the max-core statistic instead.

**Decode is kernel-bound, not launch-bound.** Graph replay is neutral
(56.312 vs 56.293 tok/s), GPU busy is 99% on the shipping route, and a stage
recorder that costs 6.8% on that route prices the whole step. Our 2.446 GiB/token
at 56.29 tok/s is 147.8 GB/s; gufo's 2.126 GiB/token at 68.43 tok/s is
156.2 GB/s. The 1.22x raw decode gap is ~1.15x file and ~1.06x achieved
bandwidth, and we are ahead of llama.cpp on gufo's own file (127.1 GB/s).

**Prefill is the real deficit and it is a compute-efficiency deficit.** The
shipping-route trace is 98.5% kernel time, so there is no host gap to close, but
the kernels run at 8.68 TFLOP/s = 14.6% of the 59.4 TFLOP/s FP16 peak. A
controlled chunk sweep on the shipping route finds the shipping 512-row
linear/MoE chunk is the *worst* of three: 1024 is **+6.40%** and 2048 is +2.91%
at 4096 tokens.

**A dispatch defect was found and confirmed.** `GGUF_PREFILL_CHUNK_SIZES_BY_GEOMETRY`
rewrites linear/MoE chunks in the session constructor, after the resolver and
after any explicit `PrefillConfig`, so it silently overrides
`--prefill-linear-chunk-size` / `--prefill-moe-chunk-size`, and it makes
`prefill_chunk_tuning.chunk_sizes` report 1024/1024 while the session runs
512/512. The gfx1151 arch profile (256/256) never applies on the GGUF path
because the runner does not pass `target_arch` (`target_arch: null` in the
recorded tuning).

### Prefill attribution and chunk sweep (task 4)

Shipping-route `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` over two 4096-token prefills: kernel
sum 5528 ms for 8192 tokens = 0.675 ms/token against a 0.685 ms/token
unprofiled wall, i.e. **98.5% kernel time**. Per token: MoE expert WMMA 0.256 ms
(38%), dense Q8_0 WMMA 0.171 (25%), GDN 0.135 (20%), router 0.031, AOTriton
0.030.

Compute basis from the GGUF shapes (top-8/256): 2997.4 M MACs/token =
5.995 GFLOP/token (dense linear attention 33.6%, experts 34.4%, lm_head 17.0%,
dense full attention 10.0%, shared expert 4.3%, router 0.7%). At 1446.87 tok/s
that is **8.68 TFLOP/s = 14.6% of the 59.4 TFLOP/s FP16 peak** (40 CU x 512
FLOP/cycle x 2.9 GHz). Memory floor at chunk 512 is 0.187 ms/token and the
compute floor is 0.101 ms/token, so the step is 3.7x above memory and 6.8x above
compute.

Chunk sweep, shipping route, 4096 tokens, 3 reps each, 3 s settle before each
timed prefill, one session per size with `reset()` between reps:

| linear/MoE chunk | median ms | tok/s | vs 512 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 512 (shipping) | 2984 | 1372.6 | - |
| **1024** | **2805** | **1460.5** | **+6.40%** |
| 2048 | 2900 | 1412.6 | +2.91% |

~88% of core-samples are at 2000 MHz inside every prefill window because the
prefill is GPU-bound and the idle cores park; the max core holds ~5.11 GHz, so no
arm is clock-clamped and the ordering is comparable.

Dispatch defect, confirmed by direct probe: with the override live,
`PrefillConfig(linear_chunk_size=1024, moe_chunk_size=1024)` yields effective
`(512, 512)`, and the recorded `prefill_chunk_tuning` says
`chunk_sizes: {linear: 1024, moe: 1024}` while the effective
`prefill_chunk_sizes` is `{linear: 512, moe: 512}`. The gfx1151 arch profile
(`_ARCH_CHUNK_PROFILES["gfx1151"] = {256, 256}`) is unreachable on the GGUF path
because `resolve_prefill_config_for_sequence` is called without `target_arch`.

## Next

- Re-run every published gfx1151 row under the `accelerator-performance` profile
  before using it as a baseline; the published rows are 1.5-4.5% low.
- Chunk 1024 (+6.40%) is the immediate prefill win; it needs the dispatch defect
  fixed first so the value is reachable through the public flags, then the
  applicable correctness gate and a rollup update.
- Ranked port list, from the gufo source read plus in-tree lineage (see the
  companion task): (1) 1024-row linear/MoE chunk as the shipping default with the
  override defect removed; (2) the R12 dense-Q8 MMQ campaign's tiling/occupancy
  work, because the R12 design basis already measures the same 7-11 int8 TF/s
  ceiling on this GPU that this entry measures at 14.6% of FP16 peak; (3) fusion
  of the compute-only decode stages (2.48 ms/step) and the small MoE
  shared-expert/router GEMVs (0.96 ms/step at 41-50% of ceiling); (4) an int8
  decode KV copy for the 64K/128K retention their protocol shows; (5) the exact
  MTP story, since our exact path is 0.9571x AR on gfx1151 against their 1.44x.
- Limits: one host, one model, one quant, one profile, one prompt family. The
  237 GB/s ceiling is inferred from our own lm_head stage, not from an isolated
  bandwidth microbenchmark.
