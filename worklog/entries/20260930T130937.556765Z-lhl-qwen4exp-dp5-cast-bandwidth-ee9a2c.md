# DP-5's cast bandwidth measured: 192 GB/s for the HC pair, and the whole-row estimate refuted at 103.6% of roof

## Summary

DP-5's cast bandwidth is now measured, and the measurement refutes the whole-row recovery
estimate while confirming the row's target. The HC down pair runs at **192.1 GB/s = 86.9% of
the 221 GB/s DRAM roof** over 16.106 GB, so its 55.9 ms (0.36%) target stands. But apparent
bandwidth rises to **595.4 GB/s** for the smallest transfers and the total is **229.0 GB/s =
103.6% of roof**, which is impossible for DRAM traffic - so the small roles are partly
L2-served and the whole-row 87.3 ms figure is an over-estimate.

## The join that had to be discarded

The first attempt joined `captures.child.launch_census` shapes to `audit.prefill`
times - two different captures. It produced plausible per-role numbers, including
190.3 GB/s for `hc_ffn_down`, which was close enough to the roof to be believable.
What caught it was the **total: 229.0 GB/s = 103.6% of the 221 GB/s DRAM roof.** A
physical bound is what made an otherwise invisible modelling error visible.

The raw trace resolved it. The shape join turned out to be right for the HC pair
(`10,485,760 = 1024 x 10240`), and the per-role times were right; the
`launches` column could not be trusted role-by-role.

**Rule for the next analysis**: check a cross-capture join against a physical
bound before using its numbers. This is the second time this session that a
cross-capture join produced a plausible but wrong figure - the first was summing
a whole-capture kernel CSV that contained the prefill (ledger rule 11).

## Why the measured answer is better than the estimate

The estimate said 87.3 ms for the row. The measurement says **55.9 ms for the HC
pair and not much elsewhere**, because the other ten roles' traffic is partly
cache-served and would not be saved by deleting the kernel. That is a smaller
number, but it is the one an A/B can be held to - and it independently confirms
why the row's own "epilogue first" instruction was right.
