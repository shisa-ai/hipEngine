# K3 is flat at 31 tok/s across all widths while AR scales 2.1x

## Summary

A retained refresh packet contains per-width MTP acceptance and rate rows. They settle
the attribution behind the explicit-K3 deficit, and the answer is not the four-request
ceiling alone: K3 throughput is essentially width-independent, while AR scales, and
draft acceptance falls about 40 percent from C1 to C3.

## Changes

- None. Read from
  `benchmarks/results/2026-08-30-w7900-q4km-c1c8-hipengine-refresh-post-promotions.json`.

## Validation

Decode rates, medians of 2, stdev at most 0.25 percent:

| width | acceptance | p1 | AR tok/s | K3 tok/s | K3 over AR | engaged |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.789 | 0.9296 | 22.09 | 31.57 | 1.429 | 10/10 |
| 2 | 0.614 | 0.7362 | 30.84 | 29.95 | 0.971 | 10/10 |
| 3 | 0.467 | 0.5714 | 36.34 | 31.04 | 0.854 | 10/10 |
| 4 | 0.468 | 0.5710 | 40.11 | 31.52 | 0.786 | 10/10 |
| 5 | 0.520 | 0.6302 | 42.15 | 31.31 | 0.743 | 10/10 |
| 6 | 0.513 | 0.6226 | 43.79 | 30.76 | 0.703 | 10/10 |
| 7 | 0.467 | 0.5712 | 44.70 | 31.16 | 0.697 | 10/10 |
| 8 | 0.468 | 0.5710 | 45.66 | 31.45 | 0.689 | 10/10 |

Four conclusions:

1. K3 does not degrade, it is **pinned**: 29.95 to 31.57 tok/s across an eight-fold
   concurrency range, a spread of about 5 percent. AR scales 22.09 to 45.66. So the MTP
   path gains nothing from concurrency, which is the external shape of a hard lane or
   group ceiling rather than a slowdown.
2. Engagement is not the explanation. `mtp_engaged` and `mtp_budget_conformed` are 10/10
   at every width, so MTP is active everywhere and this is not the omitted
   `--generation2-diagnostic` failure mode seen earlier in the campaign.
3. Acceptance explains why K3 is worse than AR rather than merely flat. At 0.467 mean
   acceptance a three-position draft yields about 1.40 accepted tokens per cycle, and
   the p1 position itself drops from 0.9296 to 0.5710. The verify cost of three extra
   rows stops paying for itself.
4. Aggregate acceptance over the packet is 0.4966 across 3182 cycles, with per-position
   decay 0.604, 0.497, 0.375. That is the roughly 40 percent accept loss recorded in the
   superseded artifact, now with current numbers.

Scope note: these are decode-only rates from this packet and are not the matrix rows,
which are complete-wall totals. The matrix shows K3 peaking at C4 and AR higher at C8;
this packet shows the same story from the inside, that K3 has no concurrency scaling.

## Next

1. The acceptance drop with wave width is the more interesting half. If the prompt set is
   identical across widths, acceptance changing from 0.9296 to 0.571 at p1 is a
   batch-composition dependence, and batch-composition invariance is a normative gate
   here, not a tuning preference. Check prompt-set equality across widths first; if they
   match, this is correctness-adjacent and outranks the ceiling work.
2. For the flat scaling, the server already exposes resident bucket capacity and
   occupancy metrics, so the ceiling can be observed without new instrumentation by
   polling the status endpoint during a C1 and C8 run.
3. Do not spend kernel effort on K3 until both are settled. Kernel time is not what is
   pinning the rate.
