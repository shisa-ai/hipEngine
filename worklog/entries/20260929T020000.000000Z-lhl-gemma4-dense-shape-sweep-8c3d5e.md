# the dense term is pinned at 127 GB/s of weight traffic, and the lm head proves 223 is reachable

## Summary

`20260928T172000` priced the dense term at 189.8 ms and asked a specific question: *"a shape
sweep would say whether the rate is a property of the shape or of the call site."* **It is a
property of the shape, and the shape is not addressable -- `4096 -> 2816` at 7.77 TFLOP/s is at
the family's normal rate, not a slow outlier.** The "family best of 11.90" was a single
anomalous K, and the 28 ms the entry hoped to recover **is retracted.**

**But the sweep found something better.** Four shapes spanning 0.77 MB to 24.5 MB of weight all
land at **123.6 to 128.9 GB/s of weight traffic** -- within 5 percent of each other, at half of
DRAM peak. And the lm head, measured in the same entry, moves its 784 MB at **223.5 GB/s, 87
percent of peak.** So the machine can stream at 223 and the dense GEMMs stream at 127.

## The sweep

`scripts/gguf_q8_0_dense_prefill_shape_time.py`, rows=512, tile (32, 64), 200 iters:

| in | out | weight MB | ms | TFLOP/s | GB/s (16 M-tiles) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 256 | 2816 | 0.77 | 0.097 | 7.61 | 126.3 |
| 2048 | 2816 | 6.13 | 0.793 | 7.45 | 123.6 |
| **2816** | **2816** | **8.43** | **0.780** | **10.41** | **172.8** |
| 4096 | 2816 | 12.26 | 1.521 | 7.77 | 128.9 |
| 8192 | 2816 | 24.51 | 3.061 | 7.72 | 128.1 |

**K = 2816 is the outlier, not K = 4096.** It does 37 percent more work than K = 2048 in the
same 0.78 ms, and it is fast at N = 2112, 2816 and 4096 alike -- so it is a property of K. Every
other K sits at 7.4 to 7.8 TFLOP/s.

**The tile is not the lever.** The supported family is `(16|32|64) x (16|32|64)` minus
`(64,64)`, and `(32,64)` is already the tuned default with a documented sweep behind it. I
re-measured the neighbours and reproduced the ordering: `(32,64)` 7.82, `(16,64)` 6.72,
`(64,32)` 5.85, `(32,32)` 4.35. The probe's own numbers for `2816 -> 2816` (0.684 ms, 11.87
TFLOP/s) match the module docstring's recorded 0.684.

## Why the plateau reading matters more than the K anomaly

The 16 M-tiles each read the **full** weight, so a call's weight traffic is 16x the weight
matrix. Dividing that by the measured time gives a rate that is nearly identical across four
very different shapes:

```
123.6, 172.8, 128.9, 128.1, 126.3 GB/s
```

**A rate that holds across a 32x range of weight sizes is a ceiling, not a coincidence** -- the
same signature the expert MMQ kernels show at 49 GB/s. And it is not the machine's read limit:
the lm head's GEMV, pure streaming with one row of output, reaches **223.5 GB/s on the same
GPU.** A 512-row GEMM with 16x weight reuse is running at **57 percent of what a 1-row GEMV
achieves.**

That reframes the prefill gap. It is not three unrelated terms -- experts at 49 GB/s, dense at
127, attention at 174 ms. **Two of the three are GEMMs running at a fraction of a bandwidth
this GPU demonstrably provides**, and the lm head is the control that proves it.

## Retraction

`20260928T172000`'s closing estimate -- *"if the two slow shapes reached the family's best rate
the term would fall by about 28 ms"* -- is **wrong, and the entry said so itself**: *"that is an
estimate from a rate the shapes may not be able to reach, not a measurement."* Now it is
measured. `4096 -> 2816` is at the plateau for its K, and the rate it was being compared against
belongs to one anomalous shape.

## Next

- **The 127 GB/s plateau is the dense term's real addressable part**, and it is now a bandwidth
  question rather than a shape question: the lm head proves 223 GB/s is available for a pure
  read stream.
- **The K = 2816 anomaly is unexplained** and is the one place the dense GEMM exceeds 10
  TFLOP/s. L2 residency is the natural guess but does not survive contact: K = 2048's weight is
  *smaller* (6.13 against 8.43 MB) and slower.
- **Do not chase `4096 -> 2816` as a slow shape.** It is at the plateau; the number to attack is
  the plateau.
- **The expert term's 49 GB/s and the dense term's 127 GB/s now look like the same defect** at
  two magnitudes. A single explanation would cover 287.7 + 189.8 = 477.5 ms of the 687.7 ms
  prefill.
