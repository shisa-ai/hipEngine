# the expert block is 41.84 percent of the prefill at 49 GB/s, and the readback drain it was blamed on is 0.18 percent

## Summary

### The named suspect is cleared

The harness's docstring names a specific defect: `gemma4_project_experts_gate_up_mmq`
"reads the tile row count back to the host and passes it as a launch argument. The
read-back is a pipeline drain inside a 30-layer loop, and neither the census nor the
route ablation can see it."

**It is 1.240 ms over 30 calls -- 0.0413 ms per call, 0.18 percent of the prefill.** A
device-to-host readback of a scalar should cost tens of microseconds, and it does. The
reason it looked like a suspect is that the census wraps the whole call and cannot see
inside it; the reason it is not the problem is that it is genuinely small. The tile map
build is 0.356 ms in total. The two together are 0.23 percent.

### Where the block actually goes

**Two kernels are 90 percent of it.** `q4k gate_up` at 156.5 ms and `q5_1 down` at
103.9 ms are 260.4 of the 287.7 ms. Everything else -- both activation packs, the tile
map, the readback, and layer 29's separate WMMA path with its exact repair -- is 27.3 ms
between them.

### Both run at 49 GB/s, and neither unit is saturated

| kernel | ms | weight set | GB/s | MACs | TMAC/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| gate_up | 156.5 | 7.71 GB | **49.3** | 470 G | 3.00 |
| down | 103.9 | 5.14 GB | **49.5** | 243 G | 2.34 |

Both land at **49 GB/s of weight traffic, 19 percent of the 256 GB/s DRAM peak**, and at
2.3 to 3.0 TMAC/s, which is **8 to 10 percent of the roughly 30 TMAC/s an int8 dp4a
path on 40 CUs implies.** Neither number is close to a ceiling, so the kernels are
**latency-bound**: they are not waiting on bytes and not waiting on arithmetic units.
That is the same shape the down projection's own microbench found from the other
direction -- 1.82x the time for 4x the work, a large fixed component per pass.

### This corrects the previous entry again

`20260928T142500` concludes that the microbench's fixed component "is the weight stream"
at 163 GB/s. **In situ the down projection reads its weight set at 49 GB/s, not 163**, so
the fixed component is not the weight stream. What it is remains unidentified; the
1.164 ms does not scale with rows and is not the weight read. The safe statement is the
measured one: there is a row-count-independent component and it is not the weight stream.

## Next

- **The expert block is 41.84 percent of the step and is latency-bound, not saturated
  on either axis.** The lever is more independent work in flight per weight byte, and
  the only shape knob that has been tested is `ROWS_PER_PASS`: rows=64 takes 5.267 ms
  against rows=32's 2.925, which is 10 percent better per row but needs two passes
  above 32 rows. A row tile above 32 with the accumulator pressure that implies is the
  open question the constant's own comment raises.
- **Do not chase the readback or the packs.** Together they are 1.73 percent, and the
  readback is 0.18 percent. They were the harness's named suspects and the measurement
  closes them.
- **The two 49 GB/s figures agreeing to 0.4 percent across two different quantizations
  and two different kernels is itself information**: it says the rate is set by
  something shared -- the pass structure, the activation load path, or the launch
  geometry -- rather than by either kernel's arithmetic.
