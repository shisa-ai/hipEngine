# Gemma 4 serves sampled requests, and MTP accepts by exact speculative sampling

## Summary

The lead asked for probabilistic MTP for Gemma 4 after reading llama.cpp pull
request 27694, and to see whether it gives a speed bump.

The Gemma 4 generator refused every sampled request on the plain route as well
as on MTP (`temperature must be 0`), so there was no sampled baseline to
compare against. This unit adds three things:

1. `hipengine/generation/gemma4_sampling.py`. `Gemma4Sampler` computes the
   request's sampling law from a logits row (temperature, top-k, top-p, min-p,
   repetition/presence/frequency penalties, logit bias, suppressed tokens,
   seed). The shared host sampler orders the whole 262,144-entry row before
   filtering and measured 8.7 to 28 ms a row on this vocabulary, against a
   12.8 ms decode step; this one partitions for the top-k and reuses the shared
   helpers on those candidates (0.44 ms), or takes a single-precision softmax of
   the whole row when there is no top-k (0.52 ms). A first version pruned
   entries 50 nats below the maximum and did nothing: Gemma's logits are
   soft-capped at 30, so nothing is ever that far down.
2. The plain route samples through it when the request is not plain greedy; a
   plain greedy request keeps the device argmax.
3. The MTP provider's accept is `speculative_accept`: `min(1, p/q)` with
   residual resampling, with greedy as the point-mass case, so one walk serves
   both. A sampled request draws each draft from the head's law, narrowed to its
   64 most likely tokens.

W7900, production profile, budget 4, 18 prompts, two seeds, 128 tokens, whole
public call, nothing else running. Sampled plain decode is 69.2 to 70.3 tok/s
(greedy 73.7). MTP with a drawn draft: 87.8 tok/s at temperature 0.5 (1.27x),
87.0 at 0.8 (1.24x), 87.5 at 1.0 with top-k 64 and top-p 0.95 (1.25x). Against
MTP offering the head's most likely token, the drawn draft moves acceptance
68.9% -> 68.8%, 65.4% -> 66.3% and 63.4% -> 65.9%, and MTP throughput +0.2%,
+1.4% and +2.1%. So the answer to "is there a speed bump from probabilistic
drafting" is: about 2% at temperature 1.0 and nothing at 0.5, the same order
llama.cpp reports for accepted length. The larger change is that a sampled
request gets MTP at all. Greedy MTP through the unified walk is unchanged at
102.8 tok/s (1.40x).

A first measurement used a slower whole-vocabulary sampler and showed the drawn
draft losing at temperatures 0.5 and 0.8 (75.5 to 76.1 tok/s against 79.3 to
80.1) because the draft's own law cost more than its acceptance gained. The
sampler was changed and all six arms rerun; the artifact records both.

`Gemma4MTPTextProvider.draft_sampling` is the lever for the comparison arm and
has a `docs/REFACTOR.md` entry with its removal condition.

The previous unit's commit (`1bef18f19`) changed the provider's telemetry labels
under the production profile and did not run
`tests/test_live_gemma4_generate_mtp_flag.py`, whose second test pinned the old
labels and token identity. That test is repaired here to follow the profile's
verify contract.

## Validation

- Unit: the sampler's law is checked against the shared host sampler as oracle
  over nine filter combinations; the accept walk's emitted token is checked
  against the target law over 60,000 trials for a drawn and a point-mass draft;
  the provider's committed tokens are checked position by position against a
  synthetic target's law over 2,500 seeds, for both draft laws and for a head
  that disagrees with the target. Every `unit_gemma4` test passes.
- GPU and live on the W7900: `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_runner_verify_rows.py`,
  `tests/test_gpu_gemma4_assistant_forward.py`, and both attached-provider tests
  in `tests/test_live_gemma4_generate_mtp_flag.py`, run one test per pytest
  invocation.
- `hipengine serve`: chat completions at temperature 0, at 1.0 with top-k 64 and
  top-p 0.95 on seeds 1, 1 and 2 (equal, equal, different), and at 0.8 with a
  repetition penalty all returned text; `logprobs: true` returned the named
  refusal.
- Not checked on the model: that sampled MTP's output distribution equals the
  plain route's. The unit tests establish it for the accept rule and the
  provider's bookkeeping; on the model it would need many more samples than
  were run.
