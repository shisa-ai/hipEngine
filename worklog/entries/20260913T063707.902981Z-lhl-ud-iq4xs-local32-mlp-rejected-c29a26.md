# The IQ4_XS local32 sibling is not short of memory-level parallelism

## Summary

`gguf_iq4_xs_local32_gemv_kernel` is the largest single item in the UD-Q4_K_M
verifier at **10.4-11.3 ms/step (28-30%)**, running at 459-555 GB/s, which is
48-58% of the 960 GB/s roofline. Two candidate explanations were tested and
both were rejected:

1. **The split-K wave count is wrong.** An interleaved sweep of `WAVES` in
   {1, 2, 4} on six real IQ4_XS shapes at rows 2/3/4 shows the current
   `_local32_waves` heuristic already picks the best or tied value on every
   shape. `WAVES=1` is 1.6-2.2x worse on the narrow-N shapes. No win.
2. **The owner is short of memory-level parallelism.** The decode loop's
   `load -> LDS -> FMA` chain per column was hoisted into a batch of all eight
   columns' header and payload words. This is neutral at rows 2/3 and clearly
   **negative** at rows 4 (attn_qkv 63.7 -> 85.6 us, ssm_out 41.7 -> 51.2 us),
   so it was reverted.

The second experiment also produced the useful fact that the owner runs at
**168 VGPRs with 64 threads per block** - six blocks per CU, 384 resident
threads, **19% occupancy** - and that the batched form *lowered* VGPRs to 112
(raising occupancy to about 28%) while making the kernel slower. So neither
occupancy nor loads-in-flight is the binding constraint, and raising occupancy
by shrinking the register footprint is not the lever.

## Next

Do not retry either of these. The remaining lever on this owner is the decode
itself, not the launch geometry or the scheduling: phase B pays one `ds_read`
per decoded element for the fused codebook LUT, so eight LDS reads per
(256-block, column) against four global loads, and the LDS traffic is about
7.5x the weight traffic. Reducing the LDS side - for example by holding the
two nibble halves in registers across the `j` loop - is the next candidate, and
it needs the same interleaved harness used here.
