# WMMA bf16 score-GEMM closes the direct-INT8 prefill speed gap

## Summary

Task #6 follow-up (the remaining 1.39x). The direct INT8 prefill route's
flash kernel computed scores as scalar int8 dot products and exp; the
instruction budget left it 1.39x behind the BF16 oracle route even after
the GQA-grouped flash rewrite. This adds a second kernel selection,
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_INT8_PREFILL_KERNEL=wmma` (default stays `sequential`),
that computes the score block as a bf16 WMMA GEMM. int8 K is exactly
representable in bf16 and Q is already BF16-rounded by the wrapper, so
the WMMA path is a T2 reassociation only - numerically indistinguishable
from the sequential kernel, confirmed by measurement below.

## Measurements (RX 7900 XTX, Qwen3.8-27B Q4_K_M, 2 measured runs)

| length | wmma tok/s | control tok/s | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,192 | 735.6 | 766.0 (oracle) | 96.0% |
| 16,384 | 639.3 | 698.4 (default route) | 91.5% |
| 32,768 | 506.6 | 566.4 (default route) | 89.4% |

The campaign's original blocker was 164 tok/s at 8,192 (4.6x behind);
the flash rewrite took it to 549 (1.39x); WMMA closes it to 1.04x. The
16K/32K arms confirm the route now holds ~90% of default-route speed at
depth instead of collapsing (sequential attention cost grew quadratically
with context; WMMA keeps it near-linear in the GEMM until memory-bound).

## Numerics (no-mirror 4,096, teacher-forced vs BF16 reference)

mean row-KL 0.188 / max 6.46 / top-1 agreement 0.914 -> gate FAILED,
matching the sequential kernel's 0.215 within noise. The drift is the
INT8-read prefill arithmetic itself (the route property measured in the
campaign gate), not the kernel; the bf16-out contract is preserved.
`HIPENGINE_GGUF_INT8_PREFILL_DIRECT` stays default OFF. The route remains
an opt-in capacity lever: 96% of oracle speed with the same measured
quality cost.
