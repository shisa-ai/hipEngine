# Draft KV and target hidden slabs are priced through the pool budget

## Summary

The MoE MTP2 adapter allocates two resident slabs of its own -- the draft KV pair
through `_allocate_mtp_dense_kv`, and the three target-hidden buffers -- and
neither charged the KV pool budget. They were allocated straight to the device,
so an overload was discovered as a HIP OOM rather than as a budget refusal, and
no admission decision could see them. Both are now priced by name before they are
allocated, and released with the buffers they pay for.

## Changes

- `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py`: new `_mtp_dense_kv_nbytes(capacity,
  qk_head_dim, kv_heads)` returns the bytes `_allocate_mtp_dense_kv` will
  allocate, and the allocator itself now derives its two sizes from it. The
  pricing and the allocation therefore cannot drift; there is one formula.
- `hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf_moe_mtp2.py`: the adapter keeps
  `_resident_charge_tokens` (target key -> charge tokens). `_charge_resident_slab`
  prices a slab through `reserve_resident_bytes` before the mallocs, and
  `_release_resident_slab_charges(target_key)` releases one target's charges or
  all of them. Both slab sites charge before allocating: `draft_kv` sized from
  the new helper, `target_hidden_slab` sized from the three buffer sizes the site
  computes anyway. If the allocation fails after the charge, that target's charge
  is released before the error propagates. `close()` releases every remaining
  charge after the buffers are freed. A pool without the ledger is charged
  nothing, so the adapter still runs against pools that predate it.

## Validation

- `tests/test_unit_qwen35_gguf_moe_mtp2.py`: 4 new tests. The charges are
  recorded per target and by name in order; releasing one target leaves the
  other charged and releasing all empties the ledger and the pool's tokens; a
  refusal propagates with `refused_consumer == "draft_kv"`, names the consumer in
  its message, and leaves nothing charged; a pool without the ledger is skipped
  and releasing with nothing charged is a no-op; and
  `_mtp_dense_kv_nbytes` equals both the bytes the allocator requests and the
  bytes of the buffers it returns, so the price is the allocation rather than a
  second estimate. File: 12 passed.
- Bundle: `test_unit_qwen35_gguf_moe_mtp2`, `test_unit_qwen35_gguf_mtp2_seam`,
  `test_unit_qwen38_packed_c1_commit`, `test_unit_gguf_packed_verify_layout`,
  `test_unit_gguf_packed_workspace_stability`,
  `test_unit_kvcache_global_device_pool`, `test_unit_memory_admission` -- 311
  passed.
- Not covered: the two slab *call sites* are not reached by any automated test.
  The charge helpers they call are covered directly, and the size helper is
  covered against the allocator, but nothing drives a MoE MTP2 prefill far enough
  to observe the charge being taken at the site. That is the gap a live arm would
  close.
