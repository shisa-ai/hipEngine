# RT-1 serving-relevant chunk comparison: -38.51%, and numerics are not chunk-invariant at 256

## Summary

RT-1 was qualified last unit: the -4.94% compared 1024 -> 4096, the benchmark
protocol's value, while the serving path defaults to 256. This unit measures the
comparison that governs a promotion, and **the serving-relevant win is far
larger: -38.51%**.

Two things came out of it that I did not expect: the arms separate completely,
and **the chunk size is not numerically invariant at 256**.

## Result

`code-p4096`, three repetitions per arm, same protocol and cached build:

| chunk | samples (s) | median (s) | peak allocation |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 256 | 23.8427, 23.8886, 31.5102 | 23.8886 | 83,198,793,992 B |
| 4096 | 14.7967, 14.6882, 14.6832 | **14.6882** | 89,403,335,432 B |

Median **-9.2004 s (-38.51%)**; min-to-min -9.1595 s (-38.42%).

## Separation, stated carefully

4096's *slowest* repetition (14.7967 s) beats 256's *fastest* (23.8427 s) by 38%,
so the arms do not meaningfully overlap. A naive range-overlap test returns true
only because the 256 arm's outlier extends its range upward.

The 256 arm's third repetition (31.5102 s) is a 1.32x outlier against its own
median and inflates its within-arm stdev to 3.6037 s against 4096's 0.0524 s.
**|median delta| is therefore only 2.55x the larger stdev** - weaker by that
statistic than the 5.70x recorded for 1024 -> 4096. I am recording that rather
than quoting the -38.51% as though it had the same statistical footing. The
conclusion does not rest on the median: min-to-min and worst-of-4096 vs
best-of-256 are both unambiguous.

## Numerics are not chunk-invariant at 256

| | 256 | 4096 |
|---|---|---|
| `token_id` | 248068 | 248068 (identical) |
| `logits_sha256` | `e717076f...` | `7270a972...` (**differs**) |

So this is an **arithmetic change** relative to today's serving default, not a
pure scheduling one, and it must be described that way.

It is not a gate regression: the X-6 production-profile gate passes at chunk 4096
and hashes bit-identically to the chunk-1024 certification, so **4096 is the
certified configuration**. The gate requirement is satisfied by the target
configuration rather than by a new 256-vs-4096 comparison.

Worth stating plainly because it is easy to over-generalize: the route **is**
invariant between 1024 and 4096 (identical hashes, sibling packet) and is **not**
invariant between 256 and 4096. Both are measured. Neither supports a general
claim that chunk size does not affect numerics.

## Memory

Peak 83.20 -> 89.40 GB = **+6.20 GB**. GTT here is 120 GB with 121 GB of RAM
available, so 89.40 GB leaves 30.6 GB (25.5%) headroom. The cost is paid on long
prompts by the prompt that receives the win.

## Limits

- **One case, one category** (`code-p4096`). AGENTS.md forbids retaining a claim
  measured on a single fixed prompt. The 12-case, 4-category suite run is
  launched and is the evidence that would generalize or refute this.
- Prefill only; this parameter does not affect decode.
- One host (gfx1151 APU, unified memory). The +6.20 GB admission cost is
  host-specific and would be a real blocker on a discrete-memory device.
