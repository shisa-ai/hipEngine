# the expert gate/up mmq32 kernel is two-regime and the production point sits on the knee

## Summary

**I refuted the most promising prefill lead in the tree, and came away with a
characterization that says where the real headroom is.**

The lead looked strong. The gate/up expert projection is the single largest kernel
in prefill, and the 2026-06-16 probe had measured an integer-WMMA sibling at
**2.66x the scalar dot** (8.21 against 21.83 ms/call) and never promoted it, for a
procedural reason recorded in the artifact itself: *"Synthetic kernel microbench/
prototype only; no runtime dispatch/default change."*

**At gemma4's own geometry every WMMA variant is slower.**

## Attribution first, at the real operating point

Prefill is 693 tok/s at a 512-token prompt. The earlier attribution in hand was
from a ~96-token prompt where the mix is different, so I re-traced at 512 with a
warmup, which reports prefill 0.76 s (677 tok/s):

| kernel | n | ms | pct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `gguf_q8_0_prefill_wmma_kernel` | 615 | 551.6 | 24.6% |
| `gemma4_attention_prefill_kernel` | 90 | 536.9 | 23.9% |
| `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_fused…` | 87 | 517.4 | 23.1% |
| `q5_1_mmq_ds4_selected_prefill_kernel` | 87 | 349.4 | 15.6% |

The expert pair is 38.7% of prefill and the gate/up half is the largest single
kernel. Geometry from the artifact header: 30 layers, hidden 2816, 128 experts,
top-8, expert FFN 704.

## The WMMA lead, refuted

`scripts/gguf_q4_k_t16_selected_prefill_microbench.py` carries both the production
mode and every WMMA sibling, so the comparison is apples to apples. At
`--hidden 2816 --out-features-a 704 --out-features-b 704 --experts 128
--rows-per-expert 32` -- gemma4's real expert shape -- on gfx1151:

| mode | ms/call | TFLOPs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `q8-1-ds4-mmq32` (**production**) | **5.950** | **5.458** |
| `q8-1-ds4-wmma32` | 7.189 | 4.518 |
| `q8-1-ds4-wmma` | 7.297 | 4.451 |
| `q8-1-ds4-wmma64` | 7.427 | 4.374 |
| `q8-1-ds4-preview-wmma32` | 12.513 | 2.596 |

**The best WMMA variant is 1.21x slower than production.** The 2026-06-16 win was
at hidden 2048, out 4096+4096, 8 experts and 512 rows per expert -- a much larger K
and a much wider output, where tensor-core width pays. Gemma 4's expert is narrow
(704+704) and shallow in rows (32 per expert), so the dp4a path wins.

**The microbench reproduces production exactly**: 5.950 ms/call against the trace's
517.4 ms / 87 calls = 5.95 ms/call. That agreement is what makes the refutation
trustworthy rather than a harness artifact.

## What the kernel actually is: two regimes

Sweeping rows per expert at **fixed weights** (experts 128, hidden 2816, out
704+704) separates the two costs:

| rows/expert | total rows | ms | TFLOPs |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 128 | 3.065 | 0.33 |
| 2 | 256 | 3.286 | 0.62 |
| 4 | 512 | 3.292 | 1.23 |
| 8 | 1024 | 3.567 | 2.28 |
| 16 | 2048 | 4.358 | 3.73 |
| **32** | **4096** | **5.978** | **5.43** |
| 64 | 8192 | 10.729 | 6.05 |
| 128 | 16384 | 20.987 | 6.19 |
| 256 | 32768 | 41.424 | 6.27 |

**Two regimes fall out.**

- **A fixed weight-streaming floor of ~3.0 ms per call**, flat from 1 to 4 rows per
  expert where there is almost no arithmetic (0.33 TFLOPs) yet the time barely
  moves. 285.5 MB of expert weights (128 experts x 1408 out x 2816 K x 0.5625
  bytes) over 3.0 ms is **95 GB/s**.
- **An asymptotic compute rate of 3.14 T MAC/s** (6.27 TFLOPs), reached by 128
  rows per expert and holding to 256.

**Production runs at 32 rows per expert, which is exactly the knee: 5.98 ms =
3.0 ms of weight stream + 2.98 ms of arithmetic.** Neither half dominates, which is
why no single change to either one will move this kernel much, and why the WMMA
attempt lost -- it improves the compute half while the weight half stays put, and
the WMMA tile's own weight handling is worse.

## Both halves are about 2x off a plausible ceiling

- **The weight stream at 95 GB/s** against the ~199 GB/s the dense pack8 prefill
  reaches on the same device. The irreducible 285.5 MB would take 1.43 ms instead
  of 3.0 ms: **1.57 ms available, 26% of the call.**
- **The arithmetic at 3.14 T MAC/s.** RDNA3 int8 dp4a peak on this part is roughly
  59 T MAC/s (40 CU x 128 lanes x 4 MAC x 2.9 GHz), so this is **5.3% of peak** --
  about 19 cycles per dp4a per lane. Register and LDS pressure are ruled out as the
  cause: the kernel uses **40-48 VGPRs, 128 SGPRs and 1792 bytes of LDS**, which
  leaves occupancy headroom, and `__launch_bounds__(128, 2)` is a compiler hint
  rather than a cap.

## Where the compute half goes

The inner loop in `gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_ds4_mmq32_prefill_compact32_body`
is a **single serial accumulator chain**:

```cpp
for (int qword = 0; qword < 8; ++qword) {
  const uint32_t q4_word =
      (weight_cache[cr].qs[qword >> 1] >> ((qword & 1) * 4)) & 0x0F0F0F0Fu;
  dot = gguf_q4_k_mmq_dp4a(q4_word, activation_cache.qs[qword], dot);
}
```

Eight dependent dp4a per (column, row-group) pair, and the K loop compounds it: 88
K-iterations per block (`in_features` 2816 / 256-value Q4_K blocks x 8 subblocks),
each ending in a `__syncthreads()`, with the outer `acc[wsic * TM + cr] +=` also
chaining across K. The only parallelism available to hide the dp4a latency is
`TM x WNITER` independent chains, and the compiler has to interleave them across a
barrier every K-iteration.

**Breaking the chain with two or four independent accumulators combined at the end
is a small, local, testable change**, and it is the most tractable way to attack
the arithmetic half. The weight-stream half needs a different access pattern.
