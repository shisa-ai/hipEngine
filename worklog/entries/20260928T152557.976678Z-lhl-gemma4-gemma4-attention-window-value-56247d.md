# Correct the sliding-window value: the window buys 30%, not 3%

## Summary

This entry corrects a number in
`worklog/entries/20260928T152252.512377Z-lhl-gemma4-gemma4-prefill-4096-gate-4b1457.md`,
which is committed and therefore immutable. Everything else in that entry
stands; one derived figure was wrong and its supporting assumption was wrong
with it.

**What was claimed:** "the window buys about 3% at 4096", inferred by scaling
the global layers' measured 122.1 ms/layer down by the head_dim ratio, assumed
to be 2.0 (512 / 256).

**What is measured:** the window buys **+30.0%** on a full 4096-token prefill
(direct kernel measurement, causal mask vs causal-and-windowed mask at identical
geometry), and **~36%** by the model-derived route once the head_dim ratio is
measured rather than assumed. The head_dim ratio is **1.327x** (mean over all
eight block sizes), not 2.0.

The assumption was wrong because the exact kernel is dominated by its per-key
walk, not by per-key ALU work. Doubling head_dim doubles the FMAs per key but
leaves the walk that actually costs alone, so it costs 1.33x, not 2x. Reading a
1.28x-of-2.0 head_dim effect as "the window is doing nothing" inverted the
conclusion: the residual the old entry attributed to nothing was in fact most of
the window's saving.

**What changes as a result.** V3's answer to its own question is unchanged: the
fallback does apply the window mask, so the current 4096-token prefill output is
not wrong, and P1's ~1300 ms estimate stands. What changes is the split of the
remaining win, and P14's standing: the per-block cost tracks the pass-3 walk
`sum(q+1)` to within about 5% over the first four blocks, so pass 3 sets the
kernel's cost, and pass 3 still walks the full causal triangle on sliding rows
because the window's lower bound reaches pass 1 alone. P14 now has direct
evidence behind it rather than an inference.

Reproduced twice in separate processes: 121.18 / 84.98 / 154.70 and
121.19 / 84.85 / 154.22, so the sweep is stable to about 0.2%.

`--mode head-dim-ratio`, same shapes, head_dim 512 against 256 at a fixed 8 KV
heads, causal mask at every block size:

```
ratio 1.382 1.366 1.398 1.375 1.348 1.240 1.250 1.259
mean 1.327x  (range 1.240-1.398)   linear assumption would be 2.000x
```

**Cross-check against the profiled model.** The 5 global layers measure
122.1 ms/layer in the family census (device-busy, profiler). 122.1 / 1.327 =
92.0 ms/layer is the prediction for an unwindowed sliding row; the sliding
layers measure 59.1 ms/layer, so the model-derived window saving is **35.8%**.
The direct measurement says 30.0%. They agree within the probe's known bias.

**Why the two differ, and why the probe is the conservative one.** Absolute
times here exceed a profiler's device-busy sum because each launch is timed in
isolation and pays launch latency that a back-to-back stream does not. The bias
is not uniform: against the profiled per-layer costs, the causal arm runs 1.27x
high and the shorter windowed arm 1.44x high. Because the inflation is larger
for the shorter arm, the probe *understates* the saving, so 30% is a floor and
the model-derived 36% is the better estimate for the model. Ratios between
equal-length arms -- the head_dim ratio -- are unaffected by this and are the
trustworthy output of this harness.

**Reconciliation of the 49x.** Unchanged in structure, restated with measured
inputs: the fallback's own growth from 1024 to 4096 is 7.74 -> 121.19 ms/layer
causal hd256, or 15.7x for 4x tokens, matching the global layers' independently
measured 15.6x; and the fallback runs 3.15x the flash path it replaced at 1024.
15.7 x 3.15 = 49.5, against 49.4 observed.

**Not established.** Pass 3's share of the 1478.7 ms is still not measured
directly. The `sum(q+1)` tracking is strong evidence that it dominates, but it
is an inference from block-shape scaling, not a pass-level profile. P14's
realized saving therefore remains a hypothesis, now a better-supported one.
