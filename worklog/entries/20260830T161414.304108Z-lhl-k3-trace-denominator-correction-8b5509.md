# CORRECTION: the K3 9.3x was a wrong denominator; the MTP cycle is idle-heavy

## Summary

I correct `20260830T161301…`, which I committed minutes ago. Its "kernel time is 9.3x
wall, consistent with a
saturated device" conclusion is **wrong**: it divided whole-process kernel totals by one
cell's wall time. The
packet has 10 cells. Recomputed with the right denominators, and with the interval union
I had not yet computed,
the MTP cycle looks idle-heavy rather than saturated, which puts a locally fixable host
cost back on the table
and removes the "must redesign the cycle" pressure I just wrote.

- The 9.3x came from 56,282 / 6,049, where 6,049 ms was cell[0]'s two arms out of ten.
  Per-lane concurrency is

  the 42,337 ms of
  busy time, so the route is essentially serial on stream 0, as the 1.33x overlap factor shows.

## Bound on MTP device share, and its caveat

- Union busy is an upper bound on device time inside the arm windows because it also
  counts model-load and
  warmup kernels spread over a 130 s process span.
- AR's device share is independently measured at ~95% (`2026-08-30-w7900-q4km-ar-rows-
  scaling-kernel-trace.json`,
  rows 1-2, a different config - approximate when applied to width 8). Taking AR busy =
  0.95 x 24,364 = 23,146 ms
  leaves at most 42,337 - 23,146 = 19,191 ms for the MTP arm over its 35,069 ms wall:
  **MTP is at most ~55%
  device-busy**. Being an upper bound, the true share is likely lower, so the idle time is real.
- Read together with `20260830T160316…` (accept 104.4 ms per lane-cycle with uploads at
  0.1 ms), the remaining
  ~100 ms is host-side work inside `_enqueue_target_batch_accept` outside the copies, not
  a queue drain.

## Ruled out since that measurement

- `_batch_accept_resources` (qwen35_gguf_mtp2.py:2103) allocates its workspace behind an
  `is None` guard, so it
  is not a per-cycle device allocation.
- The pinned staging path is not the cost either way (0.1 ms), and `host_register` is
  called once per engine.
