# HIP DMS decode beats dense on W7900: wave-group6 producer enabled

## Summary

The user asked to replicate the FastDMS CUDA result (DMS faster than the
equivalent vLLM dense path) on our HIP stack. Baseline on W7900 GPU0 at 16K
context: DMS-BF16 decoded at 55.4 ms/step versus dense 45.98 (+20%), and
DMS-INT8 at 95.5 (+108%). After three kept optimizations the A/B stands at:

| Arm | Prefill | Decode (median) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dense BF16 | 630.2 tok/s | 45.89 ms/step |
| **DMS BF16** | 625.8 tok/s | **38.14 ms/step (17% faster than dense)** |
| DMS INT8 | 621.2 tok/s | 91.79 ms/step (follow-up below) |

The decisive change: the wave-group6 split-K attention producer —
specialized for this model's exact geometry (24 q-heads / 4 kv-heads /
dim 256, GQA group 6), with shared-Q staging, 4-wide vectorized K loads,
and warp-level reductions — was compiled only for gfx1151. Extending the
compile define to gfx1100 (f33078ed6) made it dispatch here: the kernel
measured 4.35x faster than the generic grouped producer at live 8192
(0.344 -> 0.079 ms, ~1.9x off the bandwidth roofline), with identical
output. Supporting changes: per-layer staging-upload hoist (1e706099d,
55.39 -> 54.39) and the in-place finalize mirror update (b8a864116,
54.39 -> 53.96).

Correctness: greedy tokens are identical to the pre-change DMS baseline
(same eviction decisions, same outputs) on the fixed manifest prompt;
finite logits every step; DMS HIP tests 19/19 (the build-plan test
updated to the new measured policy).

Diagnosis method: `campaign-artifacts/c1-k-sweep/dms_step_decompose.py`
(per-phase timing wrappers) plus the microbench
`scripts/dms_compact_attn_long_bench.py`. The step is GPU-bound; the
mid-forward device sync is absorbed by the launch-ahead pipeline
(deferral measured wall-neutral and was reverted), and the host finalize
is ~1 ms after the in-place rewrite.
