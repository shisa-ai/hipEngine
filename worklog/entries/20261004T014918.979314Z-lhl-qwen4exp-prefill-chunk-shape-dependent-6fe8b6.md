# The prefill chunk gain is shape-dependent: +15.6% at 4K but -4.1% at 512 on identical work

## Summary

The ladder entry (`20261004T012454`) found the canonical protocol running chunk 512
and measured `code-p4096` at +18.4% for chunk 2048. **That headline was an
over-generalisation and this entry corrects it.** A single-case sweep of a
partitioning parameter cannot distinguish "this parameter helps" from "this
parameter helps at this prompt length".

The full twelve-case suite, both arms in one session, on the canonical scoreboard
estimator (total prompt tokens / total prompt time):

| shape | chunk 512 | chunk 2048 | delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| p512 | 298.46 | 286.35 | **-4.1%** |
| p1024 | 297.72 | 306.81 | **+3.1%** |
| p4096 | 265.78 | 307.30 | **+15.6%** |
| **all twelve** | **273.84** | **305.18** | **+11.4%** |

**The 512 arm lands at 273.84, reproducing the recorded canonical weighted 274.1**,
so this comparison sits directly on the scoreboard's basis.

Every one of the four p4096 cases gains about +15%, and every one of the four p512
cases loses between 4.0% and 5.3%. Both directions are consistent across cases, so
neither is noise.

## The p512 result is the informative one

At a 512-token prompt the chunk **cannot** change the partitioning — the prompt fits
in one pass at either setting — so **the prefill work is identical**. The 4.1% loss
is therefore a *fixed cost of the larger configuration*, not a partitioning effect.

The mechanism is visible in the code: `prefill_chunk_size` feeds
`staging_token_capacity` in `plan_qwen4_exp_residency`
(`hipengine/generation/qwen4_exp_gguf.py:112`) and the scratch reservation in
`resolve_qwen4_exp_context`, so configuring 2048 allocates staging and scratch for
2048 tokens even when the prompt is 512.

**A blanket 2048 default is therefore wrong.** The gain comes from reducing the
number of passes over the model, and the loss comes from over-allocating for short
prompts. Those are separable.

## Decode regresses at every shape

| shape | chunk 512 tg | chunk 2048 tg | delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| p512 | 20.368 | 19.825 | -2.7% |
| p1024 | 19.660 | 19.041 | -3.2% |
| p4096 | 19.673 | 17.822 | -9.4% |

Counting 128 decode transitions, the composite is **+3.2% worse at p512, +1.4% worse
at p1024, and -6.2% better at p4096**.

**The decode regression is unexplained.** No mechanism was tested. It is carried
forward as an open question rather than priced in, because decode is a first-class
target of the same objective.

## The larger chunk is measurably noisier

Max prefill CV is **2.25% at chunk 512** against **6.57% at chunk 2048**; max decode
CV is 1.34% against 9.91%. I checked for an external cause and found none: no
warnings, no thermal or throttling events, no OOM, no competing process, and 84 GiB
free host memory at the end of the session. This is a property of the configuration
to weigh against the gain, not a measurement defect.

## Validation

- Both arms sequential in one session, same host, no other GPU work, identical
  command apart from `--prefill-chunk-size`.
- The 512 arm reproducing the recorded scoreboard number (273.84 vs 274.1) is what
  ties this to existing evidence rather than making it a standalone measurement.
- The p512 arm is a **control**, not a treatment: identical work at both settings,
  which is what makes the 4.1% loss attributable to allocation rather than
  partitioning.
- Cross-arm bit-identity is established for `code-p4096` (129/129, in the ladder).
  **It is not asserted for the other eleven cases** — the two arms of this suite were
  not diffed token-by-token. That is a real gap in the correctness evidence and it is
  named rather than glossed.

## Next

- **The decode regression is the blocking open question.** It is monotonic in chunk
  size and reproduced by a delayed replicate (the suite's 512 arm at 10:29 gave
  `code-p4096 tg = 19.627` against the ladder's 19.649 at 10:03, so it is not session
  drift). Nothing explains it yet.
- **The correct change is shape-adaptive, not a blanket increase**: keep a small
  chunk for short prompts and raise it only where the pass count actually falls.
  `GGUF_PREFILL_CHUNK_SIZES_BY_GEOMETRY` (`hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1151/__init__.py:1627`)
  already implements a geometry-keyed policy with a recorded short/mid-prompt
  exemption, but has **no entry for the Qwen3.8-Flash-Next geometry**, so it falls
  through. That entry is the natural place for the fix.
- **The 4096 arm is unmeasured on the suite.** Its single-case +25.6% and its
  arithmetic divergence (118/129 positions) are recorded in the ladder artifact only,
  and the existing comment says it also costs more at 64K.
- **Separate the staging over-allocation from the chunk policy.** The p512 control
  says part of the cost is allocation, so sizing staging by the chunk actually used
  rather than the configured cap may recover the short-prompt loss independently of
  the chunk choice.
