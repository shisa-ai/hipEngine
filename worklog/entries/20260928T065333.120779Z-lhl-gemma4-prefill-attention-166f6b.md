# removing every K and V load makes prefill attention 34 percent slower so it is not traffic bound

## Summary

The previous entry's traffic hypothesis is **falsified**, and in the opposite
direction to the one it predicted.

`--mask zero` skips every K/V load while leaving the key loop and the reductions
intact. The kernel gets **slower**, by 34%:

| case | mask=keep | mask=zero | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| swa 256d 16q/8kv window=1024 | 62.23 ms | 83.44 ms | **+34.1%** |
| full 512d 16q/2kv window=0 | 130.27 ms | 172.91 ms | **+32.7%** |
| 30-layer total | 2207.2 ms | 2950.5 ms | **+33.7%** |

Removing **100% of the memory traffic** costs a third of the time. The kernel is
therefore not traffic-bound, not bandwidth-bound, and a token-tiled flash kernel
that reduces K re-reads would not address its cost.

## Why this is the useful result

The previous entry proposed that K is re-read once per query token and that a
flash-style tiling kernel would remove the redundancy. I attached the
counter-evidence at the time -- the traffic estimate exceeded the part's DRAM peak,
so the model was already suspect -- and this ablation settles it against the
hypothesis rather than for it.

The kernel's own source comment said the same thing before I measured it:

> measured 147 GFLOP/s and ~5.3 us per key at 512 tokens, **invariant to the work
> inside the key iteration**

Invariance to the work is what a per-key overhead cost looks like. This ablation
extends it: the cost is invariant to the work being present *at all*. The time
tracks the number of key iterations, the mask bookkeeping, and the `logits_s[j]`
store, not the arithmetic or the bytes.

Why it gets *faster* with loads present is not established here and I am not
claiming a mechanism. The plausible reading is that the loads provide latency to
hide the loop's own dependent work behind, and with nothing to overlap the
shuffle chains and shared-memory round trips expose fully. That is a hypothesis,
not a measurement.

## What this rules out, and what it leaves

**Ruled out:** reducing K/V traffic, token-tiled flash attention, cache blocking,
and any change whose benefit is measured in bytes moved.

**Left open:** the per-key serial structure itself. Both attention paths now
measure as limited by something other than arithmetic and other than traffic --
decode by block count (`20260928T065154`), prefill by per-key overhead. For prefill
the candidate directions are the number of key iterations a block runs, the
`__syncthreads` and shuffle depth inside each, and how many keys are in flight per
block. None of those has been measured yet.

## Validation

```
env -u ROCR_VISIBLE_DEVICES PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python \
    scripts/gemma4_attention_prefill_bench.py --tokens 2048 --mask zero
```

Completed; 5 iterations per case; gfx1151, one device. The `mask=keep` rows are
unchanged from `20260928T065251` and were re-measured in the same session, so the
two modes are comparable.

The ablation removes loads, not correctness: with every mask entry zero the kernel
skips the dot for every key and writes `-inf`, which is the same path a fully-masked
row already takes in production.
