# MoE decode warp256 gate/up is 2.06x faster and blocked on a measured 4.34x max-KL tail regression; the down leg is T0 bit-exact but slower

## Summary

The decode MoE GEMV pair behind `HIPENGINE_QWEN4_EXP_MOE_DECODE_WARP` and
`..._WARP_DOWN` was measured end to end, and both legs stay default-off, each
for a different and now concrete reason.

**The down leg is exactly right and slower.** The plain warp256 Q5_1 selected
weighted-sum is **T0 bit-exact** with the production incumbent: on the 18-case
admission fixture `kl_mean`, `kl_p95`, `kl_p99` and `kl_max` are all exactly
`0.0`, `top1_overall` is `1.0`, and `exact_equal` is `True` on all 18 cases
(1,494 rows). The source reading that predicted this is confirmed - the kernel
reproduces the incumbent's reduction tree exactly, and `dequant_q5_1`'s
`d*q + m` contracts to the same `fmaf` the warp256 writes explicitly. It is
nevertheless **13.7% slower** (121.313 us / 101.292 GB/s vs 104.727 us /
117.334 GB/s at production geometry), because `__launch_bounds__(384, 2)`
reserves 12 warps for one warp per selected row while the production top-k is
10, leaving 2 of 12 warps idle. The row's premise - that widening the down
GEMV reaches the in-block comparator - is refuted.

**The gate/up leg is a real 2.06x win and a real numerical regression.** The
candidate (`gguf_q4_k_selected_dual_q8_1_dp4a_silu_warp256`) runs at
**353.831 GB/s** against the promoted dp4a's **171.594 GB/s** at production
geometry, drift one bf16 ulp at the maximum and `0.0` at p999. On DE-1's own
traffic model that recomputes to **2.792 ms/step**, about **5.9% of the
47.359 ms/step decode kernel total**. It is not promoted: measured against a
strict teacher on the same fixture it is **4.34x worse on the max row KL** than
the incumbent on `heldout_mixed_review` (0.26119 vs 0.06017) and **3.55x worse**
on `mixed_ja_en_review` (0.19023 vs 0.05364), and on the first of those it
spends a top-1 flip the incumbent does not make (0.9880 vs 1.0000). The 2026-09-10
rejection at the admission suite was correct.

Two secondary results change how the row should be read. First, the 18-case
admission suite is **also failed by the production incumbent** (three cases
exceed the 0.05 ceiling: 0.07849, 0.06017, 0.05364), so it is a stress suite
whose bars do not discriminate between the two arms, and the original rejection
was reached on a basis the shipped default also fails. Second, DE-1's
**184.66 GB/s comparator was not a roof**: the warp256 is 1.92x above it, so the
2026-09-29 conclusion that 93.7% of headroom was consumed and only 0.591 ms/step
remained understated the remaining opportunity roughly five-fold.

## Validation

- **Speed, both legs:** `scripts/qwen4exp_moe_gemv_decode_screen.py --pairs 12`
  at geometry hidden 2560 / ffn 640 / experts 512 / top_k 10 on real weights,
  host gfx1151, compiler `HIP version: 7.15.26333-0000000`. Four arms recorded
  with `median_us`, `achieved_bandwidth_gbs`, `ref_sha256` and drift.
- **T0 proof:** `--packet down` on the 18-case fixture, 1,494 rows, all four KL
  statistics exactly `0.0` and `exact_equal` on every case.
- **Binding gate:** `--packet dual --teacher` against a strict teacher emitted by
  `--emit-teacher` (`ExecutionProfile.STRICT`, both flags zeroed), comparison
  string `strict teacher (production-versus-strict, 6.1)`. Both arms reported,
  so the tail is attributed to the arm that owns it rather than inferred.
- **Required diagnosis:** exceedances are isolated (2 and 7 of 83 steps on the
  two regressing cases), minimum top-5 overlap is 3-4 of 5 and the same on cases
  with `kl_max < 0.01`, minimum logit margin is 0.0335-0.1686, and both arms are
  deterministic. This is a route-flip sensitivity, not per-element error.
- **Unit tests:** `tests/test_unit_qwen4exp_arithmetic_recovery.py` and
  `tests/test_unit_qwen4exp_disabled_selector_ranking.py` pass (8 tests) on the
  unmodified tree. No test asserts the candidate's off-state.

## Next

- The 2.06x is worth recovering without inflating the `mixed_ja_en` tail. The
  candidate's in-block drift is one bf16 ulp, so the tail is route-flip
  sensitivity: the two regressing cases are exactly the ones where the router's
  expert choice moves. A follow-up should localize which layer and which expert
  transition flips, then test whether a narrower layer scope (the same shape as
  `HIPENGINE_QWEN4_EXP_Q4_DP4A64_LAYERS`, which already excludes 5 of 48 layers)
  can avoid the sensitive layers while keeping most of the speed.
- `docs/EXECUTION-PROFILES.md` 6.1 cites the 996-row canonical packet as the
  passing reference for this flag. That fixture does not contain

  added to the reference packet.
