# the dense term is 8.9 TFLOP/s with a 1.8x spread, and the lm head is not the cost

## Summary

`scripts/gemma4_prefill_dense_probe.py` wraps `launch_gguf_linear`, the single funnel
every dense projection and the lm head passes through, and times each shape at 512 prompt
tokens. **189.8 ms of a 693 ms prefill, 27.4 percent** -- consistent with the census's
184.85 ms.

**Total: 1.69 TFLOP over 189.8 ms, 8.9 TFLOP/s average, with a 1.8x spread from 6.53 to

## The lm head is not the cost, and the hypothesis was wrong

The plan going in assumed the lm head dominated: 262144 vocab x 2816 hidden x 512 tokens
x 2 is 756 GFLOP on 784 MB of weights, an arithmetic intensity of 482 MAC per byte, so it
should be compute-bound and expensive.

**It is 3.51 ms.** Prefill computes logits for the **last token only**, so the shape is one
row, not 512 -- 1.48 GFLOP, and at 0.42 TFLOP/s that is a **GEMV**, not a GEMM. Its weight
stream is 784 MB over 3.51 ms = **223 GB/s, 87 percent of the 256 GB/s peak.** The lm head
is running where it should and there is nothing in it to recover. The 756 GFLOP figure was
the right arithmetic for the wrong shape.

## Where the dense term's spread is

**Two shapes are at 6.5 TFLOP/s against a family best of 11.90, and together they are
62.9 ms of the 189.8** -- a third of the term:

- `4096 -> 2816`, 25 calls, **44.79 ms at 6.59** -- the single largest dense term
- `8192 -> 2816`, 5 calls, 18.10 ms at 6.53

**The spread is not monotonic in K.** `2112 -> 2816` reaches 11.90 at K = 2112 while
`2816 -> 2112` reaches 10.38 at the same K, and `8192 -> 2816` is slow at K = 8192 while
`2816 -> 8192` is fast at K = 8192. So it is not a K-loop depth effect and the two shapes'
common factor is the **input width being 4096 or 8192 with an output width of 2816** --
both are wide-in, narrow-out. `2816 -> 8192` is the reverse and it is fast.

**If the two slow shapes reached the family's best rate the term would fall by about 28 ms,
4 percent of the prefill.** That is an estimate from a rate the shapes may not be able to
reach, not a measurement, and the shapes have not been run in isolation.

## Next

- **The two slow shapes are the dense term's addressable part, and neither has been run
  in isolation.** `4096 -> 2816` at 25 calls and `8192 -> 2816` at 5 calls are 62.9 ms at
  6.5 TFLOP/s against 11.90 elsewhere in the same family. A shape sweep on
  `scripts/gguf_q8_0_dense_bw_microbench.py` would say whether the rate is a property of
  the shape or of the call site -- the two differ in which layer they come from, and 25
  against 5 calls is the full-attention against sliding-window split.
- **Do not spend time on the lm head.** It is at 87 percent of DRAM peak for the traffic
  it must move, and the one shape that could have been expensive is not the shape it runs.
- **The prefill's three terms are now all priced**: experts 287.7 ms at 49 GB/s of weight
  traffic, dense 189.8 ms at 8.9 TFLOP/s, attention 173.97 ms. The dense term has the
  largest internal spread of the three and is the only one where a shape-level rate gap
  has been found rather than ruled out.
