# RT-1's short-request blocker does not reproduce, and my mechanism claim was wrong

## Summary

RT-1's second blocker was `RT-3`'s recorded short-request cost at a larger chunk.
Measured: **it does not reproduce.** A short request pays nothing - not time, not
memory - for chunk 4096. And my own explanation for why was wrong.

## Result

`code-p512`, three repetitions per arm:

| | samples (s) | median | peak allocation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| chunk 1024 | 2.0074, 2.0059, 2.0525 | 2.0074 | 83,857,109,608 B |
| chunk 4096 | 2.0114, 2.0057, 2.0379 | 2.0114 | **83,857,109,608 B** |

Wall time +0.20%, which is **0.08x** the larger within-arm spread, with the arms
overlapping. `token_id` and `logits_sha256` identical.

**Peak allocation is byte-identical.** I predicted +4.96 GB and was wrong.

## Why, from the code

`allocate_scratch` sizes every owner at `rows = min(chunk_size, max_sequence_length)`,
and the harness sets `max_sequence_length = max(768, prompt_tokens + 1)`:

| case | `max_sequence_length` | chunk 1024 | chunk 4096 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `code-p512` | **768** | min(1024, 768) = **768** | min(4096, 768) = **768** |
| `code-p4096` | 4097 | min(1024, 4097) = **1024** | min(4096, 4097) = **4096** |

For any prompt <= 1023 tokens both chunk sizes resolve to the same row count, so
they are the same execution and allocate the same workspace. The +4.96 GB appears
only once the sequence length exceeds 1024 - exactly when the larger chunk does
more work and buys the -4.94%.

## Correction to my own reasoning

I wrote that a shape-based policy would have to make *the allocation* shape-aware
rather than the chunk loop, and called that "a different and larger change than
RT-3 assumes". **That was wrong** - the allocation is already shape-aware, through
`max_sequence_length`. I had the `min(chunk, max_seq_len)` expression right and
assumed `max_seq_len` was constant across runs; it is prompt-derived.

I stated that claim as reasoning without checking the caller, and the measurement
is what caught it. Worth recording as the second time this session that a
confident mechanism claim of mine did not survive contact with the artifact.

## What this means

`RT-3`'s short-request objection does not reproduce on this host and
configuration. The remaining question is a **single memory-admission one**: can
the host afford an 89.40 GB peak on long prompts, where the +4.96 GB is paid by
the prompt that receives the -4.94%? That is narrower than "needs a shape-based
policy" and different in kind.

**Still not promoted.** No default changed. This removes a blocker and narrows the
remaining one; it does not settle it.
