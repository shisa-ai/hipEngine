# Unresolved: canonical and heldout report the prefill scope 500000x apart

## Summary

Found while reading the per-scope breakdown of the two X-7 heldout runs side by
side. **Not resolved, and recorded rather than guessed at.**

| scope | canonical (512/1024/4096 tok, 128 trans) | heldout (39-71 tok, 24 trans) |
|---|---|---|
| `shape=prefill_last` / `transition=prefill_to_c1` | **4.41452e-09** (12 rows) | **2.06179e-03** (18 rows) |
| `shape=c1` / `transition=steady` | 3.82518e-04 (1536 rows) | 1.99214e-04 (432 rows) |

`teacher_step == 0` is trajectory index 0, the **post-prefill** logits
(`scripts/execution_profile_gdn_calibration.py:190-203`). The route under test is
prefill-only: `_wmma_prefill_dispatch` declines at `rows <= 1`, so it cannot touch
a decode step.

## Why the canonical column does not read cleanly

Its post-prefill logits are identical to the exact chain to f32 precision
(4.4e-09, max 2.6e-08), while its decode logits differ by 3.8e-04. But a
prefill-only route can only reach the decode steps **through** the prefill -
through the KV cache and hidden state it produced. If the prefill output were
truly identical, the decode steps it feeds would be identical too.

So one of two things holds, and they are not equivalent:

1. The canonical post-prefill row is not actually comparing the two arms. That
   would be an accounting defect, and it would mean the canonical
   `prefill_to_c1` scope has never measured the candidate - while reporting a
   pass.
2. The post-prefill **logits** coincide while the post-prefill **hidden state**
   differs, so the row is a real measurement of a quantity that happens not to
   move.

The heldout column cannot separate them: there the post-prefill logits do differ
(2.06e-03), which is consistent with either.

## Why it is not resolved here

Distinguishing them needs the prefill hidden state compared directly, or the
canonical post-prefill row re-derived from the candidate's own runner rather than
from the trajectory arrays. I did not want to assert a mechanism from adjacent
code again - that is the error this packet has already recorded three times.

## Why it is worth recording rather than leaving

Possibility 1 would invalidate a scope the canonical protocol reports as
**passing**, which is the same class of defect as the two false passes and the
qualification-claim over-reach already recorded in this packet. A green scope
that never measured the candidate is worse than a red one.

What is not in doubt: the heldout run engages, is complete, and fails the
per-scope KL gate at the prefill->c1 transition.
