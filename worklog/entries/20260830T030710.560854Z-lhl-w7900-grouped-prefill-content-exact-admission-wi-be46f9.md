# W7900 Qwen3.8: grouped prefill measured content-exact and +107%..+176% at C4-C8

## Summary

Declaring `GGUF_C2_PACKED_PREFILL_MAX_ROWS=8` on the real mixed-length suite is a
large, content-exact admission win. Same protocol, same engine code, two packets
differing only by the capability: **160/160 generated-id comparisons identical** (80 AR
+ 80 MTP cells), and the C8 admission wall falls **23.207 s -> 9.030 s (2.57x)**.
hipEngine's prefill row goes from 0.36x-0.74x of llama.cpp current to **0.73x-1.14x**,
beating it outright at C4 (1.13x) and C7 (1.14x). Promotion is deliberately held until
the D24 AR/K3 refresh confirms the complete-wall rows.

## Changes

Evidence only - no code change in this entry. The capability remains undeclared; this

Correctness: per-cell `generated_ids` compared across the two packets - 0 mismatches in
80 AR cells and 0 in 80 MTP cells. Grouping changes only how rows are packed, so id
equality is the expected and observed result; had it failed, the capability would stay
undeclared.

Attribution, since the arms differ by more than a flag: C1 is the control on itself -
one request, nothing to group - and moves **+0.12%**, which is the same-session noise
band. Every gain above it scales with the number of lanes that can be admitted
together, and the run's own progress (widths 1-4 in 44 s vs the control's pace) matched
the per-width pattern. This is grouping, not drift.

Two honest caveats:
- C5 and C8 dip below C4/C7 (312.7 vs 318.4; 397.7 vs 426.7): grouping depends on how
  many lanes are live in the same scheduler tick, so the benefit is not monotone in
  width. The published row must be a fresh single packet, not a splice.
- Cross-session drift on this row is ~4.9% (older pre-promotion packet
  `/tmp/gfx1100-qwen38-d1-renamed.json` gives 142.2-147.3), while same-session repeats
  agree to 0.41%. Gains of 14-176% clear both bands.
