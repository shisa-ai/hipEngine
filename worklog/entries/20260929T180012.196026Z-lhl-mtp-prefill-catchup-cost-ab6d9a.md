# MT-5 measured: the MTP prefill catch-up is 8 MiB and 0.176 ms at 1K, and it scales

## Summary

MT-5's open question was "measure our MTP prefill catch-up cost at 1K first".
Measured: **8.00 MiB retained by the caller, 8.01 MiB created by the catch-up,
0.176 ms**. The cost is linear at 8 KiB/token per array and it is **memory, not
time**.

## Result

CPU-only, host-side, 9 repeats after 2 warmups, `hidden_size = 2048`:

| prompt tokens | retained (caller) | catch-up peak | total | catch-up median |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1024 | 8.00 MiB | 8.01 MiB | 16.01 MiB | **0.176 ms** |
| 4096 | 32.00 MiB | 32.01 MiB | 64.01 MiB | 2.503 ms |
| 16384 | 128.00 MiB | 128.01 MiB | 256.01 MiB | 9.973 ms |

Reference approach (one lagging row): **8.0 KiB**, constant.

## The decomposition is the substance

The two memory terms have different causes, and adding them into one headline
number would overstate the finding:

- **`retained_hidden_bytes` is not caused by the catch-up.** It is the prompt's
  hidden rows the caller already holds because the draft is primed with them
  (`draft.prime_prompt(token_ids, result.hidden_seeds)`). Any priming approach
  pays it.
- **`catchup_peak_bytes` is the catch-up's own marginal cost** - the right-shifted
  `concat([zero, hidden[:-1]])` copy, which exists so draft row `i` reads the
  target hidden from position `i-1`.

So the catch-up itself costs **8.01 MiB and 0.176 ms at 1K**, not 16 MiB.

## What it means

- **Time is not the issue**: 0.176 ms at 1K, 9.973 ms at 16K, small against a
  prefill of the same length.
- **Memory is, and it scales**: at 8 KiB/token per array, a 128K context holds
  **1 GiB retained plus 1 GiB copied** for the catch-up alone, where the reference
  holds 8 KiB.
- **The gap is structural, not a tuning miss**: the reference keeps one lagging
  row and replays the final chunk; we materialize the whole prompt's hidden state
  and a shifted copy of it. Closing it changes how the draft is primed.

## Measurement honesty

The engine has `_llama_cpp_mtp_catchup_rows` in
`hipengine/generation/qwen35_gguf.py` and the bench harness has its own
`llama_cpp_mtp_catchup_rows` in `scripts/gguf_mtp_bench.py`. I measured the
**engine's**, and the script asserts the two agree on every case, so the number
cannot drift away from the implementation the engine runs.
