# Gemma 4 GPU path cannot be bf16-resident: the projections must go through quantized dispatch

## Summary

Every Gemma 4 gfx1100 kernel built so far — rotary, MoE, router, expert forward,
layer forward, attention, the KV-cache path — takes **bf16 weight pointers**. The
target artifact cannot supply them. Measured on the real file, a bf16-resident
Gemma 4 26B-A4B is **50.5 GB** against a **48 GB** W7900.

The projections have to route through the existing GGUF quantized linear
dispatch. The refactor is bounded and mechanical: **14 call sites**.

This was found before writing the runner, which is the cheapest possible time to
find it. No kernel is wrong — the weight *interface* is.

## The measurement

`gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-UD-Q4_K_XL.gguf`, 658 tensors, 17.00 GB:

| ggml type | GB | params | bits/param |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q4_K | 8.28 | 14.72 B | 4.50 |
| Q5_1 | 5.52 | 7.36 B | 6.00 |
| Q8_0 | 2.80 | 2.64 B | 8.50 |
| Q5_K | 0.35 | 0.51 B | 5.50 |
| F32 | 0.05 | 11.5 M | 32.00 |
| **total** | **17.00** | **25.23 B** | 5.39 |

Resident cost for the same 25.23 B parameters:

- bf16 — **50.5 GB**, exceeds 48 GB
- f32 — 100.9 GB

`UD-Q4_K_XL` is not a single quant. It is four quant types plus f32 norms, and
**Q5_1 alone is 7.36 B parameters** — the second-largest group. Any quantized
plan has to cover Q4_K, Q5_1, Q8_0 and Q5_K, not Q4_K alone.

## Why the bf16 kernels are still worth keeping

They are not wasted, and they are not the wrong shape. They are the arithmetic
that sits *around* the projections — rotary, the RMSNorm family, the router
selection, the expert group-scatter, the gelu-tanh gate, the weighted
accumulate, attention. Those are weight-free or near-weight-free and are
unchanged by quantization. What changes is only how a `(rows, in) x (in, out)`
projection is computed.

The layer forward is also already orchestration rather than one fused kernel: it
launches each projection as a separate `dense_gemv_out_bf16` call. That is what
makes this refactor small.

## The refactor

`launch_gguf_linear(weight: GGUFDeviceWeight, x_ptr, out_ptr, rows, in_features,
out_features, ...)` in `hipengine/runtime/gguf_linear.py` is the established
entry point, and it already covers Q4_K, Q5_1, Q5_K and Q8_0 with both
decode-shaped GEMV and prefill MMQ variants. The change is to pass it a
quantized weight descriptor instead of a bf16 pointer.

Call sites, all in `hipengine/kernels/hip_gfx1100/gemma4/`:

| File | Sites | Projections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gemma4_layer.py` | 8 | q, k, v, o, dense gate, dense up, dense down |
| `gemma4_experts.py` | 5 | per-expert gate_up, per-expert down |
| `gemma4_router.py` | 1 | router projection (`qwen35_router_logits_bf16_f32w`) |

`Gemma4LayerPointers` stops holding raw `int` weight pointers for those seven
fields and holds weight descriptors instead. The geometry fields
(`num_heads`, `num_kv_heads`, `head_dim`, `scale`, `k_eq_v`) are unaffected.

The expert projections are the awkward part: they are *per-expert* gathers over
a stacked `(num_experts, ...)` tensor, so the dispatch needs a per-expert weight
view. The repo already has that shape in
`kernels/hip_gfx1100/quant/gguf_q4_k_selected_pack8_gemv.hip` and friends, which
take a selected-expert index — that is the family to reach for rather than
building a bf16 staging buffer per expert.

## Dispatch coverage is already complete on the raw layout

The qwen35 materializer is 2138 lines, but most of that is a resident-layout
repack system (pack8, t16, x8, qmicro) that converts GGUF blocks into tile
layouts. **None of it is required here.** `launch_gguf_linear` also serves the
``raw`` layout, where the GGUF bytes go to device unmodified and the kernel reads
the blocks as stored.

Resolving dispatch for each type in the artifact, raw layout, backend
``hip_gfx1100``:

| quant | rows=1 (decode) | rows=8 (prefill) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gguf_q4_k` | raw | raw |
| `gguf_q5_1` | raw | raw |
| `gguf_q5_k` | raw | raw |
| `gguf_q8_0` | raw | raw |

All four resolve, at both shapes. So the artifact is fully servable without a
repack step, and the Gemma 4 device materializer can be a small raw loader rather
than a 2000-line port of the qwen35 one. Repacking stays available as a later
performance step.

This is the check that makes the refactor's size knowable: the 14 call sites are
the work, and the loading side is a raw tensor upload.

## What this does not change

- The KV-cache work and the decode-shaped attention are unaffected.
- The scratch-as-capacity contract is unaffected.
- Correctness validation against the CPU reference stands — the reference is
  validated against llama.cpp on this same artifact (see
  `20260922T113807.872355Z-lhl-gemma4-0da24f.md`), and it streams from disk in
  f32 so it was never bf16-resident either.

## Rejected alternative

**Dequantize to bf16 on load.** 50.5 GB does not fit. Even a hybrid keeping only
the experts quantized needs the quantized expert path anyway, so it is the same
work with an extra residency rule to maintain.

**Use a bf16 or Q8_0 artifact instead.** Q8_0 would fit at ~27 GB, but the
requested artifact is `UD-Q4_K_XL`, and its uploader-specific mix is the point
of the exercise. Picking a different artifact to fit the kernels is backwards.
